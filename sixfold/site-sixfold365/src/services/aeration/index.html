Aeration & overseeding.

Core aeration pulls plugs out of the turf with hollow tines, so water, air and fertilizer reach the root zone instead of running off compacted ground.

Core aeration · Overseeding · Seed & fertilizer · Mowing · Edging

It is a punch, not a drill. Each tine is a short open-ended tube that goes straight down and lifts a plug of soil and thatch out with it.

We spread grass seed straight after aerating, the order Colorado State University Extension describes for thickening a thin lawn. The seed goes into open holes rather than onto the surface.

Good to know

Three things before we come.

Water if you can

A little water a day or two before helps the machine go deeper. If you can't, no problem: we can run your sprinklers for a few minutes when we arrive.

The plugs stay on the lawn

They look a little messy for a week or two, then break down back into the grass. That's part of the benefit.

Spring and fall work best

Any time the ground isn't frozen, but not when it's hot and dry.

Alongside it

The regular keeping.

Mowing, edging and fertilizing: the regular work that keeps a lawn the way you want it, on the same visit as the aeration or on its own.

Shrubs and hedges, plants taken out and bed clean-up are landscaping, and can be done at the same time.
