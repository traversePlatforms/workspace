# Post 5 — It was a grass fire, in December, and it took 1,084 homes

**Topic:** The Marshall Fire — why wildfire risk on the Front Range is not only a mountain-cabin problem
**Audience:** Suburban and plains-edge homeowners who assume this doesn't apply to them
**Register:** Friendly neighbourhood, information-first. The hardest of the five to keep out of fear selling — see the cut lines.
**Platform fit:** Nextdoor, local/HOA Facebook groups, especially subdivisions backing onto open space.
**Status:** Draft. Not published, not posted, not scheduled.

---

## The post

The most destructive fire in Colorado history didn't happen in a forest, and it didn't happen in fire season.

The Marshall Fire started on 30 December 2021, in cured grass near Marshall Road, with relative humidity around 20% and wind gusts running 70 to 100 mph. It reached Superior and Louisville in under an hour. By the next morning it had burned about 6,000 acres — small, as fires go — and destroyed 1,084 homes and seven commercial buildings, with another 149 homes damaged. Two people died. It snowed the following afternoon.

What made it possible was ordinary. A wet first half of 2021 grew tall, thick grass. Then July through December turned out to be the second warmest and by far the driest stretch in Denver's records since 1872. Fuel, then drought, then a windstorm.

The reason to bring it up isn't to alarm anybody. It's that "we're not in the mountains, so this isn't about us" is the most common thing homeowners say about wildfire on the Front Range, and Marshall is the reason it doesn't hold. Grass, a fence line, a mulch bed and a wind event are enough.

If your yard backs onto open space, the same five feet around the house matters here as it does in Evergreen.

## Lines cut, and why

- **"Could your neighbourhood be next?"** — Fear selling, and the exact register the plan rules out. The facts are frightening enough stated flat; a rhetorical question turns reporting into a pitch.
- **"Over $2 billion in insurance claims."** — Verified and available, but money is the wrong unit here. The 1,084 homes number does the work, and the dollar figure pulls the post toward the insurance topic covered in post 1.
- **"Don't wait until it's too late."** — Every competitor's copy contains this sentence. It says nothing and it dates the post instantly.
- **"Homes with 30 to 50 feet of maintained lawn between the grass and their fences fared better."** — This is reported from the Superior after-action report and would be the single most useful sentence in the post if it held up. It was **not** found in the Boulder County report that was read in full, and the Superior document was not opened. Cut pending verification; **retrieve it, because it converts the post from history into instruction.**
- **Photographs of the burn.** Not ours, not cleared, and reusing news imagery of a disaster to advertise a service is a bad look regardless of licensing.

## Fact-check log

**Verified — Marshall Fire Operational After-Action Report** (Boulder County, PDF read directly, 55 pages):

- Fire dispatched **11:05 a.m., 30 December 2021**, near Marshall Road and Eldorado Springs Road; "it spread rapidly in **cured grass fuels** (dry grass materials) with **relative humidity around 20%** and **winds up to 100 mph**."
- Wind gusts of **70 to 100 mph** at the base of the foothills, produced by a mountain wave.
- **1,084 homes and seven commercial structures destroyed; 149 homes and 30 commercial structures damaged.**
- **Two fatalities**, both of whom had been contacted by law enforcement during evacuation.
- Fire size estimated at **6,000 acres** by the morning of 31 December; winds dropped below 10 mph that morning and **four to eight inches of snow** fell that afternoon.
- A wet first half of 2021 produced "lush and tall grass growth"; **1 July to 29 December 2021 ranked 2nd warmest and by far the driest in Denver's recorded history (since 1872)**.
- Crews on Trail Ridge Drive "put out small fires in yards, on decks, on fences, trees, but efforts were overcome"; wind made it "difficult to stand up straight or open vehicle doors," with near-zero visibility.

**Verified — Colorado General Assembly 2026 tax expenditure evaluation (2026-TE4):** citing the Rocky Mountain Insurance Information Association, the Marshall Fire "resulted in insurance claims of over $2 billion, with more than 1,200 properties lost or damaged." Not used — see cut lines.

**Corrected during research:**

- **Structure counts vary by source and by what is being counted.** The Boulder County AAR gives 1,084 destroyed / 149 damaged for homes; the legislative evaluation, via RMIIA, gives "more than 1,200 properties lost or damaged," which is the combined figure. Both are right about different things, and quoting one number as the other is how this gets misreported. **The post uses the AAR's breakdown and says which is which.**
- The often-repeated "6,000 acres" is the size **estimated on the morning of 31 December**, not a final mapped perimeter. Stated as such in the post.

**Deliberately not used:**

- **The FEMA Mitigation Assessment Team homeowner's guide** (April 2025), which is the strongest available source on *why individual houses* burned or survived in this fire. **fema.gov returned HTTP 403 to a browser user agent** and the PDF could not be opened. This is the single highest-value retrieval outstanding on this post — it is the difference between a history post and a defensible-space post. **Try a mirror, or a browser fetch by hand.**
- **The NIST investigation.** Searched for and not located as a published Marshall Fire report; the NIST document that surfaced was an unrelated annual report. Not cited.
- **Cause of ignition.** Investigated, litigated, and reported with varying confidence across outlets. Irrelevant to the point the post makes, and naming a cause invites an argument in the comments that has nothing to do with defensible space.
- **Any comparison between Marshall and a named current neighbourhood.** Naming streets that "look like Superior did" is fear selling with an address attached.

**Unresolved:**

- **The 30–50 ft maintained-lawn observation** attributed to the Superior after-action report. Retrieve and verify; it is the most actionable finding in the whole Marshall literature for this audience.
- **Whether the Boulder County AAR and the Superior AAR are the same document.** The two URLs found carry near-identical titles and the Boulder County copy is the one read. The fence and lawn observation appears in search results attributed to Superior's and is absent from Boulder County's, which suggests they differ.
