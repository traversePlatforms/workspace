# Post 1 — Your insurer's wildfire risk score, and what the new law makes them tell you

**Topic:** HB25-1182 / C.R.S. 10-4-124 — the annual risk-score notice, the appeal, and the re-score request
**Audience:** Front Range homeowners holding an insurance notice
**Register:** Friendly neighbourhood, information-first. No fear selling, no premium promises.
**Platform fit:** Nextdoor, local/HOA Facebook groups. Also a door-conversation opener.
**Status:** Draft. Not published, not posted, not scheduled.

---

## The post

If you've had a letter from your home insurer this year with a wildfire risk score on it, that letter is new — and it comes with a few rights most people don't know they have.

Since 1 July 2026, Colorado law requires any insurer that uses a wildfire risk model or score to price, renew or decline a homeowners policy to tell you in writing: your score, the full range of scores your property could have been given, where yours sits in that range, which features of your property drove it, and what each mitigation action would do to it. Policyholders get it with the renewal offer. Applicants get it within 15 days of a completed application.

Two parts people miss.

You can appeal it. If you think the score or the discount is wrong and you have evidence of work you've done, you appeal directly to the insurer. They have to acknowledge it in writing within 10 calendar days and give you a written decision within 30.

And you don't have to wait for renewal. If you've done mitigation work since you last renewed, you can ask for a revised score, and the insurer has 30 days to produce one.

Insurers also have to publish which actions earn a discount and how much each one is worth.

This isn't insurance advice — read your own notice and talk to your carrier. If it's useful, I'm happy to point anyone to the statute.

## Lines cut, and why

- **"Your premium is going up whether you act or not."** — Fear selling and a prediction we cannot support. Plan §2 register rule.
- **"We can get your score lowered."** — We cannot promise a score change or a premium change. The statute entitles a homeowner to a re-score, not to a better one.
- **"Book a free assessment before renewal season."** — Urgency theatre, and Nextdoor penalises it. The information alone earns the reply.
- **"Insurers are using secret algorithms to drop Coloradans."** — True-adjacent and inflammatory. The models are trade secrets under the act, but that framing invites a fight rather than a conversation.
- **"The IBHS Wildfire Prepared Home designation is named in the statute, so get certified."** — Accurate but leads a homeowner toward a certification we cannot supply or verify for them. Held for a longer piece.

## Fact-check log

**Verified — enacted statute text read in full** (HB25-1182 signed act PDF, extracted from leg.colorado.gov bill file 40750, adding C.R.S. 10-4-124):

- Effective 1 July 2026; applies to policies **issued or renewed on or after** that date — SECTION 2.
- Annual written notice required of any insurer that provides a mitigation discount or uses a wildfire risk model or risk score to underwrite, nonrenew, price, create a rate differential, or surcharge — §(6)(a).
- Notice contents, all five: plain-language explanation (including that insurers use different models and ranges); the range of possible scores; **the relative position of this property's score within that range**; a written explanation of why, **identifying the primary features of the property that influenced it**; and the impact each property-specific or community-level mitigation action could have — §(6)(b)(I)–(V).
- Delivery timing: applicants **no later than 15 days** after a completed application; policyholders **in the offer of renewal**; non-renewed policyholders **with the nonrenewal notice**; and on request after new mitigation, **no later than 30 days** — §(7)(a)–(d).
- Appeal: acknowledge receipt **in writing within 10 calendar days**; respond with reconsideration and decision **within 30 calendar days**; denied appeals forwarded to the commissioner on request — §(8).
- Insurers must post publicly the available discounts, the actions that earn them, **the amount attached to each action**, and the appeal process — §(5).
- Applies to homeowner's policies, residential condominium units, multifamily residential housing, and the Colorado FAIR Plan — §(9), §(10).

**Corrected during research:**

- The **"10 days / 30 days"** appeal timeline is widely repeated by insurance-agency blogs and an SEO insurance-news site. Rather than cite those, the figures here come from the statute itself, which confirms them and specifies **calendar** days. Same for the 15-day and 30-day score-delivery windows, which the secondary write-ups omit entirely.
- Secondary sources describe the notice as arriving "at application, renewal, or nonrenewal." The statute is more specific and more useful — the **re-score on request** in §(7)(d) is the actionable one and does not appear in any secondary source read.

**Deliberately not used:**

- **Any claim about what a discount is worth**, in dollars or percent. The act requires each insurer to publish its own figures; there is no statewide number, and inventing one would be exactly the error the research standard exists to prevent.
- **The statutory definition of "property-specific mitigation action"** — §(1)(c) ties it to the IBHS "Wildfire Prepared Home" designation or a similar programme **with a verification and certification process**. This is genuinely important and slightly awkward: §(3)(a) separately requires insurers to account for defensible space, but the defined term leans on certification. Left out of the post because a homeowner cannot act on it cleanly and Sixfold holds no certification to offer. **Flagged for the funding one-pager and for the §4 item 5 licensing question.**
- **Premium-increase statistics.** The 2026 legislative evaluation cites RMIIA figures on Colorado premium increases; they are real but they are hail-driven, and using them here would imply a wildfire causation the source does not support.

**Unresolved:**

- **Whether the Division of Insurance has adopted implementing rules** under §(11). Not checked. Would affect notice formatting and possibly the appeal process.
- **What the notices actually look like in the field.** Six weeks of them have landed; nobody at Sixfold has seen one. Worth collecting a real example (with the homeowner's permission and details removed) before this post runs — it would sharpen every line.
