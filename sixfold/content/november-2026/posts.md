## Thu, Nov 5, 3:00 pm: See grass through the leaves? Mow them in.

**Picture:** `posts/2026-11-05-leaves-mow-in.png`. Gemini scenery picture, no people, no results. On it: kicker "Fallen leaves", headline "See grass through the leaves? Mow them in.", line "Chopped fine with the mower, a thin layer of leaves goes back into the soil.", (720) 830-4553, 365 badge.
**Season:** Seasonal by design: the picture shows fallen leaves. The words on it are seasonless.

**Facebook**

> What to do with the leaves on your lawn.
>
> CSU Extension's rule of thumb: if you can still see some grass through the leaves, the layer isn't too thick to mulch. Raise the mower to its highest setting, take the bag off and go over it at least twice, so the leaves are chopped fine rather than left whole. Research CSU cites found that mowing leaves in can raise the soil's organic matter.
>
> A thick mat is a different story. Snow mold tends to show up as the snow melts where leaves and debris piled up on the lawn over fall and winter.
>
> Too many to mow in? We do fall clean-ups. Message us here, or text a picture of your lawn to (720) 830-4553 for a free quote.

**Instagram**

>
> #coloradolawn #fallcleanup #frontrange

**Sources:** CSU Extension GardenNotes #655, Using Leaves in the Landscape (O'Connor; reviewed July 2024): see-grass rule, highest setting, bag off, two passes, shred not whole, organic matter; CSU PlantTalk 1543, Gray Snow Mold in Lawns: appears where leaves and debris accumulated over fall and winter.

**Season:** Seasonal by design: winter advice ("dry spell, no snow"); dormant lawn and bare tree in the picture.

**Facebook**

> Winter watering, the CSU way.
>
> CSU Extension notes that snow cover is often lacking here from October through March, so lawns, trees and shrubs can need some water over winter. Its advice: water during long dry spells with no snow cover, about once or twice a month, only when the air and soil are above 40°F, and at midday so it soaks in before the night freeze. New lawns are most at risk, and south- or west-facing spots and windy yards dry out faster.
>
> Check your water rules first. Denver Water customers have been under a lawn-watering ban since Oct. 1; trees, shrubs and perennials can still be watered by hand or with drip. Other water providers set their own rules.

## Tue, Nov 24, 3:00 pm: Matted, gray patches as the snow melts?

**Picture:** `posts/2026-11-24-snow-mold.png`. Gemini scenery picture, no people, no results. On it: kicker "After the snow", headline "Matted, gray patches as the snow melts?", line "Often snow mold. It rarely kills a bluegrass lawn: rake the patches lightly so they dry out.", (720) 830-4553, 365 badge.
**Season:** Seasonal by design: melting snow in the picture, late-winter advice.

**Facebook**

> Snow mold: what it is and what to do.
>
> After a long stretch of snow cover, some lawns show circular, matted patches as the snow melts, about 6 to 12 inches across, that fade from tan to gray or white as they dry. CSU Extension says it's most common on north-facing and shaded lawns, where plowed or drifted snow piled up deep, and where leaves and debris were left on the lawn over fall and winter.
>
> The good news: it rarely kills a bluegrass lawn, and CSU doesn't recommend fungicides for it. Rake the patches lightly so they dry out. If an area is slow to come back, CSU's next step is core aeration, then overseeding with the same kind of grass.
>
> Spot some this spring? Message us here, or text a picture of your lawn to (720) 830-4553 for a free quote.
