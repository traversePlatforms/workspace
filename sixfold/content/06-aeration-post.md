## The post

If a patch of your lawn greens up late, dries out first and feels hard underfoot, the problem is usually under it rather than on it.

Compacted soil stops water and fertilizer getting down to the roots — it runs off the top instead of soaking in. Core aeration is the fix. A machine fitted with hollow tines punches straight down and pulls plugs of soil and thatch out of the ground. Colorado State's guidance is holes two to three inches deep, no more than two to four inches apart, and that it only really works when actual cores come out rather than a spike pressing a hole shut.

Two things worth knowing before anyone runs one over your lawn.

**Water it a couple of days beforehand.** Dry ground will not give up a deep plug, and a shallow pass does very little.

**The plugs stay where they fall.** For a week or two the lawn looks like a goose convention has been through, then they break down back into the turf — which is the part that does the good. Sweeping them off is something done on golf greens, not home lawns.

Spring and fall are the best windows, and it can be done any time the ground is not frozen or baked hard, so there is still time this fall.

Happy to explain what to look for if you are not sure whether yours would benefit.
