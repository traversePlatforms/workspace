Everything here is yours to open without asking.

Six folders, numbered in the order you are likely to need them. If you are looking for something and it is not here, the last page says why — most of the gaps are deliberate, and a few of them matter.

01What we do

Reading in · before a first conversation

  • What Sixfold does — services, no prices. The one-page answer to "what do you actually do". No figures on it by design — safe to show anyone.
02Safety

Read once properly · then when something changes

  • Crew safety and compliance. What OSHA requires of us, which licences and certifications apply, and what is not held yet. The longest document here and the one worth your time.
  • Gear checklist. What goes on the truck.
  • Crew shirt spec. What the shirt is and why.
03Forms

On the job · print or fill in on a screen

  • Quote walk sheet, two versions — one to fill in on screen, one to print and write on. Filled in at the walk, before a price exists.
  • Job close-out sheet. Fill in the day the job ends, while you still remember. Same rows as the walk sheet on purpose — the value is the comparison.
  • What the work takes and what things cost us. Internal. A marked guess beats a blank.
  • Signing at the door — crib sheet. Internal, never hand it over. Six things to get right, and the reason each one matters.
04For the customer

Print and hand over · or email

  • Preparing your home for wildfire. The pamphlet. Most of what is in it the homeowner can do themselves, which is the point.
  • Who pays for wildfire mitigation. The funding sheet — the tax credit and the insurance law, on one page.
  • Referral packet — blank template. What goes in the folder at the end of a job.
  • What Sixfold does. Same sheet as folder 01, here because it is also a handout.
05Money

Before you answer a question about cost or credits

  • Tax credit reference notebook. What to say, and a "never say this" list. Read the never-say list even if you read nothing else.
  • Tax credit — forms the customer needs.
  • Script kit. What to say at a door and what never to say.
06Look it up

Mid-job · when you need a number or an address

  • Debris disposal directory. Every confirmed site, cheapest first. Phone ahead — rates move, and several sites bar commercial haulers.
  • Mitigation funding directory. The programmes, by county.

What is not here

And why — so nobody re-adds it by accident

Shared folder
Page 2 of 2

Two of these are not housekeeping. Read them.

The blank service agreement is not in this folder, and must not be printed from anywhere else either. Both copies still carry the old Englewood address as the seller's place of business, including on the Notice of Cancellation. A cancellation notice with the wrong address does not start the three-day clock — it leaves the job cancellable for three years, with no payment for work already done. They cannot be fixed until Sixfold's Monument street address is on file, and part-fixing them is worse than leaving them. If you need an agreement signed, call the office.

There is no launch offer yet. Two versions were drawn up and neither has been picked. Do not quote a discount, a percentage, or a "first ten properties" deal to a customer, because there is no minimum job size behind either of them.

  • Rate cards, rate sheets and hourly rates. Pricing is not in a shared folder. Two of the pricing files are marked do-not-quote because the numbers in them are known to be wrong. Prices come from the office, in writing, per job.
  • Anything naming a real customer. Past quotes and signed agreements name people, addresses and prices. They are not here, and they should not be forwarded.
  • Flyers. They carry the launch offer that has not been chosen — see above.
  • The marketing plan, the platform sheet and the start-up plan. Business planning rather than crew material. Ask if you want to read one; nothing in them is secret, they are just not what this folder is for.
  • Brand files — logo artwork, vehicle magnets, business card proofs. Ordering artefacts. Ask if you need one and a folder can be added.

If something here looks wrong

Say so rather than working around it. Several documents in this folder were corrected the day it was built, and the reason they had gone stale is that they sat in a folder nobody re-read. A document that disagrees with what you were told in person is worth a text message.

These are copies. The originals live in the project files, and this folder gets rebuilt from them — so a correction made properly reaches everyone, and a correction scribbled on a copy reaches nobody.
