Option A — we haul it away

Everything in the pile is cut down, carried out, loaded, driven off your property and disposed of. Two people, about a day and a half on site, six loads out.

What we do, in the order we do it

  • Walk it with you and agree the edge of the job. Before a saw starts we walk the pile together and mark what is in and what is out, so there is no argument later about a log at the back that nobody meant to include. Anything you want kept, you point at now.
  • Photograph the area first. Shots from fixed positions, before anything moves. These are half of the mitigation record described overleaf, and they are the reason the same photographs are worth anything to your insurer.
  • Buck the downed wood. The logs and limbs already on the ground are cut with saws into lengths two people can carry — roughly 16 inches through the heavy butt sections, longer where a limb is light enough to shoulder.
  • Gather and load by hand. The pile as it stood on the day we surveyed it, the large logs at the bottom included. Carried to the trailer rather than dragged, so the ground under it is not torn up on the way out.
  • Haul it off your property. Our truck and trailer, six trips to a wood-waste disposal site. The drive is ours; nothing is stacked at the roadside waiting for a second company.
  • Pay the tipping fees. Disposal is inside the price below and is not added to the invoice afterwards.
  • Rake down the footprint and photograph it again. The ground the pile sat on is raked out and the loose bark cleared, then shot from the same positions as the before set. You get both sets with the invoice.

The paperwork, and the help putting it together

Two pots of money exist whether or not you hire us, and both of them turn on documentation you cannot produce after the fact. That is the part we do for you, at no charge, on every job.

1 · The Colorado wildfire mitigation tax credit

The state credit covers 100% of what you pay a contractor for qualifying vegetation work, up to $1,000 for work done in 2026. Secondary treatment of woody fuels — piling, chipping, or removing it from the site is one of the four measures the statute names, and it is the statute's own wording, not ours.

What we do about it:

  • We fill in the eligibility record with you before the crew starts. Owner of record, how the land is held, and whether any grant or cost-share money is paying part of the bill — that last one changes the answer, and it is far cheaper to ask at the walk than to find out at invoicing.
  • We itemise the invoice so the qualifying work is separated from anything that is not, by the state's own measure categories, rather than handing you one number.
  • We write the work record — what was done, to which measure, and to what standard.
  • We give you the photograph log, before and after, from fixed positions, with dates.
  • We hand you the checklist for whoever prepares your return, naming the schedule the credit is claimed on and how receipts are attached. The whole file comes with the invoice, not weeks afterwards.

Where we stop: Sixfold does not prepare or file returns and does not issue a certification — no contractor can. Whether the credit applies to your return depends on your land and your income, and that is your accountant's call. Two calendar facts, neither of them ours: the Department of Revenue has not yet published the 2026 income limit, and for work done from 1 January 2027 the cap doubles to $2,000 and the credit becomes refundable.

2 · Your insurer's wildfire risk score

Since 1 July 2026, a Colorado insurer that uses a wildfire risk score to price, surcharge or non-renew your homeowners policy has to send you that score in writing once a year, explain which features of your property drove it, publish what each mitigation action is worth, and give you a discount for documented mitigation.

The part most people are never told: once new mitigation work is done you can ask for a fresh score, and the insurer has to give you one within 30 calendar days. If you think a score is wrong you can appeal it — they must acknowledge in writing within 10 calendar days and decide within 30.

What we do about it: the same photographs, invoice and work record are the documentation that request needs, and we assemble them as one package addressed to your insurer rather than leaving you to work out what they want. We will sit down and help you put the re-score request together. You send it — we do not contact your insurer for you, and nobody can tell you in advance what the discount is worth, because the law makes each insurer publish its own figures and sets no rate. If you have had a risk-score letter or a non-renewal notice, bring it out when we start: it usually names the exact thing the model does not like.

The other way to do this

The hauling is the part you can decline. Option B — we chip it on site and the chips and rounds stay with you — is $2,000, on its own sheet. The $250 between the two sheets is smaller than most people expect, and it is worth knowing why: a chipper is slower than a trailer on a pile with this much oversized wood in it, so Option B needs a second full day on site, and that day costs about what the trailer and the tipping fees cost. What the $250 buys is removal. What it costs you is the chips and the firewood.

Both sheets carry the same paperwork. Nothing on this page depends on which option you pick, and none of it is charged for either way.
