## 0 · "It is a deposit before work starts" — right, but do not print that word

Confirmed by Matthew 2026-09-15. **The intent is exactly what the contract provides**, timing
included: clause 10 and §5 both make the first $750.00 due **on or before the first day of work**,
and §7 keeps work from starting until the three-business-day cancellation period has run. So
"half, before work starts" and "the first payment" are the same event. Nothing needs changing.

**But the word `deposit` already means something else in this agreement, and it means $0.00.**

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| Agreement §5, page 1 of 5 | **"Deposit, if any, paid today — $0.00 — none"** |
| Agreement §5 / clause 10 | The first **$750.00** is due *on or before the first day of work* |
| Quote, Right to cancel | *"all payments, including any deposit, are refunded in full"* |

§5's deposit field is money handed over **at signing**. The $750 is due **later**, on or before the
first day on site. They are two different moments, and the agreement deliberately takes nothing at
the first one — which is the safer structure for a sale solicited at the residence, because it puts
every dollar on the far side of the cancellation window.

**So:** if the invoice says "Deposit $750.00" and her signed agreement says "Deposit, if any, paid
today — $0.00 — none", she has two documents using one word for two amounts. Call it **"First
payment — 50% of the fixed price, due on or before the first day of work"** on anything she sees.
Call it a deposit in conversation and internally all you like.

**In Square:** its deposit feature may label the line *"Deposit due"* on the customer's copy. Check
the preview — if it does, use the **payment schedule / instalments** option instead, which labels
them as instalments, or rename the line if Square lets you.

**The one thing that would change the paperwork:** if you decide to collect the $750 **at signing**
rather than before the first day of work, then §5's deposit field is no longer $0.00 and the
agreement has to be re-issued reading **Deposit $750.00 / Balance $750.00**. Do not collect at
signing against the current wording.

customer for money before any work has happened. No chasing language and no due date — see the
timing point in the box above.

## 2 · What to put on the invoice

**Do not invent work items for the deposit.** Nothing has been done yet: the first payment is due
*before* the first day of work. An invoice line reading "Limb 13 trees — $600" says that limbing has
been delivered.

> **The trap worth naming, because the arithmetic invites it:** limbing ($600) plus plant removal
> ($150) is **exactly $750**. It is tempting and it is wrong. It would bill two specific items as
> though they were the deposit's scope, when the agreement prices the job as one fixed sum and
> splits the *payment*, not the work. If she cancelled, or if there were ever an argument about what
> was delivered, that invoice would contradict the contract.

### Option A — recommended: full scope, 50% payment schedule

Invoice the whole $1,500.00 with the seven quoted lines, then take the first payment as a **50%
deposit or first instalment**. The line items match the quote she already has, line for line, and
the $750 is produced by the split rather than by a made-up allocation.

| # | Item | Amount |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mow three-quarter-acre property — full-property mowing | $100.00 |
| 2 | Edge and trim the entire yard — drive and walkways blown down | $0.00 *(included)* |
| 3 | Limb 13 trees — lower limbs raised, up to 4 ft | $600.00 |
| 4 | Remove 9 existing plants — pulled out, roots included | $150.00 |
| 5 | Install 6 Russian sage — labour only, on a return visit | $0.00 *(free)* |
| 6 | Remove 3 small trees — under 10 ft, felled and cut up on the ground | $250.00 |
| 7 | Haul and dispose of debris — tipping fees included | $400.00 |
| | **Invoice total** | **$1,500.00** |
| | **Due now — first payment, 50%** | **$750.00** |
| | Balance, due on completion | $750.00 |

**In Square this is native** — build the invoice at $1,500.00 and set a payment schedule of 50% now
and 50% on completion, or request a 50% deposit. Square computes the $750.00, so the two halves
cannot drift from the contract. The branded invoice header already exists at
`brand/square/sixfold-square-invoice-full-1280x648.png`.

If the tool cannot show a $0.00 line, put items 2 and 5 in the description of item 1 rather than
dropping them — *"edging, trimming and the planting visit are included at no charge"*. They are the
two lines that make the sheet persuasive; losing them silently makes the invoice look more expensive
than the quote.

### Option B — only if the lines themselves must total $750.00

Some invoicing tools will not do a deposit. Then bill **50% of each line**, and say so on every line
so it cannot be read as work completed.

| # | Item | Full | **On this invoice** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mow three-quarter-acre property — 50% first payment | $100.00 | $50.00 |
| 2 | Edge and trim the entire yard | included | $0.00 |
| 3 | Limb 13 trees, lower limbs to 4 ft — 50% first payment | $600.00 | $300.00 |
| 4 | Remove 9 existing plants — 50% first payment | $150.00 | $75.00 |
| 5 | Install 6 Russian sage, labour | free | $0.00 |
| 6 | Remove 3 small trees, under 10 ft — 50% first payment | $250.00 | $125.00 |
| 7 | Haul and dispose of debris — 50% first payment | $400.00 | $200.00 |
| | | **$1,500.00** | **$750.00** |

$50 + $300 + $75 + $125 + $200 = **$750.00**. Add a note under the table: *"This invoice is the
first of two payments on a fixed price of $1,500.00. It is not a charge for completed work; see
clause 10 of the service agreement."*

**Option A is better.** B splits every line in a way the contract does not, and a customer comparing
it against the quote has to do arithmetic to see they match. A shows her the same seven lines she
already agreed to.
