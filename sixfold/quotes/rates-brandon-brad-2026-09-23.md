## §2 — The one thing that has to be settled before anything is entered

**"labor $100 dollars an hour minimum" — is that one person's hour, or the two-person crew's hour?**

The engine prices in **man-hours** at **$70/man-hour**. So:

- **$100 per man-hour** → every price in the tool rises 43%. A two-person crew bills $200/hour,
  against Hawk's published $115–$143/crew-hour in Colorado Springs. Sixfold would be well above the
  only published local rate.
- **$100 per crew-hour** → $50/man-hour, a 29% *cut*. Below the current rate, and below the market.

The same two words move the business from clearly above market to clearly below it, and every other
figure in §1 is a price that has to be divided by this rate before it can become a time. Nothing else
can be entered accurately until it is answered.

### What the figures themselves say, which is not conclusive

Each price in §1 divided by the labour rate, converted to man-minutes, against the rate the engine
holds today:

| Line | Their figure | Implied at $100/**man**-hr | Implied at $100/**crew**-hr | Engine today |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plant removal | $16.66 / plant | **10.0** man-min | 20.0 | 12 |
| Edge | $50 / 0.75 acre | **40** man-min/acre | 80 | 60 |
| Mow | $150 / 0.75 acre | **120** man-min/acre | 240 | 150 |
| Limbing | $600 / 13 trees | **27.7** man-min/tree | 55.4 | 20 |
| One-tree job | $450 less $165 costs | **171** man-min total | 342 | 350 (mob + tree + load) |

**Read as a man-hour**, the first four land within 0.67×–1.38× of rates the engine already holds — and
**$16.66 is exactly $100 ÷ 6**, a ten-minute task, which is within 2 minutes of the engine's 12. That
number can only have been derived from a $100 hourly rate; it is the one figure in the paste that shows
its own arithmetic.

**Read as a crew-hour**, those four all run 1.33×–2.77× high — but the one-tree job total lands at
342 man-minutes against the engine's 350, which is a near-exact match and the only whole-job check here.

So the small lines favour man-hour and the only whole-job figure favours crew-hour. **Ask, do not pick.**

## §3 — Line by line: what each figure hits

### Tree felling — the largest change in the paste

**"Complete fell — 15 minutes."** The engine carries **150 man-minutes** for a 10–30 ft removal
(Wexford/TreeGuard, 2.0–3.5 man-hours at 12in DBH, badged **Competitor**). Fifteen minutes is **a tenth
of that**, and the gap is far too large to be a refinement of the same quantity.

The likeliest reading is that the two numbers measure different things: 15 minutes is **the cut** —
saw to stump — and Wexford's 2–3.5 man-hours is the tree *processed*: dropped, limbed out, bucked,
dragged to the trailer, site raked. Their own $450 supports that, because $450 cannot be built from
15 minutes of labour:

| | |
|---|---|
| Dump fee | $125 |
| One chain | $20 |
| Half a trailer of gas | $20 |
| **Costs** | **$165** |
| 15 minutes at $100/hr | $25 |
| **Total if 15 min were the whole job** | **$190** |
| **What they actually said** | **$450** |

The $285 of labour left over is **2.85 hours**, not 15 minutes. So the fell time and the job price
describe a whole job with roughly three hours of work in it — which is close to what the engine already
produces. **Entering 15 as the removal rate would underquote a tree by about 80%.** Not entering it.

### Fuel — this is a rule change, not a rate

"Half of trailer gas cost $20" is inside their price. **The engine deliberately does not bill fuel**
— it is entered as a cost, counted against margin, and shown as *not billed*, because the $70/man-hour
rate's overhead already carries vehicle and fuel. The mowing note carries the same thing: "maybe $20/30
dollars in gas."

If fuel becomes a billed line, the overhead loading in the hourly rate has to come down or it is charged
twice. Flagging rather than acting: reversing a settled rule of the engine on an aside in a price note
is not something to do quietly.

### Consumables — no line exists

"One chain is $20." The engine has **no consumables line at all** — dump, equipment hire, purchases and
fuel are the only cost inputs. A chain per tree is a real per-unit cost. This is a genuine gap the paste
has found, and adding a consumables cost line is straightforward once §2 is answered.

### Disposal — better than what the engine has, but two readings

Today the user types one dump figure. Brandon and Brad describe disposal that **scales by what is in the
trailer**, because tipping is charged by weight:

| Load | Figure |
|---|---|
| 7×14 trailer, wood-heavy | $269 |
| 7×14 trailer, mostly brush | ~$110 |
| One tree's share | $125 |
| 8×14 dump trailer of rounds | *"a lot more than that"* — **no figure given** |

That is enough for a proper preset table, and it is the single most useful structural improvement here.

**"$150-300 small trailer 150 large trailer 300 at the lowest level" is unresolved.** Two readings:
tip fees Sixfold **pays**, scaling with trailer size — or the price Sixfold **charges** per haul load.
It matters because it contradicts the line above under the first reading ($110 for a full 7×14 of brush
is below the stated $150 small-trailer minimum), and because **hauling is the one line this business has
demonstrably lost money on**. Needs an answer.

**Rounds are left out entirely.** "A lot more than that" is not a figure, and inventing one is the exact
failure mode this project has already been burned by. The engine should flag a rounds-heavy load as
unpriced rather than guess it.

### Equipment hire — clean

"Chip renter pricing, 320 for 24 hours." A real, current rental figure. Drops straight into the
equipment-hire cost line as a preset. **No conflict with job 2's $269**, which is mower $215 + edger $54
— checked, because the $269 in §1 for a wood-filled trailer is a coincidence, not a mis-mapping.

Note this prices the *machine*, not the work: **chipper feeding is still 120 man-min/hour and still
Unbacked.** Checked twice now; no Colorado operator publishes a chipping labour rate.

### Landscaping — the closest agreement in the paste

Mow $150 and edge $50 on the same ¾ acre. Read as man-hours these come out at 120 and 40 man-min/acre
against the engine's 150 and 60 — the tightest match of anything here, and the first outside
corroboration these two rates have had. Job 2 was also ¾ acre mown and edged, so this is the same
property shape.

### Limbing — this contradicts a figure that was already corroborated

**$600 for 13 trees**, against Brandon's earlier **$975 typical** for the same 13 trees on job 2.

The $975 is not just an older number — it is the **only figure in Brandon's entire market-value column
that ever found independent support**: Hawk publishes $75 as the floor for trimming a tree under 30 ft,
and 13 × $75 = $975 exactly. The new $600 is 38% below it and below Hawk's published floor.

Not overwriting one with the other. Both are Brandon's, on the same 13 trees, and which one stands is
his call.

### Scope removals

- **"No stump grinding"** — retires two lines (set-up per stump, per diameter inch) and the 811 locate
  flag with them. Both were **Unbacked**, so nothing sourced is lost.
- **"Not planting"** — retires the plant-setting line. Also Unbacked in effect.

Recommendation: mark these **not offered** in the UI rather than delete them, so the decision is visible
and reversible, and so a walk sheet that still has the field does not silently drop a count.

### Aeration — no line exists, and this is now the headline service

"¾ of an acre. Bryce said 11-1400 for aeration." Aeration leads Sixfold's positioning as of 2026-09-23,
and **the engine has no aeration line at all.** This is the biggest gap the paste exposes.

Two problems before it can be added: **who Bryce is** — he appears nowhere else in this repo, and whether
this is Sixfold's price, a subcontractor's quote or a competitor's is the difference between a rate and a
market-check figure. And **$1,100–1,400 for ¾ acre is high** for core aeration; it wants a second look
before it prices anything.

### The $450 → $600 uplift

"Estimate around $450 for that. Call it $600 to meet the average cost." That is a **33% uplift from a
cost-up figure to a market-matched price**, and the engine has no concept of it — it goes cost-up,
rounds down to $50, and stops. Worth deciding whether that uplift is a general policy or was specific to
a one-tree callout, but it is not blocking.

### §5a — Height-driven tree pricing, added the same day

Matthew asked for a tree entered by its actual height, priced per foot off the 30 ft anchor. It is in:
a line that takes **"7, 12, 25"** (or **"4x7"** for four trees the same size), prices each one, and shows
a per-tree breakdown with a 5–30 ft ladder underneath.

**The model is `base + per-foot`, both editable**, defaulting to **0 base + 1.00 man-min/ft** — which is
their 30 man-minutes divided straight across 30 ft, the pure per-foot model as asked. Raising the base
gives the other shape without a second control.

**It hard-stops at 30 ft, and that is not a preference.** Extrapolating 1 min/ft upward:

| Height | Per-foot model | Already on the books | |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 ft | 45 man-min | 330 (Wexford) | **7.3× underquote** |
| 60 ft | 60 man-min | 330 | 5.5× |
| 70 ft | 70 man-min | 570 | **8.1×** |

Anything over 30 ft is refused, listed in amber, kept out of the price, and flagged to the banded lines.
An eight-fold underquote on the biggest and most dangerous trees is not a rounding error.

**Below 10 ft the model contradicts Sixfold's own other figure**, and the page says so rather than
hiding it. A 7 ft tree prices at 7 man-minutes; the sapling line, allocated off job 2, says 30 for the
same tree. Both are Sixfold numbers. With no base time the model implies a small tree costs almost
nothing to take down, but setup, drag-out and cleanup happen whatever the height — which is the argument
for a base. **Only a real timing settles it.** A flag fires whenever a sub-10 ft tree is priced on a
zero-base line.

Kept **beside** the sapling and 10–30 ft lines rather than replacing them: job 2's calibration uses
`qty.sap`, and saved jobs carry both ids, so removing them would silently reprice every job on file. A
flag fires if both are used on one sheet.

**Two persistence keys were bumped** — the shared rate doc `config/rates-v2` → `-v3`, and the local
draft key → `-v3`. Without this the stored table silently wins over the file and the team keeps quoting
the old prices. This was the trap already on record from the hosting analysis; it applies to the
artifact's own `db` too, not just to a hosted build.

The one-tree reconciliation is the reassuring one: $550 sits between their own two figures without
