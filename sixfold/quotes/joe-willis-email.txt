Thanks again for letting me look at the pines on your property. You have a lovely home. Brandon went through the photos, videos and details we gathered, and here's what we'd suggest.

We'd recommend chipping the trees on site, in November or December. Chipping infested pine kills the larvae under the bark. The catch is that fresh pine chips give off a strong turpentine smell that draws other bark beetles, mainly the red turpentine beetle and Ips beetles. In one U.S. Forest Service study, where pines were thinned and chipped in spring, beetle attacks on the nearby trees went from about 2% to 30%.

Those beetles are still around this time of year. Red turpentine beetles can fly into the fall, and Ips beetles have been seen attacking trees on warm days as late as November. Waiting until it's colder, and keeping the chips spread thin and away from your standing pines, keeps that risk lower. The chips would stay on your property.
