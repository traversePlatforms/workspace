What to hand your accountant, and when

There is no application and no waiting list for Colorado's wildfire mitigation tax credit. It is claimed on your ordinary state income tax return, with receipts attached.

Fill this in while it is fresh

Costs paid or reimbursed by a grant or incentive do not qualify. Your accountant needs to know this figure.

The two forms it is claimed on

DR 0104Colorado Individual Income Tax Return — your return
DR 0104CRIndividual Credit Schedule — the credit is claimed here, submitted with your DR 0104

Copies of your receipts go in with the paper return, or uploaded using the E-Filer Attachment function in Revenue Online. Both forms are at tax.colorado.gov.

Two things worth knowing before you assume a number

  • Through tax year 2026 the credit cannot exceed what you owe. If your Colorado tax bill is $600 and the credit is $1,000, it is worth $600 to you. The remainder is not refunded and cannot be carried forward.
  • For work paid for in 2027 that changes. The cap doubles to $2,000 and the excess is refunded — paid out even if it is more than you owe. Which year you pay in matters.

While the folder is open — your insurer

Since 1 July 2026, a Colorado insurer that uses a wildfire risk score to price or underwrite your policy has to tell you that score in writing every year, tell you what mitigation discounts are available, count documented mitigation in how it scores and prices the property, and let you appeal the score. The same photographs and invoice are the documentation for that. What the discount is worth is a question for your carrier — the law requires one but does not set a rate.

Two rules that make the set usable

  • Same position, same framing, both visits. Note a landmark in the description so the after shot can be lined up months later.
  • Leave the camera's date stamp on. Undated photographs are worth much less in an insurance conversation, and the dates are the only thing tying the set to the invoice.

Before these go anywhere but the customer's file

Photographs of a customer's property are not marketing material by default. Written permission from the owner is needed before any of these appear in print, on a profile, or in a portfolio — ask at the walkthrough, not afterwards.
