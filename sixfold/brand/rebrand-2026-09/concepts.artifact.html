Sixfold

Ridge, aerated — core treatments

One mark, four ways of drawing the core. The spacing, the depths and everything above the ground line are identical in all four — the only variable is the core itself, so the comparison is fair. Nothing here is a master file yet.

Where this got to

The board carried six competing concepts. It now carries one. “Ridge, aerated” is the candidate and the remaining question is how the cores are drawn. The other five are retired rather than binned — they are in the repository history if anyone wants them back.

What is settled and not up for grabs in this round:

  • Five forms, five cores. The Ridge mark has four peaks and one conifer above the ground line. One core per form, directly beneath it.
  • Each core is as deep as its form is tall. Apex heights measured off the path data: 30.5 / 38 / 48 / 60 / 72. The bottom edge is the skyline inverted.
  • The turf band and its five holes. The holes are what a hollow tine leaves.
  • The ember is the conifer’s core — the one form that is not a peak, and the smallest of the five.

Every treatment below keeps a lid of turf in the band’s own colour. That is not decoration: a plug comes out of the ground with the lawn still attached, and it is the one feature that says “aeration core” rather than “shape”. It takes the band colour because it is the same turf. An earlier attempt put a Sage cap on a Moss body — 1.20:1, separated by hue alone — and it did not read at all.

What it is

Each core is the form above it turned upside down. The mirror stops being a measurement and becomes the shape.

What it costs

Purely geometric. It is the cleanest of the four and the strongest at small size, but nothing about a wedge says soil — it reads as reflection, not as a plug.

What it is

The core in pieces, two to four depending on its length, because a real plug comes up and crumbles.

What it costs

The most honest to the material and the most characterful. The gaps are the risk: they close up in embroidery and at small size the core can read as a dotted line.

What it is

Drawn in outline rather than filled, like a sample in a section drawing, with one seam across it.

What it costs

Keeps all the visual weight in the peaks and makes the drawing feel measured. Thin strokes are the first thing lost on a vehicle magnet or a thermal receipt.

Soft taper

What it is

A slight taper to a rounded tip, one thatch seam under the turf. The quietest of the four.

What it costs

Closest to the core you would actually pull. It is also the least distinctive — at a glance it is a row of pegs.

The small-size test

A favicon is 32px and an embroidered left-chest logo is about the same in thread. This row usually decides it — a treatment that dissolves here costs a second, simplified mark to maintain forever.

Treatment 1 holds best, which is what geometry buys. Treatment 2’s gaps close and it starts to read as a dashed line. Treatment 3’s strokes thin out and go grey. Treatment 4 survives but flattens into pegs.

Locked up with the wordmark

The wordmark does not change: Palatino, tight tracking, as now. Only the core beside it moves.

Palette — still open, and independent of this

Any treatment can wear any of these. Ordered by what they cost to roll out.

A — Demote the ember

No new colours beyond one deeper green. Moss becomes primary and Ember drops to an accent. Costs least — existing tokens keep their names.

B — Turf primary

A four-step green ramp, no warm colour at all. The most decisive break from fire. The risk is blandness — green-only is what most lawn companies look like.

C — Turf and wheat

Greens plus a dry-grass gold doing ember's old job. Keeps a warm note without pointing at fire. Watch the gold — #C9A44C is 1.92:1 on Bone, so large shapes only.

The live site is the one that matters. sixfoldcolorado.com went live on 21 Sep 2026 and carries the mark in the favicon, the stylesheet and the wildfire guide page. A mark change means a re-bundle and re-upload, and the host serves a stale cache after an upload — so it has to be cache-busted and verified, not assumed.

There is a precedent for the sweep: the phone-number change in September touched 57 files and 21 PDFs and came out clean. Check the vehicle magnets have not been ordered before anyone commits — it is the one item on this list that cannot be re-run.

What happens after the meeting

  1. Team picks a core treatment, and separately a palette.
  2. The winner gets drawn as a real master: full colour, one colour, and a flat print version with the overlaps resolved — the same three files Ridge already has.
  3. Prior-use screen re-run against the new drawing. The existing screen covered the Ridge mark only, and two of its checks were never run — reverse image search and the USPTO design-code search.
  4. Palette tokens updated in one place, then the generators re-run and the PDFs re-counted.
  5. Site re-bundled and cache-busted last, once everything else matches.

Nothing in this document has been published, posted or uploaded, and no master files have been overwritten. The current Ridge mark and its four SVGs are untouched.
