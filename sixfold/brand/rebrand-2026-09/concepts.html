Sixfold

Logo rebrand — concepts for review

Six directions for the mark, ordered from a palette change to a clean sheet. Nothing here is a decision and none of it is a master file — these are drawings to argue about in a room. The team picks a direction; then one gets drawn properly.

What changed, and what that asks of the mark

The positioning moved. Aeration leads, general landscaping sits beside it, and fire mitigation is still offered but stops being the headline. Limbing, land clearing, brush removal and felling all carry on — they sit under landscaping now rather than under fire.

Three services, any one of them on its own. That matters for the mark as much as for the copy: someone booking an aeration should not be looking at a logo that tells them this is a wildfire company.

Aeration here means lawn / core aeration — hollow tines, plugs pulled out of turf. Confirmed 23 Sep 2026. The project README still has this recorded as unestablished, so that entry needs correcting. Every plug, tine and perforation below rests on it — if it is wrong, say so before the team spends time on the drawings.

Why the current mark reads as fire

Worth being precise, because it decides how far you actually have to move. Two things carry the fire read, and they are not equally strong.

SignalWhere it livesHow much work it does
Ember orangeAll four peaks — most of the mark's area Most of it. Orange triangles are flames by default.
The ascending stackFour triangles climbing left to right Some. The gesture is flame-like, but in green it reads as a ridgeline.
The coniferFront left, mossNone. It is a tree.
The rootsTen strands off the right cornerNone. It is the ground.

So Concept 1 is the control. Same geometry, no orange. If the room agrees that one is already enough, this rebrand is a token swap and the rollout is cheap. If it is not, 2–6 show what buying more looks like.

One measured constraint runs through all of this. Ember and Moss sit at a contrast ratio of 1.11:1 — they are separated by hue alone. Anyone with red-green colourblindness sees one flat shape where we see two. Green on green is the same trap: #4E9A68 against #8AA07A is 1.20:1. Every concept below separates its parts by lightness rather than hue, which is also what makes them survive the one-colour and small-size tests at the end.

Three palette directions

Independent of which drawing wins — any concept can wear any of these. Ordered by what they cost to roll out.

A — Demote the ember

No new colours beyond one deeper green. The agreed five stay, Moss becomes primary, and Ember drops to an accent held to roughly a tenth of any surface.

Costs least. The existing CSS tokens keep their names, so the site, the invoice and the Square set change by editing values, not structure.

B — Turf primary

A four-step green ramp and no warm colour at all. The most decisive break from fire, and the easiest to keep accessible — the steps are tonal, so they hold in one colour and for colourblind viewers.

The risk is blandness. Green-only is what most lawn companies already look like; the distinctiveness then has to come entirely from the drawing.

C — Turf and wheat

Greens plus a dry-grass gold doing ember's old job. Keeps a warm note — useful, because dormant and drought-stressed turf is literally what a customer is looking at when they call — without pointing at fire.

Watch the gold. #C9A44C is only 1.92:1 on Bone, so it cannot carry text or thin lines on a light ground. Large shapes only.

The six concepts

Each one is shown three ways: full colour on Pine, full colour on Bone, and one colour. The one-colour tile is not decoration — it is where a vehicle magnet, an embroidered shirt and a thermal receipt live, and it is where weak marks fall apart.

01

Ridge, retinted

Palette only — no geometry change

Pine ground

Bone ground

One colour

What it signals

A ridgeline and a treeline. Nothing else changed, and the fire read is already most of the way gone — which is the finding, not a disappointment.

What it keeps

Everything. Same silhouette, same four peaks, same tree, same ten roots. Anyone who has seen the truck still recognises it.

Where it is weak

No aeration in it at all. And the tree had to move to bone or black — green on green loses it, which is the 1.20:1 problem showing up in practice.

02

Ridge, aerated

Palette + one element swapped

Pine ground

Bone ground

One colour

What it signals

One core per form, directly under it, as deep as that form is tall. The conifer's twin is the shortest, peak four's is the deepest. The bottom edge is the skyline inverted, so the mark rhymes with itself — and every core mirrors something specific rather than sampling the ridge at an arbitrary point.

What it keeps

Peaks, tree, silhouette — and the roots, in a way. Each core carries a lid of turf in the band's own colour, a body that snapped rather than moulded, and root threads off the break, so the ten root strands the concept dropped come back as five smaller ones. The ember is the conifer's core: the one form that is not a peak, and the smallest of the five.

Where it is weak

It is a cross-section, not a photograph — a real plug is dropped on the grass, not left hanging under the turf. Deliberate, the same device Concept 03 uses, and it buys the ledge; say it out loud rather than letting someone in the trade find it. The threads are the first thing to go in embroidery, and they push the canvas to 120 units against the current mark's 116. And there are five, not six — see the open decision below.

03

Three strata

Ridge restructured

Pine ground

Bone ground

One colour

What it signals

A cross-section: canopy on top, aerated turf through the middle, root zone below. The three service lines stacked as three layers of the same ground.

What it keeps

The conifer and all ten root strands, redrawn across the width. The peaks flatten into a ridgeline, which is what finally kills the flame gesture.

Where it is weak

It is wide and detailed — a diagram more than a mark. The root strands are the first thing to disappear when this is embroidered or printed on a receipt. See the small-size test below before falling for it.

04

Ridge of cores

Clean sheet, keeps the silhouette

Pine ground

Bone ground

One colour

What it signals

Six soil cores stood on end, pulled from the six holes in the band beneath them. The three pale bands are soil strata, and the heights rise and fall rather than climbing evenly — both are there to stop the shapes reading as a bar chart, which the first drawing of this did. Stood on end is a deliberate abstraction: real plugs land on their sides, scattered. This is cores as a specimen rack, not cores as they lie on a lawn.

What it keeps

A ridge profile, tallest at the right, so it still scans like the old mark from across a car park — but built out of aeration cores instead of flames. The shortest core is ember: fire is still here, and it is the smallest of the six.

Where it is weak

It can still read as a bar chart. Uneven widths, uneven band spacing and a recessive ground line all fight that, and it is better than the first drawing, but somebody should say what they see before anyone falls in love with it. No tree, either — and the strata bands are knocked out in the background colour, so it needs its own one-colour drawing and cannot sit on a photo without a plate behind it.

05

Sixfold rosette

Clean sheet, most abstract

Pine ground

Bone ground

One colour

What it signals

Six tines around a hollow centre. “Sixfold” is a real term for six-way radial symmetry, so the name and the drawing say the same thing for the first time.

What it keeps

Nothing visual — but it keeps the count, and it is the only concept that fits a circle. Profile pictures, app icons and a bottle cap all work without a redraw.

Where it is weak

Abstract enough that nobody reads “lawn” from it cold. It also drifts close to generic tech-logo territory, and it throws away every bit of recognition built so far.

06

The tine

Clean sheet, minimal

Pine ground

Bone ground

One colour

What it signals

One hollow tine going into the turf, the hole it left, the core standing beside it, and air going in from the left. The most literal statement of the new lead service in the set.

What it keeps

Nothing, deliberately. No fire, no ember, no ridge. This is the answer if the room decides the old mark has to stop being a reference point.

Where it is weak

It can read as a syringe. The tine is drawn wide and stubby to fight that, but say it out loud in the meeting and see whether anyone else saw it first. It also says nothing at all about trees or landscaping.

The small-size test

A favicon is 32px. An embroidered left-chest logo is about the same in thread. This is the row that usually decides it — a concept that dissolves here costs you a second, simplified mark to maintain forever.

Concept 3 is the one to watch here — the root strands and the perforations both go to mush by 20px. Concepts 4 and 5 hold. Concept 1 holds because it already does, which is a point in its favour.

Locked up with the wordmark

The wordmark and its setting do not change in any of this — Palatino, tight tracking, as now. Only the mark and the colour beside it move. Three shown; the descriptor is an open question below.

Open decisions for the team

Kept deliberately short. These are the questions where the meeting is the right place to settle it, not a document.

What a change costs to roll out

Not an argument against doing it — an argument for deciding once. Counted in the repo on 23 Sep 2026:

WhatCountNote

Rendered brand PNGs40Social and Square sets; both regenerate from one script each
PDFs across the project84Includes the 25-PDF team share, which has to be rebuilt as a set
Vehicle magnet artwork324×12, 24×12 light, 24×18 — the repo shows no order placed

The live site is the one that matters. sixfoldcolorado.com went live on 21 Sep 2026 and carries the mark in the favicon, the stylesheet and the wildfire guide page. A mark change means a re-bundle and re-upload, and the host serves a stale cache after an upload — so the change has to be cache-busted and verified, not assumed.

There is a precedent for the sweep itself: the phone-number change in September touched 57 files and 21 PDFs and came out clean. The machinery exists. Check the vehicle magnets have not been ordered before anyone commits — that is the one item on this list that cannot be re-run.

What happens after the meeting

  1. Team picks a direction — one concept, or two to develop.
  2. The winner gets drawn as a real master: full colour, one colour, and a flat print version with the overlaps resolved, the same three files Ridge already has.
  3. Prior-use screen re-run against the new drawing. The existing screen covered the Ridge mark only, and two of its checks were never run — reverse image search and the USPTO design-code search. A new mark needs both before it goes anywhere.
  4. Palette tokens updated in one place, then the generators re-run and the PDFs re-counted.
  5. Site re-bundled and cache-busted last, once everything else matches.

Nothing in this document has been published, posted or uploaded, and no master files have been overwritten. The current Ridge mark and its four SVGs are untouched.

What core aeration equipment actually isVerified first-party, 23 Sep 2026, against UMass Amherst Extension. Hollow tines are open-ended steel tubes of 1/4–3/4″ inside diameter on a drum or cam-driven head — a punch, not a drill and not an auger. Set in grids from 2″×2″ to 4″×6″, so a real head carries dozens, not six.
Where the extracted plugs end upVerified first-party, 23 Sep 2026. Plugs are 2–3″ long and are deposited on the surface, then broken up with a drag mat or a mower as topdressing. Only on golf greens are they swept off. This is why Concept 02 is drawn as a cross-section. Its first version hung six equal cores below the band with nothing to say for it, and they read as tines pointing down. Matthew’s call was to keep the cores below and let their depths trace the ridge — so they now read as soil columns in section, under the holes they came from, rather than as plugs on a lawn. Knowingly not literal, and said so in the caption. A version with the cores lying on the surface was drawn and set aside: accurate, but it gave up the ledge.
How many forms the Ridge mark actually hasCounted off the path data, 23 Sep 2026, after Matthew spotted the mismatch: five — four peak triangles and one conifer. The apex heights above the ground line are 30.5 / 38 / 48 / 60 / 72 units, measured rather than estimated. Concept 02 now runs one core per form at those depths. My earlier hand-estimate of the leftmost height was 7; the real figure is 12, which is the kind of error that only shows up when the geometry is sampled instead of eyeballed.
