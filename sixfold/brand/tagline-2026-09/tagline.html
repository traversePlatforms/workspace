The tagline, after the reposition

Aeration leads now. The line on the banners, the flyer and the Square invoice was written for fire mitigation, and it does not survive the change — not because it is off-message, but because it is no longer true. Six candidates, a recommendation, and what it costs to swap.

The verdict

Retire it.

We do the work and show you who pays for it.

Recommended replacement:

From the lawn to the treeline.

Brandon's call, and it belongs in the same sitting as the descriptor — the two live in mostly the same files and sweeping them separately means sweeping twice.

Why the current line has to go

It is a funding promise, and it works because of two things that are both fire-specific: the Colorado state tax credit, which covers what a homeowner pays a contractor for qualifying vegetation work, and the HB25-1182 insurer re-score, which applies to wildfire risk.

Nobody else pays for an aeration. Nobody else pays for mowing, edging or a bed clean-up. On the service that now leads, and on two of the three beside it, the line is not merely off-message — it promises something that does not exist.

⚑ The deadline nobody set

The line is hardcoded at brand/square/build-square.mjs:129, which puts it on the Square invoice template and the customer display. Kara's aeration invoice would go out carrying a promise about who pays for it.

The rebrand path already names the invoice as the one surface with a real deadline behind it, because an invoice goes to a paying customer with whatever is on it that day. This is that deadline arriving on the copy instead of the mark.

Clear / Protect / Restore — heard, and why not

Whose it is, stated plainly: these are Brandon's three circles. They organised the first three builds of the site-walk sheet, and they are the spine of the 2026 Land & Fire flyer — "I like the wording and the messaging." The frame has real buy-in and it is not being dismissed. Both objections are about the repositioning, not the writing.

1. All three are fire verbs here

CLEAR is fuel reduction. PROTECT is defensible space — the CSFS term verbatim. RESTORE is putting the property back after the clearing. Aeration is none of the three. It would tagline an aeration-led brand with a phrase describing every service except the one leading.

2. The sequence is the banned framing

Clear → protect → restore makes restore the after-job of the clearing. That is the "landscaping is the after-job of fire mitigation" framing removed from every document in September. It would come back in as the tagline.

It is the same objection as the house on the logo board: it re-centres the thing being demoted. What survives is the three-verb spine, which is the part that reads well — candidate 2 keeps it.
