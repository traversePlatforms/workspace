InternalSending, not signing
Do not hand to a customer

Field crib sheet · emailing an agreement to be signed

You are not in the room. Two rules get harder.

Everything on the door sheet still applies. These are the two things that break when the agreement is sent instead of handed over — and both of them break quietly.

Never send it with the deadline date blank.

A blank or wrong cancellation date means the three-day window never starts and the right to cancel stays open for three years — no compensation for work already done. Emailing makes this easy to get wrong, because the deadline depends on the day they sign, and you do not control that day.

The two that are different

  1. You cannot email the part you have to say out loud The law requires you to tell the buyer orally, at the time they sign, that they can cancel. An email does not do it, and neither does the box on the form. Be on the phone while they sign. Say it plainly — "before you sign: you have three business days to change your mind, and the two cancellation forms in there are how you do it." Then write in the job notes that you said it, and when.
  2. The date is theirs, not yours — so give the window generously The deadline is midnight of the third business day after the day they actually sign. If you fill in a date based on the day you sent it and they sign three days later, you have given them a shorter window than the law requires. That is the defect.

    The rule that keeps you safe

    A window that is too long is compliant. A window that is too short is not. When in doubt, put a later date, never an earlier one. It costs you a few days of waiting and it cannot be held against you.

The sequence that works

  1. Agree the signing day on the phone first Not "sometime this week." A day. Everything below hangs off it.
  2. Fill it in completely, then send Price, scope, date of transaction, section 6, and both cancellation forms — seller name, seller address, date, deadline. The law says you complete those, not the customer. Send all five pages, never a blank template.
  3. Call them as they sign, and say it out loud See above. This is the step that gets skipped.
  4. Get the signed copy back and file it the same day With the email thread. That thread is currently the only evidence of when they signed.
  5. Then wait. Do not schedule the crew inside the window Same rule as the door: cancel after work has started and Sixfold is owed nothing.

There is no e-signature system, and that is a real gap

A PDF signed and emailed back has no audit trail and no proof of when it was signed. For a contract that can be unwound for three years, that record is the thing worth buying. Until Sixfold has a real e-signature service, keep the whole email thread — raise it with Brandon; it is not something a form can fix.

When not to send it at all

  • You have not walked the property. The price comes off a counted sheet, not a phone call.
  • The office has not agreed the price. Never send an agreement to find out what someone will pay.
  • They want to start immediately. That is a conversation, not a document — sheet 2 of the door crib sheet covers it.
  • You are not sure the cancellation rule applies because it was not sold at their home. Send the forms anyway. Including them when they were not required costs nothing. Leaving them out when they were required is the three-year problem.
