2 · The work

Felling — trees taken down
Stump grinding — ground out below grade
Slash and chipping
Rounds — cut to firewood length
Defensible space around structures
Fuel break / thinning

None of the categories above is ticked: this job is clearance and haul-off of material already on the ground. The full scope is written below.

Clearance of downed wood only — the logs and limbs already lying on the ground on the date of survey. In the order the crew does it: (1) walk the pile with the owner and mark the edge of the job; (2) photograph the area from fixed positions before anything moves; (3) buck the downed wood with saws, roughly 16-inch lengths through the heavy butt sections and longer where a limb is light enough to shoulder; (4) gather and load by hand, carried to the trailer rather than dragged so the ground is not torn on the way out; (5) haul off the property — Sixfold truck and trailer, six loads to a wood-waste disposal site, tipping fees paid by Sixfold and included in the price at section 5; (6) rake down the footprint, clear the loose bark, and photograph again from the same positions. The before-and-after photograph set, an itemised invoice and a written work record are supplied with the invoice. Location: the felled-wood pile and its footprint, as walked and agreed on the survey visit.

The material is already on the ground. What is in and what is out is walked and agreed with the owner before a saw starts; anything the owner wants kept is pointed out at that walk. No standing tree is felled under this agreement.

3 · What is not included

Unless written below, this does not cover structural work of any kind (roofing, vents, siding, decking); landscaping, replanting or reseeding; repair of irrigation, fencing or lawn; hauling non-woody debris; work on land not identified as the owner's; or permit fees.

Also excluded for this job: grinding any stump — this can be quoted separately and cannot be done the same day, because utility locates have to be called in first; felling any standing tree — if the owner wants one taken down, Sixfold will walk it and quote it separately; and removing anything that is not wood — fencing, wire, metal, building material or household waste mixed into the pile, which is set aside and stays with the owner unless a price for it is separately agreed.

Nothing added. Scope is as described in section 2.

4 · What happens to the material

Chipped on site and spread
Piled to standard for pickup
Cut to rounds and stacked — where:
✗Hauled off site and disposed of

If the owner wants any of the wood kept back as firewood, they say so before loading and the crew cuts it to rounds and stacks it instead, at no extra charge.

Terms

  1. What Sixfold gives you with the job, whether or not you ask A written scope of exactly what was cut and where, photographs from fixed positions before and after, and an itemised invoice that separates qualifying wildfire mitigation work from anything else on the bill.
  2. Sixfold does not advise on tax or insurance Sixfold does not give tax or insurance advice, does not certify work, and does not prepare or file tax returns. It supplies documentation. Whether you qualify for the Colorado wildfire mitigation tax credit is a question for your accountant, and what your policy qualifies for is a question for your carrier. Nothing in this agreement is a promise that you will receive a credit, a refund, or a discount, or that any of them will be any particular amount.
  3. What this work does and does not do This work reduces fuel and creates defensible space. It does not make a property fireproof and does not guarantee any outcome in a wildfire. No one can promise that.
  4. You confirm you can authorise the work By signing, you confirm you are the owner of record of the property, or that you have the owner's authority to have this work done, and that no part of the area to be worked is on land belonging to someone else.
  5. Boundaries, buried lines and things we cannot see You will identify the property boundaries. Sixfold will request public utility locates through Colorado 811 before any stump grinding or ground disturbance. Private and unmarked lines are yours to point out — irrigation, septic tanks and leach fields, wells, invisible pet fencing, low-voltage lighting, buried propane, and anything else not on a public locate. Sixfold is not responsible for damage to a line it was not told about and that a public locate did not mark.
  6. Clearing the way Before the crew arrives, please move vehicles, secure pets and livestock, and keep people out of the work area while saws are running.
  7. HOA rules and permits Where a covenant, HOA architectural rule, or local permit applies to removing trees, obtaining that approval is the owner's responsibility unless section 3 says otherwise in writing.
  8. Access and ground conditions Equipment has to reach the work. Driveways, lawns and soft ground can rut or mark under machinery even when the crew is careful, and that kind of ordinary access marking is not damage under clause 12.
