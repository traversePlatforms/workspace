> **⚑ What the deadline actually rests on is whether this is a solicited sale at all.**

>   seller *personally solicits* and the agreement is given at a residence. Two payments with no
>   finance charge is arguably not a credit sale, so **the Colorado statute may not reach this
>   contract at all.**

> ### ⚑ DocuSign, and the one thing that must not happen
>
> He intends to send both documents through **DocuSign** and *"fill the cancellation deadline in
> after it gets returned to me after she signs."*
>
> **That is the three-year defect, precisely.** `remote-signing` exists for this and its first
> rule is *"Never send it with the deadline date blank."* Under C.R.S. 5-3-403(3) the right to
> cancel stays open **until the seller has complied with the notice requirements**, and expires
> three years after the transaction rather than three days. 16 CFR 429.1(b) puts completing both
> Notice of Cancellation forms — seller name, seller address, date of transaction, deadline —
> on the **seller, before they are handed over.** A form completed after it comes back was blank
> when she received it, and that is what counts.
>
> **The workable sequence, from `remote-signing`:** agree an actual signing day on the phone,
> fill the deadline computed from that day, then send. If she signs earlier than agreed the
> window is longer than required, which is compliant. If she signs later it is shorter, which is
> the defect — **reissue rather than let it stand.** *A window that is too long is compliant. A
> window that is too short is not.*
>
> **And the part DocuSign cannot do:** 16 CFR 429.1(e) requires the buyer be told **orally**, at
> the time of signing, that she can cancel. An envelope does not discharge it. Be on the phone
> while she signs, say it, and note in the job file that it was said and when.
>
> **One thing DocuSign does fix.** `remote-signing` flags "there is no e-signature system, and
> that is a real gap" — no audit trail, no proof of when a PDF was signed. **DocuSign closes
> that**, and its certificate of completion is the timestamp evidence the crib sheet says is
> worth buying. That sheet is now out of date on that point.

### 2. 200 lb of seed is roughly double the book rate for the area on record

CSU Extension puts overseeding Kentucky bluegrass at **3–4 lb per 1,000 sq ft**. Job 2
recorded Kara's open ground as **approximately three-quarters of an acre** (~32,670 sq ft).

```
  200 lb ÷ 32.67 thousand sq ft  =  ~6.1 lb / 1,000 sq ft     (~1.5–2× the book rate)
  200 lb at the book rate        =  covers 50,000–66,700 sq ft  (1.15–1.53 acres)
```

So one of two things is true, and it is worth knowing which:

- **the area is bigger than job 2 recorded** — quite possible, "the whole yard" may include
  ground that job was not mowing; or
- **200 lb is generous**, and 100–130 lb would do the same job, with the difference being
  money spread on the ground.

The arithmetic is mine, off job 2's acreage. **Re-measure at the walk** rather than trust a
figure carried from another job.

### 3. No pesticide, and that is deliberate

Both agreements say in section 2 **and** section 3 that no pesticide, herbicide, weed killer
or weed-and-feed goes down. That is not padding — applying pesticide **for hire** in Colorado
needs a **CDA Commercial Applicator licence plus a Qualified Supervisor**, and Sixfold holds
neither. Fertilizer on its own does not need it.

**The trap is the product, not the service.** A bag of "weed and feed" is a pesticide. The
moment one goes on this lawn the licence question is live, whatever the invoice calls it.

*Unresolved:* I could not confirm first-party whether applying **fertilizer** for hire needs
any registration of its own. CDA runs a separate fertilizer program, but it reads as product
registration and labelling rather than applicator licensing. **One call to CDA settles it —
(303) 869-9066.**

## Decisions I made, so they can be reversed

**The maintenance agreement does not auto-renew.** Twelve months, then it stops. That is a
choice: C.R.S. 6-1-732 puts real duties on an automatically renewing consumer contract — a
renewal notice **25 to 40 days before every renewal**, and a matching cancellation route. A
fixed term that simply ends sits outside all of it. Adding a rollover clause later means
taking those duties on, so read the statute first.

**Either side can end it early on 30 days' notice** (clause 16), with any month paid for and
not begun refunded. Without that, a twelve-month commitment for both parties is a big ask on
a first maintenance contract.

**Snow is clearing only, never treating.** Clause 14 says plainly that clearing snow does not
make a surface safe to walk or drive on, and section 3 excludes ice melt, salt and sand.
⚑ **This is the clause an attorney should look at first.** Slip-and-fall on a cleared
driveway is the obvious exposure in a snow contract and no wording removes it entirely.

**Clause lists were rebalanced.** The maintenance agreement carries 17 clauses against the
template's 13, which put sheet 3 over a page. Sheet 2 now carries terms 1–10 and sheet 3
carries 11–17, with `start="11"` and `counter-reset: cl 10` moved to match, and both sheet
headers updated. All four move together or the document repeats the 2026-09-15 defect that
left the job-2 packet with no clause 8.
