## Why this document exists

Every competitor sells the work. Almost nobody explains the money. The state's own 2026 evaluation of the wildfire-mitigation tax credit found **fewer than half** of surveyed landowners had ever heard of it. If Sixfold is the crew that shows up understanding what a client can claim, that is an advantage a new company can hold without out-crediting a veteran outfit.

## The one rule that governs all of this — the stacking limit

The Colorado state income tax credit (C.R.S. 39-22-543) **explicitly excludes any cost that was paid from, or reimbursed by, a grant or incentive.** A homeowner who gets Douglas County to cover 50% of a $20,000 job can claim the state credit only against *their own* $10,000 — not the county's half. The programs below combine, but grant money and the tax credit cannot both land on the same dollar. Safe phrasing to a client: *"these can often be combined; your accountant can tell you how they interact."* A worked grant-plus-credit example on one invoice is **not** safe.

## Tier 1 — available to almost any Colorado owner

### Colorado wildfire-mitigation measures income tax credit
**CO Dept of Revenue · C.R.S. 39-22-543 · claimed on DR 0104CR with the DR 0104**

A state income-tax credit for what a landowner pays a contractor to reduce wildfire risk on their own private Colorado land. Since Aug 2024 (HB24-1036) **only third-party contractor costs qualify** — DIY equipment does not. Four qualifying measures, which must meet or exceed CSFS or DFPC standards: defensible space around structures; fuel breaks; thinning woody vegetation for the primary purpose of reducing risk to structures; and secondary treatment of the resulting fuels by lop-and-scatter, pile, chip, remove from site, or prescribed burn. That list is Sixfold's core work almost line for line.

- **Amount.** TY2025: 100% of qualifying cost, cap **$1,000**, FTI ≤ $129,200. TY2026: same 100% / $1,000; **2026 income limit not yet published** by Revenue. TY2027–2030: cap rises to **$2,000**, income test becomes **AGI ≤ $300,000**, excess over tax liability becomes **refundable**. Before 2027, excess is lost — not refunded, not carried forward.
- **Eligible owner.** Owner of record of private CO land — fee, easement, right-of-way, or a **recorded** lease. Solely/jointly/in common, but **only one individual per ownership group may claim**. Public land never qualifies. **No WUI test** — statute says only "real property located in Colorado".
- **Does NOT cover.** Property purchase price; landowner's own equipment purchase/rental; inspection or certification fees; in-kind/donated work; cost-sharing between neighbours; **any cost paid from or reimbursed by a grant or incentive.**
- **How claimed.** Homeowner files DR 0104CR with their return, receipts attached or via Revenue Online. No state form Sixfold submits.
- **New for 2027.** A separate, narrower category — "infestation mitigation measures": thinning of woody vegetation at risk of / killed by mountain pine beetle or spruce beetle. An addition, not a broadening.
- **Sixfold's role.** Itemised invoice separating qualifying from non-qualifying work + a written statement of the standard worked to. **Never "we handle the paperwork for you"**; never prepare or file a return. Pre-work eligibility questions are on Form A of the tax-credit notebook.

### Insurance premium discount for documented mitigation
**Each admitted insurer · Colorado HB25-1182, effective 1 Jul 2026 · DOI disclosure regulation (Reg 5-1-28 / 3 CCR 702-5) effective 1 Oct 2026**

Insurers using a wildfire risk model/score must send each policyholder an **annual written disclosure** of their risk score, the mitigation discounts available, and how mitigation factors into pricing — plus a **right to appeal** the score. Where a carrier's model does not already credit a property's mitigation, the carrier must apply a discount when the owner documents it. The law **does not set a percentage**.

- **Amount.** Carrier-specific. Do not quote a number to a client — there isn't one.
- **Backstop.** Colorado **FAIR Plan** (launched Apr 2025): up to $750,000 residential, actual-cash-value basis. A last resort, not a target.
- **Sixfold's role.** Photograph log + dated before/after record (Form D). Assemble the documentation package; help draft a re-score request. Do **not** contact the insurer, promise a lower premium, or put a number on any discount.

## Tier 2 — county and regional cost-share (depends where the property is)

Most follow one pattern: **free assessment first → signed award before work → homeowner pays in full → partial reimbursement to the homeowner.** Douglas County is the exception (pays the contractor directly). Nearly all are **first-come until the season's money runs out** and reopen annually.

### Douglas County Wildfire Mitigation Cost-Share Program
**douglas.co.us/building-division/wildfire-mitigation**

The strongest county program in Sixfold's service area. A **50% financial match**.

- **Amount.** Up to **$25,000** to a homeowner (50% match). Up to **$50,000** community project. Up to **$7,000** community chipping / slash pickup.
- **To apply.** Detailed scope of work, site map, and **two contractor estimates from the county's approved contractor list.**
- **Timing.** 2026 primary window ~**13 April – 13 May**; second window anticipated late 2026. ~10 months to complete.
- **Payment.** County inspects on completion; homeowner pays the contractor their share; on submission of final invoice + proof of payment, **Douglas County Finance releases the 50% match directly to the contractor.**
- **Sixfold's role.** **Getting on the approved contractor list is a gate, not a marketing task.** A homeowner needs two bids from that list; until Sixfold is on it, naming the program to a Douglas County client sends them toward money Sixfold cannot help them get. This is the highest-leverage phone call on the whole funding picture — already the project's standing #1 action item.

### COSWAP — landowner cost-share
**CO DNR / CSFS, delivered by Wildfire Adapted Partnership**

State-funded cost-share for private landowners inside a **mapped COSWAP area**. Free WAP risk assessment first, tell the assessor you want COSWAP, hire a contractor, complete the work, reimbursed **up to 50%**. (COSWAP's large workforce-development grants — $6.9M to 24 projects, Feb 2026 — fund youth corps and inmate fire crews, not homeowner work.)

- **Catch.** Only inside the mapped area; assessment must come first; work started before the signed award letter is not covered.
- **Where.** Maps shift each cycle — WAP and the county EM office confirm a given address.

### Wildfire Partners — Boulder County
**wildfirepartners.org**

Best known for its **assessment and certification** (the certificate is what several insurers want). Cash side is now a **rebate**: up to **$500 per Boulder County household** for labor and cash costs of eligible actions, one per property, first-come. Multi-family: up to $500/unit for common-area work. 2026 deadline **8 October 2026**; ~$1.625M across rebate programs. City of Boulder runs a separate **WRAP** for properties inside city limits.

### Grand County Wildfire Council — cost-share
**bewildfireready.org**

**Home Hardening Cost Share:** reimburses the lesser of **$1,000 per property or 50%**, after completion and full payment, first-come. Covers home hardening, first 5 ft (gravel + fabric), ⅛-in vent mesh. HIZ assessment required first. A separate **Fuels Reduction Cost-Share** exists for thinning / defensible space — terms not confirmed (page 404'd at research time).

### Eagle County — REALFire cost-share
**realfire.net**

A free REALFire assessment **may qualify the owner for a cost-share grant** covering a portion of the identified work. >$250,000 awarded to date; 750-home goal by 2028. Award size set by the assessment.

### Wildfire Adapted Partnership — Defensible Space Cost Share & Chipper Rebate
**wildfireadapted.org · SW Colorado (Archuleta, La Plata, Montezuma and neighbours, varying by cycle)**

WAP is the delivery partner for COSWAP and for its own **Defensible Space Cost Share**. Free HIZ assessment, signed award letter **before** contractor work, then partial reimbursement to the homeowner after paying in full — amount set by site size / vegetation / terrain (% and cap not published). A separate **Chipper Rebate** offsets chipping cost.

### Larimer County — Community Mitigation Grant
**larimer.gov/emergency/community-mitigation-grant-program**

**Not an individual-homeowner program.** Funds neighbourhoods, HOAs, local nonprofits for hazardous-fuels reduction, community chipping/slash removal, firewood banks, equipment. 2026 performance period ends **2 November 2026**. Larimer also runs a Wildfire Partner Program (Sheriff) that does assessments.

### Routt County — evacuation-corridor cost-share
**Routt County OEM + Routt County Wildfire Mitigation Council**

Landowners along priority evacuation corridors pay up front, receive **50% reimbursement** through state (FRWRM) grant funds. 2026 budget up to ~$120,000 county-wide, half state-reimbursed, plus a $10,000 Council contribution. Corridor-prioritised and pooled.

### Summit County — free chipping and CWPP grants
**summitcountyco.gov CSU Extension forest health**

**No homeowner defensible-space cash cost-share.** Provides **free chipping and haul-away** of slash piles (trees/logs/branches ≤ 12 in, no willows) on a neighbourhood-week schedule, and free fire-district assessments. CWPP Grant Program: matching grants for life-safety / critical-infrastructure / evacuation-route / education projects — not routine defensible space. For a Summit client, "money back" is the state credit and insurance, not a county cheque.

### El Paso & Teller counties — what actually exists
**Sixfold's home turf — and the thinnest county-money picture in the service area**

**No confirmed standing homeowner cost-share was found for El Paso County.** Routes for an El Paso or Teller client: the state tax credit, the insurance discount, EQIP if rural/agricultural, and whatever homeowner incentives local fire districts stand up with grant money. Teller County's CUSP runs free/low-cost slash sites and neighbourhood chipping (homeowner-facing — see the disposal directory); CSFS administers cost-share for larger watershed projects. **Confirm directly** with El Paso County Community Services / Environmental Division and with Black Forest Fire, Tri-Lakes-Monument Fire and the Woodland Park / NE Teller districts whether a current CWDG- or FRWRM-funded rebate is running. **Do not imply an El Paso County cheque exists until one is confirmed by name.**

### Jefferson County — homeowner incentives, emerging
In July 2026 the Jefferson County Sheriff's Office was awarded a **$9.67M USFS Community Wildfire Defense Grant** (five-year) that will fund mitigation staff, equipment, roadside vegetation projects, and **homeowner incentives for creating defensible space and hardening homes**. The homeowner-facing mechanism was not yet published. Watch this one — the clearest sign CWDG money is becoming Front Range homeowner cost-share.

### Other counties with programs on record (confirm first-party)
- **Delta County** (West Region Wildfire Council) — Vegetation Management Cost-Share reported at **up to 75%** of defensible space / thinning / vegetation management on private property. West Slope; verify cap and geography.
- **Front Range mountain fire districts** (Boulder Mountain Fire, Golden Gate Fire, others) — periodic district-run chipping and mitigation-grant offers, funded from CWDG/FRWRM pass-through. District by district, year by year.
- **La Plata / SW counties** — covered by WAP above.

## Tier 3 — grants that fund the work at scale (Sixfold is the hired contractor, not the applicant)

These do not pay individual homeowners. An HOA, fire district, water provider, county or nonprofit applies, wins the grant, and hires a contractor. For Sixfold this is a **business-development channel**: court the entities that can apply, be the crew they name.

### CSFS Forest Restoration & Wildfire Risk Mitigation grant (FRWRM)
**csfs.colostate.edu/grants**

The state's main competitive fuels-reduction grant. ~**$7.04M for 2026–27**, application **3 August – 8 October 2026** (pre-awards Feb 2027, complete by March 2031). Funds defensible space, mechanical thinning, prescribed fire, **slash disposal**, replanting/restoration, plus a capacity-building track (staffing, equipment, CWPP writing).

- **Who can apply.** Community groups and HOAs, local governments, fire districts, public/private utilities and water providers, state agencies, tribes, fuels-reduction nonprofits. **An individual homeowner cannot apply alone** — only in collaboration with an eligible entity, and only where the project benefits more than their own property.
- **Match.** Minimum **1:1 (50%)**; reduced to **25% match (75% grant)** in areas with fewer economic resources. Cash, in-kind, equipment, volunteer labor count.
- **Contractors.** Not eligible to apply; hired by the applicant.
- **Sixfold's role.** The HOA / fire-district-board funding one-pager targets exactly this audience; the 8 Oct 2026 deadline makes it time-sensitive.

### USFS Community Wildfire Defense Grant (CWDG)
**US Forest Service (BIL-funded), administered in Colorado by CSFS**

Federal grants to **at-risk communities**: local governments, tribes, nonprofits (incl. HOAs) assisting such communities, state forestry agencies. Funds CWPPs and implementation. Prioritises high/very-high hazard, low-income, and disaster-impacted communities. Round 3 closed 11 March 2025. Recent CO awards are large and multi-year — the Jefferson County Sheriff's $9.67M award is a CWDG grant explicitly funding homeowner defensible-space incentives.

### FEMA Hazard Mitigation Assistance — HMGP and BRIC
**FEMA, via Colorado DHSEM and a local-government sub-applicant**

**HMGP** becomes available after a presidential disaster declaration; can fund coordinated defensible space across many parcels and fuel breaks through a state/local sub-applicant. **BRIC** is the competitive, non-disaster version. Neither takes a direct homeowner application. Long lead times, heavy administration.

### Watershed and water-provider partnerships
**Denver Water "From Forests to Faucets"; Northern Water; Colorado Springs Utilities; others**

Water providers co-fund forest treatment in the watersheds feeding their reservoirs. From Forests to Faucets (Denver Water + USFS + CSFS + NRCS): ~**$96M** into **120,000+ acres** since 2010, including private land through partners. **Landscape-scale via partnerships, not a homeowner rebate** — but a private parcel inside a priority watershed can sometimes be folded into a partner-led project. If a job sits in a named source watershed, ask the county forester / CSFS field office.

## Tier 4 — federal cost-share and tax treatment for rural / ag / forest land

Reach an individual owner directly, but only if the land qualifies as working agricultural or non-industrial private forest land — not a typical WUI house lot.

### NRCS Environmental Quality Incentives Program (EQIP)
**USDA NRCS · local NRCS field office / conservation district**

The only NRCS financial-assistance program covering forestry resource concerns. Planning + cost-share payments to **agricultural producers and owners of non-industrial private forestland** (and tribes) for practices that reduce wildfire risk: **Brush Management (Practice 314)**, fuel-break construction, forest stand improvement, thinning, prescribed burning. Continuous application with competitive ranking cutoffs; Colorado payment schedules updated Q1 each federal fiscal year.

- **Land test.** Cropland, rangeland, pastureland, non-industrial private forestland, or other farm/ranch land. A standard subdivided house lot generally does not qualify.
- For a client with real acreage, "talk to your NRCS field office about EQIP before you schedule us" is a genuinely useful, no-risk thing to say.

### CSFS Forest Ag Program — agricultural property-tax classification
**CSFS + county assessor · enacted by HB90-1229**

**Not a rebate — a property-tax classification.** An owner of **at least 40 forested acres** with an approved forest management plan, managing the land to produce wood products for profit, can have it valued for property tax as agricultural rather than residential/vacant — typically a large ongoing reduction. Apply with a completed forest management plan and inspection request by **1 October**; CSFS recommends eligible properties to the county assessor annually. A thinning project done to a management plan can support the plan requirement.

## What this means for Sixfold

1. **Tier 1 is the universal pitch.** The state tax credit and the insurance discount apply to essentially every client regardless of county — the two things every rep and crew lead should describe accurately, and the only funding Sixfold can point to on a first visit anywhere.
2. **Douglas County is the one local cheque worth chasing — and it is gated.** Getting on the approved contractor list is the highest-leverage action here. Until then, describe the program but do not imply Sixfold can help access it.
3. **El Paso County, where Sixfold is based, has the thinnest county-money picture.** Do not imply a local cost-share exists there until one is confirmed by name. Sell Tier 1.
4. **Tier 3 is a reason to court HOAs, fire districts and water providers**, not homeowners. The HOA/fire-district funding one-pager and the 8 Oct 2026 FRWRM deadline are the concrete openings.
5. **The stacking rule shapes how Sixfold talks about combining money.** "These can often be combined; your accountant can tell you how they interact" is safe. A worked grant-plus-credit example is not.
6. **Every number here is provisional.** Re-verify against the primary source before any figure goes in front of a client or into a quote.

### Structural notes

- **The stacking exclusion is the load-bearing fact.** C.R.S. 39-22-543 excludes any cost paid from or reimbursed by a grant or incentive, so grant money and the state credit cannot both apply to the same dollar. Verified project knowledge, not new research.
- Most Tier-2 programs follow one pattern: *free assessment first → signed award before work → homeowner pays in full → partial reimbursement to the homeowner*. Douglas County is the notable exception (pays the contractor directly).
- Nearly every county and rebate pot is **first-come until exhausted** and reopens on an annual cycle — a "yes" in spring can be a "closed" by August.
- Sixfold's role is documentation and referral, never administration: no returns filed, no certifications issued, no insurer contacted, no discount or reimbursement amount promised. Banned phrase: *"we handle the paperwork for you."*
