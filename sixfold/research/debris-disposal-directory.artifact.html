Where the Debris Goes

A hauling reference for Colorado’s fire-mitigation regions: every disposal site we could confirm, grouped by area and ranked cheapest first.

Prepared 2026-09-08 Base Monument, CO Phase 1 — call before the first haul

The one thing to take from this

Most of Colorado’s cheap and free drop-offs are community slash programs funded for homeowners. Several bar commercial haulers outright. For a paid Sixfold job with haul-off in the contract, the usable sites are the commercial landfills, Rocky Top Resources, A1 Organics, and the Black Forest Slash & Mulch site — everything else is a homeowner-haul or chipping-day tool. Rates and season dates at these sites change every year and sometimes mid-season; re-verify before any quote that commits us to a haul-off price.

How to read this

Sixfold’s tool list is “fell trees, grind stumps, build slash piles, chip, cut rounds, haul off or leave on site.” This document is only about the haul-off line — where the material goes and what the gate charges. Three things decide the real cost of a run, in this order:

  1. Drive time. A loaded round trip is roughly $60–$120 per hour of truck-and-operator time before the gate fee. A free site 90 minutes away usually loses to a paid site 20 minutes away. Distances below are rough drive estimates from Monument and are flagged as estimates.
  2. Whether the site takes a contractor load at all. See the box below — this is the biggest filter on the list.
  3. The gate fee, quoted three different ways: per ton (weighed), per cubic yard (eyeballed at the scale house), or per load (a pickup bed or a defined trailer size). Rough conversions: loose slash ≈ 200–400 lb/cy; chipped material ≈ 500–800 lb/cy; green rounds and logs ≈ 800–1,000 lb/cy. A pickup bed is ≈ 2.5 cy — so a pickup of loose slash is a quarter to half a ton; a pickup of green rounds is a ton or more.

Within each region, sites are ordered cheapest first on their headline rate for mixed slash-and-wood, so the best deal is at the top. A site that is free but closed to contractors is still listed — a client may be able to haul their own, and a chipping-day arrangement can sometimes use it.

On the very low numbers: the $5 “wood chips” rates at SCRAP and Eagle County are quoted straight off those landfills’ own fee pages — they are that cheap because the material is already chipped (2 in max) and the site reuses it as cover. That is not a rate for slash, branches or logs, which price higher on the same schedules. Confidence on every figure is in the fact-check log: about twenty sites are confirmed against the operator’s own page today, a handful are flagged “Call” because no rate is published, and two (Chaffee, Pitkin) are carried from older reporting.

Cost tiers: Free   Low under ~$15/ton or ≤ $12/load   Mid ~$20–$35/ton or a paid load   Hot $40+/ton or wood billed as trash   Call not published.

Statewide ranking — cheapest first

Headline rate for a contractor load, best deal at the top. Units are not equivalent — a per-ton rate that looks cheap can beat a $10 load rate once a full weighed load is on the scale, and a free site 90 minutes away can cost more in truck time than a $35/ton gate 15 minutes away. Treat this as a first sort, not a routing decision.

Structural notes

  • Sixfold has no masticator / mulching head, so there is no on-site mulch-in-place option — everything not left in a slash pile is chipped, cut to rounds, or hauled. Disposal cost is a live line on most quotes.
  • Sixfold is a commercial hauler, which removes most of the free and cheap community sites from routine use. They stay relevant for (a) client-haul arrangements and (b) chipping days.
  • Distances in this document are rough drive estimates from Monument and are not sourced — planning approximations only.
  • Rates and season dates at these sites change annually and sometimes mid-season. Re-verify the sites for any region before a quote that commits Sixfold to a haul-off price.
