# Water Restrictions 2026 — what the crew needs to know before promising anything

**Status:** Draft 1, researched 2026-09-30. **Not published anywhere and not customer-facing.**
Every figure below is sourced and dated so it can be re-checked — and it will need re-checking,
because drought stages move.

> **⚑ Restrictions change without warning and this document goes stale.** Denver Water went from
> normal summer rules to a total lawn-watering ban in about five weeks. **Nothing here should be
> quoted to a customer as current without checking the provider's own page that week.** Every
> provider is linked at the foot of this file.

**Deliverables:** this file (record + fact-check log) ·
`water-restrictions-2026.html` (designed) · `Sixfold-Water-Restrictions-2026.pdf`
(JOBS entry in `sixfold/tools/html-to-pdf.mjs`).

**Who it is for:** anyone knocking doors, running a walk, or quoting a lawn job. It is organised
by **the towns we actually work**, not by which agency wrote the rule.

## The one thing that shapes everything else

**Aeration needs a watered lawn. Overseeding needs far more.**

> **⚑ Corrected 2026-10-04.** This section first said *"Aeration does not need water"* and that our
> lead service was not threatened. **That was wrong.** Colorado State University Extension: water
> thoroughly the day before so deep plugs can be pulled; water soon after; regular watering should
> follow, because the holes need water to heal; and **core aeration should not be performed on lawns
> that are totally brown or dormant as the result of water restrictions** (PlantTalk 1505, 1534).

Core aeration is a machine pass, and on a green lawn it still works under every provider's rules in
our territory: two or three allowed days a week covers the watering before and after. **What it
needs is a green, watered lawn** — which rules out lawns that are dormant, including many on
household-use-only wells (see Wells; what to sell there is Brandon's call, not settled here).

Overseeding is the exposed half. Seed needs light, frequent watering to germinate — more often
than most providers now allow by sprinkler. **A customer who is allowed to water two or three days
a week is unlikely to get a good take from an overseed**, and we do not water and do not guarantee
germination.

So the risk is not legal. It is this: **we take money for seed that does not come up, and the
customer blames us.** Everything below is about not walking into that.

## ⚑ The one most people get wrong: wells

A large share of our rural territory is on private wells, and **a well is not a loophole.**

Most private wells on parcels **under 35 acres** permitted since **8 May 1972** are
**household-use-only**. That means water for inside the house. **It cannot legally be used to
irrigate a lawn or garden at all** — not two days a week, not at night, not ever. This is a
condition of the well permit and has nothing to do with drought.

Parcels of **35 acres or more** may carry full exempt-well uses, which can include **up to one
acre of lawn and garden**.

**What this means at the door:** on a rural property, "I'm on a well so restrictions don't apply
to me" may be exactly backwards. **Ask what the well permit allows before quoting an overseed.**
If the answer is household-use-only, the seed has no legal water and we should be selling
aeration, mowing or cleanup instead.

*This is the well owner's permit and their responsibility, not ours. But quoting an overseed to
someone who cannot legally water it is a job that fails and a customer who is unhappy.*

## The closing tool nobody else is using

**Colorado Springs Utilities issues establishment permits**, and most lawn outfits never mention
them. It is the single most useful thing in this document for selling an overseed in the Springs.

- Allows watering **more often than three days a week, and during normally banned daytime hours**
- **42 days for seeding projects** (28 days for sod, flowers, shrubs, trees)
- Only needed for **sprinklers**. Drip, watering cans and hose-end with a shut-off nozzle are
  allowed any time regardless
- Apply online, or in person at the Conservation and Environmental Center, 2855 Mesa Rd
- Customer gets a printable permit and can pick up a yard sign

**⚑ Read the application requirement before promising a customer they can get one.** For **sod or
non-native grass seed**, they must submit receipts for **either**:

- **4 cubic yards per 1,000 sq ft of approved soil amendment, mixed into the soil**, or
- **a soil test from a professional laboratory**

On a ¾-acre lawn the amendment option is about **131 cubic yards**. That does not happen on an
aeration job. **The soil test is the realistic route**, and it is not something we currently
include or price.

**For native grass, there is no soil prep requirement at all** — just a receipt showing the mix,
which has to be on the utility's qualifying list.

**That makes seed choice a business decision, not just an agronomy one.** A qualifying native mix
turns the permit into a receipt. A non-native mix turns it into a soil test the customer has to
go and buy.

### Can Sixfold get the permit itself?

**Almost certainly not in our own name.** The utility's wording is *"any residential or commercial
water customer."* At Kara's house the water customer is Kara — we have no account at that address
and nothing to attach a permit to. **The permit belongs to the property, not to the contractor.**

What we can realistically do is **apply on her behalf, or walk her through it** — which is the
useful version anyway, because it costs us nothing and it is the part customers do not know about.
**Whether the utility formally allows a contractor to file for a customer is not confirmed.** The
state landscape trade association publishes a contractor-facing video on these permits, which
suggests contractors do handle them routinely, but the video could not be reviewed here and the
utility's own document says nothing either way.

**One call settles it: ESTP@csu.org, or the Conservation and Environmental Center, 2855 Mesa Rd.**

### Does it cost money?

**No fee appears anywhere in the utility's own document.** Both published copies of the
establishment permit PDF were fetched and they are byte-identical — neither mentions a fee, a
charge, or a cost.

A secondary site said **$50**. That figure is **not corroborated by the utility** and should not
be repeated to a customer. Ask on the same phone call.

### ⚑ It is not a licence, and it is not per county

Worth separating three things that are easy to run together:

| | What it is | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| **Establishment permit / irrigation exemption** | A temporary watering allowance for **one property** while new planting takes | **Per water provider**, tied to a service address |
| **Contractor registration** | A provider or town signing off on who may do the work | Per jurisdiction, where it exists at all |
| **Trade licence** | e.g. the Colorado Springs tree licence for trees 15 ft and over | Per jurisdiction. Separate question, already tracked |

**The unit is the water provider, not the county.** El Paso County alone contains Colorado Springs
Utilities, Town of Monument, Woodmoor, Donala, Triview and Palmer Lake — six providers, six sets
of rules, in one county. **A permit from one is worth nothing at an address served by another.**

What is known about the schemes in our territory:

| Provider | Scheme | Terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Colorado Springs Utilities** | Establishment permit | **42 days for seed**, 28 for sod. Lifts the three-day limit and the daytime ban. Soil test or amendment receipts for non-native seed |
| **Castle Rock Water** | **Temporary Irrigation Exemption** | **30 consecutive days for seed**, 21 for new turf. Water any day — **but the time-of-day rule still applies** (before 8am / after 8pm). **⚑ Not available during July** |
| **Woodland Park, Town of Monument, Woodmoor, Donala, Triview, Palmer Lake** | **Unknown** | **Not checked.** Do not assume a scheme exists |

**Castle Rock also runs a "Registered Landscape Professional" programme**, and its resource page
carries the exemption forms — which is the closest thing in our territory to the licence-per-area
question. **Whether registration is required or voluntary, what it costs, and whether it lets a
contractor file exemptions for a client are all unconfirmed.** Castle Rock Water conservation team,
**720-733-2306**.

## What to say, and what not to

**Say:**

- *"Aeration still works on your watering days: water the day before and soon after."* True on a
  green lawn everywhere in our territory. (Corrected 2026-10-04; it first said "works fine under the
  current restrictions" with no mention of watering.)
- *"Which water provider are you on?"* Ask it on every lawn walk. It is a one-line question that
  changes what we should sell.
- *"If you're on Colorado Springs Utilities, there's a permit that lets you water more often while
  new seed comes in. I can tell you how to get it."* True, useful, and almost nobody says it.
- *"Water the lawn a day or two before we come out."* Now written into the agreements.

**Do not say:**

- Anything that sounds like a promise the seed will come up. We do not water and we do not
  guarantee germination — that is in the agreement, and it needs to match what we say at the door.
- A specific watering schedule for a provider without checking it that week.
- *"You're on a well so the restrictions don't apply."* Often the opposite.

   rather than new restrictions. **Denver escalated hard in late August and I could not find a

   depend on. **One phone call settles it.**
2. **Donala, Triview and Palmer Lake were not pinned down.** All three serve parts of Monument
   and Gleneagle. Donala draws from the depleting Denver Basin aquifer, which is the profile most
   likely to restrict. Not checked.
3. **Three things about permits, all answerable in two phone calls.** (a) Whether Colorado
   Springs lets a contractor file on a customer's behalf — **ESTP@csu.org**. (b) Whether there is
   a fee at all; a secondary source said $50 and **both copies of the utility's own PDF are silent**,
   so do not quote a price. (c) Whether Castle Rock's Registered Landscape Professional programme
   is required of us to work there, and what it costs — **720-733-2306**.
4. **Whether the other six providers have an establishment scheme at all.** Woodland Park, Town of
   Monument, Woodmoor, Donala, Triview and Palmer Lake were not checked. **Do not assume.**
5. **Whether 12 October is too late to seed at all.** Cool-season overseeding is normally a
   late-summer job. If mid-October is past the window, that is a bigger problem for the Kara job
   than the watering rules are, and **dormant seeding** — seed down late, germinates in spring —
   may be the honest version. Not researched.
7. **What to sell on a household-use-only well where the lawn is brown.** CSU says don't aerate a
   dormant lawn, and this file still tells the crew to "sell aeration, mowing or cleanup" there. **A
   sales decision for Brandon**; the app's well-town sheets say the same until he decides.
6. **Our live Meta ad says nothing about water.** "Fall Aeration 2026" is running at $50/day and
   is aeration-led, which is the safe half. Worth a read-through now that this is known.

### ⚑ A sourcing note worth keeping

Two sites — `water-restrictions.com` and `lawnbyseason.com` — confidently asserted that Colorado
Springs is under "Stage 1 Mandatory, twice per week, odd Monday/Wednesday, even Tuesday/Thursday."
**That is Denver and Aurora's rule, misattributed to Colorado Springs.** Both were blocked and the
figures re-taken from csu.org and CPR.

This is the same failure that set the research standard in `CLAUDE.md`: content farms recycling
confident, wrong specifics. **If a watering schedule cannot be found on the provider's own site,
it does not go in a quote.**
