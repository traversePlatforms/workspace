Safety & Compliance Guide

What the law requires of a chainsaw-and-chipper crew in Colorado — OSHA, workers' comp, licences, certifications — organised by what you are doing, not by which agency wrote the rule. Bold lines are for the crew. Citations are for the office.

Prepared 2026-09-16 Base Monument, CO Federal OSHA jurisdiction Not legal advice

The finding that shapes everything else

Sixfold's tree work is not covered by OSHA's logging standard. It falls under OSHA's General Industry rules (29 CFR 1910) plus the General Duty Clause. OSHA's own citation guidance for tree care draws the line by scale: “a simple tree removal using a chain saw to cut down a tree, and a chipper to dispose of the branches and trunk pieces would not likely fall under the Logging operations standard,” and “the removal of a tree, or even several trees, from a residential lot, does not constitute logging.” That is a Sixfold job. Trimming is excluded outright. So the rules that apply are the general ones — PPE, first aid, noise, tools, machine guarding, fuel, electrical, injury reporting — which is what this document is.

Two things to know about the ground under this document

The guidance above (CPL 02-01-045, 2008) is marked archived on OSHA's site, but it says it stands until a tree care standard exists, none does, and OSHA's current tree care page still routes everything to general industry. A tree care standard is coming — RIN 1218-AD04 is on the federal agenda with a proposed rule projected for late 2026. When it is final, Part B here gets rewritten. And Colorado is federal-OSHA territory: no state plan, so federal OSHA enforces directly. Nearest office: Englewood Area Office, (303) 843-4500.

Part A — Before anyone works: what Sixfold has to have

A1  Workers' compensation insurance — required by state law, and not held

Colorado Division of Workers' Compensation  ·  C.R.S. Title 8, Articles 40–47  ·  penalty at § 8-43-409

The single largest legal exposure the company carries, and it is Colorado law, not OSHA. The Division's own words: “If you have one or more employees working for you in Colorado, you must have workers' compensation insurance and maintain it at all times. This applies to all employers, regardless of whether the employees are part-time, full-time, or family members.”

Who counts
“Anyone who gets paid for their services is presumed to be an employee by law.” To be a contractor instead, the worker must be both “free from direction and control in the performance of the work” and “have an independent business doing that specific kind of work.” “Paying someone with a 1099 does not make them a contractor.” A crew member who works Sixfold's jobs, under Sixfold's direction, with Sixfold's saws, and has no tree business of their own, is an employee under this test however they are paid.
The LLC exception
Members of an LLC are considered employees too, but may file a rejection of coverage if they own at least 10% and are a member. Brandon can reject for himself. Nobody else on the crew can.
The penalty
Per day without cover: up to $250 for an initial violation, $250–$500 per day for any subsequent one. If someone is hurt while uninsured, the state's Uninsured Employer Fund pays the worker and then “will seek reimbursement for expenses from the uninsured employer” — and any penalty the employer is issued, “the employer must pay that penalty itself.”
How to get it
Any of 500+ licensed carriers, or Pinnacol Assurance, which is required to cover any Colorado employer — nobody can be refused. Premium cannot be deducted from wages.
Once covered
Display the WC 50 injury poster · record every lost-time injury · report every injury to the carrier within 10 days · give injured workers the designated medical provider list · file the return-to-work form.
Why this is firstIt is required today, it fines by the day, and it is a precondition of the Colorado Springs licence in A3. It and A2 are one conversation with Brandon and an agent, not two.

A2  General liability insurance — not a statute, but required everywhere that matters

Required by the Colorado Springs tree licence ($1,000,000)  ·  by the CDA pesticide licence  ·  by any customer who asks

No Colorado statute makes a tree or landscape business carry general liability. The Colorado Springs licence does, at $1M minimum, as a precondition of applying. The pesticide licence requires proof on file. And a homeowner asking “are you insured?” means this. The standing brand rule bars claiming to be insured until it is true.

StatusNot held. Same conversation as A1.

A3  Licences

No Colorado state licence for tree work or landscaping  ·  licensing is municipal and varies by town  ·  pesticides are the exception

Colorado Springs
The home market licenses the core work. Anyone paid to “cut, train, prune, shape or remove trees which are 15 feet or higher” needs a city Tree Service License. The trigger is the tree's height, not the method — trimming counts. To hold one: $110 licence · $1,000,000 general liability · $100,000 workers' comp · 40-question exam, 80% pass, $120, via City Forestry (719) 385-5942 · three photos of pruning work. The insurance is the wall, not the exam.
Every other town
Monument, Palmer Lake, unincorporated El Paso County, Douglas County, Woodland Park, Castle Rock — not checked. Each needs its own code read before Sixfold works trees there. Add them here as they are confirmed.
Pesticides
A state licence, and it bites the landscaping side. Colorado's Pesticide Applicators' Act: a Commercial Applicator is “any person… who engages in the business of applying pesticides for hire.” Business licence $350, plus a Qualified Supervisor licence for the individual — general exam, category exams, on-the-job experience — and liability insurance on file. Herbicide is a pesticide. Fertiliser alone is not; “weed and feed” is. If Sixfold sprays anything for a customer, this applies before the next application. If it does not, nobody on the crew sprays, and a customer asking gets a no.
811
Colorado One Call, already on the walk sheet. Stumps need the locate first; it takes three business days. Not a licence — a legal requirement before the grinder touches the ground.
Crew rule until the Springs licence existsIf a tree in scope is 15 ft or taller inside Colorado Springs city limits, stop and call in. Do not trim it, do not fell it.

Part B — On the job: what OSHA requires of the crew

All of this is OSHA General Industry (29 CFR 1910) as applied to tree care by CPL 02-01-045 and OSHA's current tree care topic page. The bold box in each card is the rule the crew follows. The citation is for the office.

B1  On every job, regardless of the work

1910.132(h) PPE paid by employer  ·  1910.151(b) first aid  ·  1910.95 noise  ·  1910.1030 bloodborne pathogens

PPE — the employer buys it
1910.132(h): protective equipment “shall be provided by the employer at no cost to employees.” The only exception that matters here is ordinary steel-toe boots the employee may also wear off the job. Helmets, eye protection, hearing protection, chaps, gloves — Sixfold pays. A written hazard assessment (1910.132(d)) is what says which item is worn for which task.
A trained first-aider and a kit
1910.151(b), verbatim: “In the absence of an infirmary, clinic, or hospital in near proximity to the workplace… a person or persons shall be adequately trained to render first aid. Adequate first aid supplies shall be readily available.” A rural property is not near a hospital. For a saw crew the kit needs trauma dressings and a tourniquet, because the injury that matters is a deep cut.
Hearing
Chain saws and chippers “emit high noise levels” (OSHA's words). Above 90 dBA averaged over 8 hours, protection is mandatory; above 85, the employer must run a hearing conservation program — a noise measurement, protection at no cost, an annual hearing test, training. A chainsaw is well above both.
Bloodborne pathogens
1910.1030 reaches whoever renders first aid to a bleeding colleague. Gloves in the kit; the first-aider knows to wear them.
Crew ruleOne trained first-aider on every crew, a stocked kit on the truck, ear protection whenever a saw or the chipper is running, and Sixfold pays for every piece of PPE except your own boots.

B2  Running a chainsaw

1910.132(a) cut-resistant leg protection  ·  1910.242(a) kickback device  ·  General Duty Clause via ANSI Z133 §6.3  ·  1910.266(h) felling practice

Chaps
OSHA's directive tells inspectors to check that chainsaw operators wear cut-resistant leg protection under 1910.132(a). The only defence is proving chaps create a greater hazard in the specific case — and that burden is the employer's.
The saw itself
1910.242(a) requires a kickback-reduction device; a saw without one is citable. Chain brake working, low-kickback chain fitted.
No drop-starting
OSHA names this specifically as citable under the General Duty Clause because ANSI Z133 makes the hazard recognised: start the saw on the ground or firmly supported.
Felling
Sixfold's fells are small and residential — general industry, not the logging standard. But the logging standard's felling section is OSHA's own statement of safe practice and the General Duty Clause reaches recognised hazards: plan the fall, clear the retreat path, keep everyone else two tree-lengths back, watch for hung trees and spring poles.
Crew ruleChaps on before the saw starts, no exceptions. Chain brake on, saw started on the ground. Helmet, eyes, ears, gloves, boots. Anything over a roof, fence, line or drive gets called in — the walk sheet already counts those separately.

B3  Running the chipper

1910 Subpart O machine guarding  ·  1910.269(r)(2) within 10 ft of a line

Subpart O applies to the chipper: infeed guards and the feed-control bar stay on and working. Chippers are the most lethal machine in this trade, which is much of why OSHA has a tree care page at all.

Crew ruleNever reach into the infeed. Feed butt-end first, stand to the side, never climb onto or over the hopper. Guards and the feed bar stay on. And nothing within 10 ft of a power line — see B5.

B4  Fuel, oil and fire

1910 Subpart H (1910.106) flammable liquids  ·  1910.106(d)(7) fire control where fuel is stored

Subpart H governs gas and mix in portable containers. OSHA's extinguisher standard does not reach extinguishers for outdoor use, but 1910.106(d)(7) requires “suitable fire control devices, such as portable fire extinguishers” wherever flammable liquids are stored — which includes the truck.

Crew ruleApproved safety cans, closed, out of the sun, not in the cab. A charged extinguisher next to the fuel. Refuel with the engine off and cooled, on bare ground, not in the slash — this is a fire mitigation crew in fire season.

B5  Near power lines

1910 Subpart S — non-qualified employees prohibited  ·  1910.269 line-clearance work

OSHA's directive: “If employees conducting removal operations are not qualified employees, this activity would be prohibited by 29 CFR 1910, Subpart S.” Sixfold has no line-clearance qualified employees.

Crew ruleAny tree touching, leaning toward, or within 10 ft of a line is not a Sixfold job until the utility has de-energised or cleared it. Stop and call in. This is the rule behind the walk sheet's line warning.

B6  Heights — ladders, climbing, lifts

1910.23 ladders  ·  1910.67 aerial lifts

Sixfold does neither today: everything is felled from the ground and limbed at four feet. That is the right place to stay until there is training, equipment and a written rule for it.

Crew ruleNo climbing, no ladder work on trees, no lift. The walk sheet's “over 80 ft — call in first” is this rule.

B7  Landscaping — mowing, trimming, edging, beds

No dedicated standard  ·  PPE, Subpart O guarding, 1910.242 powered hand tools, Subpart H fuel all apply

Eye protection for string trimmers and edgers, hearing for mowers, the mower's discharge guard stays on. If pesticides enter the picture, the CDA licence in A3 does too — and the label is the law: apply exactly as the product label says, wearing what it says.

Crew ruleSame as saw work, minus chaps. Nobody sprays anything until the office says the licence exists.

Part D — If someone gets hurt

The clocks start immediately, and they are short

29 CFR 1904.39 federal reporting  ·  1904.1 small-employer exemption  ·  Colorado DWC 10-day carrier report

First minutes
Stop the saw. The first-aider takes over. Deep cut: direct pressure, tourniquet if it does not stop, call 911. Do not move a fall casualty. Do not drive them yourself if 911 is faster.
OSHA — 8 hours / 24 hours
Verbatim: “Within eight (8) hours after the death of any employee as a result of a work-related incident, you must report the fatality to OSHA.” And “within twenty-four (24) hours after the in-patient hospitalization of one or more employees or an employee's amputation or an employee's loss of an eye… you must report.” Phone the Englewood Area Office (303) 843-4500, or 1-800-321-OSHA, or osha.gov. This applies to every employer however small — 1904.1 exempts companies with 10 or fewer employees from keeping the injury log, and in the same sentence says all employers must still report these four events.
Workers' comp — 10 days
Once insured: report every injury to the carrier within 10 days, hand over the designated provider list, keep the lost-time record. Before Sixfold is insured, the claim goes to the Uninsured Employer Fund and the fund comes after Sixfold for the money.
Write it down
Even with the log exemption: what happened, when, who, what changed afterwards. It is the only defence if anything is questioned later, and it belongs in the close-out sheet's “what was different” box.
Crew ruleHospital, amputation or eye: the office is told the same hour so the 24-hour OSHA clock is met. A death: within eight hours. These are federal deadlines and they do not wait for the insurer.

Required by state law at one employee. Not held. Fines by the day. Blocks the Springs licence. First.
2  General liability
Required by the Springs licence, the pesticide licence, and every customer who asks. Not held. Same conversation as 1.

Blocked on 1 and 2. Until held: no work on trees 15 ft+ inside city limits.

Cheap. Required now.

A noise measurement and an annual hearing test. Required now.

Either the CDA licence, or a standing “we do not spray.”
