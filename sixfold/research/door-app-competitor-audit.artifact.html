Door App Competitor Audit

How our door app compares with eleven products sold to door-knocking, landscaping and wildfire companies, and what we are taking from them.

For Brandon, team meetingPrepared 3 Oct 2026Prices checked 3 Oct 2026
Where we lead

Nothing on the market puts fire hazard, trees, lawn size and wildfire funding on the same door. The door-knocking apps sell contact data and storm maps; the measuring tools are desk tools for quoting.

Where they lead

Homeowner phone numbers and emails, manager tools (territories, leaderboards, contracts), and precise measuring for bids.

What we're doing

Taking the best of what works for them. The first three, a route around every won job, a call list when fire weather hits and a fire check report for the homeowner, are built and go live with the next update.

What our app is

Built in-house and running on the Sixfold website. Every door in 11 towns (30,464 doors) carries its wildfire hazard rating, how close trees are to the house, its lawn size with the aerial photo it was measured from, the county's appraised value (Douglas and Teller), and the Teller County wildfire plan's rating where the community is named (62 neighbourhoods).

At the door the crew sees the tax credit, grant and insurance rules, logs outcomes (with no signal if need be), notes extra work such as gutters, branches over the roof or irrigation, and books quotes. Admins get Quotes to build, won and lost, a Results screen showing which doors turn into jobs, Communities (HOAs and Firewise sites ranked by high-risk doors), fire-weather alerts, walking routes and a Jobber export. There are no per-person fees, and the data comes from free public sources.

Where we're ahead

  1. Fire, lawn and trees on every door. None of the door-knocking apps checked has wildfire hazard, trees near the house or lawn size.
  2. Funding at the door. Tax credit, county grants and the 2025 insurance law, with what the crew may and may not say. Not found elsewhere.
  3. Communities. HOAs, Firewise sites and county plan ratings grouped to pitch a whole community as one job.
  4. Our own conversion data. SalesRabbit charges for national "buyer propensity" scores. Our Results screen learns from our own doors.
  5. A source and a date on every figure. The lawn photo shows exactly what was counted.
  6. Clean data. No scraped contact lists.

Where they're ahead

  1. Homeowner contact data, licensed, with texting compliance handled (GlassHouse, SalesRabbit, HailTrace).
  2. Manager tools: territories per rep, leaderboards, commission calculators, live rep tracking, contracts in the app.
  3. Precise measuring for bids: turf, beds and hardscape measured separately, done for you in about 90 minutes (SiteRecon, Beam AI).
  4. "Your customers were hit" alerts (HailTrace).
  5. Marketing around existing jobs (GlassHouse).
  6. On-site detail: Firemaps maps a house by drone to the centimetre and works with cities.
  7. Native phone apps. Ours is a web app; on the iPad home screen it behaves much the same.

For Brandon to decide

  1. Licensed homeowner contact data. Worth buying, or not? If yes, only through a licensed provider and only for doors we have already spoken to.
  2. Do Not Call registration. The federal list is free for our area codes (719, 303, 720), and Colorado's list is free for businesses under five employees. Registration is still required to download them. Whether our follow-up calls also need the Colorado Attorney General's telemarketer registration is a question for the attorney.
  3. Jobber. The export is ready if we adopt it; nothing changes if we don't.
  4. Leaderboards. Worth adding? The proposal leaves out live location tracking.
