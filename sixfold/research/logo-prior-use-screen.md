# Logo Prior-Use Screen — the Ridge mark

**Prepared:** 2026-08-19
**Subject:** the Sixfold Ridge mark as it now stands — four ember slopes, a moss conifer at the left, a ground line, ten thin roots off the right corner.
**Companion to:** [name-clearance-screen.md](name-clearance-screen.md), which covers the *word* "Sixfold". This file covers the *drawing*.

> **This is a preliminary screen, not a clearance, and not legal advice.** Two hard limits shaped it, both stated up front rather than buried: **I cannot run a reverse image search** — the standard first tool for this question — because I have no way to submit an image to one. And **the USPTO's own design-code search is unreachable from here**, which is the one search that would authoritatively answer "does someone own this." What follows is everything obtainable without those two, which is more than nothing and less than an answer.

---

## 1. Headline

**Nothing found resembles the mark.** Across thirteen Colorado operators in the same trade whose logos I downloaded and viewed directly, and the US trademark register searched by name, there is no mark combining flat geometric ascending triangles with a tree and roots.

The important nuance for the conversation with Brandon: **the ingredients are generic, the arrangement is not.** Mountains and conifers are among the most heavily used design elements in this sector — there are 11,430 registered figurative marks carrying a mountain or conifer code in the two relevant service classes alone. What no one else appears to be doing is drawing them as **flat overlapping triangles stepping up in opacity**, and nobody at all is drawing **roots**.

Two further records surface on Justia (HIGHSOUND, 86233448 and 98761195) but match only on *owner* name — Sixfold Solutions, LLC, a consumer electronics business. Not name conflicts.

**None is in class 37 (construction, land clearing) or class 44 (forestry, agriculture, landscaping).** The trade is clear on the register.

**Two updates to the earlier name screen**, which could not reach USPTO at all:

1. It flagged **Sixfold AI** (New York, insurance-underwriting AI, well funded) as the one real conflict and reasoned they were "likely to have filed." **No US registration in their name was found in either database.** That weakens the urgency somewhat — but absence in a mirror is not proof of absence, and it does not change the recommendation to have an attorney look.
2. A **new** registration has appeared since that screen: Goodroot LLC's SIXFOLD in class 35, registered 2026-05-26. Marketing services, not land services, so not a trade conflict — but it is the closest thing to a live commercial claim on the bare word, and it is three months old.

## 4. How crowded the category is

From TMview, registered figurative marks carrying Vienna design codes 06.01.02 (mountains) or 05.01.05 (conifers):

| Scope | Count |
|---|---|
| All offices, Nice classes 37 + 44 | **11,430** |
| US portion, mountains, all classes | 577 |
| US portion, conifers, all classes | 1,961 |

**Treat the US figures as an undercount, badly.** TMview reports only **2** mountain-coded marks in class 37 across the entire US register, which cannot be true — the USPTO classifies designs under its own scheme rather than Vienna, and that data travels into TMview incompletely. The all-offices number is the honest one, and the direction it points is unambiguous: a mountain or a conifer in this trade is close to unregistrable as a distinguishing element on its own.

## 5. Which parts of the mark are actually distinctive

| Element | Assessment |
|---|---|
| A conifer | Generic. Thousands of registrations. |
| Mountain/triangle peaks | Generic. |
| A horizontal ground line | Generic. |
| **Overlapping triangles stepping up in opacity, ascending left to right** | Uncommon as drawn, and the closest thing the mark has to a signature. Gradient/overlapping mountain marks exist as a design trope, but the flat stepped-opacity treatment ascending in a row was not found in the trade. |
| **Roots descending from one end of the ground line** | Nothing comparable found in the sector. Root imagery exists in logo design generally, but paired with a tree trunk, not hanging off a baseline beneath a mountain range. |
| **The two together** | No prior use found. |
