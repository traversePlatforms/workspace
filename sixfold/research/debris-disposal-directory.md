---

## How to read this

Sixfold's own tool list is "fell trees, grind stumps, build slash piles, chip, cut rounds, haul off or leave on site." This document is only about the **haul off** line — where the material goes and what the gate charges.

Three things decide the real cost of a disposal run, in this order:

1. **Drive time.** At Front Range fuel and labour, a loaded round trip is $60–$120/hr of truck-and-operator time before the gate fee is paid. A free site 90 minutes away usually loses to a paid site 20 minutes away. Distances below are rough drive estimates from **Monument** (the operating base) and are flagged as estimates.
2. **Whether the site takes a contractor load at all.** Most of Colorado's cheap and free drop-offs are **community slash programs funded for homeowners**. Several bar commercial haulers outright; others require the crew to register the client's property address on arrival. This is the single biggest filter on the list — see the next section.
3. **The gate fee**, which is quoted three different ways: **per ton** (weighed), **per cubic yard** (eyeballed at the scale house), or **per load** (a pickup bed or a defined trailer size). Rough conversions for planning: loose slash ≈ 200–400 lb/cy; chipped material ≈ 500–800 lb/cy; green rounds and logs ≈ 800–1,000 lb/cy. A pickup bed is ≈ 2.5 cy. So a pickup of loose slash is ¼–½ ton; a pickup of green rounds is a ton or more.

Within each region below, sites are ordered **cheapest first** on their headline rate for mixed slash-and-wood, so the best deal is at the top. Where a site is free or near-free but closed to contractors, that is called out — it is still listed because a client may be able to haul their own, and because a chipping-day arrangement can sometimes use it.

**On the very low numbers:** the $5 "wood chips" rates at SCRAP and Eagle County are quoted straight off those landfills' own fee pages — they are that cheap because the material is *already chipped* (2 in max) and the site reuses it as cover. That is **not** a rate for slash, branches or logs, which price higher on the same schedules. Confidence on every figure is in the fact-check log: about twenty sites are confirmed against the operator's own page as of 2026-09-08, a handful are flagged "call" because no rate is published, and two (Chaffee, Pitkin) are carried from older reporting.

## The contractor-access problem — read first

| Site type | Typical contractor rule |
|---|---|
| **Community slash sites run by CUSP** (Bailey, Fairplay, Divide) | **"No Contractors Please."** Homeowners and property owners only. Stated on baileyslashsite.com. |
| **County slash/mulch programs** (Douglas, Jefferson, El Paso/Black Forest) | Built for residents. Jeffco and Black Forest do not publish a contractor ban but price and rules assume a homeowner; Douglas County is "residents only, bring ID." Confirm per program whether the crew may deliver on a resident's behalf. |
| **Boulder County Community Forestry Sort Yards** | **Contractors allowed**, but "required to complete a short registration form for each client's property" — address and owner name per load. Free. |
| **Commercial landfills & organics yards** (Front Range Landfill, DADS, Larimer, SCRAP, Eagle, Pitkin, Milner, Rocky Top, A1 Organics) | Open to commercial haulers at the posted rate. This is where Sixfold's routine haul-off volume goes. |

**Practical read:** for a paid Sixfold job with haul-off in the contract, the usable options are the commercial landfills, **Rocky Top Resources** (Colorado Springs), **A1 Organics** (Front Range), and the **Black Forest Slash & Mulch** site (no stated contractor ban, but a hard 6 ft × 8 in size limit that rules out rounds and stumps). Everything else on this list is a homeowner-haul or chipping-day tool, not a tip site for the crew.

**Structural notes:**
- Sixfold has **no masticator / mulching head**, so there is no on-site mulch-in-place option — everything not left in a slash pile is chipped, cut to rounds, or hauled. That makes disposal cost a live line on most quotes.
- Sixfold is a **commercial hauler**, which removes most of the free and cheap community sites from routine use (see "The contractor-access problem"). The free sites are still relevant for (a) client-haul arrangements and (b) chipping days.
- Distances in this document are **rough drive estimates from Monument** and are not sourced — treat as planning approximations only.
- Rates and season dates at these sites change annually and sometimes mid-season. Re-verify the sites for any region before a quote that commits Sixfold to a haul-off price.
