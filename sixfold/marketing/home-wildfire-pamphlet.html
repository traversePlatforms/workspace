Off the wall and out to the road

Two numbers do most of the work

30 ft

Firewood, propane and slash — all of it at least that far from the house.

Uphill

Stack firewood uphill of the house or level with it, never below — and never between trees or under the deck. Propane: level with the house where you can.

10 ft

Clear flammable vegetation that far around woodpiles, propane tanks and gas meters.

Burning?

Call the sheriff or your fire department first. Restrictions change constantly.

Driveway

Thin 30 ft back from each side, house to main road. A house number at the road, noncombustible and reflective — a crew that cannot find you cannot defend you.

credit

Colorado pays your contractor's invoice

100% of what a contractor charges you for mitigation on land you own, up to $1,000 — if your federal taxable income is under that year's limit ($129,200 for 2025; 2026's is set at year end) and the work meets CSFS standards. Contractor invoices only; not your own labour, rented kit, or anything a grant covered. It cannot exceed your Colorado tax bill, and the excess is lost. From 2027: $2,000, refundable, limit $300,000 AGI. Claim on the DR 0104CR with receipts.

Your insurer must show you its wildfire score

Since 1 July 2026, an insurer using a wildfire score to price or decline a Colorado policy must tell you the score, the range it sits in, what drove it, and what each mitigation action would do to it. Done work since renewal? Ask for a re-score — 30 days. Disagree? Appeal: acknowledged in 10 calendar days, decided in 30.

Colorado wildfire · a homeowner's guide

Embers start most of the homes that burn

Wind carries burning debris more than a mile ahead of a fire. It lands in your gutter, under your deck, in the mulch against your wall. That is the part you can do something about.

Embers Land on anything that will take a spark. The usual cause.
Flame Reaches the wall when fuel sits within five feet of it.
Heat Lights a wall with nothing touching it at all.

Start here, before anything outdoors

Seven places a house catches

Work outward from the wall, not inward from the property line. Every number on the drawing is a job you can do in a weekend.

1

Class A roof. No wood or shake shingle.

2

Clear gutters, roof and deck of needles more than once a year.

3

1/8-inch metal mesh on every vent. Standard rodent screen is 1/4 and lets embers through.

4

Screen under decks and stilt foundations. Store nothing there.

5

Tempered glass, two panes or more. Windows fail before the building does.

6

Six inches of clearance between the ground and the bottom of the siding.

7

Non-combustible fence within five feet. A fence on the house is part of the house.

+

Fibre cement, brick and stucco beat wood siding under radiant heat.

Firefighters may not be there. They engage only when it is safe for them to. Prepare the house to stand on its own.

Then work outward

Three zones, measured from the wall

Build them around every structure — house, garage, shed, barn.

Zone 1

0–5 FT · STRIP IT

Rock, gravel or bare earth. No shrubs, mulch, firewood or deck storage. Rake needles within 5 ft of every deck and structure. Clear 10 ft around the chimney.

Zone 2

5–30 FT · THIN IT

Grass to 4 inches. 10 ft between crowns — or leave small groups of two or three, 30 ft apart. Prune up 6–10 ft, or a third of the tree — whichever is less. Pull ladder fuels and ground juniper. Shrub clumps 2½× their mature height apart.

Zone 3

30–100 FT · OPEN IT

6–10 ft of crown spacing, with real gaps between clumps so fire has to drop to the ground. Take the woody debris away rather than scattering it.

These figures are for ponderosa, Douglas-fir and mixed conifer below 9,500 ft. Piñon-juniper, lodgepole, spruce-fir, Gambel oak and aspen all differ — aspen should generally not be thinned at all. On slopes, increase every spacing.
