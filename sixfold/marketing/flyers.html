Before you pay for fire mitigation

We do the work and show you who pays for it.

Two separate parties will help cover mitigation on your property, and most homeowners are told about neither. Neither is a grant you wait in line for.

Colorado pays the contractor's invoice

The state's wildfire mitigation tax credit covers 100% of what a contractor charges you for qualifying vegetation work, up to a cap. No application, no waiting list — you claim it on your state return. Income limits apply, and the cap rises in 2027.

C.R.S. 39-22-543 · Colorado Dept. of Revenue

Your insurer has to show you its working

Since 1 July 2026, a carrier that uses a wildfire risk score to price your policy must give you that score in writing every year, say what drove it, and give a discount for mitigation you can document. You can ask for a re-score once work is done.

Colorado HB25-1182, effective 1 July 2026

~450

property owners in the entire state claimed the wildfire mitigation tax credit in tax year 2023. The legislature's own evaluation of the programme says the binding constraint is awareness — almost nobody knows it exists.

Colorado wildfire · a homeowner's guide

Embers start most of the homes that burn.

Wind carries burning debris more than a mile ahead of a fire. It lands in your gutter, under your deck, in the mulch against your wall. That is the part you can do something about — and most of it is a weekend, not a contractor.

What is in the guide

  • The seven places a house catches — roof, gutters, vents, under the deck, windows, the gap below the siding, the fence
  • Why the vent mesh size is the whole point — an ember goes straight through the 1/4-inch screen most houses have
  • The three zones, measured from your wall — what to strip, what to thin, what to open, with the actual distances
  • The two numbers that do most of the work — where the firewood, the propane and the slash have to sit
