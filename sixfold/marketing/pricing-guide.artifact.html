Rates as of 2026-08-24 Area Arapahoe · Douglas · Jefferson · the foothills Estimates Free and written

Internal draft. Not printed, not sent, not published — see the two gates at the foot of this sheet.

Every price here is a Colorado competitor’s own published rate, minus 5%.

Not a guess at the market and not an average of guesses. Each table names the operator the figure was cut from, so you can open two tabs and check it. Where a company publishes a range because the job genuinely varies — tree height, stump diameter, acreage — both ends of that range come down 5%.

Almost nobody in this business publishes prices at all. Of eight Colorado operators we screened, one published a full rate card, and it serves the ski counties.

Where your tree lands inside its band comes down to what is underneath it. A pine standing in open ground at the back of a lot is the bottom of the band. The same pine leaning over a roof, a fence line or a neighbour’s shed is the top of it, because it comes down in pieces on ropes instead of in one cut.

Standing dead and beetle-killed price on the same table. They are usually faster to cut and less predictable to drop, and those two cancel out.

Diameter is measured at the widest point where the stump meets the soil, root flare included — the same way both Denver-metro companies above measure it. A stump that looks like 14 inches at eye level is often 22 at the ground, so measure low.

Softwood in open soil sits at the bottom of the per-inch band. Hard wood, tight access, rock or landscaping packed in around the stump, or surface roots running out across a lawn sit at the top.

Stump work cannot be booked same-day

Colorado 811 lists stump grinding by name as excavation. The utility locate has to be requested and the lines marked before a grinder touches the ground — two business days by statute, three in practice. We file the locate, it is free, and it is not billed to you.

Book the felling now and the grinding for the following week, or book both at once and take the later date. Anyone offering to grind your stump this afternoon is skipping that step.

The first three cost nothing because the material never leaves your property and the saw is already running. Chipping costs money because it is a second machine and a second pass. Hauling costs money because it is a drive and a tipping fee.

About the rounds. This is green softwood off your own ground. It needs a season to dry before it burns well, and we do not split it or season it. Front Range firewood sellers get $150 to $300 for a delivered cord of seasoned softwood — rounds in a heap in your yard are not that, and nobody should tell you they are.

Piles headed for a county slash site get stacked differently to piles headed for your own burn. Say which at the estimate.

Burning is not offered. Pile burning is permit-gated and seasonal, and we do not hold the permit. We cut and stack; the burn is between you and your fire district.

07

What we don’t do

Each with the reason, so you can tell a limit from a dodge

  • Forestry mulching and mastication. No masticator and no mulching head in our fleet. Acres of dense brush that want a machine rather than a saw get referred out rather than quoted badly.
  • Tree work inside the City and County of Denver. Denver Revised Municipal Code Chapter 57 requires a Tree Service Company licence to prune or remove a tree for pay anywhere in the city. We work Arapahoe, Douglas, Jefferson and the foothills. Denver proper is a licence question, not a capability one.
  • Pile burning. Permit-gated and seasonal.
  • Emergency and storm call-outs. A damaged tree resting on a structure is a different job with different risk, and Colorado companies charge 25 to 50 percent more for it. We don’t take it.
  • Claim handling and tax advice. Section 08 gives you the facts and the paperwork. Which of it applies to your return is a question for your accountant, and anyone in a truck who tells you otherwise is guessing at your tax position.

Help paying for it

Both of these exist whether or not you hire us

Colorado tax credit $1,000

100% of what you spend on qualifying vegetation work, up to $1,000, subject to an income limit. Rising to $2,000 and refundable from tax year 2027, income limit $300k AGI, running through 2030.

Claimed statewide, TY2023 ~450

Roughly 450 people in the entire state claimed it. The legislature’s own evaluation found the problem is that almost nobody has heard of it.

Your insurer, since 1 Jul 2026

A Colorado insurer using a wildfire risk score to price or non-renew your policy must now send you that score in writing each year, tell you what discounts exist, and discount for documented mitigation. You can appeal the score.

The credit does not cover structural work — roofs, vents, siding. It covers the vegetation.

Every job comes with the file. An itemised invoice separating the qualifying work, plus before-and-after photographs of the areas treated. That is what your accountant needs and what your insurer will ask for. Whether you qualify is their call, not ours.

If you have had a risk-score letter or a non-renewal notice recently, bring it to the estimate. It usually names the specific thing the model doesn’t like.

Internal — not part of the customer sheet

Two gates before this is released

Neither is a copy problem. The customer-facing sheet above is finished.

1. Insurance is not in place

No general liability and no workers compensation. This sheet quotes felling trees over roofs and fence lines at up to $3,800 a tree. Handing it to a homeowner advertises work that cannot be safely taken on uninsured, and the first claim ends the company.

The sheet is deliberately silent on insurance rather than claiming it — but silence stops being enough the moment the sheet is the thing that wins the job. Get the quotes, bind the policy, then release.

2. The launch offer contradicts the rate card

The two flyer offers on the table are “bring us your quote and we’ll halve it” and “first ten properties, 40% off.” Either one printed next to a card that says 5% under market tells a customer the published number is fiction. Pick one:

  1. Publish the card, drop the percentage offer. The card is the offer, and it is the harder one for a competitor to answer.
  2. Run the launch offer first, publish the card when it ends. Ten discounted jobs is a budgeted cost with an end date.
  3. Keep both and price the offer off the card — “40% off our published rate” is coherent where “40% off” floating in space is not.

Recommendation is the first. A published number in a market of “call for a quote” is a durable advantage; 40% off is a one-season one, and it sets an anchor the business then has to climb back from.

Smaller, still open

  • The phone number is a Surge line. If Sixfold is getting its own, it belongs on the card before the card is printed.
  • The Douglas County cost-share is deliberately absent. It pays 50% up to $25,000 and requires two bids from the county’s approved contractor list, which Sixfold is not on. It goes on the day that changes.
  • Denver’s licence packet could not be read — classes, exam, insurance minimums and fees are in a PDF that would not parse. The requirement is confirmed; the terms need a call to (720) 913-0651.
  • The chipping hourly is the weakest number here. No Colorado operator publishes a chipping rate — three were checked and all say “call.” Re-anchor $118 the first time a competitor’s written quote is seen on a job.
  • The removal table is Colorado Springs pricing. No Denver-metro operator publishes a size table. Front Range metro rates are plausibly higher, which would mean 5% understates the discount.

Where the numbers come from

Every price is a named operator’s published figure minus 5%. Tree removal by size, labour hourly and per-acre thinning: Hawk Tree Service, Colorado Springs. Stump grinding per inch: A Stump Man Tree Services, Littleton, whose stated service area includes Monument. Stump minimum, the over-36-inch adder and grindings haul-away: Flatirons Stump Removal — their standard $189, not their $169 promotion, because pricing against someone’s discount is not pricing against the market. Per-acre defensible space: CSFS via the General Assembly’s 2026 tax credit evaluation. The resort comparison: Bear Claw Fire Mitigation, re-checked 2026-08-24 and unchanged. Mileage: Firewood Factory, Wheat Ridge — a delivery rate, used because no tree company publishes one.

Rejected during research: the “$1,700 per acre average, $1,050–$2,100 range” figure that search returned three separate ways attributed to CSFS. It is not a statewide average — it is the Northeast-area lodgepole pine figure with its context stripped by aggregators. The same error was caught in this project’s market research on 2026-08-18 and is still circulating. Also rejected: a “$96–$129 Denver stump removal average” that sits below the published minimum charge of every Colorado stump grinder found.
