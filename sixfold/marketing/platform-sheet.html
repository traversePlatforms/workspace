Which platforms, in what order, and what has to be true first

Prepared 2026-08-18 Companion to Marketing plan §7 Scope Named accounts, not channel strategy

Draft. Nothing here has been published, posted, scheduled, or registered.

A twenty-dollar domain is blocking a free channel.

The marketing plan ranks ten channels. This sheet names the actual platforms and sorts them by what gates each one. Working through that produced one finding that changes sequencing: every platform binds ownership to the email used at signup, and the only address Sixfold has belongs to Surge. The domain was already blocking print. It now blocks the two free platforms as well, which moves it ahead of insurance in the order of work.

01

The gate before every platform

Register the domain first

Register sixfoldland.com and stand up an email on it before creating a single account.

The only address available right now is m.sharpe@surgeftw.com, which is Surge's domain. Signing up with it does three things, all bad:

  • It makes Sixfold's accounts traceable to Surge, on top of the shared address at 333 W Hampden. §2 of the plan already flags the related-party structure as a live exposure; this makes it publicly visible rather than visible only to the books.
  • Ownership transfer on a verified Google Business Profile is a recovery process, not a settings change. Doing it later means a week of support tickets against a listing that is by then carrying reviews.
  • Any platform that mails a verification code to a business address inherits the Surge address problem permanently.

Why this reorders the plan

Domain registration was already item 3 on the README's next-up list, blocking print. It now blocks platform setup too — and platform setup is otherwise free and available today, while everything in tier 2 waits on insurance quotes that take weeks. A cheap task is holding up the only work that can start immediately.

02

Tier 1 — set up now

Free, and gated on nothing but the domain

Gate

Domain and email only. No insurance, no certification, no portfolio required.

Google Business Profile

Set up first

Where a homeowner checks whether a contractor exists before calling

The one non-negotiable, and the container the phase 1 reviews accumulate in.

Set it up as a service-area business with the address removed. Google's guidance is that a business without a storefront and permanent on-site signage is not eligible as a storefront and should be listed as service-area, and that a business not serving customers at its address should remove it. Sixfold qualifies on both counts — which solves the shared-address problem for free.

Constraints, from Google's documentation

  • Service areas are specified by city or postal code, not by radius. Up to 20 distinct areas.
  • The overall area should not exceed about 2 hours of driving time from where the business is based. Monument out to the El Paso, Teller and southern Douglas County foothills sits comfortably inside that, so the whole phase 1 target geography is coverable.
  • Service-area edits go through review and can take up to 48 hours to appear.

Two things to get right at setup. Categories determine which searches the profile can surface for, and the primary one is weighted most heavily — pick against what a homeowner would actually type, not how the trade describes itself. And the service area is a claim about where Sixfold works, not a limit on it, the same principle as the site copy rule in §5 of the plan.

Do not

No "licensed and insured" in the profile description. The §1 hard rule covers platform bios exactly as it covers print.

Nextdoor

Claim now, pays later

Neighbourhood-scoped — the same geography the canvass thesis rests on

Structurally the closest match to the plan's core argument. Wildfire risk maps to specific streets, Nextdoor is organised by those streets, and it is where a homeowner who has just opened an HB25‑1182 risk-score notice asks what it means. §2's point — that the trigger event arrives by mail with no vendor attached — describes a Nextdoor thread as accurately as it describes a doorstep.

Claiming a Business Page is free. What matters is where the value actually comes from, because it is not cold posting:

  • Recommendations are the mechanism. Nextdoor's model is neighbour-to-neighbour, and a recommendation surfaces in the recommender's own neighbourhood. A completed job in a foothill subdivision generates visibility in that subdivision — precisely the geography the canvass wants, and precisely the geography an Monument-based page cannot reach on its own.
  • So the page's value is downstream of phase 1 jobs, not upstream of them. Claim it now so recommendations have somewhere to land from job one. Do not expect cold leads before there are jobs.

Two cautions

Nextdoor punishes overt selling. The register has to match the door script in §2 — the change in the law, what it means, here is the one-pager. Sales posts get flagged by neighbours, and being flagged there is worse than being absent.

It compounds with the canvass rather than adding to it. If a canvasser irritates a street, Nextdoor is where that gets posted, with a company name attached, to exactly the neighbourhood being worked. Confirm solicitation permits and drill the script before the page is live and the canvass is running.

Facebook — local and HOA groups

Join, read, wait

Where FRWRM conversation happens ahead of the 8 Oct deadline

Groups, not a business page, and emphatically not ads. Neighbourhood and HOA groups are where board members discuss grant applications, and where the same insurance-notice question gets asked as on Nextdoor.

Same register rule. Most local groups carry an explicit no-promotion policy and a moderator who enforces it — answering a question usefully is permitted almost everywhere, posting an offer is not. Read each group's rules before posting once.

A business page is worth creating only as the thing a group comment can link back to. It is a credential, not a channel.

Tier 2 — gated on insurance

Highest intent in the market, and all of it locked

Gate

General liability and workers comp in force. Nothing in this tier can be joined, advertised, or referenced in copy before then.

Blocked Douglas County approved contractor list

Channel #2 in the plan. Requirements unknown — needs the phone call to the Building Division. Feeds homeowners who already hold cost-share funding and must obtain two bids from the list.

Blocked Other county cost-share programmes

The same work applies to any county running one. Worth asking on the Douglas call which neighbouring counties operate equivalents.

Unconfirmed Fire district & CSFS vendor lists

Not confirmed to exist as formal lists. One question on the county call, one to a district office. If real, they are free referral channels feeding pre-funded work.

Copy rule carries over

The §1 rule against advertising the Douglas County cost-share before listing applies to platform bios and posts as well as to print — and to what a canvasser says out loud.

Tier 3 — phase 2 and later

Real, but they lag on content or proof

Gate

Completed jobs, photographs, and reviews. These convert against evidence that phase 1 has not produced yet.

Phase 2 The site at sixfoldland.com

The funding explainer is the search asset (§5). Lags because it needs content and real job costs to publish pricing from.

Shoot now, publish later YouTube and Instagram

Strong raw material: felling, chipping, fixed-angle before-and-afters. Footage cost is already zero, because §4 requires those photo sets on every job for insurance documentation anyway. Builds brand rather than local leads, which is why it is not tier 1.

Phase 3, capped Paid search

The plan's read stands — a capped test at most, and only once a site and reviews exist to convert against.

05

Deliberately not doing

Decided against, with the reason recorded

Lead marketplaces — Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor

Skip

Not a cost objection, a strategy objection. Their model sells the same lead to several contractors at once, which turns every conversation into a price comparison. Sixfold's entire differentiator is a two-minute conversation about where the money comes from, and that conversation does not survive a lead form. They also push toward lead volume when phase 1 needs ten well-documented jobs, and they compete on exactly the credential axis §1 concluded Sixfold loses this year.

Revisit only if phase 1 and 2 fail to fill winter capacity — as a stopgap with a hard spend cap, not as a channel.

Skip Paid social & display

Unchanged from §7. Fire mitigation is a considered, geographic, trigger-driven purchase, and there is nothing to convert against yet.

Skip LinkedIn

Weak fit. HOA and fire-district board members are volunteers reached through their district, their management company, and their own email threads — not a professional network. FRWRM is a relationship motion, not a platform one.

Skip TikTok

The content would perform. The audience is not Front Range homeowners with a risk score and an insurance notice on the counter, and phase 1 cannot service leads it cannot geographically qualify.

06

Order of operations

A real sequence — each step unblocks the next

  1. Register sixfoldland.com; set up a forwarder or mailbox on it
  2. Create the Google Business Profile as a service-area business, address hidden, categories chosen deliberately
  3. Claim the Nextdoor Business Page
  4. Read the rules of three to five local and HOA groups covering the phase 1 geography; join, do not post yet
  5. Decide the review ask before job one — see below
  6. Insurance bound → apply to the Douglas County list and anything else in tier 2
  7. Phase 2 → the site, then the video decision

The review ask needs a named platform

§4 requires asking for the review at completion, on site, phone in hand. That instruction is incomplete until it names a platform, because asking for two at once reliably produces neither.

Google first, every time — it is where the next homeowner checks. Nextdoor second, and only from customers in the foothill subdivisions where a neighbourhood recommendation carries the geographic value described above.

Blocked on

Check each platform's review policy before writing the ask script. Both restrict incentivised reviews, and the exact wording a contractor may use is a live compliance question, not a style question. Not verified here.

07

Fact-check log

What is sourced, what was corrected, what is still open

Verified — first-party sources

  • Google Business Profile service-area rules: address should be removed where customers are not served at the business address; a business without a storefront and permanent on-site signage should be listed as service-area; areas specified by city or postal code with up to 20 areas; overall area should not exceed about 2 hours of driving time from base; edits take up to 48 hours after review — Google Business Profile Help, service areas and manage your business address
  • Nextdoor Business Pages are free to claim, and the free tier includes collecting recommendations and posting to selected neighbourhoods with an adjustable radius — Nextdoor Business, free business page

Corrected during research

  • Secondary write-ups state that free Nextdoor business posts reach only neighbours within two miles and are capped at twice per month. Nextdoor's own page states unlimited free posts with manually selectable neighbourhoods and an adjustable radius. The two conflict and the first-party source wins, so the aggregator figures are not used. A textbook instance of the pattern the research standard exists for — three of the sources carrying those figures were SEO content sites.

Unresolved — flagged, not guessed

  • How far Nextdoor free-post reach extends from a Monument business address. Nextdoor documents an adjustable radius but states no maximum for the free tier. This determines whether Nextdoor is a cold channel into the foothills or purely a recommendation surface. The sheet assumes the conservative reading; confirm in the account once the page exists.
  • Google and Nextdoor review-solicitation policies — what a contractor may ask for on site. Blocks the closeout script wording.
  • Whether fire protection districts or CSFS maintain formal vendor lists. Assumed possible, not confirmed.
  • Nextdoor and Facebook group promotion rules per group. Vary by moderator; read before posting.
  • Google Business Profile verification method for a service-area business with a hidden address, and whether verification must complete before the address can be hidden. Community threads report friction; not confirmed against Google's documentation.
  • Lead marketplace terms. The shared-lead model is the structural objection and is well established, but current per-lead pricing and exclusivity options for this trade were not researched, because the recommendation is to skip them regardless.
