## What D is for

The rate sheets answer "what does it cost." D answers "what do you actually do." It is the sheet for the moment before price is on the table: a first knock, an HOA or fire-district board, a stall at a chipping day, an email to someone who has not asked for a quote yet.

**Same visual system as A, B and C** — the style block and the Ridge mark are lifted verbatim from `pricing-onesheet.html` rather than restyled, so the four sheets read as one company. The only new CSS is a service row with no amount column, the scope band and the zone strip.

**One page is the spec**, verified by generating the PDF and counting. Adding the zone strip pushed it to two and it had to be trimmed back — the usual trap on these sheets.

## The two regulated lines, and how they are worded

These are the only two claims on the sheet that could get Sixfold into trouble, and both are worded against the constraint rather than around it.

**Tax credit.** The sheet offers *an itemised invoice that separates the qualifying vegetation work, a written scope, and before-and-after photographs — what the claim needs attached*, then says plainly: **"We cannot file your return and this is not tax advice; whether you qualify is your accountant's call."**

The rejected phrasing is *"we handle the paperwork for you"*, which reads as tax preparation. Supplying documentation is a service; filing a return is a licensed activity and a conflict of interest besides, since Sixfold is the vendor whose invoice is being claimed.

**Insurance premium.** Brandon's term is "assist with insurance premiums." The sheet states the statutory facts — since **1 July 2026** an insurer using a wildfire risk score must disclose the score, what drove it, and what each mitigation action would do to it, and a homeowner may request a re-score after work — then what Sixfold supplies (the record) and then the limit: **"The insurer decides what it is worth; we cannot promise a discount."**

Three things are deliberately absent:

- **Any promise of a lower premium.** C.R.S. 10-4-124 entitles a homeowner to a re-score, not to a better one. The discount duty in §(4) is conditional — it applies only where the insurer does not already incorporate mitigation into its models — so no homeowner is owed a discount and the sheet must not imply one.
- **Any dollar or percentage figure.** Each insurer publishes its own; there is no statewide number, and inventing one is exactly what the research standard exists to prevent.
- **Anything touching a claim.** A premium discount is not an insurance claim, so documenting mitigation is not public adjusting. Handling or negotiating a claim would be, and Sixfold holds no licence for it. The sheet stays entirely on the underwriting side of that line.

## Fact-check log

Every factual claim on D is either carried from work already verified first-party in this project, or is a statement about Sixfold's own operations.

**Verified first-party, 2026-08-25** (in the course of the pamphlet audit, same session):

- **1 July 2026** effective date, the disclosure contents, and the right to request a revised score — HB25-1182 / C.R.S. 10-4-124, enacted act PDF read directly. §(6)(b)(I)–(V) and §(7)(d).
- The conditional nature of the discount duty — §(3) and §(4), and the 2026-08-21 project review that first caught it being overstated.
- **Tax credit mechanics** — Colorado Department of Revenue *Income Tax Topics: Wildfire Mitigation Measures* for the contractor-invoice rule, the receipts requirement and the DR 0104CR route. No credit figures are printed on D, so the income limits and caps are not in play here.

**Carried from the pamphlet, verified against the CSFS *Home Ignition Zone* guide (2021) and its checklists:**

- **6–10 ft** of branch clearance from the ground — Zone 2 pruning rule, p. 8.
- **10 ft** between tree crowns in Zone 2; **6–10 ft** in Zone 3 — pp. 8–9.
- Slash kept **30 ft or more** from the house — Home Ignition Zone checklists.
- Zone distances 0–5, 5–30 and 30–100 ft, measured from the wall, built around each structure.
- Zone 1's non-combustible surfacing, which is what the landscaping line refers to.
- Zone 3's preference for taking woody material off rather than scattering it.
- Ground juniper and Gambel oak named as the brush worth removing — both called out by name in the guide.

**Statements about Sixfold, not verified against anything external:** the nine services, the disposal options, the free written estimate, and the fixed-price-before-work term. These are Brandon's to confirm.

**Colorado's three-business-day cancellation right** on a contract signed at the customer's home — C.R.S. 5-3-401 to 5-3-405 and 16 CFR 429, both read first-party when the agreements were built 2026-08-21. Carried over verbatim from sheet A, where it already appears.
