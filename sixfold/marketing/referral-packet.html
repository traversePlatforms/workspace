Getting money back3 of 6 · The state tax credit

Statewide · claim it for this project · C.R.S. 39-22-543

The Colorado wildfire-mitigation tax credit

A credit on your Colorado state income tax return for what you paid a contractor to reduce wildfire risk on your own land. No application and no waiting list — it is claimed with your return, using the invoice in this folder.

How much
100% of the qualifying cost, capped at $1,000 for tax years 2025 and 2026. The cap rises to $2,000 for 2027–2030, and in those years the credit becomes refundable — paid to you even if you owe no state tax. Income limits apply; ask your accountant which one applies to your year.
Applies to
Any private land in Colorado. Only paid third-party contractor work counts — your Sixfold invoice does; your own labour or equipment does not.
Qualifying work
Creating defensible space, building fuel breaks, thinning woody vegetation to protect structures, and treating the resulting slash — chipping, hauling, lop-and-scatter, piling — done to Colorado State Forest Service or Division of Fire Prevention & Control standards. Your invoice separates this work from anything that does not qualify.
How to claim
You or your accountant file Form DR 0104CR with your Colorado return for the year you paid. Keep the invoice and the “standard the work was done to” line from page 2. Sixfold does not prepare or file returns.

One rule if you combine programs. The credit excludes any cost that was paid or reimbursed by a grant or cost-share. Grant money and the credit cannot both land on the same dollar. If you do receive a grant, ask your tax preparer how it is treated.

Worth checking. The credit is for work whose main purpose is reducing wildfire risk to structures. If your project was mostly cleanup of downed wood, ask your accountant whether it meets that purpose test — and keep the invoice either way.

Getting money back4 of 6 · Your insurance

Statewide · claim it for this project · Colorado HB25-1182

The wildfire-mitigation discount on your homeowners insurance

Since July 1, 2026, Colorado law requires an insurer that uses a wildfire risk score to factor the mitigation you have actually done into your score and your price — and to give you a discount for documented mitigation.

What the law says
Your insurer must tell you your wildfire risk score in writing every year, tell you what mitigation discounts are available, factor completed defensible-space work into the score, give a discount for documented mitigation even if it has no formal model of its own, and let you appeal the score.
How much
There is no set percentage — it varies by carrier. Expect it to count in your favour; do not expect a specific number, and we will not promise one.
What to send
Page 2 of this folder and the before-and-after photos. That is the package carriers ask for: what was cleared, where, to what spacing, with dated pictures from fixed positions.
What to ask
Ask your carrier or agent to re-evaluate your wildfire risk score or apply the mitigation discount. If the score looks wrong, use the appeal right the law gives you.

A note you can send with it

“I have completed wildfire mitigation work on my property at address, finished on date. Enclosed is the contractor's written record of the work and dated before-and-after photographs. Under Colorado HB25-1182, please re-evaluate my wildfire risk score and apply any mitigation discount available on my policy. Please also confirm my current score in writing.”

If you cannot get or keep coverage: the Colorado FAIR Plan offers residential cover as a last resort. A backstop, not a target.
