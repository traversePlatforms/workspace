## The gate that comes before every platform

**Register `sixfoldland.com` and stand up an email on it before creating a single account.**

Every platform binds ownership to the email address used at signup. The only address available right now is `m.sharpe@surgeftw.com`, which is Surge's domain. Signing up with it does three things, all bad:

1. It makes Sixfold's platform accounts traceable to Surge, on top of the shared address at 333 W Hampden. §2 of the plan already flags the related-party structure as a live exposure; this makes it publicly visible rather than just visible to the books.
2. Ownership transfer on a verified Google Business Profile is a recovery process, not a settings change. Doing it later is a week of support tickets against a listing that is by then carrying reviews.
3. Any platform that emails a verification postcard or code to a business address inherits the Surge address problem permanently.

Domain registration is already item 3 on the README's next-up list, blocking print. It now blocks platform setup too, which moves it ahead of insurance in sequencing terms — it is a twenty-dollar task blocking a free channel.

## Tier 1 — set up now, gated on nothing but the domain

### Google Business Profile

The one non-negotiable. It is where a homeowner checks whether a contractor exists before calling, and it is the container the reviews from phase 1 accumulate in.

Set it up as a **service-area business** with the address removed. Google's own guidance is that a business without a storefront and permanent on-site signage is not eligible as a storefront and should be listed as service-area, and that a business not serving customers at its address should remove it. Sixfold qualifies on both counts, and it solves the shared-address problem for free.

Practical constraints, from Google's documentation:

- Service areas are specified **by city or postal code**, not by radius. Up to 20 distinct areas.
- The overall service area **should not exceed about 2 hours of driving time** from where the business is based. Monument out to the El Paso, Teller and southern Douglas County foothills sits comfortably inside that, so the phase 1 target geography is fully coverable.
- Edits to service areas go through review and can take up to 48 hours to appear.

Two things to get right at setup. **Categories** determine which searches the profile can surface for, and the primary category is weighted most heavily — pick against what a homeowner would actually type, not against how the trade describes itself. And **the service area is a claim about where Sixfold works, not a limit on it** — same principle as the site copy rule in §5 of the plan.

**Do not put "licensed and insured" in the profile description.** The §1 hard rule applies to platform bios exactly as it applies to print.

### Nextdoor

The platform whose structure most closely matches the plan's core thesis. Wildfire risk maps to specific streets; Nextdoor is organised by those streets; and it is where a homeowner who has just opened an HB25-1182 risk-score notice asks what it means. The plan's §2 argument — that the trigger event arrives by mail with no vendor attached — describes a Nextdoor thread as accurately as it describes a doorstep.

Claiming a Business Page is free. What is worth understanding is **where the value actually comes from**, because it is not cold posting:

- **Recommendations are the mechanism.** Nextdoor's model is neighbour-to-neighbour recommendation, and a recommendation surfaces in the recommender's own neighbourhood. That means a completed job in a foothill subdivision generates visibility *in that subdivision* — which is precisely the geography the canvass wants and precisely the geography an Monument-based business page cannot reach on its own.
- **So the page's value is downstream of phase 1 jobs, not upstream of them.** Claim it now so recommendations have somewhere to land from job one. Do not expect it to produce cold leads before there are jobs.

Two cautions:

- **Nextdoor punishes overt selling.** The register has to be the same information-first tone as the door script in §2 — the change in the law, what it means, here is the one-pager. Sales posts get flagged by neighbours, and being flagged on Nextdoor is worse than being absent from it.
- **It cuts both ways with the canvass.** If a canvasser irritates a street, Nextdoor is where that gets posted, with a company name attached, to exactly the neighbourhood the canvass was working. Confirm the solicitation permits and drill the script *before* the page is live and the canvass is running — the two risks compound rather than add.

### Facebook — local and HOA groups only

Groups, not a business page, and emphatically not ads. Neighbourhood and HOA groups are where FRWRM conversation happens among board members ahead of the **8 October 2026** deadline, and where the same insurance-notice question gets asked as on Nextdoor.

Same register rule. Most local groups have an explicit no-promotion policy and a moderator who enforces it; answering a question usefully is permitted almost everywhere, posting an offer is not. Read each group's rules before posting once.

A business page is worth creating only as the thing a group comment links back to. It is a credential, not a channel.

## Tier 2 — gated on insurance

These are the highest-intent platforms in the market, and every one of them requires proof of general liability and workers comp to join.

- **Douglas County approved contractor list** — channel #2 in the plan. Requirements unknown; needs the phone call to the Building Division. Feeds homeowners who already have cost-share funding and are required to obtain two bids from the list.
- **Other county cost-share and approved-contractor programmes** — the same work applies anywhere running one. Worth asking on the same call which neighbouring counties operate equivalents.
- **Fire protection district and CSFS vendor lists** — not confirmed to exist as formal lists. Worth one question on the Douglas County call and one to a district office. If they exist, they are free referral channels feeding pre-funded work.

Nothing in this tier can be advertised or referenced in copy until listing is actual. The §1 rule against advertising the Douglas County cost-share before listing applies to platform bios and posts as well as print.

- **The website at `sixfoldland.com`** — the funding explainer is the search asset (§5). Lags because it needs content.
- **YouTube and Instagram** — the raw material is genuinely strong: felling, chipping, and fixed-angle before/afters of a zone. Builds brand and credibility rather than local leads, which is why it is not tier 1.

## Deliberately not doing

**Lead marketplaces — Angi, Thumbtack, HomeAdvisor, Networx and equivalents.** Not a cost objection, a strategy objection. Their model sells the same lead to several contractors simultaneously, which turns every conversation into a price comparison. Sixfold's entire differentiator is a two-minute conversation about where the money comes from, and that conversation does not survive a lead form. They also push toward lead volume when phase 1 needs ten well-documented jobs, and they compete on exactly the credential axis §1 concluded Sixfold loses this year.

Revisit only if phase 1 and 2 fail to produce enough pipeline to fill winter capacity — as a stopgap, with a hard spend cap, not as a channel.

**Paid social and display.** Unchanged from §7. Fire mitigation is a considered, geographic, trigger-driven purchase, and there is nothing to convert against yet.

**LinkedIn.** Weak fit. HOA and fire-district board members are volunteers reached through their district, their management company, and their own email threads — not through a professional network. The FRWRM approach in §4 is a relationship motion, not a platform one.

**TikTok.** The content would perform. The audience is not Front Range homeowners with a wildfire risk score and an insurance notice on the counter, and phase 1 has no capacity to service leads it cannot geographically qualify.

## Order of operations

1. Register `sixfoldland.com`; set up a forwarder or mailbox on it
2. Create the Google Business Profile as a service-area business, address hidden, categories chosen deliberately
3. Claim the Nextdoor Business Page
4. Read the rules of three to five local and HOA groups covering the phase 1 target geography; join, do not post yet
5. **Decide the review ask before job one** — see below
6. Insurance bound → apply to the Douglas County list and anything else in tier 2
7. Phase 2 → site, then the video decision

### The review ask needs a named platform

§4 requires asking for the review at completion, on site, phone in hand. That instruction is incomplete until it names a platform, because asking for two at once reliably produces neither.

**Google first, every time** — it is where the next homeowner checks. Nextdoor second, and only from customers in the foothill subdivisions where a neighbourhood recommendation carries the geographic value described above.

**Check each platform's review policy before writing the ask script.** Both platforms restrict incentivised reviews, and the exact wording of what a contractor may ask for is a live compliance question, not a style question. Not verified here.

## Fact-check log

**Verified — first-party sources:**
- Google Business Profile service-area rules: address should be removed where customers are not served at the business address; a business without a storefront and permanent on-site signage should be listed as service-area; service areas specified by city or postal code with up to 20 areas; overall area should not exceed about 2 hours of driving time from base; service-area edits take up to 48 hours after review — [Google Business Profile Help, service areas](https://support.google.com/business/answer/9157481?hl=en) and [manage your business address](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2853879?hl=en)
- Nextdoor Business Pages are free to claim, and the free tier includes collecting recommendations and posting to selected neighbourhoods with an adjustable radius — [Nextdoor Business, free business page](https://business.nextdoor.com/en-us/getting-started/business-page)

**Corrected during research:**
- Secondary write-ups state that free Nextdoor business posts reach only neighbours **within two miles** and are capped at **twice per month**. Nextdoor's own page states unlimited free posts with manually selectable neighbourhoods and an adjustable radius. The two conflict and the first-party source wins, so the aggregator figures are not used. Textbook instance of the pattern the research standard exists for — three of the sources carrying those figures were SEO content sites.

**Unresolved — flagged, not guessed:**
- **How far Nextdoor free-post reach actually extends from a Monument business address.** Nextdoor documents an adjustable radius but does not state a maximum for the free tier. This determines whether Nextdoor is a cold channel into the foothills or purely a recommendation-harvesting surface. The sheet assumes the conservative reading — recommendations, not cold reach — and it should be confirmed in the account once the page exists.
- **Google and Nextdoor review-solicitation policies** — what a contractor may and may not ask for on site. Blocks the closeout script wording.
- **Whether fire protection districts or CSFS maintain formal vendor lists.** Assumed possible, not confirmed.
- **Nextdoor and Facebook group promotion rules** per group. Vary by moderator; read before posting.
- **Google Business Profile verification method** for a service-area business with a hidden address, and whether verification must complete before the address can be hidden. Community threads report friction here; not confirmed against Google's documentation.
- **Lead marketplace terms** — the shared-lead model is the structural objection and it is well established, but current per-lead pricing and exclusivity options for this trade were not researched, because the recommendation is to skip them regardless.

**Standing:** nothing in this sheet has been published, posted or scheduled, and nothing will be without explicit per-request authorisation. Claiming a profile is not publishing; posting to one is.
