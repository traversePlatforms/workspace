## Long description — for the Square Online "About" section

> Sixfold Colorado is a fire mitigation company based in Monument, Colorado, working across the Front Range.
>
> We fell trees, grind stumps, pile and chip slash, cut rounds, and either haul it away or leave it stacked where you want it. Defensible space around the structures, fuel breaks, and thinning — to Colorado State Forest Service standards.
>
> Most people are never told that two separate parties will help pay for that work.
>
> Colorado's wildfire mitigation tax credit covers 100% of what you pay a contractor for qualifying vegetation work, up to a cap. There is no application and no waiting list — you claim it on your state return. And since 1 July 2026, if your insurer uses a wildfire risk score to price your policy, they have to tell you that score in writing every year and give you a discount for mitigation you can document.
>
> Income limits apply to the credit, and what your own policy qualifies for is a question for your carrier. But almost nobody asks, because almost nobody knows to.
>
> So every job we do comes with the paperwork, whether or not you ask for it: a written scope of exactly what was cut and where, and photographs from fixed positions before and after. One document set, and it serves your tax return and your carrier both.
>
> Call (720) 772-6146 or email Contact@SixfoldColorado.com and we'll come and walk the property.
>
> Not tax or insurance advice — confirm your own position with your accountant and your carrier.

**About 1,300 characters.** Trim from the top: the second paragraph is the most cuttable, then the "income limits apply" paragraph — though cutting that one costs the honesty that makes the rest credible.

## What must not go in the description or any Square field

Same standing rules as every other Sixfold surface:

1. **"Licensed and insured", "certified", "bonded", "Firewise".** None of it is true yet, and a profile claim is the kind that gets screenshotted.
2. **The Douglas County cost-share, or any county cost-share by name.** Hard rule until Sixfold is on the approved contractor list.
3. **Anything implying a track record** — years in business, job counts, "trusted by", review counts. The company is new. What it leads with instead is the funding knowledge, which is checkable.
4. **"We handle the paperwork for you."** Banned phrasing, in print and in conversation. The approved framing is *"every job comes with the paperwork, whether or not you ask for it"* — Sixfold produces the scope and the photos; it does not file returns, contact carriers, or touch claims.
5. **Any mention of Surge.** The related-party structure is real and unsettled; a Square profile is not where it gets disclosed first.
6. **A price, a discount, or "free estimates."** The launch offer is still unpicked; committing to it on a permanent surface pre-empts that decision.
