The work

Three things, and they usually turn up on the same property.

A property that needs the fuel taken out of it generally needs the ground put back afterwards. We do both, and the clearing in between.

One

Fire mitigation

We fell trees, remove and trim them whole, take out brush and dead fall, pile and chip slash, and cut rounds to length.

What happens to the wood is your call — chipped on site, hauled away, or left stacked where you want it. Left where it falls is the third option and it is what happens by default.

Defensible space around the structures, fuel breaks, and thinning by the acre, worked to Colorado State Forest Service standards for spacing and clearance.

If you want to know what those standards actually say before anyone quotes you, it is all in the wildfire guide — including the parts you can do yourself in a weekend.

Two

Land clearing

Brush, undergrowth and dead fall taken off a lot, a pasture or an easement — by the acre, where that is the sensible unit rather than tree by tree.

Same disposal question as above, and it is one of the things settled at the walk rather than discovered halfway through. Hauling costs differently from chipping, and both cost differently from leaving it stacked.

Three

Landscaping

Mowing, edging, beds and planting — the ground put back in order once the fuel is out of it.

Usually the same job rather than a second one. A property that has just had a crew and a chipper on it does not look finished until this part is done, and booking two outfits to do one property is how a month goes by.

This is what the current crew and kit can take on. A working list, not a fixed scope — written from what is in the truck, not from what we are willing to quote. If what you need is not on it, ask. Based in Monument, working El Paso, Teller and southern Douglas counties and the Front Range foothills; further out is a phone call, not a refusal.

How it goes

Walk it, work it, hand it over.

  1. 01

    The walk

    We come out and walk the property with you, counting what is actually there — trees by height, brush by area, what the access will take, and what you want kept. It goes on a sheet, and the quote comes off that sheet rather than off an impression formed in the driveway.

  2. 02

    The work

    Photographs from fixed positions before anything comes down, then the felling, the clearing and the disposal option you picked. Anything you asked to keep gets pointed out at the walk and left on the property.

  3. 03

    The handover

    We walk the finished job with you, and you get a folder. It is the same folder every time, and it is handed over whether or not you asked for it.

What you keep

Every job comes with the paperwork, whether or not you ask.

  • What we did on your property — a written record of the work and the standard it was done to. This is the page your insurer wants.
  • Your itemised invoice — with the wildfire-mitigation work separated out, because that is what the state credit is claimed against.
  • Before-and-after photos — dated, from fixed positions.
  • Getting money back — the state credit and the insurance re-score, with what each one needs from you. What a discount is worth is the insurer's decision, not ours.
  • Programs in your county — the ones that pay toward future mitigation. All of them need approval before work starts, which is why they are in here for next time rather than this time.
  • A card, a magnet and a sticker — so the number is on the fridge when a neighbour asks who did the work.

One document set, and it serves your tax return and your carrier both. You get it at the end of the job as a matter of course.

Sixfold produces the record. It does not file your return, contact your carrier, or handle a claim — those stay with you and your advisers, and the folder is built so they have what they need without a phone call to us.

And if anything about the work is not right, call. The crew that did it is the crew that comes back.
