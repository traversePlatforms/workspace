Who pays for it

Two parties will help cover this. Most people are never told about either.

Neither of them is a grant you wait in line for. One is a line on your state return; the other is a number your insurer now has to show you. Both need the same paperwork, and that is the part a contractor can actually do for you.

~450

property owners in the entire state claimed the wildfire mitigation tax credit in tax year 2023. The legislature's own evaluation says the binding constraint is awareness — almost nobody knows it exists.

2026 legislative evaluation of C.R.S. 39-22-543

One · the state

Colorado pays your contractor's invoice, up to a cap.

The wildfire mitigation tax credit covers 100% of what a contractor charges you for qualifying vegetation work on land you own — currently up to $1,000.

There is no application and no waiting list. You claim it on your state return, on the DR 0104CR, with your receipts.

What counts
Contractor invoices only, for work that meets Colorado State Forest Service standards. Not your own labour, not rented kit, and not anything a grant already covered.
Who qualifies
There is an income limit, and it is on federal taxable income — $129,200 for tax year 2025. The 2026 figure is not set until the end of the year.
The catch, before 2027
The credit cannot exceed your Colorado tax bill. Anything above it is lost — not refunded, and not carried to next year.
From tax year 2027
The cap rises to $2,000, the credit becomes refundable, and the income limit rises to $300,000 AGI — a much wider gate than the figures alone suggest, because AGI is the higher of the two measures. It runs through 2030.

C.R.S. 39-22-543 · Colorado Dept. of Revenue, Income Tax Topics: Wildfire Mitigation Measures · HB26-1289 and the Office of the State Auditor's 2026 evaluation for the 2027 figures

Two · your insurer

Since 1 July 2026, they have to show you their working.

If your carrier uses a wildfire risk score to price or decline a Colorado policy, it now has to tell you what that score is — in writing, every year.

Four things, specifically: the score, the range it sits in, what drove it, and what each mitigation action would do to it.

That last one is an effect on the score, not a dollar figure. No insurer will quote you a saving in advance, and anyone who does is guessing.

30 days
Done the work since your last renewal? You can ask for a re-score rather than waiting a year for one.
10 then 30
Disagree with the score? Appeal it. Your carrier has 10 calendar days to acknowledge and 30 to decide.
What it is worth
The insurer's decision, not ours. Carriers using a score must give a discount for mitigation you can document — but the size of it is theirs to set.

Colorado HB25-1182, effective 1 July 2026 · C.R.S. 10-4-124(6)(b)(V)

Both of them want the same thing

A written record, and photographs.

The credit is claimed against an invoice that separates the qualifying work. The re-score is argued with evidence of what changed. One document set does both.

That is why every Sixfold job ends with a folder rather than a handshake, and why it is handed over whether or not anyone asked for it.

What Sixfold does not do: file your return, contact your carrier, or handle a claim. Supplying documentation is a service. Filing a return is a licensed activity, and we are the vendor whose invoice is being claimed — which is a conflict of interest as well as a competence question.

HOA & fire-district boards

There is a third pot, and your board applies for it, not us.

State and county money exists for community defensible-space and fuel-break projects. Contractors cannot apply for it — fire districts, HOAs, nonprofits and utilities can. Sixfold is the contractor you hire once you have it.

The same written record comes back with the job, which is usually what a board has to show its funder. If you are putting a project together, we'll walk it and bid it.
