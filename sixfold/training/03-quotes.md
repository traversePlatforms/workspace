# Quotes

Walking a property and getting it to a price. One `## ` heading = one lesson in the app (heading: Quotes).
The crew count; the office prices. Nothing here carries a rate on purpose.

## Walking a lawn
Source: Sixfold aeration agreement sections 2-4 and clauses 4-5 (Brian Skelton / Kara Rosenstrauch builds); sixfold/research/water-restrictions-2026.md.

The walk is where the job is won or lost. Walk the lawn with the owner if you can.

### Ask
- Which water provider are they on? (City utility, a district, or a well.)
- On a well: what does the permit allow? Household use only means no lawn watering at all.
- Is the lawn green, or brown and dormant? Don't aerate a dormant lawn.
- What do they want: aeration only, or with overseed and fertilizer?

### Look for
- Sprinkler heads, valve boxes and invisible pet fence. Get the owner to point them out. Private lines that aren't marked are theirs to show us.
- Septic tank and leach field, a well head, buried propane, low-voltage lighting.
- Slopes, rock, tight gates and anything the machine can't reach.
- Beds, new planting or bare ground they want left alone.

### Tell them
- The plugs stay on the lawn and break down in a few weeks. That's how it's meant to work.
- Water the lawn a day or two before we come. Dry, hard ground gives a poor plug.
- We request utility locates through Colorado 811 before the job, so the date allows for that.

### Record it
Use the walk in the app or the walk sheet. Write what you saw, not a price.

## Walking a tree or clearing job
Source: sixfold/operations/quote-walk-sheet.md (section 1 counts, section 2 what it takes, decisions worth keeping).

Use the quote walk sheet: on the phone (the fillable PDF works with no signal) or printed. It has no prices on it, on purpose.

### Section 1: count what gets charged
- Trees to take down, in the height bands on the sheet.
- How many of those come down over a roof, fence, line or drive. This is the row that turns a one-cut tree into a piece-by-piece removal, and it doesn't show in a tree count.
- Trees to limb instead of remove.
- Logs to buck, and the round length.
- Dead fall, brush, branches.
- Chipper hours, slash piles to build.
- Stumps: how many, and the diameter at the ground with the root flare. One that looks 14 inches at eye level is often 22 at the soil.
- Loads to haul off. Count the pile twice and write both counts: it's the number that moves the cost most and is got wrong most.
- What stays on site instead.

### Section 2: what the job takes
- Crew people and days.
- Chains, gas, mix and bar oil.
- Chipper, trailer, stump grinder: ours or rented, and the rental cost.
- Dump fees per load: call and confirm today's figure.
- Truck trips, and whether a second trip is needed (the 811 locate is the usual reason).

### Section 3: notes
Everything else that will change the job. If it surprised you, write it down.

## Prices: the office sets them
Source: sixfold/agreements (fixed price, payment schedule), the door crib sheet, the aeration ad rules.

### The rule
You count. The office prices. The Quote builder in the app turns the counts into a price, and only admins use it.

### Never
- Never give a price at the door or on the walk. There's no flat aeration rate: every lawn is quoted from its walk.
- Never fill in a price the office hasn't agreed.
- Never leave a signed agreement with the price blank.
- Never quote from an old price sheet or from memory.

### What the customer gets
- A fixed price for the work written on the agreement. Travel isn't billed.
- For aeration, the seed, fertilizer and machine are inside the price.
- Payment in two halves: the first payment on or before the first day of work, the rest when the work is done. Call it the "first payment", never a "deposit".
- Anything extra goes on a signed change order before the work, or it isn't owed.

## Jobs we don't take, or call in first
Source: sixfold/research/crew-safety-and-compliance.md (B5, B6); the walk sheet's call-in rules; the Colorado Springs tree licence note; the door crib sheet (811 notice).

Some things stop a quote on the spot. Say "I'll have the office call you about that part" and move on.

### Not a Sixfold job
- Any tree touching, leaning toward or within 10 feet of a power line, until the utility has made it safe. We have no line-clearance qualified crew.
- Anything that needs climbing, a ladder in a tree or a lift. We work from the ground.
- Weed spraying or any pesticide. We aren't licensed to apply them.
- In Colorado Springs city limits: cutting, pruning, shaping or removing any tree 15 feet or taller. The city requires a tree service licence for that, and Sixfold doesn't hold one.

### Call the office first
- A tree over 80 feet.
- Stump grinding or digging: Colorado 811 needs at least two business days' notice before any ground disturbance, so it can't be same-day.
