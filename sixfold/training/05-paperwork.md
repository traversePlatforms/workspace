# Paperwork

Signing agreements, the three-day cancellation window, and getting paid. One `## ` heading = one lesson in the app
(heading: Paperwork). These follow the two field crib sheets in sixfold/agreements (field-instructions, remote-signing),
which govern if anything here differs. Not legal advice.

## Signing at the door
Source: sixfold/agreements/field-instructions (sheet 1). 16 CFR 429; C.R.S. 5-3-401 to 5-3-405; C.R.S. 6-1-105(1)(p).

### Why this matters
If the customer cancels inside the window, Sixfold gets nothing for work already done. And if the cancellation forms are missing, wrong or left blank, the three days never start: the customer can cancel for three years, still with nothing owed for the work.

### Before you hand the paperwork over
- Date it the day they actually sign. Not the day you walked it or wrote the quote.
- Work out the deadline: midnight of the third business day after signing. Business days are every day except Sunday and federal holidays, so Saturday counts.
- Write the deadline in three places: page 2 section 6, and both cancellation forms.
- Fill in both cancellation forms yourself: seller name, seller address, date of the transaction, deadline. The law says we fill these in, not the customer.

### As they sign
Say it out loud: "You have three business days to change your mind, and these forms are how." Pointing at the box isn't enough.

### After
- Leave them a signed copy of every page and both cancellation forms.
- Never ask anyone to give up the right to cancel. Asking is itself a violation.
- If they cancel: refund everything within ten days, no deductions, and tell the office that day.

## Starting work and the three-day window
Source: sixfold/agreements/field-instructions (sheet 2). C.R.S. 5-3-402(5), 5-3-405(3); 16 CFR 429.0(a)(3); C.R.S. 9-1.5-103(3)(b).

### You can start, but it's at our risk
Nothing stops work starting the same day. But if they cancel inside the window, we refund everything and are paid nothing for the work done. So plan the job so the window costs least.

### What to do first, and what to hold back
- Do first: work we'd be repeating anyway, like hand work and clearing close to the house.
- Hold back: anything we can't get back. Haul-off, chipping, stump grinding.
- Stump grinding can't be same-day anyway: Colorado 811 needs two business days' notice before digging.

### The emergency exception is narrow
All three must be true:
- The customer contacted us. If we knocked, it doesn't apply.
- There's a real emergency at this property: a hazard tree over a bedroom, storm damage. Not "before the party", not fire season in general.
- They write the statement themselves, by hand, dated and signed: what the emergency is, why it can't wait, that they want us to start now, and that they know they're giving up the three days. No form, no dictating.
If you're talking someone into calling it an emergency, it isn't one.

## Sending an agreement to sign
Source: sixfold/agreements/remote-signing (emailing / DocuSign); the Brian Skelton and Kara Rosenstrauch DocuSign setup notes.

Everything about signing at the door still applies. Two things get harder when you're not in the room.

### Agree the signing day first
On the phone, agree a day. The deadline depends on the day they sign, and you don't control it. If in doubt, write a later deadline, never an earlier one: a window that's too long is fine, one that's too short is the defect.

### Fill it in completely, then send
Price, scope, the date, section 6 and both cancellation forms. Send every page, never a blank template.

### Be on the phone while they sign
Say it out loud: "Before you sign: you have three business days to change your mind, and the two cancellation forms in there are how you do it." Then note on the job that you said it, and when.

### In DocuSign
- Don't put any signing boxes on the two cancellation form pages. Tagged, DocuSign would make them sign their own cancellation to finish.
- Download the signed copy and the certificate of completion into the job file. The certificate proves when they signed.
- If they signed later than the day you agreed, the deadline you wrote is too short: void it and send again.

### Don't send one at all when
- Nobody has walked the property.
- The office hasn't agreed the price.
- They want work to start immediately. That's a conversation (see Starting work).

## Getting paid
Source: sixfold/agreements (clause 10 and section 5); the customer packet rules in sixfold/quotes.

### Two halves
- First payment: half the fixed price, on or before the first day of work.
- The rest: when the work is done.

### Words matter
Call it the "first payment", never a "deposit". The agreement says no deposit is taken at signing, and an invoice that says "deposit" contradicts it.

### What the money can and can't do
The first payment holds the date. It doesn't change their right to cancel: if they cancel inside the window, it comes back in full.

### How they pay
Electronic transfer, cash, or a check made out to Sixfold, LLC.
