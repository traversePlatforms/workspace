# Sales

Sixfold's sales lessons: the conversation from hello to a booked walk, and what we add once they've said yes.
Built from Surge's door method, rebuilt for Sixfold's services and Sixfold's rules. What changed and why is in
[README.md](README.md). One `## ` heading = one lesson in the app (heading: Sales).

## The five steps
Source: Surge "5 Steps to Build Value", rebuilt for Sixfold.

Every good door conversation runs the same five steps, in order.

### 1. Introduction
First impression, lasting impression. SEE: smile, eye contact, enthusiasm. Say who you are, that you're with Sixfold and why you're there inside thirty seconds (see The first thirty seconds).

### 2. Short story
Who we are, what we're doing, why we're doing it. Keep it short and simple: two sentences, then let them talk.

### 3. Presentation
- Make it about their place. Look at their lawn, their trees or their yard with them, not at a brochure.
- Put something in their hands: your card or the flyer. Holding it gives them a feeling of ownership.
- Package it: aeration, overseed and fertilizer in one visit, with the machine, seed and fertilizer brought by us.
- Speak like a professional: "dollars", not "bucks".
- We don't give a price at the door. Every lawn and every lot is different, so someone walks it and the office gives a fixed price. That walk is what you are selling.

### 4. Close
- Ask questions they will say yes to: "You'd like the lawn thicker next spring?" "Mornings or afternoons are easier?"
- Assume the booking: "I can have someone walk it Thursday afternoon or Saturday morning. Which is better?"
- Close with confidence. Always be closing.

The close at the door is a booked walk. Tap Quote booked and book the walk while you're standing there.

### 5. Rehash
Once the walk is booked, offer the second thing: overseed with the aeration, monthly care, the trees close to the house. Never stop at the first yes. See Adding the second service.

## Objections: AIR and FFF
Source: Surge "Objections", rebuilt for Sixfold with Sixfold's real objections and true answers.

A negative comment is not a no. Two ways to answer one:

### Agree, Ignore, Resume (AIR)
"I totally understand. Anyway, what I wanted to show you is..." and go back to your point.

### Feel, Felt, Found (FFF)
"I hear you. My last neighbour felt the same way until they found out..." and give one true fact. Only use "my last neighbour" when it happened.

### What people say, and what's true to say back
- "I'm in a hurry." "I'll be fifteen seconds, for next time."
- "How much is it?" "Every lawn's different, so we don't guess at the door. We walk it and you get a fixed price, and the quote's free."
- "I do it myself." "Plenty of people do. The difference is we bring the machine, the seed and the fertilizer, and it's done in one visit."
- "I already have a guy." "Good. If he doesn't aerate, we can do just that part."
- "I need to ask my spouse." "Wouldn't want to get in the way. Let's book the walk for when you're both home, and they can ask me anything then."
- "Not now." "No problem. Spring or fall?" Then mark Not now with that come-back date.
- "We're under water restrictions." "Aeration still works on your watering days: water the day before and soon after. Which water provider are you on?" (See Water, wells and seed.)
- "I'm on a well." "Do you know what your well permit allows for the lawn?" If it's household use only, sell aeration on a green lawn, mowing or cleanup, not seed.
- "Will the seed come up? Do you guarantee it?" "We don't guarantee germination, because it depends on water and weather. We'll do the aeration, seed and feed properly and tell you exactly how to water it."
- "Can you spray the weeds while you're here?" "We don't apply weed killer or any pesticide."
- "Is this legit?" "Fair question. Here's my card. Look us up at sixfoldcolorado.com, or call the number on it."

## The closing circle
Source: Surge "Closing circle", rebuilt for Sixfold.

A no at the door is usually a "not yet". The closing circle is how you keep the conversation moving without arguing.

### The circle
- Objection: they give you a reason.
- AIR or FFF: agree, then move on or tell what others found.
- Rebuttal: one true fact that answers the reason.
- Popularity: what people around here are choosing (only if true).
- Real urgency: the season, the schedule, their watering days (only if true).
- What they'd miss: a thin lawn next spring, the gap in the schedule (only if true).
- Close: ask for the booking again.
- Rehash: once they say yes, offer the second service.

Then round again. Don't stop at the first no.

### When the circle stops
- A clear, firm no.
- They step back and start closing the door.
- They ask you to leave.
Thank them, leave a card if they'll take one, mark the door and go. Never argue.

## Building value honestly: GIFTS
Source: Surge "Gifts", rebuilt for Sixfold. The scarcity and urgency examples were changed: see README.

GIFTS is how you make what we do feel worth it. Every line has to be true.

### G: what everyone else is doing
People want what their neighbours have. "Most people on this side of town aerate in the fall." "Our most popular add-on is the overseed." Say it only when it's true. Point at the house down the street only if you really did that house.

### I: indifference
Be the opposite of a pushy salesperson. Nobody wants to be sold. "It's up to you." "You don't have to decide today." Relax, step back, let them come to you.

### F: what they'd lose
People hate losing something more than they like gaining it. Use what is real: the fall window for aeration closes when the ground freezes; this week's openings fill; seed down too late may not take this year. Never say "last day", "sold out" or "price goes up tomorrow" unless it's true. A made-up deadline or shortage can be a deceptive trade practice under Colorado law.

### T: tone
It's not just what you say, it's how you say it. The same words land differently said calm, warm and sure than said fast and loud.

### S: sense of urgency
Move with purpose. "Super quick." "Two seconds." Get the phone out and start booking while you're talking. Action beats hesitation.

## Adding the second service
Source: Surge "Steps to Rehash", rebuilt for Sixfold. The first step changed: Sixfold takes no payment at the door.

### 1. Secure the first yes
The walk is booked in the app, or the agreement is signed. Never start on the second service before the first one is secure.

### 2. Thank them and say what they did
"Thanks. You're down for Thursday, and someone will walk the lawn and give you a fixed price."

### 3. Segue
"While I've got you..." or "You don't hit home runs if you don't swing, right?"

### 4. Rebuild the reason
Give one or two true points about the second service. For example:
- Overseed: "Aerating opens the soil, and that's the best time to get seed into it."
- Monthly care: mowing, feeding and snow clearing on a monthly plan.
- Trees: "Those two spruce are right against the house. We do wildfire mitigation as well, if you ever want someone to look."
- Landscaping: beds, cleanup and planting, as its own job.

### 5. Ask
Offer two options, matched to their tone. "Do you want the seed with it, or just the aeration?" Then stop talking.

### 6. Close on a no
If they say no to the bigger option, offer one smaller step: "Want me to have them look at the trees while they're walking the lawn? No charge to look."

## Do's and don'ts
Source: Surge "Dos and Don'ts", rebuilt for Sixfold. One rule reversed: see the first don't.

### Do
- Sell with integrity.
- Think long term: offer monthly care where it fits. The standard lawn plan is month to month.
- Close more than once. Use the closing circle.
- Mark every door in the app, the same day.
- Rehash everyone who says yes.
- Get the details right on every booking: name, phone, the walk day.

### Don't
- Ever skip telling someone who signs that they can cancel within three business days. Say it out loud. The law requires it (see Signing at the door).
- Misrepresent Sixfold or what we do.
- Argue with anyone.
- Send them to the website unless they ask.
- Rehash before the first yes is secure.
- Swear.
- Be creepy, needy or pushy.

## What we never say
Source: sixfold/agreements/field-instructions (door crib sheet), the water-restrictions research, the aeration ad rules and the agreements.

Some things sound helpful and get Sixfold in trouble. Don't say them, even as a joke.

### About Sixfold
- Never say licensed, insured, bonded, certified or Firewise.
- Never say we'll beat someone's price, or quote a competitor's.

### About money
- Never give a price at the door. There is no flat aeration price: the walk sets it.
- Never promise a tax credit amount, a refund or an insurance discount.
- Never name a county cost-share programme as if Sixfold is on its approved list.
- Call the first half a "first payment", never a "deposit".

### About the work
- Never promise the seed will come up. We don't guarantee germination.
- Never promise watering. We can water the ground in on the day, with the owner's own system. After that it's theirs.
- Never offer weed killer, weed and feed or any pesticide.
- Never agree to extra work by word of mouth. Changes go on a signed change order first.

### About landscaping
Landscaping is a service on its own. Never call it the cleanup after fire mitigation. "Book one, or book them together."

## Water, wells and seed
Source: sixfold/research/water-restrictions-2026.md (researched 2026-09-30, corrected 2026-10-04).

### The one thing to know
Aeration needs a green, watered lawn. Overseeding needs much more water. If a customer can only water two or three days a week, an overseed may not take, and we don't water and don't guarantee germination.

### Ask on every lawn
"Which water provider are you on?" One question, and it changes what we should sell.

### Say
- "Aeration still works on your watering days: water the day before and soon after."
- "Water the lawn a day or two before we come out."
- On Colorado Springs Utilities: "There's a permit that lets you water more often while new seed comes in. I can tell you how to get it."

### Wells
A well is not a way around the rules. Most wells on lots under 35 acres are household use only, and can't legally water a lawn at all. Ask what the well permit allows before offering seed. If it's household use only, sell aeration on a green lawn, mowing or cleanup.

### Don't
- Promise the seed will come up.
- Quote a watering schedule without checking it that week.
- Say "you're on a well, so the restrictions don't apply."
- Aerate a lawn that's brown and dormant from water restrictions.
