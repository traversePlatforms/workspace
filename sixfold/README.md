### Animation rules for the marks
- **Sweep** rotates, and only in 60° steps — it has true sixfold rotational symmetry, so a sixth of a turn lands it back on itself.
- **Ridge** never rotates. Its peaks grow from shortest to tallest. Tilting it was explicitly rejected.
- **Ridge roots** draw downward *after* the ridge has landed (0.58–0.78s) — the mark builds up before it anchors.
- Ridge animation no longer keys off `:nth-of-type`. The tree is a group of four shapes, so delays sit on explicit classes `g1`–`g5` (growth) and `r1`–`r4` (roots).

## The strategic finding everything rests on

**Every competitor sells the work. Nobody sells the money.** Four parties will pay for fire mitigation and most homeowners know about none of them:

1. **State tax credit** — 100% of costs up to $1,000 now; **$2,000 and refundable from tax year 2027**, income limit rising to $300k AGI, extended through 2030, with "thinning woody vegetation" added as qualifying work. Roughly **450 claims in the entire state** in TY2023. The legislature's own evaluation says awareness is the binding constraint.
2. **Douglas County cost-share** — 50% up to $25,000 (plus $50k community, $7k chipping). **Paid directly to the contractor.** Awardees get 10 months to complete. Requires two bids **from the county's approved contractor list**.
3. **State FRWRM grants** — $7.04M open, deadline **8 Oct 2026**. Contractors cannot apply; fire districts, HOAs, nonprofits and utilities can. Sixfold is the contractor they hire.
4. **Insurance** — **HB25-1182 took effect 1 July 2026.** Insurers must disclose each property's wildfire risk score annually and give a discount for documented mitigation. Score notices are landing now.

That positioning also solves the new-company problem: Sixfold cannot out-credential a veteran-owned outfit with certified sawyers, but it can be the one that shows up with the paperwork.

## Grounding facts for the marketing plan

- **Demand and workability run opposite.** Dormant season (late fall–early spring) is when the work is easiest and safest. Demand peaks in spring as fire season approaches. The quiet months are for building the machine.
