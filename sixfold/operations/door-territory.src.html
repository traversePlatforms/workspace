Door territory from public records

Which doors to knock, and why that one

Internal · discussion

Every county publishes its assessor roll. El Paso County's is free, official, updated weekly, and downloads as a spreadsheet — ownership, land use, lot size and value, parcel by parcel. Nothing about this is scraped or bought. The question for this meeting is not whether we can have the data. It is what we do with it.

The two fields that do the work

Lot size

  • A direct proxy for how much lawn there is.
  • Lets us skip the doors where a machine visit cannot pay — condos, tiny lots.
  • And skip the other end: acreage that is pasture, not irrigated turf.
  • We can sort a street by job size before anyone drives to it.

Owner's mailing address

  • When it differs from the property address, nobody who decides lives there — it is a rental or an absentee owner.
  • Those doors waste a knock. The person answering cannot say yes.
  • They are not worthless, they are a different channel: post, not doorstep.

What a rep would actually carry

A walk list, in street order, with the reason for each door already on it — so the opening line is an observation rather than a pitch. Illustrative rows, not real addresses:

For the room

Does a rep want the biggest lots first, or the tightest cluster? Biggest lots mean bigger jobs and more driving. A dense block means more doors an hour and smaller tickets. We can sort it either way — pick one and we will know in a month which was right.

Door territory — what is not decided

The gaps, named rather than filled in

Internal · discussion

The same file finds fire mitigation work

Overlay the state's wildfire hazard mapping and the parcels sort themselves again: treed acreage, high hazard rating, a structure worth defending. That is the list where the funding story actually applies — and it is the one pitch we have where somebody else may pay for the work.

⚑ What is not decided, and should not be decided by me

Knocking is clean. Calling is not.

Parcel records carry addresses and owner names — no phone numbers. Adding phone numbers turns this into a cold-call list, which brings in the federal Do Not Call rules and per-call penalties. Door-to-door carries none of that. If we want a calling operation, it is a separate conversation with real compliance work in it, not an extension of this.

Solicitation permits — unchecked

Several Front Range municipalities require a solicitor's licence to knock doors commercially, and they differ between Monument, Colorado Springs and unincorporated El Paso County. Nobody has checked which apply to us. That is a phone call per jurisdiction and it should happen before the first route, not after a complaint.

Teller and Douglas are not built

Our service area spans three counties and this sheet only describes El Paso's data. The other two publish their own rolls in their own formats. One county is a fair test; three is a project.

No route has been driven

Everything above is what the data can do. None of it has met a real street yet. The honest first step is one block, one afternoon, and a count of what actually happened at those doors.

What I need from this meeting

Which suburb we test first · whether we sort by lot size or by density · who walks it · and whether anyone already knows the permit position. With those four answers the first walk list can exist this week.
