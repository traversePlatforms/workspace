**Printable version:** [quote-walk-sheet.html](quote-walk-sheet.html) → [quote-walk-sheet.pdf](quote-walk-sheet.pdf) — **one page, verified at 1.**

One sheet on a clipboard. **Section 1 is what can be counted and charged for. Section 2 is what the job burns or needs. Section 3 is a notes box for everything else.** It carries no prices.

**There are two fillable versions**, and with the paper sheet that makes **three files carrying these fields. A field added, dropped or reordered has to change in all three.**

- **The web form** — [quote-walk-form.artifact.html](quote-walk-form.artifact.html), published at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/033bdc08-544e-47e5-acf0-323e2a3ccc35. Saves each walk to a shared log the desk can open, keeps a local draft while you type, exports a walk as CSV. **Hand-written, not generated from the print source.** Needs a browser and, to save to the shared log, a connection.
- **The fillable PDF** — [Sixfold-Quote-Walk-Sheet-FILLABLE.pdf](Sixfold-Quote-Walk-Sheet-FILLABLE.pdf), added 2026-09-14, generated by [build-walk-form-pdf.mjs](build-walk-form-pdf.mjs). A real AcroForm: 52 fields, two pages. **Works with no signal and no account** — fill it on the phone, save it, send it. This is the one for a property at the end of a dirt road.

**Which to reach for:** the PDF when the crew just needs to capture and send; the web form when the desk wants the walk in the shared log without retyping. They do not sync with each other.

## What is on it

**Section 1 — the work, what gets counted and charged.** Trees to take down in four height bands; how many of those come down over a roof, fence, line or drive; trees to limb rather than remove; logs to be bucked and the round length; dead fall; brush, branches and twigs; chipper feeding hours; slash piles to build; stumps with count and diameter inches measured at the ground; loads to haul off, counted twice; what stays on site instead.

**Section 2 — what it takes.** Crew people × days; chains and sharpenings; gas, mix and bar oil; chipper, trailer and stump grinder with *ours or rented* and the rental cost; anything else hired or bought; dump fees per load, called and confirmed; truck fuel by round trips; and whether a second trip is needed, with the 811 three-day locate named as the usual reason.

**Section 3 — additional notes.** Four ruled lines and a prompt.

**Foot of the sheet.** No price is given on the property. And the stop rule: over 80ft, on a structure, under tension, or near a line.

## The lines, and why they were wrong

Matthew went to send the sheet to Brandon on 2026-09-14 and stopped: *"the lines on there arent really lined up with filling it out."* He was right, and there were three faults stacked on top of each other.

1. **Two lines per row, a few points apart.** Each answer cell held a `.rule fill` span from the shared register. That span draws its own underline `1.05em` down from the **top** of the cell — while the row's own `td` bottom border sits at the **bottom** of the row. On a single-line row those landed about three points apart, so every row had a doubled line and the pen went between them.
2. **On any row whose label wrapped, the rule floated.** The span stayed up beside the first line of the label while the row grew taller underneath it, leaving the write-on line stranded in the middle of the cell with no relationship to the row at all. **Six of the twenty-four rows wrapped**, including the three that matter most on a walk — *loads to haul off*, *stumps to grind* and *dump fees*.
3. **There was nowhere to write.** Rows were 8.6pt of text on 3.2pt of padding, printed at the tool's 0.8 scale. That is under a millimetre of clear paper under the label.

**The fix, and why it is this one.** The write-on line is now the row's own bottom border, and there is no `.rule` span left in either table. A border is at the row base *by construction*, so it cannot drift out of alignment however the label wraps or however the row grows — the class of bug is gone rather than tuned out. It spans the answer column exactly, because that cell carries `padding-right: 0`. It is darkened to `--ink-soft` while the label side stays `--tint-edge`, so one continuous rule per row still reads as *write on this half*.

Then the space to use it:

- **The label column went 3.5in → 4.45in**, and `.unit` hints went to 6.8pt and `white-space: nowrap`. **Nothing wraps now**, which both removes fault 2 and gives back roughly 150px of page.
- **That reclaimed height went into the rows** as bottom padding — the pen gap. **7pt.**

**The pen gap is at 7pt and the ceiling is 7.5.** Measured, not guessed: 7.5pt prints one page, 8pt spills to two. It sits at 7 so a label getting one word longer does not silently cost a page. Raise it only with a page count in front of you — and `node ../tools/html-to-pdf.mjs quote-walk-sheet` prints that count on every run, so there is no excuse for eyeballing it.

**The fillable form was not touched and did not need to be.** No field was added, dropped or reordered, so the two files are still in step; and the form's answers are real inputs, which never had the alignment problem in the first place.

**One thing not fixed, because it is a judgement call.** The notes box is still four ruled lines at 17pt, which prints at about 4.8mm — narrower than a school exercise book. Three lines at 22pt would be far better to write on and costs nothing in height. Left alone because how much someone writes there is Matthew's call, not mine.

## Decisions worth keeping

- **No prices, anywhere.** Until the rebuild is done, the failure mode is somebody helpfully remembering a rate.
- **"Ours, or rented?" is on every machine row.** A rented chipper is a cost that shows up on the job; an owned one is a cost that shows up as wear and does not get counted at all. Both need to be visible before a rate can be built.
- **Dump fees are call-and-confirm.** Today's figure, not last year's.
- **"Count the pile twice, both counts."** The load count moves cost most and is got wrong most.
- **Stump diameter at the ground, root flare included.** One that looks 14in at eye level is often 22 at the soil.
- **"How many come down over a roof, fence, line or drive"** is its own row. It is the single thing that turns a one-cut tree into a rigged, piece-by-piece removal, and it is not visible in a tree count.
