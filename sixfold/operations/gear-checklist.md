### Gear is not the gate

General liability and workers comp are the gate, and no purchase substitutes for them. A $400 pull rope does not make felling over a roof survivable if the roof breaks. Everything below assumes the four gate items on the minimum-viable-start sheet are closed first.

The related copy limit is unchanged and applies to this list too: **owning ANSI-rated gear is not a certification and does not license anything.** Nobody says "licensed," "insured," "bonded," "certified" or "Firewise" until each is true. Buying a helmet that meets Z89.1 does not make Sixfold "ANSI compliant" in a sentence a customer reads.

### Two standards govern the list

Sixfold's work sits on top of two documents, and it is worth knowing which one is which:

- **OSHA 29 CFR 1910.266, Logging Operations** — federal law, enforceable, and it applies to Sixfold's work directly. "Logging operations" is defined as operations associated with felling and moving trees and logs from the stump to the point of delivery, expressly including *marking danger trees, felling, limbing, bucking, chipping, loading, unloading, storing and transporting.* That is a description of a defensible space job. It binds the moment anyone other than the owner swings a saw.
- **ANSI Z133-2026, Safety and Health Requirements for Arboricultural Operations** — the industry consensus standard, not law in itself, but it is what an insurer, an expert witness and a county contractor list will measure against. **The 2026 edition took effect 27 May 2026** and supersedes the 2017 edition. ISA gives the digital PDF away at no cost through its web store. Download it — it is the single cheapest credential-adjacent thing available, and reading it is free.

Where the two differ, the list follows the stricter one and says so.

## Part 1 — Buy before job one

The smallest set that lets a crew work a residential defensible space job legally, safely and with the paperwork the funding channels want. Nothing here is optional and nothing here is expensive relative to one claim.

### 1.1 PPE — priced per person on site, not per crew

A visitor, an estimator and the owner standing in a drop zone all need this. Buy sets, not one set.

| Item | Standard | Why it is on the list |
|---|---|---|
| Helmet / hard hat | ANSI Z89.1 | Required by Z133 3.3.4 for all arboricultural operations, and by 1910.266(d)(1)(iv) wherever falling or flying objects are possible. Get **Class E** — Z133 requires Class E near electric supply lines, and residential lots have service drops |
| Eye protection | ANSI Z87.1 | Z133 3.3.6; 1910.266(d)(1)(vii)(A) |
| Face protection — mesh screen | ANSI Z87.1 | 1910.266(d)(1)(vii)(B) requires face protection where facial injury is possible, **naming chipper operation specifically.** Logger mesh screens are permitted for chain-saw work. A screen over Z87 glasses covers both; the standard says the separate eye device is not needed where face protection covers eyes and face |
| Hearing protection | — | Z133 3.3.5 requires it above an 8-hour TWA of 85 dBA. A chipper and a saw both clear that. Muffs on the helmet is the practical answer |
| Chain-saw leg protection (chaps or trousers) | ASTM F1897 **and** ASTM F1414 | Z133 3.3.8 requires both standards for ground chain-saw work. 1910.266(d)(1)(vi) separately requires cut-resistant leg protection covering **the full length of the thigh to the top of the boot on each leg** |
| Chain-saw boots | ASTM F1818 | 1910.266(d)(1)(v): foot protection that covers and supports the ankle, and **cut-resistant construction for chain-saw operators.** Waterproof or water-repellent |
| Work gloves | — | 1910.266 mandates hand protection specifically for handling wire rope; everything else is judgement. Buy them anyway |
| High-visibility vest | ANSI/ISEA 107 | Z133 3.5.3, required for anyone exposed to moving roadway traffic. Front Range cul-de-sac work triggers this more often than it looks |

**The one exception worth knowing:** 1910.266(d)(1)(vi) does *not* require leg protection for a climber where the employer can demonstrate that wearing it poses a greater hazard in that situation. This is a real exception, not a loophole — but it is the employer's burden to demonstrate, and it does not extend to anyone on the ground.

### 1.2 Saws and the cutting kit

- **Felling saw** — the big saw. Bar length matched to the largest stem actually being sold, not to the largest tree imaginable.
- **Limbing saw** — a lighter second saw. Two saws is not luxury; it is what stops a job when one is pinched or down.
- **Spare chains, at least two per bar** — a dull chain is the most common cause of a bad cut and a lost afternoon.
- **Files, file guide, depth-gauge tool, bench vise or stump vise** — field sharpening. Every chain gets touched up at the truck, not at the shop.
- **Scrench and the saw's own tool kit; spare spark plugs, spare air filter, spare bar-nut.**
- **Bar oil and pre-mixed or measured two-stroke fuel** — in approved, clearly marked containers. Z133 3.6.4 requires exactly that, and 3.6.2 requires fuelling with the engine shut down, from approved containers, **at least 10 ft from any operating equipment.**
- **Chain brake, working, on every saw.** 1910.266(e)(2)(i) requires a chain brake on any saw put into service after the standard's effective date, and prohibits removing or disabling any kickback device. Z133 6.4.3 prohibits drop-starting outright.
- **Muffler and spark arrester in place.** See §1.4 — in Colorado this is a fire-restriction requirement, not just a maintenance one.

### 1.3 Felling and ground handling

- **Felling wedges — plastic, several, at least three sizes.** Z133 9.4.10: wedges should be used where needed to stop the bar binding. Plastic only near a saw chain.
- **A dead-blow or splitting maul to drive them.**
- **Felling lever / cant hook** — for stems too small to wedge and rounds too heavy to roll by hand.
- **Pull rope with enough length to clear the tree.** Z133 9.4.5 sets the geometry: uninvolved workers stay back **at least two times the height** of the stem, and involved workers handling ropes or winches stay back **at least one-and-a-half times the height.** The rope has to be longer than that arithmetic, or someone stands where they should not.
- **Throwline and throw weights** — to set a pull line high without climbing. This is the single item that most often removes the need to climb at all.
- **Come-along or hand winch, plus slings and shackles.** Z133 9.4.9: where a piece falling wrong would damage property, a rope, block, come-along or winch **shall** be used to control the direction of fall. On a residential lot with a house, a fence and a neighbour, that is most trees.
- **Flagging tape and marking paint** — the agreement's own scope section asks how removals were identified: *flagged, painted, walked and agreed.* The gear that answers that question costs almost nothing and settles disputes.

### 1.4 Fire kit — the Colorado-specific one

This is the section most out-of-state checklists get wrong, and it is regulatory rather than optional.

Under **Stage 1 fire restrictions**, operating a chainsaw is prohibited unless you have all three of:

1. a **USDA Forest Service or SAE-approved spark arrester**, properly installed and working;
2. a **chemical fire extinguisher**; and
3. **one round-point shovel.**

Read the order that is actually in force before assuming the numbers. The BLM Colorado River Valley order read first-party for this checklist (COG02-25-01) specifies an extinguisher of **not less than 8 oz capacity by weight** and a shovel of **at least 36 inches overall length**. Secondary write-ups of Colorado Stage 1 restrictions state 35 inches. Rather than pick between them, buy past both: **a 5 lb 2A:10B:C extinguisher and a 36-inch-plus round-point shovel**, which satisfies every version found.

Z133 3.6.10 wants the same spark arrester, to SAE J335 or USDA FS 5100-1, wherever one is required. 1910.266 requires spark arresters on machine exhaust pipes, and portable fire extinguishers **on each machine and vehicle** — 1910.266(d)(4), no restriction stage required.

So the standing fire kit is:
- **Extinguisher on every machine and every vehicle** — not one shared extinguisher in the truck.
- **A round-point shovel, 36 in or longer**, that lives with the saw and does not get borrowed for the stump job.
- **Spark arresters on everything with an engine**, checked, because they clog.
- **Water — a backpack pump or at minimum a 5-gallon can.** Not required by name in the orders read; it is what actually puts out a chip-pile smoulder.
- **A fuel spill kit** — absorbent, a rag bag. Z133 3.6.3 requires spilled fuel to be cleaned up or protected from ignition, and 3.6.7 requires fuel-contaminated clothing changed.

**Also check the restriction stage before every job in fire season.** Stage 2 typically prohibits chainsaw use outright in some windows. That is a scheduling fact as much as a gear fact, and it belongs on the job briefing.

### 1.5 First aid and emergency

- **A first-aid kit meeting ANSI Z308.1**, which is what Z133 3.2.2 requires. 1910.266(d)(2) is more specific about placement: a kit **at each work site where trees are being cut, at each active landing, and on each employee transport vehicle** — so at least two, not one. Appendix A to 1910.266 sets a mandatory minimum content list (gauze pads including two at 8×10 in, roller bandage, two triangular bandages, wound-cleaning agent, scissors, tweezers, adhesive tape, adhesive bandages, latex gloves, resuscitation equipment, two elastic wraps, a splint, a blanket, and directions for requesting emergency assistance).
- **A bleed-control kit on top of that** — tourniquet, pressure dressing, gauze. A chain-saw leg wound is an arterial-bleed scenario and the Z308.1 minimum is not built for it. This is the cheapest life-saving item on the page.
- **Two people trained in first aid and CPR.** Z133 3.2.5: where two or more workers are at a work location, at least two trained in first aid/CPR shall be available. There is an exception allowing one where new hires are trained within three months of hire — so it is a hiring-plan item, not just a purchase.
- **A charged phone, and a plan for where there is no signal.** Z133 3.2.1 requires all personnel instructed in 911 and other emergency calls. Foothill lots lose signal.
- **The property address written down** — including a mile marker or gate code where there is no house number. The dispatcher will ask.

### 1.6 Site control and the neighbours

- **Cones and caution tape** — for the drop zone that Z133 3.4.11 requires to be established before anything is dropped or lowered.
- **Tarps** — lawn protection, chip control, and gathering small debris. The cheapest thing that makes a finished job look professional.
- **Plywood or track mats** for a driveway, if the chipper or grinder is going across it.
- **White marking paint** — for the 811 locate, below.
- **Colorado 811 before any stump grinding.** Colorado 811 lists tree root removal and stump grinding as excavation requiring a locate. The statute, C.R.S. 9-1.5-103(3)(b), sets the notice at **at least two business days, not counting the day of notice**; Colorado 811's own excavator page says two in one place and three in another, so **plan on three business days and pre-mark the area in white** as they require. Z133 5.6.4.4 makes the same demand of the operator.

### 1.7 The documentation kit

This is the part that makes Sixfold Sixfold, and it costs less than a chain. Every funding channel in the positioning wants evidence, and the evidence has to be captured on the day.

- **A phone or camera for same-angle before-and-after photographs**, shot from fixed positions — the marketing plan's phase 1 harvest step, and also what an insurer's documented-mitigation discount asks for.
- **The tablet or printed pack** carrying the service agreement, the two cancellation notices, and the internal field crib sheet — see [../agreements/](../agreements/).
- **The tax credit client forms** — eligibility record, work and cost breakdown, claim file checklist, photograph log — from [../tax-credit-notebook/](../tax-credit-notebook/).
- **The funding one-pager**, in quantity, because it is the campaign.
- **A measuring wheel or long tape** — defensible space is sold in distances (the first five feet, 30 feet for firewood, zone boundaries) and a written scope that names a distance needs the distance measured.
- **A clipboard that survives weather.**

## Part 2 — Buy when the job requires it

### 2.1 Climbing and working aloft

**Do not treat this as a shopping list. Treat it as a decision.**

Climbing is a different trade from ground felling, with its own standard, its own training and its own failure mode, and Sixfold's marketed service lines do not require it: defensible space, thinning and fuel breaks are ground work. Climbing arrives when a hazard tree cannot be dropped whole — over a roof, over a fence, into a drainage — and that is a real fraction of residential work.

Three options, and the honest read is that the third is right for phase 1:

1. **Buy the gear and climb.** Cheapest per job, most expensive in every other way: it needs training and it needs a second trained person on site, always.
2. **Rent or hire an aerial lift.** Removes the climbing but adds Z133 §5.6.1 in full — a whole equipment class with its own inspection and operating requirements — and a lift cannot reach many foothill back yards.
3. **Subcontract the climbing removals** to an established climber with their own insurance and their own gear, and keep the ground work, the chipping, the grinding and the paperwork. Loses margin on those jobs. Protects everything else. **Recommended until GL and workers comp are in force and someone is trained.**

If and when Sixfold climbs, Z133-2026 §8 is prescriptive and these are the numbers:

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Climbing line and work-positioning lanyard | Minimum diameter **0.44 in (11 mm)**; synthetic fibre; minimum breaking strength **5,000 lb (22.24 kN)** without terminations when new; working elongation no more than **7% at 500 lb**; identified by the manufacturer as suitable for tree climbing (Z133 8.1.5) |
| Saddle | Compliant with a governing standard and marked accordingly — **ASTM F887** if manufactured after 1 January 2018 (Z133 8.1.3) |
| Carabiners | Self-closing **and self-double-locking**, gate mechanism requiring **at least two consecutive deliberate actions** to unlock, **5,000 lb** major-axis (Z133 8.1.9) |
| Snap hooks | Self-closing and self-locking, **5,000 lb** major-axis minimum (Z133 8.1.8) |
| Prusik loops, split-tails, hitch cords | **5,000 lb** minimum breaking strength when new (Z133 8.1.6) |
| Screw links | **5,000 lb** tensile, securely tightened (Z133 8.1.11) |
| Second means of being secured | The climber shall have a climbing line **and at least one other means of being secured** on their person at all times (Z133 8.2.4) — and **two means while running a saw aloft** (Z133 6.4.5) |
| Hand saw | **Required**, not optional: a climber pruning or removing shall have a hand saw, either scabbarded or folding (Z133 8.2.5) |
| Climbing spurs | Gaffs of a type and length suitable for the tree; manufacturer's sharpening and replacement schedule followed (Z133 8.1.16). **Removals only** — spurring a tree that stays is damage |
| Throwline, throw weights, cube bag | For setting the line from the ground and inspecting the anchor before loading it |
| Top-handle saw | Only aloft, only for a trained climber, with a lanyard attachment point |
| Rigging: lowering rope, blocks, friction device, slings | Z133 9.4.7 first requires the arborist to judge whether the tree can even take the loads |
| Aerial rescue capability | Z133 3.2.4 requires anyone who may face a rescue decision to be trained for it. Annex F is the flowchart |

**The rule with teeth, before any of that gear:** Z133 3.4.7 and 8.2.2 require a second arborist, trainee, or worker trained in emergency procedures **within visual or voice communication for any arboricultural operation above 12 ft (3.65 m).** One person cannot climb. That is a staffing cost, not a gear cost, and it is the reason option 3 above is the phase-1 answer.

**Ladders** — Z133 §7.5 governs them and they are the low-altitude version of the same problem. A ladder does not make working aloft a one-person job.

### 2.2 Electrical — the item that is training, not equipment

There is a service drop over most Front Range driveways, and Z133 §4 classifies arborists into four electrical levels. **An untrained crew is Electrical Level 1 — unqualified for any voltage — and shall not perform work where electrical hazards exist, shall not approach within the Table 1 minimum approach distance, and shall not cause vegetation to violate it.** No purchase changes that classification.

The gear implications are small and the operational implication is large: **look up before the job briefing, and decline the tree that is in the wires.** 1910.266 defers to 29 CFR 1910.333(c)(3) for the same situation. Refer those to the utility.

### 2.3 Material handling, as volume grows

- **Rakes, a chip shovel, a pitchfork, a broom** — cleanup is half of what the customer sees.
- **Skidding or log tongs, a timber jack** — for moving rounds without a machine.
- **Splitting maul or a hydraulic splitter** — only if firewood becomes a line. The research file's read stands: softwood cordwood at $150–$300 delivered in the Denver metro is margin recovery on wood already on the truck, not a business. Do not buy a splitter to chase it.
- **A tarp system or chip box** for the trailer, ratchet straps, cargo net.
- **Wheelbarrow.**

### 2.4 Truck, trailer and the compliance question underneath them

- **Ratchet straps and chains rated for what is on the trailer**, and rated tie-down points.
- **Trailer lights, spare tyre, jack, wheel chocks, breakaway cable checked.**
- **Seat belts used** — 1910.266(d)(3) requires them for machine and vehicle operators, "securely and tightly fastened."
- **Extinguisher in every vehicle** — as above.

**The open question, flagged rather than answered:** a truck plus a loaded chipper often clears a **10,001 lb combined gross vehicle weight rating**, and that number is where federal motor carrier rules begin for interstate commerce, with a separate Colorado intrastate regime. Whether Sixfold's actual truck-and-trailer combination crosses it — and therefore whether a USDOT number, markings, driver qualification files or medical certificates apply — **was not researched for this checklist and should not be guessed.** Weigh the combination and ask the insurance broker at the same time as the GL quote. This costs nothing to ask and is expensive to discover at a roadside inspection.

### 2.5 Consumables and the maintenance shelf

Not glamorous, and it is the difference between a crew that works a full day and one that drives back to Monument at 11am.

- Bar oil, two-stroke oil, fuel, in labelled approved containers
- Spare chains, files, rim sprockets, bar-nuts, spark plugs, air filters, starter cord
- **Chipper knives and a spare set, plus the anvil clearance gauge** — dull knives are how chippers get fed dangerously
- **Stump grinder teeth and pockets, plus the wrench** — teeth are consumables and they go fast in Front Range decomposed granite
- Grease gun, hydraulic fluid, funnels
- Zip ties, duct tape, baling twine, contractor bags
- Replacement PPE — chaps that have taken a hit are done, not repairable

## What this list deliberately does not cover

- **Mastication.** No masticator or mulching head, by decision. The rent-versus-subcontract call is a phase 2 item in the marketing plan and is not reopened here.
- **Prescribed burning or pile burning.** A different permit regime entirely, county by county, and not a service Sixfold sells.
- **Cranes and knuckleboom loaders** (Z133 §5.6.6) and **aerial devices** (§5.6.1) — both are whole equipment classes with their own requirements, and neither is in the plan.
- **Pesticide and herbicide application** (Z133 §10) — licensed activity, not sold.
- **Prices.** Every figure in this file traces to a standard or a statute. Not one price does, and a plausible invented price is exactly the failure mode this project has already been bitten by. Price the list at a real supplier and record what it actually costs.
