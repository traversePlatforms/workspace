Crew shirt — orange body, black mark

Brandon and Brad's direction: safety-jacket orange, without obviously being a hi-vis. This checks whether the Ridge mark actually survives that, and says what to order.

⚑ The mark cannot be screen printed as it is drawn. The four mountain shapes are set at 42%, 55%, 70% and 85% opacity. Screen printing is ink or no ink — there is no 55% of a colour. A printer handed this file will either halftone it into dot screens, which turn to mud on a textured tee at chest size, or flatten every shape to solid and destroy the depth the mark is built from. A one-colour garment version has to be drawn before anything is ordered.

1Contrast — black is fine on every candidate

Measured as WCAG contrast ratios. A logo is a graphic, so 3.0:1 is the floor; everything here clears it comfortably in black.

Bone fails on every orange. A light mark on an orange shirt is out — it is the one combination that does not work, and it is the one people reach for first.

2Size — where the mark gives out

Same mark flattened to solid — which is what a printer would do — on Ember, at the sizes it would really be printed. Note what flattening costs: the four mountains merge into one silhouette and the layering the mark is built from is gone. Left chest is the risk case; the back print is large enough that nothing is in doubt.

The small tree at the base is the thing to watch. Its trunk is a 3.2-unit bar in a 94-unit mark — at 3 in that is about 0.10 in wide, and at 0.8 in it is 0.027 in, well under the ~0.03 in a screen holds reliably. The three wavy root strokes are finer still, which is why the PDF builders in this repo already drop them.

3On the shirt

Ember body, black lockup, drawn at true proportion — the tee is the same renderer the merch concept board uses. The mark never goes on a shirt alone. A homeowner watching a crew work needs to know who is on their property, so the name goes with it, and the back carries the phone so a neighbour can act on it. The left-chest print looks small because a 3.5 in lockup on a 20 in chest is small; that is the real ratio, not a stylised one.

4What to order

  • Body colour: Ember. It is already the brand orange, it is warm rather than fluorescent — which is exactly "safety vibe, not obviously hi-vis" — and it contrasts at 6.68:1 with black. Match it to a real blank against a physical swatch, not a screen.
  • Ink: one colour, pure black. Pine is the brand near-black and is indistinguishable from black on fabric while costing an ink match. Not worth it.
  • Placement: left-chest lockup at 3.5 in wide, back print at 9 in. Skip sleeve and cap prints until a simplified mark exists.
  • Smallest type on the garment: 0.09 in cap height. Below roughly that, a screen fills in the counters and the letters close up on a textured knit — the same failure as the trunk bar. At 3.5 in the word COLORADO is near that floor, so it is the first thing to check on a press proof.
  • Method: screen print. Embroidery cannot hold the root strokes or the overlapping mountains at any wearable size.

⚑ “Land & Fire” is not on these mockups, deliberately. The brand files record it as a placeholder descriptor, not a settled line — and a placeholder is a bad thing to put on thirty shirts. The lockup uses Sixfold Colorado, which is the settled public name. If Land & Fire is now final, say so and it drops under the wordmark in one line of the generator.

Before a single shirt is ordered Draw a one-colour garment version of the Ridge mark: mountains flattened to solid with separation held by outline or knockout gaps instead of opacity, root strokes dropped, trunk bar thickened to at least 0.03 in at the smallest size it will be used. That file becomes the merch master and gets reused on every future piece.
