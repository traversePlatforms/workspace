Why this sheet looks the way it does — see invoice-spec.md
  • Full scope, split payment. All seven quote lines appear, and the $750 comes from the 50% split — not from allocating two tasks to the first payment. Limbing + plant removal is exactly $750, which is the trap.
  • No qualifying split. Job 2 is landscaping only. A "non-qualifying" subtotal here would frame a standalone service as the leftover half of a fire job.
  • The zero lines stay. They are what makes the sheet read as good value; dropping them makes the invoice look dearer than the quote.
  • "First payment," never "deposit" — §5 of the signed agreement already uses "deposit" to mean $0.00.
  • Due date is not the issue date. Work does not start until the three-business-day cancellation period has run.

Why this sheet looks the way it does — see invoice-spec.md §3
  • The split is the whole point. The service agreement promises "an itemised invoice that separates qualifying wildfire mitigation work from anything else on the bill," and this invoice is what the customer claims the credit with.
  • Haul-off sits in the qualifying column when it is treating slash from qualifying work. It is usually the largest line, so putting it in the wrong column is the most expensive mistake available here.
  • The standards line is on the invoice because the packet tells the customer to keep it — and only goes on if it is true of that job.
  • No credit amount, no cap, no income limit. Separating the work is Sixfold's job; deciding whether the customer qualifies is their accountant's.
  • ⚑ Nothing upstream tags a line as qualifying. Until the walk sheet and the quote engine carry that flag, these columns are decided by hand at quoting time and written into the job file.
