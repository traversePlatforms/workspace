What it is

The field tools are Sixfold's own web app for door-to-door sales and the office work that follows. It runs on the Sixfold website, so there's nothing to install from an app store. Added to a phone's or iPad's home screen, it opens full screen with the Sixfold icon, like any other app.

Putting it on the home screen: the sign-in screen, and the tools screen until it's done, offer it. On Android, and in Chrome or Edge on a computer, Install the Sixfold app opens the browser's own install box: one tap. iPhones and iPads don't let any website install itself, so there Add Sixfold to your Home Screen shows the four taps (Safari, Share, Add to Home Screen, sign in once more). It disappears once the app is installed; Not now hides it on the tools screen for two weeks.

Light or dark: the app follows each device's own setting, so a phone in dark mode is dark and a computer in light mode is light. The tools screen has Look: Auto, Light, Dark, kept on that device. Light reads better outdoors in sun; dark is easier at night.

It does three jobs:

  • Tells the crew which doors are worth knocking and why: every owner-occupied home in Sixfold's 11 towns, scored for fire hazard, lawn size and trees close to the house, with the money each homeowner can get for fire work.
  • Records what happens at every door: quote booked, not now, no answer, no soliciting, plus phone numbers, emails, photos, notes, extra work spotted, fire checks and walk appointments. All of it saves with no signal.
  • Runs the follow-up in the office: the pipeline from first call to won or lost, quote pricing, customers and their visits, results, team numbers, territories and sign-ins.

Door rounds, screen by screen

Colorado overview

The first screen. A Colorado card shows doors visited across all towns, then El Paso, Douglas and Teller counties, each listing its towns with a progress ring, doors still to knock, high-risk doors left, doors that have all three drivers (fire, lawn, trees), and quotes booked. Tap a town to open it. Nearest town finds where you are and opens the closest town, sorted by distance. The town picker at the top switches towns anywhere and lists "Colorado · all towns" first.

A town's neighbourhoods

A town card at the top (doors visited, quotes booked), the fire-weather alert strip, and Around our jobs: jobs won in this town in the last 60 days, each with the nearest doors still to knock within half a mile and an opener that names the street, never the customer. Then the neighbourhoods, each with a progress ring, doors to knock and chips: high-risk doors, large and medium lawns, trees against the house, doors with all three drivers, a county wildfire-plan rating where one exists, a nearby Firewise community, due-back doors, no-answers to try again, and a "no soliciting" flag.

The bottom bar sorts the list:

SortPuts first
LawnThe most large and medium lawns still to knock. The default for a phone that hasn't picked a sort, because aeration leads.
Big lotsThe biggest average lot size.
NearestThe closest neighbourhoods, by distance from you.
FireThe most high and very high fire-hazard doors still to knock.
TreesThe most doors with trees against the house or heavy within 30 ft.
DoorsThe most doors still to knock: a long session in one place.
OddsThe most quotes expected, from the rate Sixfold's own doors book at, by fire hazard and lawn size. Admins see it now; it switches on for everyone after 150 conversations and becomes the default then.

Each phone remembers its own choice. A neighbourhood flagged "no soliciting" sinks to the bottom of every sort.

A neighbourhood's doors

Opens in street order: grouped by street, the street the neighbourhood is named after first, in walking order. One door is open as a full card (the next to knock); every other door is one line with its chips and distance. Tapping a line opens it. Other views: Nearest (by distance from you), Route (a walking order through every door, with the first stops opened in Google Maps), and Map (every door on a street map, coloured by fire hazard; tap a pin for its full card).

The Show doors with filter (High fire · Large or medium lawn · Trees close) narrows the list to doors with every driver picked, and says how many match. No-answer doors wait in their own section and come back on the list after two days; not-now doors come back on their date; finished doors sit under Done, with Undo.

Search

Find an address on the tools screen, or the magnifying glass in the top bar. Type the street name, with the house number if you have it (words in any order; "drive", "road", "north" match DR, RD, N), and pick the result. It opens that door's full card in its neighbourhood, from any town. A house number or a street type on its own ("100", "drive") asks for the street name. It shows up to 25 matches and needs signal.

Your own numbers

My stats: today next to recent days (doors, people talked to, quotes booked, numbers given, photos), with tips drawn from your own figures. My doors: every door you acted on, what you did and what it shows now, with a jump straight to its card and a way to change a come-back date.

The door card

Everything about one door, top to bottom.

What it tells the crew before they knock

  • Fire hazard: very high, high, moderate, low, very low, or "fire map: not rated" (town or farmland cells).
  • Lawn: large, medium, small, little or none, or mostly trees, with the photo year. Tap it to see the lawn outlined on the aerial photo (green is counted, blue is grass left out) and to correct it from what you can see.
  • Trees: against the house, heavy within 30 ft, some, a few, or none. Tap for the figures by distance band.
  • Acres, county value (Douglas and Teller) and owner of record, plus "New owner since …" when the house has sold since the last county pull.
  • Money: what this door qualifies for, in pills: the state tax credit (or "company owner, see an accountant"), the insurance law, and the county's grant situation. Tap for the full sheets, with what to say and what not to say.
  • Water: aeration needs the lawn watered the day before and soon after; what this town's water provider allows; and, in the six towns that are mostly wells (Black Forest, Franktown, Larkspur, Sedalia, Divide, Florissant), the well rule for a lot that size. None of those 13,061 doors is on 35 acres or more, so every one says a well there may not water a lawn. Tap for the water sheet: This town, Wells, Permits, Say.
  • Satellite: an aerial view with the property line drawn on.

Recording what happens

  • Quote booked, Not now (pick when to come back: a month, three months, spring, fall, a date, or no date; or flag a no-soliciting sign) and No answer. A toast offers Undo.
  • Add info opens one panel: phone (saying whether it was given at the door or looked up, since a looked-up number has to clear the Do Not Call lists), email, photos (only with the owner's OK, the property not people), note, extra work (gutters, branches over the roof, dead trees, irrigation, bare lawn, beds and others), and fire check. Saved items get a tick.
  • After Quote booked, the same panel opens at the top with Book the walk first: a day, a time or "time to agree", and the walks already booked that day so they don't collide. Then Add to calendar (reminders a day and an hour before) and Text a confirmation, sent from the crew member's phone, only to a number given at the door.

The homeowner fire check and report

A 17-item visual check of the home ignition zone from the Colorado State Forest Service guide (the house, 0–5 ft, 5–30 ft and around the property), each marked done, needs work or couldn't check. A second check after the job shows Before and Now side by side. Homeowner report turns it into a one-page report: what to do first, the full check, the door's photos, and how to use it with an insurer and for the tax credit. It states plainly that it is a visual check, not an inspection, certification or guarantee.

Customers and backups

The customer list is the part of the app that remembers people after the sale. Admins only, on the tools screen under Office.

Getting someone onto the list

  • From a won door: in the Pipeline, a door marked Won has Make customer. It carries over the owner's name, the address, the town, the won price, and a phone number or email if one was given at the door. A number recorded under a previous owner is left behind.
  • From a won enquiry: a website, Meta, Nextdoor or phone lead marked Won has the same button. It carries the name, phone, email and town, and picks aeration or fire from what they asked about.
  • By hand: + Add a customer for anyone who came another way.

Tapping Make customer twice opens the same customer rather than making a second one.

What is due

Due this week sits at the top of the list: active customers whose next visit falls in the next seven days, with overdue ones marked. Logging a done visit on a monthly plan sets the next due date a month after that visit (a year on a yearly plan), so a mow a day early still moves it on. A visit from weeks ago typed in late never pulls the date back. A visit on the 31st is next due on the last day of a shorter month. One-off and custom customers keep whatever date you set.

On the doors

A door that belongs to an active customer shows a dark Customer tag on its card, for crew as well as admins, so nobody knocks it cold or pitches a job Sixfold already does there. On the Map view the customer's dot has a dark ring, and the legend says Customer.

Today's route

When anyone is due today or overdue, or has a visit booked for today, Due this week offers Route for today: those stops in driving order (nearest first from where the phone is, when it knows), opened in Google Maps. Customers from door rounds go by their door's spot on the map; the rest by address. Google Maps takes up to ten stops.

Asking for a review

Ask for a review on a customer's page opens a text to their number: "Hi Grant, thanks for having Sixfold Colorado out. If you have a minute, a Google review helps others find us," with Sixfold's Google review link. It greets by first name (from a county owner name, the first name after the comma; a trust or a company gets "there"). Sent from the admin's own phone, the same words to every customer: asking only the happy ones is against Google's rules. The button shows once the review link is in money.json (links.googleReview).

Keeping a copy

  • Download customers (spreadsheet): every customer with plan, price, next due, visits done and spend, as a CSV that opens in Excel or Google Sheets.
  • Download a full backup: one file holding every log the app keeps: door outcomes, phones, emails, notes, photo records, extra work, fire checks, walks, quotes sent, results, territories, goals, leads, customers and visits. Each log is included exactly as it sits on the server, with the path it came from.
  • Nightly copies on the server: a job on the server saves the same backup automatically every night and keeps the last 30, listed under Keep a copy. Any of them downloads as the same file as a fresh backup, so a mistake from last Tuesday can be undone from last Monday's copy.

Rebuilding from a backup: each entry goes back to its path beside the website (sixfold-data/...) as its own file. The app reads those logs every time it opens, so putting them back puts everything back. The door lists themselves are rebuilt from the build files on the computer. The sign-in codes and photos are not in the backup: the codes stay in the private folder, and the photos are listed by name so you can see what is missing. People added in Accounts are not in it either; if the private folder were lost, add them again there.

Download a backup to a device every week, and keep it somewhere private. The nightly copies sit on the same server as the app, so they don't survive losing the server; a copy on a computer or in Drive does. When nobody has downloaded one for 7 days, admins see a reminder at the top of the tools screen. Backups hold customer names, addresses and phone numbers.

Quote builder

Prices a job from what the walk counts, by building up labour and costs rather than guessing a number. Pick the job type (Lawn, Land or Fire) and the sheet shows only the lines that job uses: mobilisation, haul-off, trees by height, limbing, slash, mowing, edging, aeration and others. Each rate says whose number it is (priced, allocated, competitor or unbacked) and why. It shows man-hours, break-even, margin, a market check and anything given away free.

  • Opened from a booked door (Build quote), it fills the job name with the address, picks the county's funding, and offers the lawn measured from the door's outline: the lawn counted near the house and all the grass, in square feet and acres. Nothing fills in until you tap Use.
  • Fire jobs show what the customer can claim: the tax credit, the county's grant and the insurance rules.
  • Aeration has no Sixfold rate yet, by decision: it shows the two market anchors and flags the line instead of pricing it.
  • It never shows a price on the property; the scope draft it exports says so.

How it saves, online and off

The app is a single web page talking to one program on Sixfold's SiteGround server. The door lists sit in a data folder beside the website, never inside it, so they can't be downloaded by address.

  • Every save is added to a log, never overwritten. Each town has its own log for each kind of record (outcomes, phones, notes, photos, walks and so on). The newest entry for a door is what shows, and nothing is lost if two people save at once.
  • No signal is fine. Anything saved without signal waits on the phone, marked "not sent yet", and sends by itself when signal returns, dated by when it was tapped. The app opens with no signal too, from the copy saved on the phone, along with neighbourhoods already opened there.
  • Each door belongs to one town, even where towns overlap, so a mark made under one town never shows the door as still open under another.
  • Photos are shrunk on the phone before sending and stored outside the website folder.
  • Search reads a small address index the server builds for itself, and rebuilds whenever the door lists change.
  • Customers and visits work the same way: every change is a new line in the customer log, and the newest value of each field is what shows. An edit to a phone number leaves the earlier number on record.
  • Backups save themselves every night on the server (the last 30 are kept), and download from the Customers screen. See Customers and backups.

Sign-in, privacy and security

  • Codes: six digits, one per person. Eight wrong tries from one place lock that place out for 15 minutes, and a flood of wrong codes from many places pauses new sign-ins for up to an hour. Phones already signed in keep working.
  • Staying signed in: a sign-in lasts 45 days, but every tap re-checks the person against the current list. Removing someone, giving them a new code, demoting them or signing them out takes effect on their next tap.
  • Where codes live: the server's codes file and the app's accounts file, both in a private folder beside the website, never inside it. The ordinary accounts list never carries codes; Show PINs needs your own code again and is logged.
  • What each role sees: the full export (every door with names and addresses), the customer list, the customer spreadsheet and the full backup are admins only. Crew see the doors they knock, their own numbers, and the Customer tag on a door.
  • Backups: each download is logged with who took it. The file never contains a sign-in code.
  • Phone numbers: each number records whether it was given at the door or looked up. Only door-given numbers get Call and Text buttons without a warning; looked-up numbers must clear the national and Colorado Do Not Call lists first.
  • Photos: only with the owner's OK, of the property, not people.
  • Search: up to 25 addresses a time, a street name required, and 60 searches per person in ten minutes, so a stolen phone can't pull the whole list. Results carry no owner names.
  • Search engines: the app's pages are marked not to be indexed.

Limits and known gaps

  • The data is a snapshot. Aerial photos are from 2023, the lidar from 2018 (El Paso) and 2020 (Douglas), the fire map from 2023. Owners and sales are as of the last county pull. Updating means re-running the build on a computer and uploading.
  • Water rules move without warning. They were checked on 30 Sep 2026; check the provider's page that week before telling a homeowner a schedule. Donala, Triview, Palmer Lake and Cripple Creek weren't checked, and only Colorado Springs and Castle Rock are known to have a permit for new seed.
  • The fire rating is for the area, in cells about 270 m across. The satellite view and the fire check are the per-house look.
  • Lawn sizes are estimates from a photo. Shade, garden beds and dead grass can read wrong; the crew can correct any door. Six Black Forest lawns couldn't be outlined.
  • The owner of record may not be who answers. Trusts, rentals and recent sales all happen.
  • County values are only for Douglas and Teller; wildfire-plan ratings only for Teller.
  • Search and the office tools need signal, except the customer list, which opens from the copy on the phone once it has been opened with signal. Knocking, marking and recording don't need signal either. Saving a customer or a visit does.
  • Printing the homeowner report from the home-screen app on an iPad hasn't been tested yet. It works in a browser tab.
  • Meta ad leads still arrive in Meta's own Leads Center first; someone has to download them and import them (Pipeline, Import leads from Meta). Nothing pulls them in by itself.
  • Customers are not billing. There are no invoices, payments or contracts in the app. Prices and visits are a record of the work; the signed agreement stays in DocuSign or Drive and is linked.
  • Getting a backup off the server is by hand. The nightly copies stay on SiteGround; the reminder asks an admin to download one weekly. Putting one back is a File Manager upload, not a button.
  • Removing a visit hides it; the log keeps the original line. It does not move the next due date back, so fix that with Edit. There is no delete for a whole customer: set them to Ended.
  • Downloading the backup from the home-screen app on an iPad or iPhone hasn't been tested. iOS may open the file instead of saving it; a browser tab, or a computer, is the sure way.
  • Add a person needs the server to be able to write to its private folder. It hasn't been tried on the live site yet.
  • Overgrowth year over year waits for late-spring photos, in 2027.

Updating the app

Changes are built and tested on a computer first, against a full copy of the app (57 automatic test suites, over 1,000 checks, run before every upload), then uploaded through SiteGround's File Manager.

  • The app: summit-de59ae83/index.html into public_html/summit-de59ae83/. That folder skips SiteGround's cache, so a new version shows up straight away.
  • The server program: api/rounds.php into public_html/api/.
  • Quote builder: summit-de59ae83/quote.html, generated from its source file. The app links to it with a version stamp, so phones never keep an old copy.
  • Door data, lawn outlines: into sixfold-data/rounds/, beside the website, never inside it. Money sheets and water rules (one file, money.json) go in sixfold-data/rounds/docs/; correcting a rule is an upload of that file alone.
  • The morning email: tools/morning-email.php into sixfold-tools/, run by a second cron job every hour: php /home/customer/www/sixfoldcolorado.com/sixfold-tools/morning-email.php. It sends once a day, at the first run after 6 am Colorado time, whatever clock the server keeps, through the same Gmail login as the website form.
  • The nightly backup job: tools/nightly-backup.php into sixfold-tools/, beside the website, never inside it. A SiteGround cron job (Site Tools → Devs → Cron Jobs) runs it once a day: php /home/customer/www/sixfoldcolorado.com/sixfold-tools/nightly-backup.php. It refuses to run from a browser.

The exact files and sizes for each upload are kept in DEPLOY-ROUNDS.md, and every upload is checked on the live site afterwards.
