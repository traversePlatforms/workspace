Gear checklist · fire mitigation

The kit · what the marketed work actually requires

Everything that has to be in the truck, and what it is there for

The equipment needed to deliver the six service lines on the signed agreement — felling, stump grinding, slash and chipping, rounds, defensible space, fuel break and thinning — plus the work that turns up whether or not it was sold. Climbing is the big one of those, and it has its own sheet.

Gear is not the gate

General liability and workers comp are the gate, and nothing on this list substitutes for them. A pull rope does not make felling over a roof survivable if the roof breaks. And owning ANSI-rated equipment is not a certification. A helmet that meets Z89.1 does not put the words licensed, insured, bonded, certified or Firewise into a sentence a customer reads. None of them are true yet.

What is already owned

Saws for felling and limbing, a stump grinder, a chipper, haul capacity. Every machine on the sellable service list is in hand. What is missing is not machines — it is the safety, fire, medical and documentation kit that has to ride with them, and that is most of what follows. The one machine gap is unchanged and not reopened here: no masticator or mulching head, which is a quoting decision.

The two standards this list is built on

DocumentWhat it is, and why it binds Sixfold
OSHA 29 CFR 1910.266
Logging Operations		 Federal law — enforceable, with penalties. It defines logging operations as felling and moving trees from the stump to the point of delivery, expressly including marking danger trees, felling, limbing, bucking, chipping and transporting. That is a description of a defensible space job. It binds the moment anyone but the owner runs a saw.
ANSI Z133-2026
Arboricultural Operations		 The industry consensus standard — not law in itself, but what an insurer, an expert witness and a county contractor list measure against. The 2026 edition took effect 27 May 2026 and supersedes 2017. ISA gives the digital PDF away at no cost — download it.

Where the two differ, this list follows the stricter one and says which.

How the list is organised

  • Sheets 2–5 — buy before job one. PPE, the cutting kit, the Colorado fire kit, first aid, site control, documentation. Nothing optional, nothing expensive relative to one claim.
  • Sheet 6 — climbing, which is a decision before it is a purchase. Plus the electrical classification, which is training and cannot be bought at all.
  • Sheet 7 — buy when the job requires it. Material handling, the trailer, consumables, and what this list leaves out.

No prices anywhere on these sheets. Every figure here traces to a standard or a statute; not one price would, and a plausible invented number is exactly the failure this project has already been bitten by. Price the list at a real supplier and record what it actually cost.

PPE, counted per person on site — not per crew

The one exception worth knowing

1910.266(d)(1)(vi) does not require leg protection for a climber where the employer can demonstrate that wearing it poses a greater hazard in that particular situation. It is a real exception, not a loophole: the burden of demonstrating it sits with the employer, and it does not extend to anyone standing on the ground.

Working chain brake on every saw1910.266(e)(2)(i) requires one and forbids disabling any kickback device. Z133 6.4.3 prohibits drop-starting outright

Two saw rules that are equipment, not habit

The muffler stays on and the spark arrester stays in — see sheet 4, where in Colorado that stops being maintenance and becomes a condition of running the saw at all. And the chain brake is engaged or the engine shut off any time the saw is not actively cutting — before starting, setting it down, or moving both feet on the ground (Z133 6.4.6).

Felling and directional control

Plastic felling wedges, several, three sizesZ133 9.4.10 — wedges stop the bar binding. Plastic only, near a chain
Dead-blow hammer or splitting maul to drive them
Felling lever / cant hookFor stems too small to wedge and rounds too heavy to roll
Pull rope, long enough to clear the arithmetic below
Throwline, throw weights, cube bagThe item that most often removes the need to climb at all
Come-along or hand winch, slings, shackles, blockZ133 9.4.9: where a piece falling wrong would damage property, a rope, block, come-along or winch shall be used. On a lot with a house, a fence and a neighbour, that is most trees
Flagging tape, marking paint, axe or hatchetThe agreement asks how removals were identified — flagged, painted, walked and agreed. This is the gear that answers it

The rope has to be longer than the standoff, or someone stands where they should not

Z133 9.4.5 sets the geometry of a manual felling operation. Workers not involved stay back at least two times the height of the stem. Workers who are involved — ropes, work lines, come-alongs, winches — stay back at least one-and-a-half times the height. Both hold until the saw operator says it is safe to approach. Buy rope against that number, not against the tree.

Also required before the cut, and free

  • A planned retreat path, cleared — 9.4.6. For the saw operator it runs roughly 45° either side of a line opposite the intended fall.
  • A drop zone, established before anything is dropped or lowered, with people excluded from it — 3.4.11.
  • A job briefing before each day or shift — hazards, procedures, assignments, PPE — 3.4.3. It should be documented, and that document is the cheapest evidence of competence Sixfold can generate.
  • A tree inspection including the root collar, before anyone works on it — 3.4.5.

Fire kit and medical

Buy before job one · 3 of 4

The fire kit is regulatory in Colorado, and the medical kit is not the one in the catalogue

Fire — the sheet most out-of-state checklists get wrong

Under Stage 1 fire restrictions, running a chainsaw is prohibited unless all three of these are present: a USDA Forest Service or SAE-approved spark arrester properly installed and working, a chemical fire extinguisher, and one round-point shovel. Not recommended — a condition of being allowed to cut.

Buy past the disagreement

The order read first-party for this sheet — BLM Fire Prevention Order COG02-25-01 — specifies an extinguisher of not less than 8 oz by weight and a shovel of at least 36 inches overall. Secondary summaries of Colorado Stage 1 restrictions say 35 inches. Rather than settle it, buy past both: a 5 lb 2A:10B:C extinguisher and a 36-inch-plus round-point shovel. Orders are issued jurisdiction by jurisdiction and their wording varies — read the one in force for the county you are working.

Extinguisher on every machine and every vehicle1910.266(d)(4), and it applies with no fire restriction in force at all. Not one shared extinguisher in the truck
Round-point shovel, 36 in or longerLives with the saw. Does not get borrowed for the stump job
Spark arresters on everything with an engine, checkedZ133 3.6.10: SAE J335 or USDA FS 5100-1. They clog
Backpack water pump, or at minimum a 5-gallon canNot named in the orders read. It is what actually kills a chip-pile smoulder
Fuel spill kit — absorbent, rag bagZ133 3.6.3 requires spills cleaned up or protected from ignition; 3.6.7 requires fuel-contaminated clothing changed
Check the restriction stage before every job in fire seasonStage 2 can prohibit chainsaw use outright in some windows. A scheduling fact as much as a gear one — put it on the job briefing

Charged phone, and a plan for no signalZ133 3.2.1 requires everyone instructed in 911 and other emergency calls. Foothill lots lose signal

Water, more than anyone thinksFront Range summer, altitude, PPE

Check the kit against Appendix A to 1910.266, which is mandatory, rather than against whatever a supplier calls a logging kit: gauze pads including two at 8×10 in, a roller bandage, two triangular bandages, a wound-cleaning agent, scissors, tweezers, adhesive tape, adhesive bandages, latex gloves, resuscitation equipment, two elastic wraps, a splint, a blanket, and written directions for requesting emergency assistance.

One item on this sheet cannot be bought

Two people trained in first aid and CPR shall be available wherever two or more workers are at a work location — Z133 3.2.5. There is an exception allowing one where every new hire is trained within three months of their hire date, which is the practical route for a company this size. Either way it is a hiring-plan item, and it wants scheduling now rather than the week before spring.

Call 811 before any stump grinding — it is excavation

Colorado 811 lists tree root removal and stump grinding as excavation requiring a locate. The statute, C.R.S. 9-1.5-103(3)(b), sets notice at at least two business days, not counting the day of notice — but Colorado 811's own excavator page says two in one place and three in another. Plan on three business days, and pre-mark the area in white paint, flags or stakes as they require. Z133 5.6.4.4 puts the same duty on the grinder operator, and the practical consequence is scheduling: a stump job cannot be sold as same-day.

Climbing, aloft, electrical

Working aloft · not a shopping list

Climbing is a decision before it is a purchase

Nothing Sixfold markets requires climbing — defensible space, thinning and fuel breaks are ground work. It arrives when a hazard tree cannot be dropped whole, over a roof or a fence, and that is a real fraction of residential jobs.

OptionWhat it costs
Buy the gear and climbCheapest per job, most expensive every other way. Needs training, and a second trained person on site every time.
Rent or hire an aerial liftRemoves the climbing, adds Z133 §5.6.1 entire — and a lift cannot reach many foothill back yards.
Subcontract the climbing removalsKeep the ground work, the chipping, the grinding and the paperwork; give away the margin on the climb. The phase 1 answer, until GL and comp are in force and someone is trained.

The rule with teeth, before any of the gear below

Z133 3.4.7 and 8.2.2: a second arborist, trainee, or worker trained in emergency procedures shall be within visual or voice communication during any arboricultural operation above 12 ft (3.65 m). One person cannot climb. That is a staffing cost, not an equipment cost, and most of the reason the recommended option is the third. Ladders (§7.5) do not escape it. And 3.2.4 requires anyone who may face a rescue decision to be trained for it — Annex F is the flowchart.

Electrical — the item that is training, and cannot be bought

There is a service drop over most Front Range driveways. Z133 §4 sorts arborists into four electrical levels, and an untrained crew is Electrical Level 1 — unqualified for any voltage. A Level 1 arborist shall not work where electrical hazards exist, shall not approach within the Table 1 minimum approach distance, and shall not cause vegetation to violate it either. No purchase changes that. The gear implication is nil; the operational one is large: look up during the job briefing, and decline the tree that is in the wires. Declining it costs one job; taking it costs the company.

Chipper and grinder — three things to measure on the machines Sixfold already owns

Z133 5.6.2 sets an infeed geometry: a hopper not less than 85 in from the blades — or the feed-roller pinch point — to ground over the hopper centre line; a flexible anti-kickback device on hand-fed rotary chippers; and on mechanically fed machines a quick-stop and reversing bar reachable across the top and both sides. Nobody reaches past the plane of the hopper while anything turns, and panels stay closed until everything has stopped and the key is pocketed.

For the grinder — 5.6.4: guards in place whenever the wheel turns, everyone clear, the operator at the controls until it stops, and eye and hearing protection for everyone at the site.

Flagged, not answered — ask the broker at the same time as the GL quote

A truck plus a loaded chipper often clears 10,001 lb combined gross vehicle weight rating, where federal motor carrier rules begin, with a separate Colorado intrastate regime alongside. Whether Sixfold's combination crosses it — and so whether a USDOT number, markings, driver qualification files or medical certificates apply — was not researched for this checklist and should not be guessed. Weigh it. Free to ask, expensive to discover at a roadside inspection.

What these sheets deliberately leave out

  • Mastication. No masticator or mulching head, by decision. Rent-versus-subcontract is a phase 2 call.
  • Prescribed and pile burning — a different permit regime, county by county, and not a service Sixfold sells.
  • Cranes, knucklebooms, aerial devices, pesticide application — Z133 5.6.6, 5.6.1 and §10. Whole classes of equipment and licensing, none of it in the plan. And prices, per sheet 1.
