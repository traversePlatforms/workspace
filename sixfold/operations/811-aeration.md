# Colorado 811 and aeration: the question to ask, and how to file a locate

**Draft, 2026-10-06. Nothing sent, nothing filed.** For Brandon (or Matthew) to send or use. Background: C.R.S. 9-1.5-102
excludes "routine maintenance on existing planted landscapes" from excavation: an activity done at least once a year that
disturbs the ground no more than 4 inches by mechanical means, and the law names aerators and overseeders. Verified current
on 2026-10-06 (no 2026 bill changed it). The open question is a lawn being aerated for the first time.

## 1. The email to the commission

**To:** cdle_safetycommission@state.co.us (Underground Damage Prevention Safety Commission, Colorado Department of Labor
and Employment, Division of Oil and Public Safety; phone 303-318-8525)
**Subject:** Does first-time core aeration count as routine maintenance under C.R.S. 9-1.5-102?

> Hello,
>
> We're Sixfold, a lawn care and landscaping company in El Paso County. We'd like to confirm when core aeration needs an
> 811 locate.
>
> Our understanding is that C.R.S. 9-1.5-102 excludes "routine maintenance on existing planted landscapes" from
> excavation, where routine maintenance is a regular activity that happens at least once per year, disturbs the earth no
> deeper than four inches by mechanical means, and the listed equipment includes aerators and overseeders.
>
> Our work is core aeration (and overseeding) of existing lawns, with cores under four inches deep. Could you confirm:
>
> 1. Does core aeration of an existing lawn count as routine maintenance when it is the first time that lawn has been
>    aerated, or does the "at least once per year" wording mean a locate is needed for a first-time job?
> 2. Does aerating or overseeding the same lawn more than once a year (spring and fall, or on a monthly plan) still count
>    as routine maintenance?
> 3. Is the four inches measured as the depth the tines penetrate?
>
> Thank you,
> [Name], Sixfold, LLC
> (303) 915-1045 · Contact@SixfoldColorado.com

Before sending: measure a few cores, or check the aerator's tine length, so the "under four inches" line is true.

## 2. Filing a locate (when one is needed or wanted)

- **Phone:** dial 811 or 1-800-922-1987.
- **Online:** Colorado 811's ticketing system (Exactix, launched 2026-01-30): https://www.colorado811.org/professional-excavators/excavator-requests.
  It needs a user account. Single-address requests work after sign-up; the fuller tools need training and a short
  competency exam. Help: 877-318-8188, onlineservices@co811.org.
- **Have ready:** the caller's name and phone; the excavator's name and phone (Sixfold); the property address; the start
  date; what the work is ("core aeration, cores under 4 inches" or "stump grinding").
- **Timing:** at least two business days before work, not counting the day of the request.
- **What it doesn't cover:** 811 marks public utility lines only. Sprinkler lines, invisible pet fence, septic and other
  private lines are the owner's to point out at the walk.
