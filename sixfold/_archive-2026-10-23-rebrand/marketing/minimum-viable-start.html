01

The concept in six lines

If someone asks what the business is on day one, this is the whole answer. No aeration, no second service line, no acreage contracts.

What
Defensible space by hand. Fell, limb, grind stumps, pile slash, chip, cut rounds, haul off or leave on site. Zone 1 and Zone 2 work close to the house — exactly where machines struggle and homeowners are nervous.
Who
Homeowners, direct. El Paso County first, southern Douglas County second. Both are a short drive from Monument. Mobilisation distance is the cost that kills a cheap launch job.
The hook
Four parties will pay for this work and most homeowners know about none of them. Every competitor sells the cutting. Sixfold turns up knowing where the money is.
The pitch
“Can I walk the property and tell you what a programme would want cut?” A free walk is a low-friction yes, and it produces the lead, the quote and the paperwork in one visit.
The price
Quoted per property against a $3,200–$6,100 benchmark for a one-acre defensible space job. CSFS figure via the 2026 state legislative evaluation. Read section 06 before quoting anything.
Getting paid
Deposit on scheduling, balance on completion, invoice from the LLC bank account. The account exists. That is the only piece of financial infrastructure this needs to start.
02

The gate: four things that must be true before a saw runs

These are not marketing tasks and they are not optional. Everything else on this sheet can be improvised; these cannot.

  • General liability in force. Indicative Colorado range $900–$2,600/yr, $1M/$2M typical minimum. Broker-site figures — get real quotes. Cutting trees over someone's roof without this is the single largest exposure in the business.
  • Workers comp, the moment anyone else swings a saw. Colorado has a strict one-employee threshold. Indicative $7–$13 per $100 of payroll. Solo, it is a different question; with one helper it stops being one.
  • The Surge relationship structured on paper. Sixfold and Surge are separate companies sharing one address. Using Surge people for Sixfold revenue is a related-party arrangement with tax and liability consequences. Settle it before the first door is knocked, not after.
  • A number a customer can call and a name on the invoice. Phone (303) 915-1045 is live. Email currently routes to a Surge address — acceptable for the first jobs, replace before anything is printed in volume.

The honest read: insurance is the only thing on this entire sheet that costs real money before revenue, and it blocks almost everything downstream. If one call gets made this week, it is the broker.

The kit

Nothing needs to be bought to start. One gap needs to be known and priced around.

Already have

  • Saws — felling and limbing
  • Stump grinder
  • Chipper
  • Haul capacity
  • An LLC and a bank account
  • Canvassing know-how nobody else in this trade has

The one real gap

  • No masticator / mulching head

That rules out mastication at scale and loses on price against mulching competitors on open acreage. It does not touch Zone 1 and Zone 2 work near the house, which is what is being sold. Price the gap in; do not pretend it isn't there.

Skip for now

  • Truck wrap
  • Uniforms
  • Rented mastication
  • Any new machine

Revisit mastication as a rental or subcontract decision before spring, not before job one.

05

One channel, one asset

No website, no ads, no social presence is required to sell the first ten jobs. Doors and a piece of paper are.

Walk the streets that back onto open space

  • Since 1 July 2026 insurers must send every Colorado policyholder an annual wildfire risk score and the mitigation discounts available. Those notices are landing now.
  • A homeowner holding one has been told they have a problem and given nobody to call.
  • Open on the change, not the sale. Hand over the one-pager. Ask for the walk, not the contract.
  • Log every door — address, outcome, what you saw in the yard. That log is next spring's target list.

Check the solicitation rules per municipality first. Getting run off a neighbourhood in month one costs more than the permit.

Stand near people already carrying slash

  • Jefferson County SLASH sites run weekends through 25 Oct 2026, $24 per 6 cubic-yard load.
  • Everyone in that queue has self-identified as having more slash than they can handle and is paying to solve it.
  • Confirm site rules on contractor presence before turning up.

Free, seasonal, and the highest-density prospecting available before winter.

Then, from job one: a Google Business Profile and a review asked for on site, phone in hand, at completion. Free, and the only credential a brand-new company can build quickly. Asking by email a month later gets a third of them.

07

What is deliberately not being done yet

All of this is in the full plan. None of it is required to complete a paid job, and each one is a week that does not get spent on doors.

  • A website. Phase 2. The one-pager does the work a site would do, in the hand, at the door.
  • Paid search and paid social. Expensive against established operators, and neither can target a street. Doors can.
  • Choosing the logo and the offer version. Brandon's calls, and neither blocks a job. Print short runs until they settle.
  • The Douglas County approved-contractor list. Worth applying to early, but it gates a referral channel, not the ability to work. And nothing about the county cost-share may be advertised until Sixfold is actually listed.
  • Certifications and accreditation. Sixfold cannot out-credential a veteran-owned outfit with certified sawyers this year. It can be the one that shows up with the paperwork.
  • HOA and fire-district grant work. Real money, slow relationships. Worth a conversation before the 8 Oct FRWRM deadline, but it will not pay for anything this autumn.
  • Aeration, in any form. The entity exists for it and the mark is built for it. Until which kind is established, it is not described anywhere.
  • A second service line, an office, a hire, a wrap. All of it after ten completed jobs, not before.

Two exceptions worth doing while waiting on insurance quotes — both cheap now and expensive later: register a domain — done, SixfoldColorado.com, 2026-08-21 — and book a twenty-minute trademark call on classes 37 and 44 with Sixfold AI, Inc. as the conflict to raise. Neither blocks a job; both get costlier the day a truck is wrapped.

08

Day one to first paid job

Ordered by what blocks what, not by what is most interesting.

  1. Call an insurance broker. General liability, and workers comp priced for one and for two people. Everything downstream waits on this.
  2. Call the Douglas County Building Division. What the approved contractor list requires, when the next cost-share window opens, how long listing takes. The county site blocks automated fetch, so this cannot be desk-researched. Twenty minutes, highest leverage available.
  3. Settle the Surge labour question. Arm's-length contract, or reps engaged by Sixfold directly. Before the first door.
  4. Write the quote sheet and the scope sheet. One page each. Half a day, once.
  5. Print the one-pager. Short run. The copy changes as programme details get confirmed.
  6. Walk one street. Foothill edge, backs onto open space. Log every door. The purpose of the first street is to find out what the script gets wrong.
  7. Book the first walk-through, quote it, do it. Photograph before and after from the same fixed positions — from the property line and from the house.
  8. Ask for the review on site, at completion, phone in hand. Then hand over the written scope and the tax-credit paperwork.
  9. Repeat nine more times. Ten jobs, ten photo sets, eight to ten reviews. That is the whole of this year's goal.
09

Lines that may not be said

Not preferences. These are the ways a brand-new company ends its own credibility.

Licensed and insuredNot on a flyer, a card, a truck or a website until general liability and workers comp are actually in force.
We're on the county's approved listNot until Sixfold is. Pointing a homeowner at money they can only claim with two bids from a list you're not on produces a dead lead and a bad first impression.
Certified / accredited / FirewiseNo standing that has not been verified as held. Competitors lead with this language; borrowing it is unrecoverable.
You'll get $2,000 backThat is tax advice from a tree company. The fact, with its source, is: the state credit covers 100% of costs up to $1,000, and rises to $2,000 and becomes refundable for tax year 2027. Then point at their accountant.
Your insurer is going to drop youNo urgency theatre and no fear selling. The homeowner already has the fear — the notice in their mail supplied it. The job is to be the calm one with the answer.
