Both use the Ridge mark, since it is the stronger performer on anything a homeowner holds. Swap to Sweep by changing one symbol if that is the direction Brandon picks.

Why the picture is what it is

A flyer gets about a second on a doorstep before it is read or binned, so the scene has to say “wildfire” and “this is what we do to your property” before a single word is read. It shows a house with room around it, the forest held back, and the glow already on the horizon — the job, the reason and the stakes in one image.

It is drawn in the same geometry as the logo. Conifers are stacked triangles, the ridgeline is triangles, the mark is triangles, and the service bullets are triangles — so the imagery reads as part of the brand rather than stock art dropped on top of it. The dashed arc is the defensible space; anyone who has had a county mailer will recognise it, and anyone who has not still reads it as “a safe distance.”

Side by side

What actually differs

Both get you cheap jobs, photos and reviews. The difference is who controls the number and whether there is a reason to call today.

A — Half your quoteB — First ten, 40% off
Who sets the priceThe competitor who quoted themYou
Your floorOpen — a lowball quote halved can land under costKnown before you print it
Reason to call nowNone. The offer is always thereTen slots and a date
Who callsPeople already shopping — they have a quote in handAnyone, including people who had not started looking
ReviewsAsked for in person, at completionSame — deliberately kept off the paper and moved to the walkthrough
What it says about youCheapest on the streetNew and hungry, which is true and forgivable
Raising prices laterHard — you trained the market on halfEasy — the discount had a stated reason and an end
Real riskSomeone brings a $9,000/acre resort-county quote and you owe them a $4,500/acre job on a Front Range lotTen discounted jobs is a known, budgeted cost

If you want A’s hook without A’s exposure, the middle path is to keep the headline and add a floor: “Bring us your quote — we’ll beat it by 40%, down to our minimum.” Same magnetism, and the minimum is yours to set.

Before either one prints

Fix list

Neither flyer says “licensed and insured.” That line is on almost every contractor flyer in Colorado and it is the first thing a homeowner looks for — but you do not have general liability or workers comp yet, and putting it on paper before you do is the kind of claim that follows a company around. Get the insurance, then add the line. It will do more for conversion than either discount.

  • Do not add the Douglas County cost-share to the flyer yet. It pays 50% up to $25,000, but it requires two bids from the county’s approved contractor list and Sixfold is not on it. Advertising it before you qualify sets up a promise you cannot keep.
  • Phone number — done 18 Aug 2026. (303) 915-1045, live on both flyers and in both social captions.
  • The email is now a Sixfold address. Contact@SixfoldColorado.com is in the fine print on both, replacing the Surge one on 2026-09-18. A homeowner who looks it up lands on Sixfold. The domain still does not resolve, so no web address is printed anywhere — add one to the fine print the day hosting is live.
  • Domain — resolved. SixfoldColorado.com is owned (2026-08-21) and is now printed on both flyers, on Matthew's instruction that the site will be live before anything goes out. That inverts the dependency: do not print at volume until the domain resolves, because a flyer outlives a dead link. sixfoldland.com was never registered and is superseded.
  • Date on Version B. Still [DATE] in the fine print. The deadline is what makes it work; without it, it is just a discount.
  • Decide the minimum job size before either offer goes out. On a small lot the discount can leave the job below the cost of getting the truck there.
  • Get a photo release in the paperwork. Neither flyer trades on before-and-afters any more — that ask moved to the walkthrough, where it belongs. You still need the release, because every job produces photos for the portfolio and for insurance documentation. Get written permission at signing, not after.
  • The tax credit line is accurate as written and worth keeping on both. 100% of costs up to $1,000 for the current tax year, income limits apply, roughly 450 claims statewide. It rises to $2,000 and becomes refundable in 2027. It is the single most useful thing on the page and no competitor is saying it.
