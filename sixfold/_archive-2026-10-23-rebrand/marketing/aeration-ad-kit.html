The assets for a fall aeration push: a responsive search ad written to Google's limits, the negative keywords that stop it paying for septic tanks, and a thirty-second film built around the one shot nobody else uses. Every line traces to a source in §08.

01

Why the existing ad kit does not carry over

There is no money story for a lawn.

The Ad Script Kit runs on one idea: every competitor sells the work, nobody sells the money. Four parties will pay for fire mitigation and almost no homeowner knows it, so the ad is about the money rather than the chainsaw.

Aeration has none of that. No tax credit, no insurer discount, no county cost-share, no grant. Reskinning that kit would produce the same ad with its engine taken out.

What transfers is the shape of the idea, not its content. Be the one that turns up knowing how the work actually goes. For aeration that is a smaller and truer gap: everyone sells a pass over the lawn, and nobody explains what it does, when it works, or why the plugs are left lying there.

Water it two days before

Dry ground will not give up a deep plug, and a shallow pass does very little. Useful to the reader whether or not they ever call.

The plugs are meant to stay

They look untidy for a fortnight and then break down back into the turf. That is where the benefit is. Sweeping them off is a golf-green practice.

Do not claim to be the honest one. Carry the information instead. “Unlike other companies, we tell you the truth” is a claim, and it is the kind a reader goes and tests.
02

Two gates before any of this spends money

1 · The search ads need the repositioned site live

The local build leads with lawn aeration. sixfoldcolorado.com still leads with fire mitigation — the 21 September build. Point search ads at it as it stands and someone clicking “lawn aeration” lands on a page headlined fire mitigation, and Google scores landing-page relevance against the query, so the mismatch costs money on every click before anything else goes wrong.

Re-bundle and upload before the first click, not after. The form problem compounds it: a submission is logged but no email arrives, so paid clicks would produce leads sitting in submissions.log until someone remembers to open it.

2 · There is still no aerator and no aeration rate

Ads generate calls. A call asks what it costs and when you can come. There is no machine in the gear record and no aeration line in the quote engine, so both answers get improvised at the moment a stranger is deciding whether to trust a new company.

Pushing this fall is a fair call for a small test. Worth having a price you are willing to be wrong about before the first ad runs, rather than during it.

06

The filmed ad, for Brad

Every aeration ad on the internet opens on the machine. Open on a plug in a hand.

A soil core is a genuinely odd-looking object — a two-to-three-inch cylinder of earth with grass still attached to the top. Most people have never seen one close up and have no idea it is what the service produces. Hold it to camera before anything else happens and the viewer has already learned something, which is the whole strategy in one frame.

The machine is in the ad. It is not the first thing in the ad — the same rule the fire kit set about the chainsaw.

Thirty seconds

BeatOn screenWords
0–4sClose on a hand holding one plug, turf side up“This came out of a lawn about ten seconds ago.”
4–10sWide: the machine working, plugs dropping behind it“It’s called core aeration. Hollow tines, straight down, two to three inches.”
10–18sGround level: water pooling on hard ground, then soaking into an aerated patch“Compacted soil sheds water instead of taking it. That’s why the same patch goes brown first every year.”
18–24sThe lawn covered in plugs, then the same lawn later, clear“The plugs stay. They look untidy for a fortnight and then they’re gone back into the turf.”
24–30sCrew walking the property with the owner. Mark and number“We’ll walk it with you and tell you what it needs. Sixfold Colorado.”

Sixty seconds — add these two beats

Before the machine, about 8s. “One thing worth doing before anyone turns up — water it a couple of days beforehand. Dry ground won’t give up a deep plug.” This is the line that earns a share, because it is useful whether or not they ever call.

After the plugs beat, about 40s. “Seed goes down straight after, while the holes are open. That’s the part that thickens it.”

07

Safe to say, and never to say

Safe as written — every one traces to §08

“Hollow tines, straight down, two to three inches.”

“Holes two to three inches deep and no more than two to four inches apart.”

“It only does much if actual cores come out, rather than a spike pressing the hole shut.”

“Water it a couple of days beforehand — dry ground won’t give up a deep plug.”

“The plugs stay on the lawn and break down back into it.”

“Spring and fall are the best windows, and it can be done any time the ground isn’t frozen or baked hard.”

Changing the numbers is where this goes wrong.

Any price, discount or “free estimate.” Standing rule, and the site auditor greps for it.

“Licensed and insured”, “certified”, “bonded.” None of it is true yet.

Any guarantee that the lawn thickens, greens up, or that seed takes. It depends on water and weather, and the customer agreement says so in writing.

Anything implying a crew size, a booking limit or a job count. There is no aeration track record and the ad should not imply one.

“We’re the honest ones.” Carry the facts; do not editorialise about carrying them.
