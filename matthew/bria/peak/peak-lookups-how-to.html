Peak desk look-ups and outreach

For Bria. Page 1: filling in the gaps on the Peak Pickleball desk (a company's website, main phone and general email, and where each came from). Page 2: reaching out to sponsor and expo leads for Matthew. Claire does the same work on her own account; the desk keeps the two of you off each other's cards.

1. Signing in

  • The desk is at traverseplatforms.com/peak-desk-28d38d/. Matthew gives you your own six-digit code. Don't share it.
  • Your account shows the companies and the expo organisations, and a card for each. Switch between Sponsors and Expo leads at the top.
  • Open a card from Companies (search by name, or start with the ones still at To contact).

2. Where you may look

  1. The company's own website: its Contact, About or Locations page, and the bottom of every page.
  2. Its Google Business listing: search the company and town in Google; the panel shows its website and phone.
  3. The chamber of commerce member directory (in Colorado Springs, the Colorado Springs Chamber & EDC).

Nowhere else. General and role contacts are what we want: info@, events@, sponsorships@, marketing@, the front desk or main line. A person counts only when the company's own website names them for sponsorships, events or marketing, with the work email or phone it shows.

Never
  • No people-search sites, ever: Whitepages, Spokeo, BeenVerified, TruePeopleSearch, FastPeopleSearch and the like. That is Matthew's standing rule.
  • No personal cell phones, home addresses or personal email accounts.
  • Never guess an email address. Copy it exactly as the source shows it, or leave it out. "firstname@company.com" because it looks likely is a guess.
  • Look-ups are only for finding a company's own published contacts. Outreach (page 2) is only to contacts already on the card.

3. Filling in a card

  1. Website (sponsors): type it in the "Their website" box and tap Save and look up. The desk reads the site for free and lists the phones, emails and pages it found, with where on the site. Wrong website? Tap Change website. Expo cards have no website box: put the website in your note.
  2. Look them up again any time with the button under "Looked up". There is no paid look-up on your account, and none is needed.
  3. Phone or email: tap + Add a contact. Leave Name empty for a general contact. Role: "General inbox", "Main line" or "Events office". Type the email or phone exactly as the source shows it. Save contact.
  4. If it says "Already on the desk?", the detail is there already: tap "Add the new details to ..." only if it is the same contact, otherwise leave it and say so in your note.
  5. Mistake? Tap Edit (the pencil) on the contact and correct it.
  6. Mark looked up: in that box, write what you found and where, then tap Mark looked up. Do this on every card you open, even when you found nothing.

Example note: Website from their Google listing. Main phone (719) ... from the Contact page of their site. General email info@... from the site footer. No sponsorship contact on the site.

4. When sources disagree, or you're unsure

  • Two sources with different phones or emails: add neither. Write both in the note, with where each came from.
  • A national company (Kaiser, Molson Coors): look for the Colorado Springs or Colorado office first, and say in the note which office it is.
  • Can't find it in the three places: write that in the note and move on.

5. What stays with the sellers

Stages, owners, tours, proposals, prices, the Inbox, My leads and removing anything are the sellers' and Matthew's. Your account can't do them. If you see something they should know, put it in a note, or tell Matthew.

Reaching out for Matthew

You call, leave voicemails and send short follow-up emails to Peak sponsor and expo leads on Matthew's behalf. Everything you start counts as his: the card becomes his if nobody owns it, and replies to your emails go to his private My leads, not to you or the team. The desk still shows your name on what you did.

Your scripts and emails are drafts until Matthew approves them. On the desk each shows "Draft: waiting for Matthew's OK", and a draft email can't be sent. Don't make calls from a script until it says "Approved by Matthew".

6. Where it is on the desk

  • Open a card. Under the contacts is Reach out for Matthew: the call script and voicemail for that list, the buttons to log what happened, the follow-up email, and Warm reply.
  • More, My scripts and emails, shows all of them in one place. Read them before you start.
  • Sponsors and Expo leads each have their own script, voicemail and email. The scripts say "this is Bria with Peak Pickleball": the desk puts in your first name.

7. The lock

  • If a card says "Someone on the team is working this one", leave it. Brandon, Cai, Nneka or Lance has it, someone is answering their email, or Claire has already reached them. Don't call, email or note it as contacted. (Claire sees the same on the cards you reach.)
  • A card that says it is past first contact (a tour, a proposal, signed, Not now, Lost) is Matthew's or the team's now.

8. Logging a call or voicemail

  1. Write a short note first if there is anything to say (who you spoke to, what they asked).
  2. Tap what happened: Call: no answer, Call: talked, Wrong number, Left a voicemail, Emailed (not from the desk) (for example a website contact form), or Other.
  3. Pick a follow-up day if they asked you to call back. Next follow-up on the card does the same.
  4. Logged the wrong one? Remove it from the card's Activity (only what you logged).

9. Sending the follow-up email

  • Pick who it goes to (only people already on the card), tap Preview, then Send from Peak Partnerships.
  • The words are fixed: the desk fills in their first name and the company. You can't change them.
  • At most 25 a day (your own 25: Claire's count separately), Monday to Friday, 9 am to 4 pm Colorado time. Never to someone marked Don't contact or on the do-not-email list (the desk blocks them).
  • If the card says Matthew emailed them from the Peak email list, call instead: the desk won't send them another email for two weeks.

10. What to say, and what not to

  • Say only what is in the script: Peak is the indoor pickleball center at Chapel Hills Mall, 24 courts on two levels; for expos, about 80,000 sq ft over two levels and the April 2026 Senior Lifestyle Expo.
Never
  • No prices, packages, rates or discounts. "Matthew puts the options together after a short conversation."
  • No dates and no promises: no tour times, event dates or "we can do that".
  • No member numbers, player counts or "largest" claims.
  • No parking, power, floor or load-in answers for expos: write the question in the note.

11. A warm reply

When someone says yes, wants a tour or a call, or asks the price:

  1. "Matthew will be in touch to set that up. What's the best number or email for you?"
  2. Under Warm reply: hand it to Matthew, write what they said, their name and the best number or email, and tap Hand it to Matthew.
  3. Matthew gets a J.A.R.V.I.S. alert straight away and a follow-up for today. Stop there: don't call or email them again unless he asks.

If someone replies to your email, you won't see it: it goes to Matthew's My leads and he answers it.

12. If they ask you to stop

"Will do." Then tap Don't contact on that person. Only Matthew or a seller can take it off.

13. Your numbers

The team sees your calls, voicemails and emails in "by person" on Today and the report, as "Bria (for Matthew)".
