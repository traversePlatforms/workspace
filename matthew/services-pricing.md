## The thesis

The market rate for this work is well documented and higher than most people
assume. A local marketing agency is paid an average of **$1,779 per client per
month**, and roughly half of all agencies hold a retainer minimum of **$2,000/mo
or less** — so the middle of the market is a four-figure monthly retainer, not a
few hundred dollars.

The entry strategy is **not to undercut that by half.** Cutting to the bottom
buys the worst clients in the market and caps the business at a rate that cannot
later be raised. Every price below sits roughly **20–25% under the comparable
market midpoint** — enough to win a decision against a named agency, not enough
to signal that the work is cheap.

The founding-client discount is time-boxed, not permanent: these rates hold for
**twelve months from signature**, then step to standard. That is stated up front
so the increase is a scheduled event rather than a renegotiation.

## 1 — What is actually being sold

Everything below has been built at least once in this project. Nothing here is
aspirational.

### Web

- **Custom coded websites** — Astro, not a page builder. Dark and light themes,
  responsive, content driven from data files so facts are auditable in one place.
  A 29-page site has been built end to end.
- **Landing and campaign pages** — single pages built per offer or per storm event.
- **Site copywriting** — researched, every factual claim traced to a primary source.
- **Content hubs** — an `/insights/` pattern: article pages generated from a data
  file, so publishing an article is a data edit rather than a template edit.
- **Forms, lead capture, and tracking** — capture, routing, and the attribution
  needed to know which channel produced a lead.
- **Ongoing** — edits, new pages, performance, uptime.

### Brand and design

- **Logo marks** — SVG, including animated variants, with a defined animation rule
  per mark rather than a generic fade.
- **Palettes and type systems**, documented as tokens so they survive a rebuild.
- **Social profile and banner sets**, sized per platform.
- **Branded QR codes**, print-black and full-colour.
- **Photo retouching** — background removal, headshot cutouts, mark removal.
- **Print** — flyers, one-pagers, service agreements, field instruction sheets,
  checklists. Print-tested to an exact page count and delivered as PDF, because
  screen measurement lies about page fit.
- **Infographics and boards** — dense, hand-authored, on brand tokens.

### Content and social

- **Platform-native drafts** — LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X, and neighbourhood
  surfaces such as Nextdoor and local groups. Written per platform, not one post
  reposted five times.
- **Carousels and feed squares**, built at real size in destination form.
- **Alternate hooks and cut lines** kept with every draft, so a post can be
  lengthened or re-angled later without re-researching it.
- **Fact-checking to primary sources**, with a written log of what was verified,
  what was corrected, and what stayed unresolved. This is the differentiator —
  see §6.
- **Content calendars and scheduling.**

### Local and lead generation

- **Google Business Profile optimisation** on a client-owned profile.
- **Review engines** — the request mechanism, not purchased reviews.
- **Local Services Ads** setup, gated on the client's licence and insurance.
- **Meta and Google lead ads** — build, creative, copy, capped and testable.
- **Speed-to-lead design** — the operational rule that decides whether a lead
  converts, which is a staffing decision more than a software one.
- **Funnel and attribution design.**

### Strategy and research

- **Marketing plans** — phased, with a budget envelope, metrics, and a risk register.
- **Platform sheets** — which channels, in what order, and what gates each one.
- **Competitor benchmark studies** — reading the actual sites, page by page.
- **Compliance screens** — state advertising law, door-to-door law, trademark
  clearance. See §7.

## 2 — The three tiers

**Public copy does not use the word "retainer"** — his call, 2026-08-24. Say
"monthly", "the monthly tiers", or the tier name. The one-page sheet has been
stripped of it; this file keeps it because it is internal.

**Revised 2026-08-24.** The monthly rates now carry a commitment discount — $100
off Foundation, $200 off Engine, $400 off Pipeline — for signing up for a month
rather than paying weekly. Weekly is unchanged and stays a quarter of the
*standard* monthly rate, so the discount is what monthly billing buys.

| | **Foundation** | **Engine** | **Pipeline** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Monthly, committed to a month** | **$700** | **$1,400** | **$2,800** |
| Standard monthly, before the discount | $800 | $1,600 | $3,200 |
| **Weekly** | **$200** | **$400** | **$800** |
| Hours held | ~8/mo | ~20/mo | ~40/mo |
| Effective rate, at the monthly price | ~$88/hr | ~$70/hr | ~$70/hr |
| Market comparable | $1,000–1,500 | $1,779–2,500 | $4,000–5,000 |

**The arithmetic to say out loud on the call.** A month averages 4.33 weeks, not
four, so a monthly payment absorbs the fifth week that lands roughly every third
month. On Engine, a year weekly is 52 × $400 = **$20,800**; a year monthly is
12 × $1,400 = **$16,800**. Monthly is about 19% cheaper and asks for thirty days'
notice; weekly costs more and buys the right to stop in a week. Say it plainly —
a client who works it out later assumes the rest is also arranged in your favour.

**Margin warning, flagged 2026-08-24 and not yet resolved.** Effective rates at
the monthly price are **$70/hr on Engine and Pipeline** — at the surveyed
freelance average of $71.59/hr, and well below the agency average of $98.90/hr.
The earlier check that the discount came out of agency margin rather than out of
the hourly **no longer holds at these rates.**

It gets tighter with the free build (§3). Engine year one: $16,800 of retainer
minus a $4,500 site is **$12,300 for ~240 hours, about $51/hr.** Pipeline year
one: $33,600 minus an $8,000 site is **$25,600 for ~480 hours, about $53/hr.**
Both are below the freelance average, and the build work is front-loaded into
month one while the revenue arrives over twelve. That is survivable as a
client-acquisition play for the first few clients; it is not a rate to run a book
of business on. Put the twelve-month term in the agreement so an early exit does
not leave the build unpaid, and revisit these numbers before the fourth client.

Foundation carries a higher effective rate on purpose. Small engagements cost
more per hour to run — the same setup, reporting, and context-switching spread
over a quarter of the work. Do not let it drift down.

### Foundation — $700/mo · $200/wk

For a business whose digital presence does not currently work. The goal is that a
customer who searches the name finds something correct.

- Your site hosted, maintained and updated, up to 7 pages. **The retainer runs a
  site; it does not build one** — build fee separate, see §3. An existing site is
  picked up as it is, with no build fee.
- Two content updates a month.
- Google Business Profile set up and optimised, on the client's own profile.
- Four social posts a month, one platform, fact-checked.
- One-page monthly report.

### Engine — $1,400/mo · $400/wk

**The tier to sell.** Price the other two so this one is the obvious answer.

- Everything in Foundation.
- Website up to 15 pages, unlimited small edits, one new page a month.
- Twelve social posts a month across two platforms, plus one designed asset —
  carousel, infographic, or feed square.
- Local search: weekly profile posts, review engine, citation cleanup.
- One campaign landing page a quarter.
- Lead capture forms with call and form tracking.
- Monthly working session, plus the report.

### Pipeline — $2,800/mo · $800/wk

For a business that already has demand and needs the system that catches it.

- Everything in Engine.
- **Paid media managed** — Meta and Google, creative and copy included, on up to
  **$10,000/mo of ad spend**. Above that, 12% of the excess. Client pays the
  platforms directly and holds the accounts.
- Twenty posts a month across three platforms, plus two designed assets.
- Two researched articles a month to the content hub.
- Speed-to-lead system and attribution, built and maintained.
- Quarterly strategy sheet; print collateral as needed.
- Weekly report.

## 3 — Build fees

A retainer cannot absorb a site build. These are separate, and quoted before the
retainer starts.

| Build | Price | Market comparable |
|---|---|---|
| Starter site — up to 7 pages | **$2,200** | $2,000–8,000 |
| Standard site — 8 to 15 pages | **$4,500** | $5,000–8,000 |
| Custom site — 16 to 30 pages, data-driven | **$8,000** | $8,000–15,000 boutique |

**Terms:** half at signature, half at launch. Nothing goes live before the second
half clears.

**The lever, revised 2026-08-24:** the build fee is **waived entirely** on a
twelve-month Engine or Pipeline commitment — up to 15 pages on Engine, up to 30
on Pipeline. It was half price until 2026-08-24. This is the single strongest close available to a new
provider — it moves the client's objection from a large one-time number to a
monthly one, and it buys a year of revenue. Use it deliberately, not by default,
and never on Foundation.

## 5 — Payment and terms

- Retainers billed **in advance** — monthly on the 1st, weekly on Monday.
- **Thirty days' notice** to cancel a monthly retainer; **one week** on weekly.
- Build fees 50/50, second half before launch.
- Founding rates hold **twelve months from signature**, then step to standard.
  Put the step date in the agreement.
- Client owns everything delivered on final payment. Source files included —
  do not hold a client's own site hostage as a retention mechanism. It works
  once and it ends the referral.
- Client pays ad platforms directly and holds the ad accounts. Never run client
  spend through a personal card.
- **Portfolio use requires written permission**, per client, in the agreement.

### What makes a bad fit

Say no to these, out loud, early. A new business's worst mistake is a full
calendar of clients it is losing money on.

- A client who needs Engine work at the Foundation price. Quote the right tier
  or decline; discounting into a loss is not a client win.
- Anyone wanting reviews written, bought, or filtered.
- Anyone wanting a guarantee of ranking, lead volume, or revenue.
- Video production. It is not offered — see §6.
- Anyone who will not sign the compliance language in §7.

## 6 — The differentiator, and the honest limits

**What is genuinely different:** every factual claim in every deliverable traces
to a primary source or a named outlet, and each piece carries a written
fact-check log — what was verified, what was corrected, what stayed unresolved.
This is not a standard practice, and it is demonstrable in a way that "we care
about quality" is not. Lead with a real fact-check log in the pitch.

**Do not claim a proprietary or unique process.** The delivery method is not
differentiated. The research standard is.

**What is not offered, and should be said before it is asked:**

- **Video production and editing.** Footage cannot be reviewed here. Work from a
  transcript, a description, or an exported still, or refer the job out. Do not
  sell it and subcontract it blind.
- **On-site photography and videography.**
- **Owning a client's Google Business Profile or Local Services Ads account.**
  See §7 — this is a platform rule, not a preference.
- **Legal advice, public adjusting, or claim handling.**
- **Ad budget.** The client pays the platforms.

## 7 — Compliance gates

These apply to the client sectors most likely to buy — roofing, restoration,
home services — and they kill specific sentences, not whole campaigns. Screen
copy before it ships.

**Colorado restoration and insurance advertising — C.R.S. 6-22-105.**
Advertising that the client will pay or waive an insurance deductible is barred,
as is acting as a public adjuster. The rebate carve-out is post-contract only.
This kills "$0 out-of-pocket," "we handle your claim," and every variant. If a
restoration client's existing copy already says it, flag it in writing before
touching the account — inheriting it is not a defence.

**Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads.** Google bars lead-generation
companies from owning Business Profiles, and LSA requires a verified profile plus
the client's own licence and insurance. The profile is always the client's; the
service sold is management. Price and describe it that way, in the agreement.

**Door-to-door sales law.** Any agreement signed at a customer's home is
regulated. A defective cancellation notice reopens the sale for **three years**,
and a cancellation after work has started can leave the seller with nothing.
No waiver is possible. If a campaign ends in an in-home signature, the client's
paperwork is a campaign risk even though it is not your liability — raise it.

**Guarantees.** Do not put a ranking, lead-volume, or revenue guarantee in
writing, in any tier, at any price.

## 8 — One flag worth raising before this goes out

> **Answered 2026-09-16: a separate entity, Matthew's own, branded Stringline.** Neither a Surge
> sub-offering nor a trade name of Surge. The flag below is kept as the record of why it mattered.

This offering overlaps Surge's own line of business. Surge sells outsourced sales
and marketing; this sells marketing services to other companies. Whether that is
a conflict, a subsidiary offering, or a separate entity is a call to make
deliberately and probably in writing, before the first client — not after one
asks who they are contracting with.

It also decides the branding on this sheet, which is currently neutral. Under the
Surge name, everything on it becomes Surge public copy and inherits the full
restriction set. Under a separate name or your own, it does not.

**Unresolved and deliberately left out:**

- **No Denver or Colorado-specific rate data was found.** Every figure above is

  quote-shopping before the first proposal goes out.

  line. The weekly column is built as asked and is defensible arithmetic, but it
  will be unfamiliar to buyers who have shopped other providers. Expect to
  explain it rather than have it recognised.
