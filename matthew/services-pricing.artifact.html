Services & Pricing · Rate Card

What the work is worth, what to charge for it, and the number under each one that makes it defensible

Built 2026-08-24 Status Internal draft Source marketing/services-pricing.md Tiers 3, weekly and monthly

Nothing sent, quoted, proposed or published. Numbers are a working position, not an offer.

Branding is deliberately absent

This sheet carries no logo because the entity question in §8 is open. Under the Surge name every line becomes Surge public copy and inherits the full restriction set. Under a separate name it does not. Decide that before the sheet is shown to anyone.

The middle of this market is a four-figure monthly retainer. The entry play is to sit just under it, not to halve it.

A local marketing agency is paid an average of $1,779 per client per month, and roughly half of all agencies hold a retainer minimum of $2,000/mo or less. Cutting to the bottom of that buys the worst clients in the market and sets a rate that cannot later be raised. Every price here sits about 20–25% under the comparable market midpoint — enough to win a decision against a named agency, not enough to signal that the work is cheap.

01

What is actually being sold

Every item has been built at least once — nothing here is aspirational

Web Built

Custom coded sites in Astro, not a page builder — dark and light themes, responsive, content driven from data files so facts are auditable in one place. A 29-page site has been built end to end. Plus landing and campaign pages, researched site copy, content hubs where publishing an article is a data edit, forms and lead capture, and the attribution that says which channel produced a lead.

Brand and design Built

SVG logo marks including animated variants, each with a defined animation rule rather than a generic fade. Palettes and type systems documented as tokens so they survive a rebuild. Social profile and banner sets, branded QR codes, photo retouching and cutouts. Print work — flyers, one-pagers, agreements, field sheets — tested to an exact page count and delivered as PDF, because screen measurement lies about page fit.

Content and social Built

Platform-native drafts for LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, X and neighbourhood surfaces — written per platform, not one post reposted five times. Carousels and feed squares at real size in destination form. Alternate hooks and cut lines kept with every draft so a post can be re-angled later without re-researching it. Calendars and scheduling.

Local and lead generation Built

Google Business Profile optimisation on a client-owned profile, review engines, Local Services Ads setup gated on the client's licence, Meta and Google lead ads built capped and testable, speed-to-lead design, and funnel plus attribution design.

Strategy and research Built

Phased marketing plans with a budget envelope, metrics and a risk register. Platform sheets naming which channels in what order and what gates each one. Competitor benchmark studies read page by page. Compliance screens for state advertising law, door-to-door law and trademark clearance.

02

The three tiers

Both columns buy the same work — the difference is commitment, not scope

Weekly is priced at exactly one quarter of the monthly rate. Fifty-two weekly payments therefore come to thirteen months of billing against twelve: weekly costs about one extra month a year and buys the right to stop on a week's notice, monthly is cheaper and asks for thirty days.

Say the weekly premium out loud

State the thirteen-months-against-twelve arithmetic on the call, before the client works it out. A buyer who finds it later assumes the rest of the sheet is also arranged in your favour, and the premium is small enough that volunteering it costs nothing and buys the whole document credibility.

The check that matters

Effective rates land between the surveyed freelance average of $71.59/hr and the agency average of $98.90/hr. That is the test of whether a discount is survivable: it has to come out of the agency margin, not out of the hourly.

Foundation carries the highest effective rate, on purpose. Small engagements cost more per hour to run — the same setup, reporting and context-switching spread over a quarter of the work. Do not let that number drift down to match the others.

Terms: half at signature, half at launch. Nothing goes live before the second half clears.

The lever — halve the build fee for a twelve-month commitment

The build fee is cut by half on a twelve-month Engine or Pipeline commitment. This is the strongest close available to a new provider: it moves the objection from a large one-time number to a monthly one, and it buys a year of revenue. Use it deliberately rather than by default, and never on Foundation — that tier does not carry enough monthly revenue to fund the giveaway.

05

Payment and terms

Written down now, because every one of these is cheaper to set than to renegotiate

  • Retainers billed in advance — monthly on the 1st, weekly on Monday.
  • Thirty days' notice to cancel a monthly retainer; one week on weekly.
  • Build fees 50/50, second half before launch.
  • Founding rates hold twelve months from signature, then step to standard. Put the step date in the agreement, so the increase is a scheduled event rather than a renegotiation.
  • Client owns everything delivered, on final payment, source files included. Do not hold a client's own site hostage as a retention mechanism — it works once and it ends the referral.
  • Client pays ad platforms directly and holds the ad accounts. Never run client spend through a personal card.
  • Portfolio use requires written permission, per client, in the agreement.

What makes a bad fit — say no out loud, and early

A new business's worst outcome is a full calendar of clients it is losing money on. Decline these rather than discounting into them:

  • A client who needs Engine work at the Foundation price. Quote the right tier or walk — a loss-making client is not a client win.
  • Anyone wanting reviews written, bought or filtered.
  • Anyone wanting a guarantee of ranking, lead volume or revenue.
  • Video production. It is not offered — see §6.
  • Anyone who will not sign the compliance language in §7.
06

The differentiator, and the honest limits

One real claim beats five unprovable ones

What is genuinely different

Every factual claim in every deliverable traces to a primary source or a named outlet, and each piece carries a written fact-check log — what was verified, what was corrected, what stayed unresolved. That is not standard practice in this market, and it is demonstrable in a way that "we care about quality" is not. Lead the pitch with a real fact-check log; §9 of this sheet is one.

Do not claim a proprietary or unique process. The delivery method is not differentiated and saying otherwise is the first thing a competent buyer tests. The research standard is the differentiator, and it survives the test.

Not offered — say it before it is asked

  • Video production and editing. Footage cannot be reviewed here. Work from a transcript, a description or an exported still, or refer the job out. Do not sell it and subcontract it blind.
  • On-site photography and videography.
  • Owning a client's Google Business Profile or Local Services Ads account. That is a platform rule, not a preference — see §7.
  • Legal advice, public adjusting, or claim handling.
  • Ad budget. The client pays the platforms.
07

Compliance gates

These kill specific sentences, not whole campaigns — screen copy before it ships

The gates below apply to the client sectors most likely to buy this work: roofing, restoration and home services.

Gate

Colorado restoration and insurance advertising — C.R.S. 6-22-105

C.R.S. 6-22-105 — effect on copy

Advertising that the client will pay or waive an insurance deductible is barred, as is acting as a public adjuster. The rebate carve-out is post-contract only.

This kills "$0 out-of-pocket," "we handle your claim," and every variant of both. If a restoration client's existing copy already says it, flag it in writing before touching the account — inheriting the sentence is not a defence for continuing to run it.

Gate

Google Business Profile and Local Services Ads — the profile is always the client's

Google bars lead-generation companies from owning Business Profiles, and Local Services Ads requires a verified profile plus the client's own licence and insurance. The service sold is management of a client-owned profile. Price it that way and describe it that way in the agreement, because the alternative is not available at any price.

Gate

Door-to-door sales law — the client's paperwork is a campaign risk

Any agreement signed at a customer's home is regulated. A defective cancellation notice reopens the sale for three years, and a cancellation after work has started can leave the seller with nothing. No waiver is possible. If a campaign ends in an in-home signature, raise this with the client — it is not your liability, but it is the thing that turns a working campaign into a refund queue.

Never

No ranking, lead-volume or revenue guarantee in writing. Any tier, any price, any client.

08

The flag before it ships

One decision that belongs to you, and is cheaper made now

This offering overlaps Surge's own line of business. Surge sells outsourced sales and marketing; this sells marketing services to other companies. Whether that is a conflict, a subsidiary offering, or a separate entity is a call worth making deliberately and probably in writing — before the first client, not after one asks who they are contracting with.

It also decides the branding on this sheet, which is currently blank on purpose. Under the Surge name, everything here becomes Surge public copy and inherits the full restriction set. Under a separate name, or your own, it does not.

Unresolved — deliberately left open

  • No Denver or Colorado-specific rate data was found. Every figure above is national or international. Local rates are plausibly below the national midpoint, which would compress the discount this sheet is built on. Worth one round of local quote-shopping before the first proposal goes out.
  • Weekly retainers are genuinely uncommon. No survey found reports them as a billing method at all — BrightLocal's list has day rates at 9% and no weekly line. The weekly column is built as asked and the arithmetic is defensible, but it will be unfamiliar to buyers who have shopped other providers. Expect to explain it rather than have it recognised.
