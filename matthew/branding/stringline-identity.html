Mark directions

The line you check everything against

A stringline is the taut string pulled between two pins to set a dead-true line — every measurement on the job references it. All three directions share that horizontal run, so they read as one family. The difference is what each one says about it.

A — Taut Line recommended

Straight over crooked

The true line, and the wobble it corrects. Says the thing the studio actually sells — a checked claim against an unchecked one — without a word of copy.

B — The Snap alt

The moment it marks

The snap that leaves the line on the deck. More energetic, and the dust gives a texture to reuse in print. Loses the "versus" idea that makes A argue for itself.

C — The Datum alt

The graduated reference

Reads as measurement and precision. The most literal, and the most like an engineering firm — which is a risk when the buyer is a roofer, not an engineer.

Animation rule for A — not a fade. The line is pulled: it draws from the left pin to the right over 0.62s on an overshooting curve, the far pin seats as it arrives, and it fires once. That is a rule specific to this mark, which is what the rate card promises and a generic fade does not deliver. Reload to watch it. Disabled under prefers-reduced-motion.

Recommendation

Take A. It is the only one of the three that makes an argument. B and C illustrate a tool; A illustrates the difference between a checked claim and an unchecked one, which is the whole basis of the offering and the one thing a competitor cannot copy by lowering a price.

It also survives being small, because at 16px the crooked line drops out and you are left with two pins and a rule — still legible, still the same idea.

Wordmark

The mark sits on the line, not beside it

Archivo Black at −0.045em, with the mark carrying the line into the word. The descriptor is set in mono — it reads as a specification rather than a slogan, which is the right register for a studio selling verifiability. It says what is sold and claims nothing.

Palette

Taken from the tool, not from a trend

Chalk-line chalk is blue — the standard temporary layout colour — and red is the permanent one. That gives the brand a primary and an accent with a reason behind each, and it lands in a hue neither Surge (green on near-black) nor Sixfold (ember on pine) occupies, so the three never get confused in a folder or on a screen.

Chalk BluePrimary. The line itself. Links, the mark, one emphasis per view. #1E5AC8
Chalk DustTints, fills, chart areas, the settled-dust texture in print. #B9CCEE
GraphiteText and the dark ground. Cool-biased, so it sits under blue without going muddy. #171B21
SlateSecondary text and the crooked line. A grey pulled toward the blue, deliberately. #5C6673
PaperLight ground. Drafting stock, not cream — cream is every other studio's default. #EEF0F3
Red ChalkOne job only: a correction, a rejected figure, a killed claim. Never decoration. #B03A2A

Type

Three roles, two families

Archivo carries the voice; IBM Plex Sans reads at length; IBM Plex Mono handles anything that is a figure, a source, or a specification. That third role is not decorative — a studio whose deliverables all carry a fact-check log needs a face that makes a table of sources look like evidence rather than body copy.

Before this goes on anything

What is still open

01

The entity question, first. This offering overlaps Surge's line of business. Whether Stringline is a separate entity, a trade name, or a Surge sub-offering decides who the client contracts with — and a name cannot be registered sensibly until it is settled. See services-pricing.md §8.

02

USPTO class 35 search. Could not be reached from here — the portal is a JavaScript application. This is the one real gap in the clearance screen, and it is twenty minutes of an attorney's time.

03

Domain. stringlineco.com is the recommendation. stringline.com is a parked for-sale listing rather than a live business — worth a price check first.

04

Two out-of-state "StringLine Construction" companies exist — Kansas and the Gulf Coast. Different class, different state, neither in marketing. But since the buyers are contractors, the descriptor has to do the work of saying which side of the job this is.

05

Pick the mark, then the rest follows: favicon set, social profile and banner set, and the rate sheet rebuilt off the Horizon-branded version it currently lives in.
