Why the topics matter

  • Classes turn into members: about 45% of free-class takers go on to a paid plan (Peak's figure, 11 Nov). The free class got no posts.
  • Open Play has room: half of Open Play sessions ran with 2 or fewer players over the last year (Peak's CourtReserve data). Quiet times got no posts.
  • The one membership post (Peak Flex, 21 Oct) had no price, no detail and no link, and drew the fewest Facebook reactions of the month (5).
  • Sponsors and the April expo need a visible, steady audience to sell to. Neither appeared, and LinkedIn, where sponsors look, has no posts.

What is working

  • Real people, real moments. The Give 'Em Heaven veterans reel (26 Oct) was the best post on both platforms: 31 likes on Instagram; 28 reactions, 7 shares and 1,500 plays on Facebook.
  • Event coverage is thorough when it happens: six Facebook posts over the tournament, with 18- and 8-photo albums.
  • Partner content travels: the two veterans posts were shared 15 times on Facebook.
  • The right offer is in the bios: "Start here → FREE Learn to Play Class".
  • The audience is there: earlier Peak reels reached 13,000, 4,100 and 3,700 plays when they promoted something or taught a tip.
  • The reputation is strong: 4.9 stars from 585 Google reviews; 100% recommend on Facebook. Instagram's name now includes "Peak".

Peak is being outgrown on Instagram

Instagram followers, 22 Oct and 13 Nov

Springs Pickleball posted at least 20 times in those 22 days, to Peak's 10, and its November posts included things to sign up for: a Thanksgiving event, a kids' camp, the Winter Classic, league sign-ups, a member perk. Why it grew so fast is not visible from outside.

The holes, most costly first

  1. Posts don't promote what fills courts and plans. In 31 days: no posts on the free class, paid classes, Open Play times, leagues, sponsors, the pro shop or the April expo, and one membership post without a price.
  2. No next step. None of the 12 posts has a booking or sign-up link, and Facebook posts, where a link works, carry none. The tournament link appeared once, as a comment. The link page in the Threads bio still leads with Mother's Day open play, Breakfast Club (May) and Wheelchair Nationals (June).
  3. No steady rhythm. Two silent weeks; two-thirds of the month's posts in six days. The silent week was the run-up to the registration deadline.
  4. The local-discovery channels are idle. Google Business Profile: no owner updates and no owner replies visible on 585 reviews. Facebook Events: none listed, not even the tournament or the Halloween social. The 970-member Peak Pickleball Group Page on Facebook: Peak's page last posted there on 9 Sep. LinkedIn: 99 followers, no posts.
  5. Comments go unanswered. 10 member comments on Instagram and 9 on Facebook, with no reply from Peak visible on any of them. Messages and response times can't be seen from outside.
  6. Dead links in Peak's own systems. CourtReserve's Instagram icon opens a profile that no longer exists (@peak.pickleballco). Facebook's About section links a TikTok with no video since May. YouTube has one video from about a year ago, Peak Golf's Instagram has 8 followers and no post since April, and Nextdoor is still unclaimed.
  7. The same post everywhere. Facebook and Threads get word-for-word copies of the Instagram caption, hashtags included. One tournament reel never reached Facebook.
  8. Meta tags six recent posts "AI content", visible to members. The cause can't be seen from outside; it is worth checking which app adds it.

What we can't see from outside, and need to measure results

  • Meta Business Suite (90 days): reach, views, profile visits, link and bio taps, follower growth, top posts, Story views, message volume and response time.
  • Google Business Profile performance: searches, views, calls, website and "Book online" clicks, directions, reviews by month.
  • Google Analytics: visits arriving from Facebook, Instagram and the link page, and what those visitors did next.
  • CourtReserve: weekly registrations for the free class, the tournament and socials, so posts can be matched to sign-ups.

Quick fixes that cost nothing

  • Point CourtReserve's Instagram icon to @peakpickleball.us.
  • Replace the out-of-date buttons on the link page (Paage) with the free class, court booking and the next event.
  • Add a booking or sign-up link to every Facebook post, and a "link in bio" line on Instagram.
  • List each public event as a Facebook event.
  • Remove the TikTok link from Facebook's About section, or start using TikTok.
  • Claim the Nextdoor page and start replying to Google reviews.

Set against the current set-up

  • As described to us on 2 Nov, social media sits inside one in-house role that also covers video editing, photography and video at events and daily play, graphic design, and the pro shop (banners, merch, shirts, logos), with a wish for more time out front with members.
  • Social alone, done to the list above, is close to half a full-time week, before any of that role's other four jobs.
  • The last 30 days fit that picture: strong coverage when an event is in the building, then no time left for planning, promotion, replies or the quieter channels. That is a capacity problem, not a talent one.
