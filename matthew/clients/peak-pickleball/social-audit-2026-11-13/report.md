Prepared for Matthew Sharpe, 13 Nov 2026. Matthew's own client work (not Surge's). Everything below was read logged out and read-only on 13 Nov 2026 between 22:48 and 23:10 UTC: no sign-ins, no likes, follows, comments or messages. Peak's accounts are run by Peak's in-house team. Tyler-facing summary: `Peak-socials-last-30-days.pdf`. Talking points for Matthew only: `Peak-socials-talking-points-INTERNAL.pdf`.

Evidence: `captures/` (page text, metadata and Facebook timeline data), `screenshots/`, `posts-30d.csv` (one row per post per platform, 32 rows), `inventory-2026-11-13.csv` (every account, compared with 22 Oct). Builders: `tools/build-posts.cjs`, `tools/build-pdf.mjs`.

## 1. What was posted

**12 pieces of content in 31 days.** Each went to Instagram. 11 of them were also posted to Facebook and 8 were copied to Threads. Facebook also shared one post from the owner's personal profile. That is 31 platform posts, but only 12 distinct posts.

| Week (Wed to Tue) | Instagram posts | What |
|---|---|---|
| 14 to 20 Oct | 0 | Nothing posted. The last post before this was 12 Oct, so the gap ran 8 days. |
| 21 to 27 Oct | 3 | Peak Flex offer (21 Oct), Veterans Tournament registration (22 Oct), Give 'Em Heaven veterans reel (26 Oct) |
| 28 Oct to 3 Nov | 1 | Halloween Courtside Social (29 Oct, two days before the event) |
| 4 to 10 Nov | 7 | Veterans Tournament coverage, 6 to 10 Nov |
| 11 to 13 Nov | 1 | Veterans Day (11 Nov). Nothing on 12 or 13 Nov. |

- **Posts came on 9 of the 31 days.** Two silent weeks: 14 to 20 Oct, and 30 Oct to 5 Nov (7 days). That second gap was the week before the Veterans Tournament, when registration closed on 2 Nov.
- **8 of the 12 posts (67%) fell in one six-day stretch,** 6 to 11 Nov.
- **Formats:** 4 reels and 8 photo posts (6 of them carousels or albums). No Stories can be seen logged out. The highlights on the profile (Veterans, Wheelchair, Golden Ticket, Events, Community, Start!, Peak Golf) show Stories have been used before.
- **Topics:** 10 of the 12 were about veterans: the Veterans Tournament, the Give 'Em Heaven veterans programme and Veterans Day. One was a membership offer (Peak Flex) and one was the Halloween social.
- **Captions:** median length was 230 characters on Instagram. Most posts carried 4 or 5 hashtags, and 2 carried none. The hashtags change from post to post; #peakpickleball is on 5 of the 12.
- **Calls to action:** none of the 12 captions has a booking or sign-up link. Facebook allows links in posts; none of the 11 had one. The tournament registration link appears once, as Peak's own comment under the 22 Oct Instagram post. The 22 Oct caption says "go to our website under Events". The 26 Oct reel says "Registration is required" without saying where.
- **Duplication:** the Facebook and Threads captions are word-for-word copies of Instagram's, including the hashtags. On Facebook, hashtags do little, and a caption with no link sends no one anywhere. One Instagram reel (10 Nov tournament recap) never went to Facebook. Facebook ran "Day 2 was electric" the morning after Instagram did.
- **Meta "AI content" labels:** Meta shows an "AI content" tag on 4 of the Instagram posts (21 Oct, 29 Oct, 7 Nov, 10 Nov) and on 2 Facebook posts (21 Oct, 29 Oct). We can't see why from outside. It is worth finding out which editing app adds it, because the tag is visible to members.

- **Best:** the 26 Oct Give 'Em Heaven veterans reel. It had 31 likes and 3 comments on Instagram, and 28 reactions, 2 comments, 7 shares and 1.5K plays on Facebook. - **Lower than the last window:** Instagram averaged 23 likes per post on the 11 posts from 2 to 21 Oct (22 Oct audit). This window averaged 15.3.
- **Reach beyond followers is possible.** Earlier Peak reels, still on the Facebook Reels tab, reached 13K plays (PickleFest "come hungry" promo), 4.1K (a pickle-wall tip), 3.7K (the 2-year anniversary) and 2.9K. This window's three reels reached 820 to 1.5K. Promotion and tips reels have travelled further than coverage reels.

## 3. Holes and issues, ranked by impact

1. **The posts don't push what Peak says it needs.** Peak's goals: classes (45% of free-class takers go on to pay, per Tyler on 11 Nov), filling Open Play (51% of sessions had 2 or fewer players, per the 4 Nov baseline), memberships and Starter upgrades, sponsors, and the April expo. In 31 days there were 0 posts on the free Learn to Play class, 0 on paid classes, 0 on Open Play times or quiet slots, 0 on leagues, 0 on sponsors (one nonprofit partner aside), 0 on the pro shop, 0 on the expo, and 1 on membership, without a price or link.
2. **No link or next step in any post.** No caption links to booking, and no post says "book here". The Instagram bio says "Start here → FREE Learn to Play Class 👇". Threads' bio link goes to the Paage page, whose top buttons are still Mother's Day open play, Breakfast Club (May), Golden Ticket and Wheelchair Nationals (June).
3. **Bursts, then silence.** Posts came on 9 of 31 days, two weeks had none, and two-thirds of the posts landed in six days. The quiet week was the run-up to the tournament's registration deadline.
4. **Rivals are growing faster.** Springs Pickleball added 390 Instagram followers in 22 days to Peak's 25, and passed Peak.
5. **The best channels for local discovery get no posts.**
   - **Google Business Profile:** 585 reviews and 4.9 stars, but no owner updates and no owner replies visible.
   - **Facebook events:** none listed, even for the Veterans Tournament, the Halloween social or Thanksgiving.
   - **Facebook group:** Peak's own page hasn't posted in the 970-member group since 9 Sep. Members and a league organiser keep it going.
   - **LinkedIn,** where sponsors and corporate bookings would look: 99 followers and no posts.
6. **Comments get no visible replies.** Members left 10 comments on Instagram and 9 on Facebook, and no reply from Peak is visible on any of them. They were cheers rather than questions. DMs, where questions usually go, can't be seen from outside.
7. **Dead and abandoned accounts still linked from Peak's own pages.**
   - CourtReserve's Instagram icon points to @peak.pickleballco ("Profile isn't available").
   - Facebook's About section links the TikTok, which has 10 followers and no video since 14 May.
   - YouTube has 1 video, from about a year ago.
   - Peak Golf's Instagram has 8 followers and no post since 21 Apr.
   - Nextdoor is still unclaimed.
8. **Copy-paste across platforms.** Captions are the same everywhere, including Instagram hashtags on Facebook and Threads. Each platform gets a lighter version of the same post.
9. **Meta's "AI content" tag** shows on 6 posts. The cause is unknown and worth checking.
10. **Promotion comes late.** The Halloween social was posted two days before the event. The tournament registration post went up 11 days before the deadline, and nothing followed it in the last week.

**Improved since 22 Oct:** Instagram's display name now includes "Peak" ("Peak Pickleball | Colorado Springs"). It was "Indoor Pickleball Club | Colorado Springs".

## 4. What's working

- **Real people and real moments.** The veteran player story reel and the medal carousels drew steady likes and player comments, and the Give 'Em Heaven reel was the best post of the month on both platforms.
- **Event coverage is thorough when it happens.** On Facebook alone, the tournament got 6 posts over 5 days, including 18-photo and 8-photo albums.
- **Facebook shares.** The two veterans posts were shared 15 times between them. Partner content travels.
- **The bios carry the right offer:** "Start here → FREE Learn to Play Class".
- **The reputation underneath is strong:** 4.9 stars from 585 Google reviews, and 100% recommend on Facebook.
- **Peak has had reels reach well beyond its followers** (13K, 4.1K, 3.7K plays), so the audience is there when the content gives people a reason to watch.

## 5. Workload: what running Peak's socials properly takes

**This is an estimate built from a task list. It is not a sourced figure.** It assumes a steady week aimed at Peak's own goals:

- 4 feed posts a week on Instagram and Facebook, 2 of them reels, written for each platform.
- Stories 5 days a week.
- A Facebook event for each public event.
- A weekly Google update and a reply to every Google review.
- A weekly LinkedIn post for sponsors and corporate.
- A weekly post in the Facebook group.
- Comments and DMs checked daily.
- A monthly report.

| Task | Assumption | Hours / week |
|---|---|---|
| Plan the week: content calendar, check the CourtReserve schedule for quiet slots and upcoming events | weekly | 1.5 |
| Shoot on the floor (classes, open play, leagues, members) | 2 sessions × 1.5 h | 3.0 |
| Edit reels | 2 reels × 1.25 h | 2.5 |
| Design graphics and carousels (offers, schedules, sponsor thank-yous) | 2 × 0.75 h | 1.5 |
| Write captions with a CTA and link, adapted per platform | 4 posts × 20 min | 1.5 |
| Publish or schedule to Instagram, Facebook and Threads (plus YouTube Shorts if kept), and check each one | 4 posts × ~20 min | 1.5 |
| Stories | 5 × 15 min | 1.25 |
| Community: comments, DMs, tags, group questions | ~20 min a day × 7 | 2.3 |
| Google Business Profile: weekly update, review replies, Q&A | weekly | 0.75 |
| Facebook events | 1 × 20 min | 0.3 |
| LinkedIn post for sponsors and corporate | 1 × 30 min | 0.5 |
| Facebook group post and moderation | weekly | 0.4 |
| Monthly report, link-in-bio and profile upkeep | 2 h a month + 30 min a week | 1.0 |
| **Total, normal week** | | **about 18 (range 15 to 20)** |
| Event weekend (tournament, social) | shooting all day, same-day posts, recap | **+6 to 10** |

**Set against the in-house role.** At the 2 Nov meeting, Peak described one in-house role covering:

- social media marketing
- video editing
- photography and video at events and daily play
- graphic design
- the pro shop (banners, merch, shirts, logos)

That person also wants more time out front with the community. So social media is one of five jobs in that role, and the estimate above is close to half a full-time week on its own. The last 30 days produced 12 posts, 8 of them in one tournament week. That pattern fits a role that posts when there is an event in the building and has no time left for planning and promotion. It is not a question of skill.

**Not known (ask, don't assume):**

- the in-house team's weekly hours
- how those hours split between social, the pro shop and other duties
- whether anyone else posts or answers DMs (Facebook shared the owner's personal post on 8 Nov)
- whether there is a content calendar or an approval step
