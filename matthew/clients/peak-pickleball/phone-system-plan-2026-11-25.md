## Step 1: find out what they have (one visit or one call with Tyler, ~30 min)
Ask Tyler (or whoever pays the bill):
1. Provider and plan name (a recent bill tells us: monthly cost, number of lines, contract end date, early-termination fee).
2. The main number(s) customers call, and who the number is registered to (needed to port it if we switch; the account PIN too, kept by Tyler).
3. Handsets: how many, where (front desk, back office, anyone's cell), make/model (are they VoIP desk phones or a landline?).
4. Internet at the desk: provider and whether the phones run over it (decides whether a switch to VoIP is plug-in or a re-wire).
5. What happens now when someone calls: who it rings, for how long, then what (voicemail? rings forever? busy?).
6. Voicemail: whose box, who checks it, how often, does anyone get it as email/text.
7. Hours: staffed front-desk hours vs. open hours; what callers should hear after hours.
8. What callers mostly ask for (court booking, open play, lessons, memberships, events/expo rental, sponsors, lost-and-found). Front desk
   can tally a day or two on paper.
9. Anything they want kept (the number, a greeting, a person's line) and anything that annoys them.

Also: make 3 test calls ourselves (staffed hours, busy hours, after hours) and write down exactly what a caller gets.

## Step 2: design the call flow (on paper, Tyler signs off)
Draft to agree before touching anything:
- **Greeting** (short, under 15 s): "Thanks for calling Peak Pickleball. For court reservations and open play, press 1. For memberships
  and lessons, press 2. For events, private rentals and sponsorships, press 3. For anything else, stay on the line." Exact options come
  from the Step 1 tally; max 4 options; the website/app line ("book any time at ...") said once.
- **Option 1 / 2:** ring front desk ~20 s, then back office ~20 s (or both together after the first ring), then voicemail.
- **Option 3:** ring the person who handles events/sponsors (Tyler, or a sponsor seller), then their own voicemail.
- **After hours:** hours + "book online at ..." + voicemail; no ringing an empty desk.
- **Voicemail:** one shared office box (not a personal cell), emailed/texted to the front desk group with the transcription, so nothing
  waits for someone to dial in. One owner who clears it daily.
- **Missed-call text-back** (optional, Tyler's call): an automatic "Sorry we missed you, book at ... or we'll call you back" text.
  Only if the provider supports it and Peak is happy texting callers.
- Holiday/closure greeting that staff can switch with one tap.

## Step 3: fix in place or switch
- **If they're on a VoIP system that can do the flow above** (most can: RingCentral, Grasshopper, Ooma Office, Google Voice for Business,
  Spectrum/Comcast Business VoiceEdge, etc.): set it up on what they have. Cheapest, no number move.
- **If it's a landline or a system that can't do menus/ring groups:** compare 2 or 3 business VoIP options on the features Peak needs
  (menu, ring groups, voicemail-to-email with transcription, app on staff phones, missed-call text, call stats). Prices checked on each
  provider's own pricing page on the day, not from memory. Keep the number (port it); switch over on a quiet day, old service cancelled
  only after the port completes.
- Decision, provider and cost: Tyler's, from a one-page comparison.

## Step 4: set up, test, hand over
- Build the menu, ring groups, voicemail, after-hours and holiday modes; record the greeting (Peak staff voice, or a text-to-speech
  greeting if they prefer).
- Test every path from an outside phone (each option, staffed, busy, unanswered, after hours, voicemail lands in email).
- One-page front-desk sheet: what the menu says, how to switch holiday mode, how to check voicemail, who owns what.
- Two weeks later: check the call log (answered vs missed, voicemails returned) with Tyler.
