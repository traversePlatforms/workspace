Who to target, and how to get the meeting

For Brandon and Matthew · 19 Oct 2026 · internal, not for sponsors

The rule: the businesses most likely to sponsor are the ones that sell something worth thousands to local homeowners, older adults or families, and already pay for local advertising. Peak's own 28 sponsors prove which kinds buy here. Start with their competitors and neighbours.

Who decides

  • Small and local businesses: the owner. Ask for them by name.
  • Hospitals, banks, dealerships: the marketing or community relations manager.
  • Staff memberships: HR or the benefits manager.

Where the leads come from

  1. Peak members who own businesses. Ask at signup and at the front desk; invite them to a sponsor night.
  2. Current sponsors. Two referrals each, and their direct competitors.
  3. Chambers and networking groups. Member directories, plus mixers to host at Peak.
  4. Who already advertises locally. Local TV and radio ads, billboards, mailers, expo vendors.
  5. Map searches. Every business in a target market within a few miles of Chapel Hills.

Getting the meeting

Invite them to the courts, don't pitch on the phone. A free group lesson for their team gets the decision-maker into the building, and the 24 courts sell themselves.

Touches

  1. Day 1: walk in or call; offer the free team session.
  2. Day 2: email the sponsor sheet and two times to visit.
  3. Day 7: call back.
  4. Day 14: last touch, then set a reminder for next quarter.

"Hi, I'm with Peak Pickleball at Chapel Hills. A lot of our members are exactly your customers. Can I bring your team in for a free session on the courts and show you what we do with local businesses?"

Running the meeting

  1. Tour the courts first.
  2. Ask: who's your best customer, what is one worth, what marketing works now?
  3. Offer one or two options built for their goal, not the whole menu.
  4. Show the payback: yearly price divided by what one customer is worth.
  5. Leave with a decision date.
