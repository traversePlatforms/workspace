What to sell beyond court signs

  • Naming the four championship courts, the dink courts, the fitness room or the golf simulator.
  • "Peak Premier League presented by…" (no league sponsor today) and presenting spots at tournaments.
  • Member emails, social posts and a members-only offer.
  • Booth and demo days on site; staff memberships and team events for the sponsor's employees.
  • Backing the veterans and adaptive programs by name.
  • One sponsor per category, charged as a premium.

How Brandon sells it

  1. Leads: members who own businesses, current sponsors' referrals and competitors, chambers, businesses already advertising locally.
  2. The meeting: invite them for a free team session on the courts instead of pitching on the phone.
  3. The proposal: their goal, Peak's audience and events, one or two options, and the payback: yearly price ÷ what one customer is worth (a $1,500 patient pays back a court in 8).
  4. Close: Peak signs the contract; renewal date set.

What keeps them paying

  • A monthly results report per sponsor: custom QR evaluations, offer redemptions, leads from booth days, event attendance, photos.
  • Renewal talks 90 days early, with the reports in hand.
  • Commission norm: 15–20% on new deals, about 10% on renewals, in writing before selling.

Memberships online and in person

Where it stands: Drop-in $12/hr · Play $39/mo · Flex $99 · Unlimited $159 · Platinum $199 · Student $9, all month to month, signup through CourtReserve. There's a free Learn to Play class, and Peak is on Wellhub for employer wellness. Its site lists no first-month offer, no direct corporate or group plan, and no way to pause a membership.

Online

  1. Ads to the free class, not to a membership. Facebook and Instagram lead ads, and Google search ads for "indoor pickleball Colorado Springs". Short form: name, phone, email.
  2. Follow up within 48 hours of the free class or a first drop-in, by text or call, with a first-month offer.
  3. Email everyone who has ever booked: drop-ins and free-class players are the cheapest people to convert.
  4. A "which plan fits you" guide on the membership page: once a week → Play; three or more times a week → Unlimited.
  5. A corporate and group signup page: other clubs give 10% off for 1–5 employees, 20% for 6–50, 25% for 51+.
  6. Google Business Profile kept current: photos, events, hours, reviews answered.
  7. Referrals: reward Unlimited and Platinum members when a guest they bring joins.

In person

  1. The mall: a staffed table inside Chapel Hills offering the free class or a first hour.
  2. Front desk: every free-class player and drop-in leaves with the right plan pitched and their details taken.
  3. Employers: pitch staff memberships and team events to local companies, alongside the sponsor pitch.
  4. Military: Fort Carson alone employs 32,410 people. Peak gives only 5% off; a unit or spouse-group offer and booths at base events.
  5. Seniors: the 55+ Senior Club through churches, HOAs, 55+ communities and senior centres.
  6. Events: health and fitness expos, festivals, farmers markets: paddles out, free-class signups.
  7. Students: the $9 plan at schools and colleges.

Keeping members

  • A paid pause option for winter travel and injuries, instead of a cancellation.
  • Leagues, mixers and ladders: members with friends at the club stay longest.

What Matthew builds

  • The ad campaign and lead form, with leads landing in one pipeline Brandon's team works.
  • Automatic follow-up texts and emails after a free class or drop-in.
  • A weekly dashboard: leads → free classes → members, by source.
  • For sponsors: the prospect list, pipeline, proposal maker and monthly reports.

Ask Peak for first

Active members by plan, monthly visits, how many free-class players join, current sponsors' prices and renewal dates, and access to their CourtReserve customer list for emails.
