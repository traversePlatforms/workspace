Sponsor add-ons: the tech side

The pitch in one line: most local sponsorships are a logo on a wall with no numbers. Peak's sponsors get proof: a monthly report showing what their sponsorship did.

Ready to start (Matthew sets it up; Peak only has to say yes)

  1. Tracked QR codes. Each sponsor gets its own code for court signs, banners and table tents. Every scan is counted, by week and by sign, and goes to the sponsor's page or offer.
  2. Monthly sponsor report. One page, sent each month: QR scans, clicks on the sponsor's links, and opens and clicks on any Peak email that features them. Website and TV numbers are added once Peak gives access.
  3. Member deals with codes. The sponsor offers Peak members something (for example a discount). Each deal gets a code or QR, so the sponsor sees real redemptions, not just "exposure". Peak never hands over member details.

Needs something from Peak first

  1. Sponsor slides on Peak's TVs. A rotating sponsor reel, plus "Court 4 presented by …" boards, with a log of how often each sponsor showed. Needs: how many TVs and what runs them.
  2. Partner spot in member emails. A "This month's partner" block in Peak's emails to members, with clicks counted. Needs: Peak's OK and who sends member emails today.
  3. Sponsor page on Peak's website. A profile for each sponsor with tracked links. Needs: the website login.
  4. Staff sign-up link. For sponsors who include memberships for their team: their own sign-up link, so they see how many staff joined and played. Needs: how members sign up today (CourtReserve).
  5. "Presented by" tournaments and leagues. The sponsor's name on brackets, results and the TV boards, and in the results email. Needs: where results live today.

Later (bigger; check with Matthew first)

  1. Event sign-ups for the sponsor. A tablet sign-up at events (a raffle, a free lesson). Entries go to the sponsor only if the person ticks a consent box.
  2. Court cameras and replays with the sponsor on screen. Needs cameras; a premium tier.
  3. Wi-Fi sign-in page with the sponsor on it, if Peak's network can do it.

What not to promise

  • No guaranteed numbers (scans, views, visits). The report shows what happened; it doesn't promise a count.
  • No member lists or contact details, ever. Sponsor leads only from people who opted in.
  • No prices yet. Say "we'll put a price on it with the package".
  • Nothing from "Needs something from Peak" until Peak has said yes and given the access.
Questions on any of these: Matthew. Best openers: 1, 2 and 4 (QR codes, the monthly report, the TV reel). They're cheap to run, sponsors can see them, and they answer "what did I get for my money?" at renewal.
