What I do

Five things, and I do all of them myself.

Most of this gets sold as five separate contracts by five separate companies. It is the same five things either way.

Websites

Sites written in code rather than assembled in a page builder, so they load fast and nothing breaks when a plugin updates. Landing pages for a specific offer or season. Copy for the pages, forms that capture leads, and the tracking that says which channel sent them. This page is a fair sample — I built it the same way I would build yours.

Design and print

Logos, colours and type set up as a system so they still work in two years. Flyers, one-pagers, door hangers, service agreements, field sheets and checklists — tested to an exact page count and handed over as a print-ready PDF. Social profile and banner sets, branded QR codes, photo cleanup and cutouts.

Social and content

Posts written for the platform they go on instead of one post copied across five. Carousels, infographics and feed graphics. Articles for your own site. Every post comes with alternate openings and the lines I cut, so it can be reused later without starting over.

Local search and leads

Your Google Business Profile set up properly and posted to, a system for asking customers for reviews, and Google and Meta ads built with a spend cap so a test cannot run away from you. Plus the unglamorous part — how fast someone answers a new lead, which decides more than the ads do.

Research and planning

A written plan with phases, a budget, and what we measure. What your competitors are actually doing, read page by page rather than guessed at. And a check on the advertising rules for your trade before copy goes out, which matters more in some trades than others.

How it goes

What actually happens after you get in touch.

A call, about thirty minutes

You tell me what the business does and where the work currently comes from. I ask what you have already tried, including what didn't work, which is usually the more useful half.

I write it down and send it to you

A page or two: what I think the problem is, what I would do first, and what it costs. If I think you do not need a retainer I will say so — a one-off build and a Google profile is the right answer more often than people selling retainers admit.

You pick a tier, or you don't

No pressure call afterwards. The written version is there so you can think about it without me in the room.

First two weeks

Access, accounts, and the parts that are broken. Google profile, tracking, and whatever is currently costing you leads gets fixed before anything new gets built.

Then it is a routine

Work goes out on a schedule, you get a report, and we talk once a month. You can stop with thirty days' notice on monthly, or a week on weekly.

Worth knowing

A few things worth knowing before you sign.

Most of this only comes up after someone has signed, so it may as well be on the page.

I don't guarantee rankings, lead volume or revenue

Nobody can, and anyone putting a number in writing is either guessing or counting on you not checking. I will tell you what I expect and I will show you the numbers either way.

I don't do video production or on-site photography

It isn't my trade. I will work with footage or photos you already have, or point you at someone who does it properly, rather than sell it and quietly hand it to a stranger.

Your Google Business Profile stays yours

I manage it, I never own it. Same with your ad accounts — you pay Google and Meta directly, on your own card, and if we stop working together nothing gets switched off.

You own everything, including the files

Website, source files, logos, artwork. Once it is paid for it is yours to take anywhere.

Anything I write for you, I check first

If a number or a claim goes on your website or in an ad, it comes from an original source and I keep a record of what I checked. It is not a clever process, it is just doing it — but a lot of marketing copy is confidently wrong and it is your name on it, not mine.

My rates are lower than the agencies here, on purpose

I am building a client list, so I priced under the market rather than at it. That holds for twelve months from the day you sign, and I will put the date in the agreement so it isn't a surprise later.
