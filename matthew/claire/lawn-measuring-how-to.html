Measuring lawns by hand

For Claire. Your measures are the true lawn sizes that the app's lawn estimates get checked against. Work from your list "First 25 lawns" and the Google Earth file "Lawns to measure.kml".

1. Signing in

  • Google Earth Pro is the free desktop program (not the website). Install it from google.com/earth, "Earth versions", "Google Earth Pro on desktop".
  • Open Lawns to measure.kml with File, Open. Each house has a pin and a yellow lot line, numbered as on your list.
  • The app: open traverseplatforms.com/ty-25604fff/ in Chrome or Edge (Traverse Lawn & Yard). Type your own six-digit code, which Matthew gives you. Don't share it. The first time, read the terms and tap to agree.
  • Your sign-in only does one thing: find a house and save your measure. It opens straight on "Save a lawn measure". Sign out is at the bottom.

2. Finding the house

  • In Google Earth, double-click the house's number under Places on the left.
  • Press R so you look straight down with north up. Turn off 3D Buildings under Layers, so roofs don't lean over the grass.
  • Check the date at the bottom of the screen ("Imagery date"). If it is winter, or older than 2023, click the clock (Historical Imagery) and pick the newest summer picture.
  • The yellow lot line comes from county records and can be a few feet out, more on big rural lots. Trust the fence and the grass edge, not the line.

3. Tracing grass only

  • Tools, Ruler, the Polygon tab. Set the units to Square Feet.
  • Click point by point round the edge of the grass, and close the shape. Read the Area. Clear it and do the next piece.
  • Trace each piece on its own (back, front, side strip) and add them up, e.g. back 1,904 + front 441 + strip 249 = 2,594.
  • Count: grass a mower would cut, right up to the edges. Grass under a tree counts when the lawn carries on under it.
  • Leave out: flower and shrub beds, rock, mulch, driveways, paths, patios, decks, pools, sheds, and tree crowns over a bed, rock or mulch. Evergreens (pine, spruce) have no grass under them: leave the crown out.
  • Tall, dry field grass that isn't mowed: don't add it in. Write its size in the notes on its own.

4. Noting trees and beds

  • In the Notes column, write the pieces and what you left out: big trees over the lawn (how many, leafy or evergreen), beds round the edges, anything you couldn't see.
  • Say what your total includes: grass only, or grass under the trees too.
  • Write the imagery date you used.

5. Saving the measure in the app

  1. Sign in at traverseplatforms.com/ty-25604fff/.
  2. Find the house: type the house number and street from your list (e.g. 355 Ingalls) in the box. Tap the house when it shows. Leave the town off if nothing comes up.
  3. Check it is the right house: the address, the photo with its yellow lot line, and the point to paste into Google Earth's search box (Copy). The app shows no lawn size of its own, on purpose.
  4. Save the measure: type the total in square feet (numbers only). How: Google Earth. What it includes: Grass only or Grass under the trees too. Pick "Grass and beds" only if you truly couldn't separate them, and say so in your notes.
  5. Check the number, then tap Save my measure. The card then says "Measured by hand" with your figure. A typo matters: tap Change and save the right number. You can change your own measures, not anyone else's.
  6. Still write the total and your notes on your list: the notes (pieces, trees, picture date) only go on the list.
Measures you wrote down before your sign-in was ready: enter them now the same way, one house at a time, then tick them on your list.

6. If you're unsure

  • Don't guess. Skip the lot and write why: "picture too old", "can't tell grass from ground cover", "house not built yet in the picture", "lot line doesn't match the fences".
  • A skipped lot with a reason is useful. A guessed number makes the check worse.
  • Questions go to Matthew.
Ground rules
  • Measure by hand in Google Earth Pro only. Don't save, screenshot or download Google's pictures, and don't put them into any other program.
  • This is desk work: never visit or contact the homeowners.
  • Your list holds home addresses. Keep it private, and don't send it anywhere.
