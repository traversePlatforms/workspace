### Term 4 — escrow is in as a tick-box, not as a decision I made for you

`terms-agreed` recorded this as *"not understood"*, so nothing has been conceded. §3 now ends
with **check one**: Owner holds the title certificate, or an escrow agent does. The escrow
option names all five things a clause needs to be a clause — who the agent is, who pays, what
they hold, what releases it, what returns it, and what happens if the agent folds.

**The one thing it fixes outright:** the clause states the certificate is held **unsigned and
in Matthew's name**, and that Matthew does not endorse it into escrow. That is the difference
between the fair version of escrow and the dangerous one. A signed title in a third party's
hands can be filed by whoever holds it.

**Still to ask Brandon, in these words:** does he mean the unsigned title held by an agent, or
a title Matthew has already signed over?

### Term 6 — the insurance half was already there

Checked rather than assumed. The **Vehicle Insurance Agreement already requires** Matthew to be
named **additional insured** and **loss payee** (§2), a **certificate of insurance before
handover** (§4.1), and **written notice from the insurer before cancellation, non-renewal or
lapse** (§4.3). That is the whole of what term 6's insurance half asks for, and no change was
needed. Its coverage limits in §1 are **still blank**.

**"Dual registration" does not exist as a Colorado mechanism**, so §5.1 is now a **check one**:
Owner's name alone, or both names on the title joined by **"OR"** or **"AND"**. The clause says
in terms that Section 3 governs the title regardless of what registration says, so this cannot
accidentally move ownership.

⚑ **"OR" lets either name act alone. "AND" requires both signatures.** If both names go on with
"AND", Matthew cannot transfer or sell the truck without Brandon's signature — which is the
same exposure as the lienholder idea that was already rejected once, arriving by a different
door.

### Term 5 — the 90-day clause is the first thing in this deal that can cost Matthew money after handover

Drafted tightly, because *"clearly as a result of my time with the truck"* is exactly what a
dispute turns on. §6.6 names the covered systems, excludes cosmetics and wear items, makes
**Exhibit A the baseline** so anything recorded at delivery is out, requires **written notice
within 7 days and before any repair is authorized**, lets Matthew inspect and get a second
opinion at his own cost — and carries a **cap, which is blank.**

**Fill the cap in.** Uncapped, a single transmission or engine fault can cost more than the
entire down payment.

## ⚑ What these terms do to the money

The prior finding was that the deal is **guaranteed negative for Matthew** in outcome (b) — if
Brandon hands the truck back, the deposit is refunded **in full**, so Matthew keeps **$0** for
the use of his truck and the period he could not sell it.

**Nothing in the six terms changes that, and two of them make it worse.**

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit at risk for Brandon if he walks | $8,000, fully refundable | **$7,000, fully refundable** |
| Term | 6 months | **12 months** |
| Plus the ROFR period after it | 12 months | 12 months |
| **Total period Matthew cannot sell** | **18 months** | **24 months** |
| What Matthew keeps if Brandon walks | $0 | **$0** |
| New cost on Matthew | — | **§6.6, capped at [blank]** |

So the term doubled, the deposit fell $1,000, Matthew picked up a repair obligation, and the
walk-away outcome still pays him nothing. Against a **~$24,000 instant cash offer that was real
on 2026-09-24** (Carvana and Carfax both), outcome (b) is two years of holding a depreciating
asset he cannot sell, for zero.

**This is not an argument against the deal.** Matthew read the likelihood as **80/20 that they
buy it out**, and if they do, none of the above happens. It is an argument about **what the 20%
costs**, and right now the 20% costs him everything and Brandon nothing.

### The one fix that was decided and is still not in the terms

From 2026-09-24, and it did not come up on 2026-09-29: **keep the deposit whole but make part
of it non-refundable** — earned if Brandon walks, credited if he buys. The figure discussed was
**~$2,500**.

It is the only change that makes outcome (b) non-negative, and it is easier to ask for now, not
harder: Brandon's exposure on a walk-away drops from $7,000 to $4,500, which also makes the
no-lien position easier for his side to accept. **It is not in the document. It needs a
decision, not a draft.**

### And the line that still decides everything

**Total Purchase Price, §8(a), is still blank.** Break-even against just taking the $24,000
cash was estimated at **$25,500–27,000**, and that is only break-even against the *dealer*
number. An option with no stated price is the most common way these fail.
