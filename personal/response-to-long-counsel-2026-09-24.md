Point by point:

## 1. Cross-default on the Insurance Agreement — agreed, with one carve-out

Agreed that 12.1 shouldn't make every breach an instant default. Happy to limit it to material
breaches not cured within 10 days, matching 12.5.

Two exceptions I'd keep as immediate: **coverage actually lapsing** (Insurance Agreement §4.4) and
**a misstatement to the insurer** (§3). Those are the two situations where the truck is on the road
uninsured in my name, and a 10-day cure doesn't help either of us. The administrative obligations —
the renewal declarations page, the 5-day notice of a policy change — take the cure period.

## 2. Notice mechanics — agreed in full

He's right, and it's a drafting error rather than a position. Email notice will be effective **when
sent** to the address in the agreement, with the sender keeping proof of sending. Certified mail
stays as an alternative. I'll drop "and acknowledged".

## 3. Security for the $8,000 — agreed in substance, not via a lien

I'm not willing to put a lienholder on the title. Once one is noted I can't clear it later without
your signature, and §1 of the agreement currently represents clear title, which a lien contradicts.
For a six-month term on a refundable deposit it's heavier than the exposure.

Taking his option (b) instead, and adding to it:

- A covenant that I will not sell, pledge, encumber or allow any lien against the truck during the
  Term. (§9 already stops me selling; this extends it to liens.)
- A representation that there is no lien, judgment, garnishment or security interest against me or
  the truck as at signing.
- An undertaking to tell you within 5 days if one arises.

That's the same protection for the realistic cases and it doesn't leave either of us needing the
other's signature to clean up a title afterwards.

## 4. Refund and return — mostly agreed

- **Refund timing:** the blank is mine to fill. Proposing **5 business days from return**, not at the
  moment of return — I need to be able to look at the truck and get a quote rather than settle up
  standing in a driveway.
- **Joint return report:** agreed, and it's a good idea. A signed Exhibit C at return, mirroring the
  Exhibit A condition report at delivery. Anything not on it isn't deductible.
- **You may cure damage yourself before return:** agreed. Cheaper for you and it avoids the argument.
- **Late refund:** agreed — statutory interest and reasonable attorney's fees if I'm late. I'd want
  that mutual, applying to anything either of us owes the other late.
- **Deductions only for repairs actually performed:** not agreed. If the truck comes back with damage
  and I sell it as-is, I never perform the repair but the loss is still real. Standard stays a
  documented written estimate from a qualified shop. Happy to add that you may get a competing
  estimate and that the lower of the two applies.

## 5. Risk of loss and the 11.3 gap — agreed on the cap, with a carve-out, and a conflict to fix

He's right about 11.3. The Total Purchase Price is a negotiated number, and the gap between it and an
insurance payout isn't a loss you caused. Agreed to cap your exposure at the **actual cash value gap**
and to limit it to your negligence or wilful misconduct.

Carve-out: where the shortfall exists because **coverage lapsed or the insurer was misled**, I need to
be made whole. That's the one scenario where the gap is caused rather than incidental.

**A conflict he should know about:** Insurance Agreement §5.3 says almost exactly what 11.3 says, and
§7.1 of that agreement says it *governs over the Main Agreement on insurance questions*. If we cap
11.3 and leave §5.3 alone, the cap doesn't do anything. Both need the same amendment or the change is
cosmetic. Flagging it rather than leaving it to be found later.

On the basic allocation — whoever is in possession bearing risk of loss — I'd like to keep that.

## 6. Insurance — largely answered by the attached agreement

The arrangement was never for you to drive on my personal policy. See the attached: your own policy,
me as additional insured and loss payee, and honest disclosure to the insurer that you're the regular
and principal operator of a vehicle titled to someone else.

He's right that business use is the sensitive part. §4 of the main agreement permits ordinary personal
and business driving, so whatever policy you place needs to cover business use explicitly. If your
broker says it can't be done on a personal policy, tell me and we'll deal with it before signing
rather than after.

I'm confirming the same thing with my own carrier.

## 7. Owner default remedy — agreed

Reasonable, and I have no objection. Happy for him to draft a symmetrical clause covering my
obligations: refunding on time, delivering title on completion, and not selling during the Term.

## 8. Default consequences — agreed on the crime clause, partly on the cap

**Crime clause:** agreed. §4 lets you lend the truck to a licensed driver, so it's wrong that
someone else's conduct could put you in default. Limiting 12.4 to crimes by you or with your
knowledge.

**Uncapped losses:** agreed to exclude indirect and consequential damages. Not agreed to cap direct
losses at $8,000 — the truck is worth considerably more than the deposit, and a cap at $8,000 would
mean serious damage costs you nothing beyond what you'd already get back.

## 9. Mechanical failure that isn't your fault — fair gap, here's a proposal

He's right that 6.2 is silent on this. Proposing:

- Routine maintenance on the manufacturer's schedule stays yours (as drafted).
- A non-fault mechanical failure: **you cover the first $500 per incident, I cover the balance**, with
  me approving the shop for anything above that.
- Anything caused by neglect, misuse or a missed service interval stays yours in full.

Open to a different split — the number matters less than having one.

## 10. Financing — need to know

As drafted §8 requires cleared funds before title moves, which is right for a cash purchase and wrong
for a financed one. **Are you financing the balance, or paying cash?**

If you're financing, we'll close through a title company so the lender's payment and the title
assignment happen simultaneously. If it's cash, I'd rather not redraft it.
