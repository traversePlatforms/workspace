The Traverse Manual

Everything the app does: what all six installs share, what each edition has that the others don't, what each role can do, how the phone and the computer differ, and where every piece of data comes from.

Checked against the code (traverse/app, the edition build and the server API) on 13 October 2026. Home counts are from the current build files and can differ slightly from live.

01

How it fits together

Traverse is one app, written once, and built into separate installs. Each install is a complete copy with its own web address, sign-ins, data folder, phone-alert key and customer records.

One source: traverse/app→ Edition build switches parts on and off→ Six installs on traverseplatforms.com→ Later: one install per paying company

Rules that hold across all of them

  • Client data never crosses installs. A company's customers, notes, bookings and phone numbers stay in its own install. Pest's "neighbours on service" comes only from that company's own customers.
  • Sixfold is lawn and yard only. Hail, roofs, sun, pests and crime stay in the Traverse editions. Fire stays in Sixfold.
  • Each edition has its own example client (Example Roofing Co, Example Lawn & Land Co, and so on) until a real company signs.
  • A paying company gets its own install, made from its trade's edition with its own name, colour and web address (new-client.cjs, then build-editions.cjs). Your super sign-in comes with it.
  • A new market gets its own install, not a state switch. Dallas–Fort Worth is built this way and tabled.
02

What every install does

This is the shared core: the same in all six installs, with each edition's own words swapped in. Sixfold books a "Walk", Pest an "Inspection", Solar a "Survey", Security a "Consult".

Signing in and the device

  • Six-digit code, picked by an admin. No runs (123456) and no single repeated digit.
  • One phone or tablet and one computer per code. Signing in on a second phone signs the first one out and tells it why. A company can turn this off in its settings.
  • English or Spanish, switched on the sign-in screen or in Profile. Legal text stays in English.
  • Light, dark or automatic look, kept on each device.
  • Installs to the home screen like an app, and works with no signal: marks, notes and photos wait on the phone and send when signal comes back. Signing out warns if anything hasn't sent yet.
  • Profile photo, shown in the corner and on the team screens.

Finding the doors

  • Towns in a dropdown, grouped into fold-out counties.
  • Neighbourhoods inside each town, each with homes left to knock, how much is done, quotes booked and a score badge. Sorts include map order, nearest, best, score, and fire or lawn where that edition has them.
  • Territories: neighbourhoods assigned to you show on your home screen.
  • Find an address searches every town at once.
  • Map view with a pin for each home on the list. Grey dots mark homes that aren't on the list: the owner lives elsewhere (rental or second home), gets mail at a PO box, couldn't be checked, or is a business. Tapping one says "Not on the door list".
  • Route: walking or driving directions through the next stops, opened in Google Maps (about 9–10 stops at a time).
  • Sort & filter on a neighbourhood's doors, plus a door score from 1 to 10. The score is 75% how much the house needs the trade and 25% how able the owner is likely to be, from county value and how many homes nearby are owner-occupied. It scores the house, never the person.

The door card

Top
Address, distance from you, score and the reasons behind it.
Facts
Lot size, county value, owner of record, "New owner since…" when the owner changed between builds, a Customer tag, and "Assigned to…".
Trade sections
Whatever the edition carries (see section 03). On a phone each section shows its first line, with More and Less.
Outcomes
Walk booked (the edition's word), Quote sent, Not now with a come-back date, No answer (back on the list after a rest, 2 days by default), No soliciting.
Add info
Phone numbers (a main number plus up to 4 more), email, photos (with the owner's OK, the property not people), a note typed or spoken (voice notes), extra work spotted from the edition's own list, and a fire check (Sixfold and Yards).
Booking
Book the walk at the door. It goes on the calendar, and a booked door leaves the knock list.
Off the list
Customers, booked, quoted and walked doors stay off the knock list until a come-back date or a rest period brings them back.

Your own day

  • Home screen: greeting, today's doors, people talked to and quotes, weather alerts, tips.
  • My stats and a personal goal you set yourself.
  • Calendar: walks, come-backs, lead follow-ups and your own entries. It syncs to your phone's calendar (Apple or Google) through a private link, and making a new link stops the old one.
  • Training: the company's own lessons. The editions start empty, and admins write them.
  • Weather alerts across the top for the warnings that edition picked, push alerts to the phone once turned on, and a Live radar map (NOAA radar every 5 minutes, with a colour key) in the editions.

The office side (admins, and managers for their team)

  • Pipeline: door bookings and website or Meta enquiries, from first call to won. Web leads have no owner until an admin or manager assigns one; a door booking belongs to whoever knocked.
  • Customers: plans (one-off, monthly, quarterly and others), visits and what's due this week. Import a spreadsheet up to 500 rows at a time (addresses matched to doors, no doubles), or export.
  • Results: which doors turn into jobs, by outcome, neighbourhood, person and extra work.
  • Team stats with the leaderboard inside it, territories, the Plan table for assigning doors, and the Office dashboard.
  • Accounts: add, change, sign out and remove sign-ins, plus Flags for unlikely travel (doors marked too far apart for the time between them) and 3 or more phone switches in a day. Flags come from door addresses only; the app never sends anyone's location.
  • Downloads log: every export is recorded. Usage: which screens and buttons people use, never what they type.
  • Backups: nightly copies on the server, and a reminder to download one to a device if nobody has for a week.
  • Jobber export of booked doors (a spreadsheet) in every install.
  • Sample door: one made-up door with every part of that edition's card filled in, for demos. Nothing done there is saved.

Only in Sixfold

  • Quote builder: prices a job from the yard-walk counts at Sixfold's rates. Admins only, still in testing, needs signal.
  • Skip trace: owner phone numbers from BatchData, a paid provider. Each number is checked against the Do Not Call list before any Call link shows. Admins only, and each lookup costs money.
  • Field guide: the 33-lesson Sixfold Field Guide in Training, with the branded PDF and a "What to say at the door" card.
  • Morning email for admins at 6 am: leads to call, today's and tomorrow's walks, customers due this week, yesterday at the doors. Any admin can also ask for it in the app.
  • One-off properties: single-door towns, admins only, for a job outside the towns.
  • Sixfold's Google review link on its funding sheet. Yards has the same sheet with the link blank.

Shared with Yards: everything on the Yards list below (lawn size, lawn plan, trees, wildfire, fire check, funding, water, Communities, frost and rain, soil, townhome lawns).

Not in Sixfold: the Company screen and billing (it isn't a paying client), Live radar as a tile, and anything for roofing, solar, pest or security.

Roofing

Storm slate
CardHail, wind, roof, photos, permits
Towns23 + 4 commercial test
AlertsStorm, tornado, high wind, heat
CRM linkJobNimbus

Only in Roofing

  • Hail per door: every day since April 2020 that radar put hail of ¾ inch or more within a few hundred metres, with the size. Labelled as a radar estimate. Source: NOAA MRMS radar hail size (MESH), daily files from Iowa State's archive.
  • Hail measured on the ground nearby: spotter and Storm Events reports with sizes. Source: NOAA Storm Events (about 3 months behind) plus National Weather Service spotter reports. The server adds the last 14 days of spotter reports itself.
  • What time the hail fell ("about 3–5 pm"), from 2-minute radar files sampled every 20 minutes on each storm day.
  • Wind per door: damaging gusts and wind damage reported nearby, measured or estimated. Source: NWS Local Storm Reports via Iowa State. Reports are sparse, so a home with none doesn't mean no wind.
  • Storm list: pick a storm, see the doors it hit, their roofs, who owns them and who to call. Admins only.
  • Hail screen: storms from the last two years (five for a big one, 2-inch hail or 75 mph wind) and the streets they hit, with a hail map per storm. New storm days are added automatically, at most every 12 hours.
  • Roof settings: your price per square by roof material and the hail size that damages each material. Nothing shows until you fill it in; the app never invents prices.
  • Commercial test towns: 5,082 commercial buildings in three hail spots, plus a showcase town of six demo buildings. Each card names the owner or registered agent (from the state business register) and has a Search this owner button. Admins only.
  • Hail layer on the desktop map. Admins only.
  • Heat and high-wind alerts on top of storm and tornado warnings.

Shared with Solar (both have the roof switch on):

  • Roof size and pitch from USGS laser scans.
  • Year built from county assessors.
  • Roof material: read off aerial photos by a model trained on Douglas County's recorded materials, or the county's own record where Adams or Boulder record it.
  • Roof photos: the State of Colorado's 2020 aerial, a 2023 USDA close-up, and the house outline drawn on top.
  • Building permits (re-roofs here, solar in Solar).
  • Roof job tracker: signed, claim, adjuster, approved or denied, materials, installed, cancelled. It shows the Colorado roofing-contract cancellation windows (C.R.S. 6-22-103 and 6-22-104).
  • JobNimbus: send booked doors and walks to JobNimbus with the roofer's own key, or download a spreadsheet. Admins only.

Also on the Roofing card: FEMA's risk ratings for the area and the Census survey for the block.

Yards

Towns22
AlertsStorm, tornado, freeze, frost
RankingAeration-first

Only in Yards and Sixfold

  • Lawn size per door: Large, Medium, Small, Little or none, or Mostly trees. Green and under 1 metre tall in the 2023 USDA aerial (via Microsoft Planetary Computer), with Meta/WRI tree heights, inside the county lot line. Crew can correct it at the door, and their answers build the calibration set.
  • Lawn plan: seed rates, fertiliser and mowing height by month, from CSU Extension guidance.
  • Townhome lawns: in El Paso County towns, a lot with almost no yard outside the house is flagged "Townhome: the HOA keeps the lawn". It's scored down, and a neighbourhood that's mostly townhomes shows a "pitch the HOA" banner.
  • Trees near the house in the state's defensible-space zones (0–5, 5–30 and 30–100 ft): against, heavy, some or clear. Source: USGS 3DEP laser scans (El Paso 2018, Douglas 2020, Teller 2023–24) and Microsoft building outlines. About 150 Teller homes still read from a tree-canopy map.
  • Wildfire hazard per home, from the USDA Forest Service Wildfire Hazard Potential map (2023).
  • Fire check: a defensible-space checklist saved on the door.
  • Funding: county and state cost-share programmes a home qualifies for. Water: the water provider's restrictions.
  • Communities: HOAs and Firewise sites to pitch as one job. HOA names come from the Colorado Division of Real Estate roster, Firewise sites from NFPA. Admins only.
  • HOA on the card where the neighbourhood matches a registered HOA.
  • Frost dates and rain: first and last frost (NOAA 1991–2020 normals) and the last 30 days of rain against normal at the nearest gauge (NOAA NCEI, fetched live, at most every 6 hours).
  • Soil temperature from the nearest CSU CoAgMet station within 25 miles. El Paso, Douglas and Teller show "no station near".
  • Freeze and frost alerts.
  • County wildfire plans (Teller's 62 neighbourhoods).

Also on the card: FEMA risk ratings and the Census survey.

Only in Solar

  • Sun-facing roof area split by direction (south, southeast, southwest, east, west, north, flat). Each roof is measured from USGS laser-scan elevations.
  • Shade: open, some or heavy, from trees at least as tall as the roof within 20 metres on the sunny side.
  • A system that fits: panel count and size, assuming 70% of the good roof is usable and a 400-watt panel takes about 20 sq ft. Shown as an estimate.
  • Yearly output in kWh, from NREL's PVWatts (now the National Laboratory of the Rockies) for that roof's direction and slope.
  • Power company from Colorado's electric utility territory map (state OIT), with that utility's figures from the federal EIA-861 survey (2025).
  • Xcel Energy rates: time-of-use and flat rates from the Colorado PUC and Xcel, read 12 October 2026. A rate increase was pending, so these need updating when the PUC rules.
  • Solar permits already pulled at the address.

Shared with Roofing: roof size and pitch, year built, roof material, roof photos, permits, the roof job tracker and JobNimbus. Solar has no hail, wind or storm screens.

Only in Pest

  • Why knock: facts the rep connects, with no made-up risk score.
    • Open space within 90 m (Colorado's COMaP protected-lands map, with unnamed private shapes dropped).
    • A named creek within 100 m or a pond within 150 m (USGS National Hydrography Dataset).
    • Year built, and the last sale month in El Paso County (the assessor's sale date only, never owner columns).
  • Neighbours on service: the company's own active customers within about 160 m, bookings nearby in the last 30 days, and what neighbours told the team in their door notes in the last 30 days. It only uses that company's own records. A new company fills it by importing its customer list.
  • Pest season strip: a Colorado calendar with one sourced line per pest (miller moths, elm seed bugs, mosquitoes, wasps, boxelder bugs, mice, voles), from CSU Extension, Colorado Public Radio and Illinois Extension. Re-check each spring.
  • Pest's own door-note tags: wasps, ants, spiders, mice, firewood, standing water, other company.

Also on the Pest card: trees near the house. The quarterly plan and customer import exist in every install, but Pest is where they matter most.

Only in Security

  • Property crime near the door: burglary, vehicle theft and break-ins, other theft and vandalism within ½ and 1 mile, for the last 30, 90 and 365 days, with the latest date. Never domestic, never crimes against people. Source: Colorado Springs Police open data, updated daily, at hundred-block level. Only for homes inside Colorado Springs city limits; outside them the card shows nothing, because no reports doesn't mean no crime.
  • Crime map: the last 90 days shaded in squares of about 280 m, never as points. Colorado Springs, Denver and Boulder. Boulder's police data runs about 10 days behind.
  • Crime by neighbourhood: neighbourhoods ranked by property crime reported nearby in the last 90 days.
  • Town crime figures: each police agency's last 12 reported months for burglary, vehicle theft and property crime, from the FBI Crime Data Explorer. Agencies report on different lags.

  • The same neighbours, bookings and door-note counts as Pest, plus sale month, year built and open space.
Two rules are built into Security. It shows facts, not "at-risk" scores, and crime is always counted around a door or neighbourhood, never placed on a house.

Every edition (not Sixfold)

  • Company screen: the client sets its own name, phone, email, website and brand colour.
  • Billing through Stripe. Built and switched off; see section 08.
  • Live radar tile on the home screen.
  • Trade parts shown to crew, not just admins. In Sixfold the crew see the yard parts.

05

Roles

Four roles. The server enforces every rule below, so hiding a button is never the only lock. Making someone an admin in Accounts is what gives them the office tools; nothing is tied to a particular person's code, except super.

Crew

The reps at the doors.

  • Knock, mark outcomes, add phones, emails, photos, notes and extra work
  • Book walks at the door
  • See the trade parts of the card (editions; yard parts in Sixfold)
  • Their own calendar, and teammates' calendars read-only if they're on a team
  • Their own stats and goal, Training, alerts, radar, map, Find an address
  • See neighbourhoods assigned to them

Manager

Crew, plus their own team.

  • Leads: hand out new enquiries, see their team's
  • Team stats and the leaderboard
  • Assign neighbourhoods and doors within their team, in Territories and the Plan table
  • Draw territories on the desktop map
  • Team sign-ins: add crew (always crew, always on their team), give a new random code, sign out, remove
  • Office dashboard for their team; their team's calendars

Admin

The company's office. Everything below, across the whole company.

  • Pipeline, Customers (import, export, backups), Results
  • Accounts: add anyone, pick codes, set roles and teams, sign out, remove
  • Flags, the Downloads log, Usage
  • Company screen and billing (editions)
  • Every edition's admin-only tools (Storm list, roof settings, Communities, JobNimbus, Quote builder, skip trace…)
  • Write Training lessons; set team goals; Sample door

Super

You, and only you. Set on the server; the app can never grant it.

  • Everything an admin can do, in all six installs
  • The only one who can download the Plan's door list (500 doors at most, and every download is logged)
  • The only one who can see sign-in codes
  • Switch between installs from Profile → Edition (a one-use pass that lasts 60 seconds)
  • Client admins can't edit, re-code, sign out or remove you; they see your line tagged "Traverse"

06

Phone and desktop

Same data, same roles, same server rules. Only the layout changes. The desktop layout switches on at 1,100 pixels wide or more. A tablet can choose it in Profile → Layout.

What the desktop adds

  • Top bar: Home, Doors, Plan, Storms (Roofing admins), Pipeline (admins) or Leads (managers), Calendar, Team or My stats, Office, plus Find an address and your photo.
  • Desktop home: a dashboard in place of the phone's tile grid.
  • Browser: towns, neighbourhoods and the map on one screen.
  • Workspace: door list, map and door card side by side. Pins are numbered and coloured by score, and the card's buttons stay at the bottom.
  • Map, Hybrid or Photo view, using 2023 aerials; the desktop opens on Photo.
  • Neighbourhood rings: nearby neighbourhoods show as dashed outlines you can click.
  • Hail layer on the map (Roofing admins).
  • Draw a territory on the map (admins and managers).
  • Plan table at full size: sortable trade columns, filters such as Not knocked or Nobody assigned, column picker, score bars, by-neighbourhood filter, tick and assign, a preview panel, and a spreadsheet download for admins.
  • Office dashboard with a side menu: Today, Plan the doors, Territories, Communities, Storm list, Pipeline or Leads, Customers, Calendar, Team stats, Leaderboard, Training, Results, Usage, Accounts, Company. Today shows KPI cards against goals, the team today, walks for the next 7 days, a map of the best doors left and live radar, refreshing every 60 seconds.

What the phone is built for

  • Working with no signal: changes wait on the phone and send later.
  • Voice notes: speak the note instead of typing with gloves on. Uses the phone browser's own speech-to-text.
  • Photos at the door from the camera.
  • Push alerts to the phone (on iPhone, after adding the app to the home screen).
  • The fold-up card: each data section shows one line with More and Less, under 700 pixels wide.
  • Walking and driving directions through the next stops.
  • Tools grid on the home screen: In the field, Your team (managers), and Office in fold-out groups (admins): Sales, Team, Storms and data, Setup.
  • The phone can still open the Plan table ("best on a computer") and the Office dashboard.

Switching layouts

On a computer
Profile → Layout → Desktop or Phone. Phone shows the app in a phone-sized frame, which is useful for demos.
On a tablet
Profile → Layout → Tablet or Desktop.
Devices per code
One phone or tablet plus one computer at the same time.

What refreshes by itself

  • Every 5 minutes (server): weather alerts and phone pushes; JobNimbus sync where connected.
  • Daily, 7:15 am (server): new hail storm days for Roofing.
  • Weekly, Mondays ("Traverse weekly refresh" on your PC): crime data, FBI figures when stale, ground hail reports and hail timing. Uploads only changed files.

  • Everything else changes when the towns are rebuilt and uploaded.

Limits to know

Things a client could ask about, and the true answer.

  • The name on the card is the owner of record, not necessarily who answers the door. Trusts, one spouse on title and recent sales all differ.
  • Radar hail is an estimate and reads big hail low. Ground reports are sparse. A home with no hail day listed may still have been hit.
  • Lawn size comes from a 2023 photo. It reads open lots about 8–9% high and lots under trees 20–45% low. Dormant brown lawns and homes built after 2023 are missed.
  • Crime figures stop at Colorado Springs city limits. Castle Rock police and the El Paso Sheriff publish no downloadable data, so getting it would need a records request.
  • Permits are partial in several towns. The card never says "no permit".
  • Laser scans have years. Homes built after the scan have no roof size or tree reading.
  • Soil temperature only reaches the north metro; the south shows "no station near".
  • Some services are free with no guarantee: the 2023 aerials (Microsoft Planetary Computer), radar tiles (Iowa State) and the map libraries. If one is down, that part of the screen falls back or says so.
  • Phone numbers are never looked up unless an admin runs a skip trace (Sixfold only), and texting only reaches numbers given at the door.
