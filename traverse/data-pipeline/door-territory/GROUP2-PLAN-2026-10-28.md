## What "doors" means here

Stage A writes every house in the lot band that has a property address. Each row says whether the owner lives there ("YES") or should get a postcard ("no — post"). Our town files keep only the "YES" rows. Checked on 28 Oct: 1,631 of Windsor's doors sit on exactly the same point as a fresh pull's "YES" rows. So the number to compare with the Census is the **owner-lives-here count**. In the tests that came to 52-77% of the Census figure:

## The build list (44 targets, in run order)

Places come first, biggest first: every town or CDP of 150+ Census houses where under a quarter of the houses are doors already. After the places, each county subdivision is swept as a "rural" target. A sweep keeps the land outside the places plus anything a place's pull missed. Houses already pulled by an earlier target are dropped, so a sweep never repeats a place. "Rural-only estimate" means the subdivision's Census houses minus the places inside it (each place counted in the subdivision holding most of it).

## Pueblo: where the property address comes from

On the statewide parcel layer, Pueblo has owner and mailing address on 100% of rows. It has no property address (0%) and no lot size (0%). Its land-use field holds Pueblo's own short codes ("60B", "70E", "70L"), not words. The City of Pueblo's address services on gisdata.pueblo.us now answer "Token Required". The Pueblo County GIS and online-services pages refused automated fetches (HTTP 403). The state's tax-parcel layer (GOV/PropertyTaxParels) has no Pueblo rows.

**The source used is the state's address-point layer**, from the same OIT team and the same server as the parcel layer: Address_and_Parcel/Colorado_Public_Addresses. For Pueblo it has 87,295 points. Every point is marked sharing "Public" and was updated in 2026. All but 27 carry a ParcelID, and that ParcelID matches the parcel layer's parcel_id. The new script group2/build-walklist-pueblo.mjs joins the two by parcel number. A parcel's property address is the one address point on it. In place of the missing land use it applies these filters:

- exactly one address and no unit number (apartments, duplexes and businesses drop out);
- no address point means vacant land (most of Colorado City's paper lots);
- lot size from the parcel shape, 0-10 acres as elsewhere;
- owners that are plainly organisations (LLC, INC, CHURCH, CITY OF, SCHOOL DISTRICT, HOUSING AUTHORITY ...) are dropped. Family trusts are kept.

Then it runs the usual owner-lives-here test. Tested on Blende: 347 rows (231 owner lives here) against 300 Census houses.

## One fix over Group 1's clip (affects Group 1's live counts)

group1/clip-group1.mjs treated two rows as the same house when their parcel centres were under 20 m apart. On 0.15-acre lots (about 18 m wide) that drops every other house: 74 real neighbours in Blende alone. group2/clip-group2.mjs instead dedupes by parcel number among Group 2 rows. Against our own doors it uses 5 m, which is safe because our doors sit exactly on the centroid. **Group 1's door lists were clipped the old way, so dense new subdivisions in Group 1 towns are probably under-counted.** A re-clip of Group 1's raw pulls (still on the cloud machine in ~/run/group1/raw) would show how many. That has not been done. It needs Matthew's go because it changes live towns.

## How it runs

- **Stage A, door lists (running on the cloud machine since 28 Oct 18:03Z):** group2/run-group2.sh, one target at a time, Larimer then Mesa then Pueblo. Larimer and Mesa use build-walklist-co.mjs --pieces (6 tries per page at 3 s steps, after a Larimer page got HTTP 500 on 28 Oct). Pueblo uses build-walklist-pueblo.mjs. Each pull is clipped by clip-group2.mjs. It is resumable (<key>.done), refuses to start while another parcel job runs, and stops under 5 GB free. Status goes to ~/run-status.txt, the log to ~/run-group2.log, and the counts to ~/run/group2/out/summary.tsv. It ends at "door lists ready".
- **Stage B (after Matthew looks at the counts):** register each target as a town, with build-rounds-data SOURCES, centres, groups, county in assessor/town-county.json and the survey in sample-trees. g2-berthoud merges into g1-berthoud (**done 28 Oct** in the Group 1 re-clip, group1/reclip-group1.mjs: g1-berthoud is live with 4,431 doors, so do not register g2-berthoud). Wait for "group1 fix: measuring done" on the VM first (~/run/group1/fix-measure.sh measures the re-clip's added doors after stage A). Then the usual town steps: walk-list areas, yard v2 and trees on the cloud machine, values, year built where the county files allow (to be checked for Larimer, Mesa and Pueblo), the editions' layers, rebuild, then upload in batches with backups, md5 and index.json merged.
- Rules: one parcel-heavy job at a time on gis.colorado.gov (no local pulls while the VM pulls), disk checked before every target, nothing goes live without Matthew's go.
