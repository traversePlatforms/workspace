# Group 1 plan: the gaps inside our nine counties (26 Oct 2026)

Matthew, 26 Oct: "que up group 1". Coordinator brief: the single-family and townhome houses inside the counties we already cover that are not doors yet.

**Counts:** Census ACS 5-year 2020-2024, table B25024 (one-unit detached + attached, every occupancy), per county subdivision and per place, against our door points inside each Census boundary (TIGERweb, ACS 2024). Script: group1-gaps.mjs -> group1-gaps.json; targets: group1-targets.mjs -> group1/targets.json.

**Read the gap with care.** Census counts housing units, our doors are owner-occupied homes from the walk lists: rentals, PO-box owners and businesses are left out on purpose. So a town we have fully built still shows a gap (Denver 44,782, Colorado Springs 39,697, Aurora 18,948 are mostly that). Group 1 therefore builds the places we have NO town for and the rural areas outside any place, not the renters inside our towns. Expect about 60-70% of the Census figure to become doors.

## How it runs

- **Stage A, door lists (queued on the cloud machine, starts by itself when the trade-layer run ends):** group1/run-group1.sh. Per target, one at a time: the parcels inside the Census boundary (build-walklist-co.mjs --pieces for the statewide layer; build-walklist.mjs for El Paso, whose statewide rows carry no owners, then clipped), minus every house that is already a door or was pulled by an earlier target (group1/clip-group1.mjs). Resumable (<key>.done), status in ~/run-status.txt, log ~/run-group1.log. Ends at "door lists ready".
- **Stage B (after a look at the counts):** register each target as a town (build-rounds-data SOURCES, centres, groups, county in assessor/town-county.json and the survey in sample-trees), then the usual town steps: walk-list areas, yard v2 and trees on the cloud machine, values, year built where the county files allow (Adams, Boulder, Denver, Weld, Douglas, El Paso; not Arapahoe or Jefferson), the editions' layers (roofs, solar, census, FEMA, pest, others), rebuild, then upload in batches to Sixfold and every edition (backups, md5, index.json merged).
- Rules: one parcel-heavy job at a time; disk checked before every target (stop under 5 GB); caches written as they go.
