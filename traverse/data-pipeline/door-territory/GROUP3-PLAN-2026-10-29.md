## Where each county's data comes from

Checked on the cloud machine on 29 Oct with group3/coverage-g3.mjs (results in group3/coverage.json; decisions in group3/county-sources.json): for each county, the statewide parcel layer's row count, a 1,000-row sample, a sample in a 0.75-mile box round its biggest place, and the state's address points in the same box joined by parcel number.

- **20 counties are not on the statewide parcel layer at all** (no owners, so no owner-lives-here test). Eight of them have the state's address points but nothing to say who owns the house.
- **co (26 counties):** the statewide layer carries owner, mailing address and property address. build-walklist-co.mjs, exactly as Groups 1 and 2.
- **ap (2 counties, Eagle and Custer):** the parcel rows have no property address (0-2%), so it comes from the address points, joined by parcel number: group3/build-walklist-ap.mjs, Pueblo's adapter made general (any county; parcel numbers compared without punctuation or leading zeros; a parcel row's own property address used when it has one and no point; land use dropped when it reads as non-residential; organisation owners dropped).
- **Owner names withheld:** Eagle puts one placeholder in place of every owner name and Summit leaves the name blank (mailing addresses are real in both). Doors in these two counties will carry no owner name. The owner-lives-here test does not need the name. build-walklist-ap.mjs spots a placeholder (one value on over half the parcels) and leaves the owner blank.
- **Partial counties:** property address on only part of the rows, so expect a part of the houses: Gilpin (49% in the county sample, 5% round Central City), Yuma (11% county-wide, 87% in Yuma town), Morgan (57%), Huerfano (61-68%), Teller (48% round Cripple Creek), Clear Creek (40% county-wide, 83% in Idaho Springs); Custer's address points join about a third of parcels.

## Stage B rules (group3/prep-stageb.mjs)

1. **Local PO box = lives here.** A "no — post" row whose owner mails to a PO box in the house's own ZIP or town becomes "YES" (why: "owner's PO box is in town"). This is the same test as the local_po_box count (summary-g3.mjs `localPoBox`).
2. **No owner name:** Eagle (one placeholder on every row) and Summit (blank) rows stay, with owner blank and `limited` = "no-owner-name". Their owner-lives-here test used the mailing address, which both counties give.
3. **Group 3b rows** keep their own mark: `no-owner` (address point only) or `no-occupancy` (an owner on record, no mailing address). They are "YES" rows.
4. **Ours already:** a row whose address id is a door of ours within 150 m is dropped (build-rounds-data's own same-house rule), so the build never hands a live door, and its marks, to a new town.
5. **Merges, not second towns:** g3-cripple-creek goes to cripple-creek and g3-idaho-springs to idaho-springs. Rows from the Teller sweep inside Woodland Park city go to woodland-park, inside Florissant CDP to florissant, and inside Divide CDP to divide (`group3/merge-places.json`, Census shapes). The rest of the sweep is its own town, "Teller (rural)". `group3/merge-into-town.mjs` appends merged rows to the town as new neighbourhoods. Existing neighbourhoods and door ids are untouched, a backup is made first, and the same address within 150 m is skipped.
6. A target with no "YES" row left is not registered.

## Group 3b: the skipped counties with address points (11 counties, 61 targets, 51,256 Census houses)

The state's address-point layer (Colorado_Public_Addresses) has every addressed building in these counties, but no owner and no parcel number. `group3/build-walklist-pts.mjs` pulls a whole county at once (Montrose, the biggest, is 25,146 points in 13 pages). It keeps a point as a home unless one of these applies:

- **type:** Place_Type names something else (COMMERCIAL, GOVERNMENT, UTILITY, CHURCH, EXEMPT, MILE MARKER ...). Only Delta and Montrose fill this field in. Delta: 16,749 RESIDENTIAL, 2,123 COMMERCIAL. Montrose: mostly blank.
- **unit:** it has a unit or building number. Montrose has 5,149 with a unit, Delta 2,678.
- **several:** the same address appears more than 15 m apart, or different addresses are stacked within 2 m.
- **no homes:** its **2020 Census block has no housing units** (TIGERweb tigerWMS_Census2020 layer 10, HU100). This takes out business districts, industry, parks and fields.
- **big:** it sits in or within 25 m of a **building outline** over 1,000 m² (Microsoft footprints, as yard v2 and roofs use).
- **shared:** two kept addresses sit on one outline (duplexes, a shop with a flat). Both are dropped.
- **no building:** there is no outline within 150 m. Rural points sit at the driveway, hence the wide radius.
- **where parcels exist** (Saguache, Washington, Sedgwick): the point is joined to the parcel it falls in. Lot over 10 acres, non-residential land use or an organisation owner drops it. The owner-lives-here test runs where the owner and mailing address are on record.

## For the app: the "Limited data" doors (Matthew: "just make that note in the app")

Stage B writes `b/limited-<town>.json` ({ address: reason }) for every door with a gap. What each kind lacks:

- **no-owner** (Group 3b address-point counties: Montrose, Delta, Las Animas, Alamosa, Conejos, Costilla, Bent, Mineral, plus Washington): **owner name blank** (door `owner` = ""). **No mailing address**, so no postcard or mail list. **No owner-lives-here test**: renters, second homes and absentee-owned houses are all in. **Lot size 0 / unknown** (`acres` 0), except Washington where a parcel was found. **No parcel number**, so nothing keyed by parcel ("Pull this property", owner history). **No appraised value, year built or county roof material.** Suggested label: "Limited data: no owner on record".
- **no-occupancy** (Sedgwick): owner name present, no mailing address, so whether the owner lives there is unknown. Lot size present. Suggested label: "Limited data: occupancy unknown".
- **no-owner-name** (Eagle, Summit, and any blank-owner row): owner name blank. Occupancy was tested on the mailing address, and lot size and parcel are present. Suggested label: "Owner name withheld by the county".
- What these doors still get, because it comes from location: lawn size (yard v2), trees (canopy map), building outline / roof size where the footprint exists, census block group, wildfire, FEMA, storm history.
- The fields people could fill in at the door: owner name, phone, occupancy (owner / renter / second home), lot size.

## What "doors" means, and what to expect

Stage A writes every house in the lot band (0-10 acres) that has a property address. Each row says whether the owner lives there ("YES") or should get a postcard ("no — post"). Town files keep only the "YES" rows, so the number to compare is the **owner-lives-here count**.

**Smoke test on the cloud machine, 29 Oct** (group3/test, not in out/):

| Test | Method | Census houses | Owner-occupied | Rows | Owner lives here | Local PO box |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

**The PO-box finding.** In Ignacio 199 of 304 owners mail to a PO box, 186 of them in Ignacio's own ZIP or town: a small town without home delivery. The owner-lives-here rule since Group 1 says a PO box is "no — post", so those houses drop out. Mountain and farm towns across Group 3 will do the same (Summit's parcel rows are 57% PO boxes). summary.tsv therefore has a **local_po_box** column: rows whose owner's PO box is in the house's own ZIP or town. Whether those count as "lives here" is Matthew's call at stage B. The CSVs hold everything needed, so no second pull.

**Expected owner-lives-here doors:** about **50,000-95,000** under today's rule (40-75% of the 131,900 owner-occupied houses: the high end is Group 2's rate, the low end allows for PO-box towns and the partial counties). Counting local PO boxes as well could add a large share of what is left. These are estimates; the real counts are in ~/run/group3/out/summary.tsv.

## The build list (172 targets, in run order)

A place that spans two counties is pulled in the county holding most of it. Its part in the other county comes in through that county's sweep. Places under 150 houses come in through their subdivision's sweep. No military housing in these counties.

## How it runs

- **Stage A, door lists (running on the cloud machine since 29 Oct 16:19Z):** group3/run-group3.sh in ~/run/group3, one target at a time in the order above. co counties use build-walklist-co.mjs --pieces (the Group 2 copy: 6 tries per page at 3 s steps); Eagle and Custer use build-walklist-ap.mjs. Each pull is clipped by clip-group3.mjs (Group 2's clip, unchanged: same house = same parcel number; our doors within 5 m). Resumable (<key>.done). It refuses to start while another parcel job runs and stops under 5 GB free. One parcel-heavy job at a time on gis.colorado.gov: nothing else pulls parcels while it runs.
- **Check progress:** on the cloud machine, `cat ~/run-status.txt` (lines start "group3 A:"; "pull <key> (n/172 ...)"), the log `~/run-group3.log` (one "new doors, owner lives here" line per target), and `ls ~/run/group3/*.done | wc -l`. **Done** when the status reads "group3 A: door lists ready" and ~/run/group3/g3a.vmdone exists; counts in ~/run/group3/out/summary.tsv (key, county, method, census, owner_occupied, rows, owner_lives_here, local_po_box). If it ends "... targets MISSING", look at raw/<key>.log for those keys and re-run the script: finished targets are skipped.
- **Owner names stay on the cloud machine** in ~/run/group3/raw and out. They are not copied to the laptop in stage A and are never committed (group3/raw, out, test and *.csv are gitignored).
- **After stage A (queued 29 Oct, `~/run/group3/queue-after-g3a.sh`, log also ~/run-group3.log):** the Teller sweep (status lines "group3 A: pull g3-divide-rural (173/173 ...)", then "door lists ready" again), then Group 3b (status lines "group3b A: ..."; done = "group3b A: door lists ready" + ~/run/group3/g3b.vmdone; counts in out/summary-3b.tsv). Check with `pgrep -af "queue-after-g3a|run-group3"` and `tail ~/run-group3.log`.
- **Stage B:** decided 29 Oct (local PO box = lives here; Eagle and Summit included; see "Stage B rules" and "Stage B order" above). The original note, kept for the steps: register each target as a town (build-rounds-data SOURCES, centres, groups, county in assessor/town-county.json, survey in sample-trees), merge g3-cripple-creek and g3-idaho-springs into our existing towns, walk-list areas, yard v2 and trees on the cloud machine, values and year built where the county allows (to be checked), the editions' layers, rebuild, upload in batches with backups, md5 and index.json merged. The app's COUNTY_NAME has none of these counties yet, so the towns would fold under "Other towns" until it does.
