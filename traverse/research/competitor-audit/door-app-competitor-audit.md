# Door App Competitor Audit

**For:** Brandon, for the team meeting · **Prepared:** 3 October 2026 · **Status:** for discussion

## The short version

- Nothing on the market puts **fire hazard, trees, lawn size and wildfire funding on the same door** the way our app does. The door-knocking apps sell homeowner contact data and storm maps; the measuring tools are desk tools for quoting, not knocking.
- The others are ahead on **homeowner phone numbers and emails**, **manager tools** (territories per rep, leaderboards, contracts) and **precise measuring for bids**.
- We are taking the best of what works for them. All seven are built and go live with the next update. The leaderboard shows to admins only while it is tested, and the booking-rate sort switches on once the team has 150 conversations logged.

## What our app is

Built in-house and running on the Sixfold website. Every door in 11 towns (30,464 doors) carries:

- the wildfire hazard rating and how close trees are to the house;
- the lawn size, with the aerial photo it was measured from;
- the county's appraised value (Douglas and Teller);
- the Teller County wildfire plan's rating where the community is named (62 neighbourhoods).

At the door the crew sees the tax credit, grant and insurance rules, logs outcomes (with no signal if need be), notes extra work (gutters, branches over the roof, irrigation), and books quotes. Admins see Quotes to build, won and lost, a Results screen showing which doors turn into jobs, Communities (HOAs and Firewise sites ranked by high-risk doors), fire-weather alerts, walking routes, and a Jobber export. No per-person fees; the data comes from free public sources.

## Where we're ahead

1. **Fire, lawn and trees on every door.** None of the door-knocking apps checked has wildfire hazard, trees near the house or lawn size.
2. **Funding at the door.** Tax credit, county grants and the 2025 insurance law, with what the crew may and may not say. Not found elsewhere.
3. **Communities.** HOAs, Firewise sites and county plan ratings grouped to pitch a whole community as one job.
4. **Our own conversion data.** SalesRabbit charges for national "buyer propensity" scores. Our Results screen learns from our own doors.
5. **Every figure has a source and a date.** The lawn photo shows exactly what was counted.
6. **Clean data.** No scraped contact lists.

## Where they're ahead

1. **Homeowner contact data**, licensed, with texting compliance handled (GlassHouse, SalesRabbit, HailTrace).
2. **Manager tools:** territories per rep, leaderboards, commission calculators, live rep tracking, contracts in the app.
3. **Precise measuring for bids:** turf, beds and hardscape measured separately, done-for-you in about 90 minutes (SiteRecon, Beam AI).
4. **"Your customers were hit" alerts** (HailTrace).
5. **Marketing around existing jobs** (GlassHouse).
6. **On-site detail:** Firemaps maps a house by drone to the centimetre and works with cities.
7. **Native phone apps.** Ours is a web app; added to the iPad home screen it behaves much the same.

## For Brandon to decide

1. **Licensed homeowner contact data.** Worth buying, or not? If yes, only through a licensed provider and only for doors we've already spoken to.
2. **Do Not Call registration.** The federal list is free for our area codes (719, 303, 720), and Colorado's list is free for businesses under five employees. Registration is still required to download them. **Whether Sixfold's follow-up calls also need the Colorado Attorney General's telemarketer registration is a question for the attorney.**
3. **Jobber.** The export is ready if we adopt it; nothing changes if we don't.
4. **Leaderboards.** Approved to try (3 October). Built for admins only while it is tested, without live location tracking. When should the crew see it?
