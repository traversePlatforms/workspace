# Traverse pricing — corrected (2026-10-10)

Corrected version of the "Bottom line" pricing doc Matthew shared on 2026-10-10. Same recommendation, with the
coverage figure fixed, the competitor prices checked against their own pages, two plan prices reworked and the
licensing note corrected. Fact-check log at the end. Internal: this names competitors and data sources, which the
public site never does.

## Bottom line

Price Traverse Pro at **$69 per user per month (yearly) or $89 month to month**, one trade edition included. That
is about 20% under a loaded SalesRabbit seat paid yearly ($87) and about 35% under it month to month ($137), and
it carries per-house data SalesRabbit doesn't sell.

- **Crew** (the app, no edition data): $35 yearly, $45 monthly, per user. Mid-market: between RepCard and Knockio
  ($20–$24 entry) and SalesRabbit ($49–$59).
- **Duo**: $99 a month flat for one- and two-person crews ($129 monthly), one edition included. Small lawn and fire
  crews are the easiest first buyers.
- **Company**: custom, for more than one trade or a CRM hand-off — priced as Pro seats plus what's added, so it is
  never dearer than Pro for the same team.
- **Launch**: 30% off Pro for founding companies for their first year, no contracts, and a free 14-day pilot.

## What Traverse sells

A door-to-door and phone sales app with five trade editions, each adding property data for its trade. It sits in
two markets at once: canvassing apps and property-data lead tools.

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| Doors | Every house in street order, one-tap outcomes, come-back dates |
| Phones | Call and text assigned leads (through the phone's own dialer and messages), follow-ups on the calendar |
| Appointments | Book estimates at the door, send confirmations |
| Pipeline | Leads from doors, web and ads; won jobs become customers |
| Teams and training | Crew, manager and admin roles; custom sales lessons |
| Field use | Glove-size buttons, offline mode, nightly backups |
| TraverseRoofing | Storm lists, hail and wind by house, roof size and pitch measured from laser scans, re-roof permits, roof-job windows, JobNimbus hand-off |
| TraverseYards | Lawn size, trees near the house, wildfire risk, frost and rain by town |
| TraverseSolar | Sun-facing roof area, shade, yearly output, power company, solar permits |
| TraversePest | New owners, neighbours on service, nearby water and open space |
| TraverseSecurity | Area crime figures, new and absentee owners |

**Coverage today: 364,277 homes in 28 Colorado towns** (Roofing 253,634 in 23 towns; Security 237,282; Solar
216,923; Pest 119,698; Yards 82,271). Against about 2.96 million Colorado parcels that is roughly 12%, concentrated
on the Front Range where most people live.

## Where Traverse fits: one app replacing two bills

Price against what a contractor would otherwise stack: a canvassing app plus a data tool. For roofing that is about
$87 per rep per month on SalesRabbit plus a hail-map subscription. Undercut the combined bill while matching the
app features.

The edge is per-house detail the canvassing apps don't carry: roof size and pitch measured from laser scans, re-roof
and solar permit dates, trees and wildfire risk at each house, sun figures. SalesRabbit does sell weather and hail
data as an add-on, so "data they don't have" should name these, not hail.

**Add-ons**
- Extra edition on Pro: +$20 per user a month.
- **A new metro: a one-time setup fee** (not the $99 a month first proposed). Building a metro means measuring roofs
  and trees from the laser scans, requesting permits and checking the data — hours of computer time and some of
  Matthew's. Set the fee once a few setups have been timed; waive it above a seat count if it helps close.
- Homeowner phone and email look-ups: at cost plus a margin, about $0.15 each.

Sales terms that stay off the public page: a 3-user minimum on Pro, one metro per Duo plan.

## Launch plan

1. Founding companies: 30% off Pro ($48 a user) for their first year, for feedback and a testimonial.
2. Free 14-day pilot, scored on the customer's own territory where it is covered.
3. Month to month, no setup fee: the direct contrast with SalesRabbit's contracts and implementation fee.
4. Start with yards, fire mitigation and roofing on the Front Range, where the data exists.
5. Move to list price after 10 paying companies or 90 days, whichever comes first.

## Risks to settle before charging

- **Ownership**: a written note that Traverse is Matthew's. Buyers and investors will ask.
- **Data terms**: nearly every source is public record or public domain (NOAA and Iowa Mesonet storm reports, USGS
  laser scans, USDA and State of Colorado aerial photos, Census, city and county permit records). The building
  outlines are Microsoft's Global ML Building Footprints under CDLA Permissive 2.0: anything computed from them is
  unrestricted; sharing the outline data itself means including the licence text; no use of Microsoft's name or
  logo. County assessor terms on reusing owner records differ by county — check each.
- **Coverage**: sell by metro; the Front Range is covered (see above).
- **Cost per user**: small. Hosting is a flat monthly bill and the data is almost all free; 70% gross margin is easy
  at $69. The real costs are new-metro setup and support time.

**Open question:** what do five local crew owners pay today for canvassing and data combined? That settles these
numbers better than more research.
