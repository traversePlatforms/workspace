## Why

Colorado Springs police publish every property-crime report, with its block location, as open data. That is why Colorado Springs doors carry counts within 1 mile.

The agencies policing our other towns don't publish report-level data that we found. Castle Rock PD and the El Paso County Sheriff show theirs only on LexisNexis Community Crime Map, which has no download. So those doors show no crime counts, and the town screen shows the FBI's town-wide totals instead.

A records request asks each agency for the same kind of list Colorado Springs already publishes.

## Fact-check log

**Verified:**
- The Colorado Criminal Justice Records Act is C.R.S. 24-72-301 et seq. ([Colorado Secretary of State, Title 24 Article 72 Part 3](https://coloradosos.gov/pubs/info_center/laws/Title24/Title24Article72Part3.html)).
- Records of official actions must be open to inspection. Other criminal justice records may be opened at the custodian's discretion. Requests go in writing to the agency that holds the records ([CBI, CCJRA public records requests](https://cbi.colorado.gov/about/colorado-criminal-justice-records-act-ccjra/public-records-requests)).
- Colorado Springs police publish property-crime reports with block points: dataset `bc88-hemr` on policedata.coloradosprings.gov, used by the app's build and its crime map (checked live 2026-10-06, 1,666 reports in 90 days).
- Castle Rock PD and the El Paso County Sheriff show reports only on LexisNexis Community Crime Map, with no download (checked 2026-10-06 during the security build).
