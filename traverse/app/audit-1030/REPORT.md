## B. Live radar on a tablet

**Does it work?** Yes, on all four sizes in every edition and Sixfold: the Live radar tile opens the map, the switch, the time ("as of 3:50 pm") and Play last hour all respond, no console errors from the radar itself. Layout is fine at 820x1180 and 1180x820: map, switch and colour key all visible, nothing clipped (`shots/roofing-admin-tabP-live-radar.jpg`, `roofing-admin-tabL-live-radar.jpg`).

**Why it looks pixelated.** Three things stack:

1. **The source is a coarse grid.** The app uses the Iowa Environmental Mesonet (IEM) national composite `nexrad-n0q-900913` (`index.html` `RADAR_BASE` / `RADAR_LAYER`, ~19252). IEM builds it every 5 minutes as one 12,200-pixel-wide image of the lower 48 (0.005 degrees a pixel: about 430 m east-west by 550 m north-south in Colorado), from the N0B product. One radar "pixel" is a third of a mile.
2. **The app asks for it at zoom 8 and stretches it.** `RADAR_NATIVE = 8` is passed to Leaflet as `maxNativeZoom`, so past zoom 8 Leaflet keeps the zoom-8 tile and scales it up in the browser: 16x at zoom 12, 32x at 13, 128x at 15 (the Doors map). The browser smooths the stretch, so each radar cell becomes a soft-edged block (panel A in the pictures).
3. **A tablet doubles it again.** An iPad has 2 device pixels per CSS pixel and the radar layer does not ask for retina tiles, so each stretched block is twice as large in real pixels as on a PC.

Already ruled out earlier and not re-checked: RainViewer (personal/educational use only, max zoom 7) and Open-Meteo's free tier (non-commercial).

**Recommendation: E, the NWS MRMS composite from opengeo.ncep.noaa.gov, smoothed.** It is the only free source that stops looking like pixels at street zoom: GeoServer interpolates the 1 km grid into smooth bands before sending it, so nothing is stretched in the browser, and 512 px retina tiles stay sharp on an iPad. It is quality-controlled (no ground clutter or clear-air speckle like D), covers every radar seamlessly (no picking KFTG vs KPUX), updates about twice as often as IEM, and its 2-hour time list gives the one-hour loop directly. Honest limit: it is *smoother*, not more detailed; the data is 1 km against IEM's 0.5 km. If Matthew wants street-level detail on the Doors map later, D (single-radar super-res) from zoom 13 is the add-on.

**Licence line:** NOAA/NWS data, public domain ("may be used without charge for any lawful purpose", weather.gov/disclaimer); opengeo GetCapabilities: Fees none, AccessConstraints none. Credit "Radar: NOAA NWS MRMS". Do not imply NWS endorsement. CORS is open (`Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *` on GetCapabilities, checked).

**Effort to switch** (not done; app code untouched): inside `radarEngine()` only. Replace the `L.tileLayer(RADAR_BASE + …)` with `L.tileLayer.wms('https://opengeo.ncep.noaa.gov/geoserver/conus/conus_bref_qcd/ows', { layers: 'conus_bref_qcd', format: 'image/png', transparent: true, version: '1.3.0', tileSize: 512, zoomOffset: -1, detectRetina: true, interpolations: 'bicubic', opacity: 0.6 })`; "as of" and Play read the `time` dimension from the layer's GetCapabilities (8 KB XML) instead of IEM's `n0q_0.json` and the m05m-m55m layers, passing `time=` per frame (checked: a past frame returns a different image); the colour key `RADAR_KEY` changes to the NWS palette (light rain is blue/green there, not IEM's green/yellow). Measured: about 1 s a 512 px tile, 5-60 KB each. Half a day with testing.

Recommendation: take the map off the dashboard and put a "Plan the properties ›" link in its place; keep the KPIs, team and walks. If Matthew wants a picture there, the "Show map" button from fix item 6 is the minimum, and it should then draw one town (the one with the most left) or cluster at wide zooms, never 49,662 circles.
