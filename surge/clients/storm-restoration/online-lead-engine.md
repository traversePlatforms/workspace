# Online Lead Engine — Build Sheet

**Prepared:** 2026-08-18
**Companion to:** [storm-restoration-platform-sheet.md](storm-restoration-platform-sheet.md), which settles what is allowed and who owns each account. This sheet is the other half — **how a lead actually arrives and becomes a booked inspection**, online only.
**Scope:** No doors, no bought lists, no walk-ins. Every lead in this sheet originates on a screen. The field engine still converts it.
**Status:** Plan. Nothing built, spent, published or scheduled.

> **RESTRICTED — INTERNAL, CONTEXT ONLY.** This sheet names the client, and §1 contains the commission structure, which is **never publishable in any register** — not the splits, not the stages, not the income motive. Client identity is Tier 4: sector only. Nothing here goes into a draft, a caption, alt text, a filename or a payload.

---

## The thesis

**Online-only does not change what converts. It changes where the lead starts, and it moves the bottleneck onto two things nobody currently measures or staffs.**

The field engine already works: a rep inspects, same day, free, and the client closes and repairs. Take away the door and the phone list, and everything downstream of the inspection is unchanged. What changes is the top: a lead now arrives as a form submission from someone who has never met anyone from Surge, whose interest decays by the hour, and who is simultaneously being called by three other contractors.

That makes two numbers decisive — **cost per booked inspection**, and **median minutes to first call.** Neither is tracked today. Everything in this sheet exists to produce them.

---

## 1 — What an online lead is here, and what each stage is worth

**A lead is not a form fill.** A lead is a property owner with plausible storm damage who has a free inspection on the calendar. Anything short of that is a phone number.

That distinction is not pedantry, and the reason is economic.

> **INTERNAL — never publishable, in any register.** The commission splits into three equal parts by pipeline stage — **lead, inspection, close — one third each.**
>
> - Generate the lead alone: **one third**.
> - Generate it *and* inspect it: **two thirds.**
>
> That is a 100% increase in the take, for work Surge already performs, same day, for free. Which sets the whole strategy: **the metric is never cost per lead.** It is cost per *booked inspection*, because the inspection is where the second third lives.
>
> It also reframes every cold form fill. A lead that goes unanswered does not waste one third — it wastes two. Pay structure, splits and the income motive stay out of drafts, site copy, flyers and captions entirely. This is prioritisation context and nothing else.

**The practical consequence:** an online channel that produces 100 form fills and 8 inspections is worse than one producing 30 form fills and 15 inspections, at any price. Optimise the ads, the form and the follow-up for booked inspections, and accept lower raw volume as a feature.

## 2 — Stage 0: the plumbing, before a dollar of spend

**Five prerequisites, not a sequence.** None costs money, all are load-bearing, and the order barely matters — what matters is that spending before they exist buys leads that leak out of a system which cannot hold them.

- **A destination that can take a booking.** Not a phone number. Visible **same-day slot**, address, damage type, optional photo. The homeowner's question is *when can someone look at it*.
- **Routing with a named owner and a clock.** Who receives it, on which device, within how many minutes, and what happens if they don't. A rota, not a piece of software.
- **The consent line.** One paragraph. Gate 3 of the platform sheet, and it blocks every paid channel until it is written.
- **Tracking that survives the handoff.** Source on every lead, carried through to booked, inspected and closed. Also the only proof of which leads were Surge's.
- **A written follow-up sequence.** Text within minutes, call, second call next day, one more at day four, then stop. Written down, not remembered.

> **Do not spend until the destination, the routing, the consent line and the tracking exist.** The platform sheet's gates are legal ones. These are operational, and skipping them is how a campaign produces leads, no revenue, and no explanation of why.

## 3 — Tier 1: free, and available now

**Gate:** the brand decision from the platform sheet. Nothing else.

### The client's Google Business Profile

The highest-value free asset in the entire campaign, and it is theirs — Surge requests manager access. Once in: primary category, service area, photos, seeded Q&A, and above all **reviews**.

**Reviews are the conversion asset for every other channel on this sheet.** A paid click lands on a company name, and the homeowner immediately searches that name before calling. Every dollar of Tier 2 is priced by what they find.

### The review engine

Ask at inspection completion, on site, phone in hand, **one platform named**. Google first, every time — asking for two at once reliably produces neither. This is the only item in Tier 1 that compounds; everything else is a channel, this is an asset.

### Neighbourhood surfaces after a storm

Nextdoor and local Facebook groups, same-day, information-first register: what the storm did, what a claim deadline looks like, free inspection available. Geographically identical to the swath, and the fastest-moving surface there is. Full cautions in the platform sheet — the register rule is not optional there.

### Storm-event pages

One page per named hail or wind event: the date, the counties hit, what it did, what a claim looks like, how to get looked at. Free, searchable, factual — NOAA's public storm reports supply the specifics — and it is the only organic asset that captures the exact moment of intent. Build one per event and they accumulate.

### Organic social on the existing accounts

Inspection footage on the accounts Surge already has. **Not a lead channel.** A credibility channel, which makes every paid channel cheaper by improving what the homeowner finds when they check.

## 4 — Tier 2: paid, capped, and testable

**Gate:** consent line written, tracking live, someone on the clock to answer, and credentials on the client's side.

### Meta lead ads — start here

The best structural fit, because the form can be qualified before it ever reaches a human.

- **Use the higher-intent instant form, not the volume one.** Meta offers instant form types including more volume, higher intent, and rich creative; the higher-intent version adds a review step so the person confirms their details before submitting.
- **Add qualifying questions, with conditional answers** where the wrong answer routes out. Meta supports conditional questions on instant forms.
- **Fewer and better is the goal, not cheaper.** The call room has a finite number of dials in an hour. Every dial spent on an unqualified form is a qualified lead going cold somewhere else.
- **Geo to the swath, not the metro.** The damage has a shape; the targeting should have the same shape.
- **The creative is the inspection, not the roof.** Same-day, at the door, free — the three things no competitor is advertising.
- **Real-time delivery to the person who calls.** Not a CSV somebody downloads on Monday. A Monday CSV is a bin.

### Google Search

Highest intent, highest cost, and **negative keywords matter more than keywords** — strip DIY, jobs, salary, "how to", training, and insurance-advice traffic before it spends anything. Landing page must be the licensed party's. Every headline runs through gate 2 of the platform sheet.

### Local Services Ads

The client's account, their credentials, three-to-four week screening. Start it out of season or not at all — it does not fit inside a hail response.

> **One channel at a time, capped, for one storm cycle.** Two at once means neither gets diagnosed, and the whole point of Tier 2 is to produce a cost per booked inspection that can be trusted.

## 5 — Tier 3: compounding

- **Retargeting** — once there is enough traffic to retarget and a page worth returning to.
- **Spanish-language campaigns** — the client's site is already localised and Denver metro has the audience. Needs a Spanish-speaking rep on the call or it produces leads nobody can close.
- **Inspection walkthrough video** — search-durable, unlike feed content. Depends on banked, clearance-checked footage.
- **Email and SMS to the non-converted**, within the consent that was captured and not beyond it.
- **The client's existing referral programme** — per-user referral links with configurable payouts, currently marked inactive on their site. Ask before building a second mechanism.

## 6 — Speed to lead: the operational rule

The evidence here is real but heavily distorted in circulation, so both halves matter.

**What the research says.** *The Short Life of Online Sales Leads*, Harvard Business Review, March 2011 — Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington audited 2,241 US companies with test web leads. 23% never responded at all, and the average response time among those who did was 42 hours. Firms that attempted contact **within an hour** were nearly **7 times** as likely to qualify the lead as those attempting an hour later, and more than **60 times** as likely as those waiting 24 hours or more.

**What it does not say.** The widely-quoted "call within 5 minutes and you are 21 times more likely to convert" is **not** from HBR. It comes from a separate vendor study by the company one of the authors ran, and it is repeated everywhere without that attribution. Not used here.

**The rule for Surge:** *someone attempts contact inside the hour, every hour the ads are running.* Which forces a decision rather than a preference — either the ads only run while the room is staffed, or someone carries the phone outside those hours. Both are real choices with real costs. Neither is free, and pretending the question does not exist is what produces the 42-hour average.

> **This is the cheapest competitive advantage available in the whole sheet.** Every competitor buys the same clicks in the same swath at the same price. The one who calls first books the inspection — and the inspection is the second third.

## 7 — The funnel you hold

**There are no benchmark numbers in this section, deliberately.** No credible cost-per-lead or conversion figure exists for this campaign, in this market, at this moment — and every figure published by an agency in this trade is either from a different market or invented outright. What follows is the *shape* to fill in, not the numbers to expect.

| Stage | Record | Why it exists |
|---|---|---|
| Clicks and impressions | spend, source, geo | The cost floor, nothing more |
| Form fills | source, timestamp | Raw volume — the vanity number |
| Contacted | **minutes to first attempt** | The only lever entirely inside Surge's control |
| **Booked inspections** | source | **The real denominator** |
| Inspected | same-day yes/no | Whether the promise in the ad was kept |
| Closed by the client | source | The number that actually pays |

**Two numbers run the whole thing:**

- **Cost per booked inspection**, by source.
- **Median minutes to first contact attempt.**

Everything else on that table is diagnostics for when one of those two moves in the wrong direction.

## 8 — Build order

1. **Answer gate 1 of the platform sheet** — whose brand runs the ads. Nothing below survives a different answer.
2. **Write the consent line.** One paragraph; it unblocks every paid channel.
3. **Build the destination page** with a visible same-day slot and a real booking action.
4. **Write the routing rota** — named owner, device, response window, escalation.
5. **Stand up tracking** from source through to booked and closed, and confirm it survives the handoff to the client.
6. **Write the follow-up sequence** and the review-ask script. One platform named in the ask.
7. **Request manager access** to the client's Business Profile; fix category, service area and photos.
8. **Start the review engine on the next inspection**, before any ad spend — it prices everything that follows.
9. **Publish the first storm-event page** off NOAA data for the most recent event.
10. **Run one capped Meta lead-ad test**, higher-intent form, geo to the swath, real-time delivery.
11. **Hold it for one storm cycle** and produce the two numbers. Change nothing mid-test.
12. **Then, and only then, add the second channel.**

## 9 — What kills this in this trade

- **Buying volume the room cannot answer.** The leads do not wait, and the unanswered ones cost two thirds, not one.
- **Measuring cost per lead.** It systematically rewards the cheapest and worst leads, and it will make the wrong channel look like the winner.
- **Running ads outside staffed hours.** A form submitted at 9pm and called at 10am the next day is, on the HBR numbers, a different business.
- **Letting the landing page make insurance promises.** Straight into gate 2 of the platform sheet, and the penalty lands on the estimate for every job the ad produced.
- **Routing that lives in one person's head.** It works until that person is on a roof.
- **Starting two channels at once.** Guarantees neither can be diagnosed.
- **Treating the form fill as the deliverable.** It is the raw material. The booked inspection is the deliverable.
