## The post

> Wind and hail are the largest single cause of homeowners insurance claims in the US. Across 2018 to 2022, 2.8 of every 100 insured homes filed one in an average year — more than water damage, fire and theft put together. That is Triple-I's figure, calculated from ISO data.
>
> Here is the part that surprises people who take a storm restoration sales role: the roof is one line item.
>
> A storm claim can run through siding, gutters and downspouts, windows and skylights, HVAC units sitting out in the open, and interior water damage that shows up weeks later. The commercial building across the street took the same storm the houses did.
>
> So the work is not roofing sales. It is finding damaged property, knocking, calling, following up, and running the free inspection that tells an owner what is actually up there. The repairs and the insurance side belong to the restoration company we sell for.

> We are hiring field reps for that work out of our Englewood office.

## Alternate hooks

1. *"Wind and hail are the largest single cause of homeowners insurance claims in the US."* — used. Leads with the checkable number.
2. *"2.8 out of every 100 insured homes filed a wind or hail claim last year. Somebody knocked on some of those doors."* — more vivid, but "somebody knocked" implies Surge reached them, which is not in the data.
3. *"The roof is one line item."* — best line in the post, but as an opener it has nothing to hang on.
4. *"Most people apply to this thinking it is roofing sales."* — cut. It characterises applicants, and it edges toward the misleading-job-ad criticism the company gets hit with.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"The average wind and hail claim ran $13,511 over that period."* — verified (Triple-I / ISO, 2018–2022) and cut anyway. A claim-size figure next to a commission role is an atmospheric income implication; `brand/notes/hiring.md` bars those on recruiting-facing content.
- *"Uncapped territory."* — same reason, less defensible.
- *"Storms are getting worse."* — NOAA logged 5,432 hail events in 2025 against 5,373 in 2024, which is not a trend, it is one year. Left out under the research standard.
- *"Nobody else trains reps to look past the roof."* — unverifiable and a uniqueness claim. Company spec §1.
- *"Colorado gets hammered every spring."* — true and cut. It reads as a service-area statement, and Englewood runs campaigns for out-of-state clients.
