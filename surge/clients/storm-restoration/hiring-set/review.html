01

Storm damage is not just the roof

Instagram + Facebook · tutorial carousel, monocolor
Wind and hail are the largest single cause of homeowners insurance claims in the US. Across 2018 to 2022, 2.8 of every 100 insured homes filed one in an average year — more than water damage, fire and theft put together. That is Triple-I's figure, calculated from ISO data. Here is the part that surprises people who take a storm restoration sales role: the roof is one line item. A storm claim can run through siding, gutters and downspouts, windows and skylights, HVAC units sitting out in the open, and interior water damage that shows up weeks later. The commercial building across the street took the same storm the houses did. So the work is not roofing sales. It is finding damaged property, knocking, calling, following up, and running the free inspection that tells an owner what is actually up there. The repairs and the insurance side belong to the restoration company we sell for. We are hiring field reps for that work out of our Englewood office. What did you assume this job was before you read that?

02

Small hail still costs you the roof

LinkedIn · tutorial carousel, flat style
A hailstorm that breaks nothing can still cost someone a roof. IBHS published a peer-reviewed study in Frontiers in Materials in September 2025. They froze hailstones under an inch across — sub-severe, the size nobody files on — fired them at asphalt shingles with a hail cannon, left the shingles outside to weather for a year, then hit them with two-inch stones. The shingles that had taken the small stuff were roughly ten times more susceptible to damage than the ones that had not. The authors' conclusion: frequent sub-severe hailstorms may pose a greater threat to roof longevity than previously recognised, and are a driving factor in growing US hail risk. Our read: that changes a doorstep conversation. The most common thing a storm restoration rep hears is "it was only small hail." Until last year the honest answer to that was a shrug. Now it is a citation. Which is the part of this role people do not expect. The research moves, and the person at the door has to move with it. What is a finding that changed the way you sell?

03

"Free inspection" is a documented procedure

LinkedIn · whiteboard infographic
"Free inspection" sounds like a marketing line. In this industry it is a documented procedure. The standard is Haag's test square method, pioneered by their engineers in the 1960s and now used by contractors, adjusters and engineers worldwide. It runs like this. Chalk a 10ft by 10ft square on each directional slope of the roof, staying clear of overhanging trees and the paths people walk. Inspect every shingle inside it. Record the marks and separate them by type, because hail bruising is not wear and wear is not mechanical damage. Then multiply the damaged shingles in that square by the number of roofing squares on the slope. Each slope gets its own square. Wind-driven hail does not hit every direction equally. Our read: this is the part of a field sales role nobody puts in the advert. There is a craft underneath it, it is teachable, and a rep who has it is holding something the homeowner cannot get from the sidewalk. We are hiring reps for storm restoration work out of our Englewood office. The inspection is free and usually same day. What is the most technical thing you have had to learn for a sales job?

04

Four answers at a storm-damaged door

Instagram Reel + Facebook · "When X then Y" slideshow
Four things people say at a storm-damaged door, and four answers that start by agreeing with them. None of these are clever. Every one concedes the point first, because the person is usually right about what they can see. A roof does look fine from the ground. Small hail does not look serious. Nobody has time. The answer is not to argue with any of that. It is to add the one thing they cannot check from where they are standing, and then leave the decision with them. The last one is the test: someone says they would rather wait, and the trained response is that the inspection commits them to nothing. That is most of the job, and it is the part that gets taught. The pitch matters far less than what you do in the four seconds after somebody tells you no. We are hiring field reps for storm restoration, out of our Englewood office. 05

Speed is the whole advantage

Instagram + LinkedIn · tweet-card carousel, dark
Harvard Business Review, March 2011. Across 1.25 million sales leads at 42 US companies, firms that made contact within an hour of a query were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited one hour longer. Wait a full day and they were more than 60 times behind. Qualifying meant one thing: a real conversation with the person who decides. Worth saying plainly: that is web leads, not doorsteps, and one of the three authors ran a company selling lead-response software. The direction survives both caveats. Our read: the gap between someone noticing damage on their property and someone competent standing in front of them is where this work is won or lost. Our version of the hour is the same day. If a rep is already at the door, the inspection happens right there. Free, either way. It is also the part of the job a new rep controls immediately. Not talent. Speed. We are hiring field reps out of our Englewood office. Where does your team lose the most time between interest and contact?
