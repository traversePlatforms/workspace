## The post

> "Free inspection" sounds like a marketing line. In this industry it is a documented procedure.
>
> The standard is Haag's test square method, pioneered by their engineers in the 1960s and now used by contractors, adjusters and engineers worldwide. It runs like this. Chalk a 10ft by 10ft square on each directional slope of the roof, staying clear of overhanging trees and the paths people walk. Inspect every shingle inside it. Record the marks and separate them by type, because hail bruising is not wear and wear is not mechanical damage. Then multiply the damaged shingles in that square by the number of roofing squares on the slope.
>
> Each slope gets its own square. Wind-driven hail does not hit every direction equally.
>
> Our read: this is the part of a field sales role nobody puts in the advert. There is a craft underneath it, it is teachable, and a rep who has it is holding something the homeowner cannot get from the sidewalk.
>
> We are hiring reps for storm restoration work out of our Englewood office. The inspection is free and usually same day.
>
> What is the most technical thing you have had to learn for a sales job?

## The boundary this post walks — read before approving

The post says **the industry standard is Haag's method**. It does not say Surge teaches Haag,
certifies reps in it, or follows it step for step, because nobody has told me that and I will
not infer it. What is sourced is that Surge performs the inspection, free, often same day.

If reps are in fact trained on this protocol, say so and the post gets stronger — the line
becomes "this is what we teach in week one" and it is the best recruiting sentence in the set.
If they are not, the post is still true as written. **This is the one thing to check before it
goes out.**
