## The post

> A hailstorm that breaks nothing can still cost someone a roof.
>
> IBHS published a peer-reviewed study in Frontiers in Materials in September 2025. They froze hailstones under an inch across — sub-severe, the size nobody files on — fired them at asphalt shingles with a hail cannon, left the shingles outside to weather for a year, then hit them with two-inch stones.
>
> The shingles that had taken the small stuff were roughly ten times more susceptible to damage than the ones that had not. The authors' conclusion: frequent sub-severe hailstorms may pose a greater threat to roof longevity than previously recognised, and are a driving factor in growing US hail risk.
>
> Our read: that changes a doorstep conversation. The most common thing a storm restoration rep hears is "it was only small hail." Until last year the honest answer to that was a shrug. Now it is a citation.
>
> Which is the part of this role people do not expect. The research moves, and the person at the door has to move with it.
>
> What is a finding that changed the way you sell?

templates — the ones with a text field per card — do not. **Anything carrying a
citation goes on a structured template.**

## Alternate hooks

1. *"A hailstorm that breaks nothing can still cost someone a roof."* — used. States the counterintuitive finding before any credentialing.
2. *"IBHS built a hail cannon and answered the objection reps have been shrugging at for years."* — livelier, but it makes the study about us.
3. *"'It was only small hail' was true until September 2025."* — sharp, and overstated: the study did not make small hail newly damaging, it measured what was already happening.
4. *"Ten times more susceptible."* — number-first opener. Naked, and it invites "ten times what?" before the answer arrives.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"So every no you got last spring was probably wrong."* — a landing line, and it tells reps their prospects were wrong, which is the pressure register the print rules bar.
- *"This is why we train on the research and not just the pitch."* — a training claim I cannot source. Surge teaches objection handling; nobody has confirmed it teaches this paper.
- *"Sub-severe hail is the silent killer of roofs."* — content-farm phrasing, and "silent killer" is not what the authors said.
- *"Insurers are already repricing for it."* — plausible, no source. Out under the research standard.
