## The caption

> Four things people say at a storm-damaged door, and four answers that start by agreeing with them.
>
> None of these are clever. Every one concedes the point first, because the person is usually right about what they can see. A roof does look fine from the ground. Small hail does not look serious. Nobody has time.
>
> The answer is not to argue with any of that. It is to add the one thing they cannot check from where they are standing, and then leave the decision with them. The last one is the test: someone says they would rather wait, and the trained response is that the inspection commits them to nothing.
>
> That is most of the job, and it is the part that gets taught. The pitch matters far less than what you do in the four seconds after somebody tells you no.
>
> We are hiring field reps for storm restoration, out of our Englewood office.
>
> Which of the four would you find hardest?

## The four, as they appear on screen

| When they say | A trained rep says |
|---|---|
| My roof looks fine from the ground. | Most of them do. Functional hail damage is bruising and granule loss on the shingle itself, and it is found on the roof, not from the driveway. |
| It was only small hail, nothing serious. | That was my read too. Then IBHS tested it: shingles hit by hail under one inch were about ten times more susceptible to the next big storm. |
| I do not have time for an inspection. | Fair. That is why it is same day, and if I am already here I can do it now. It is free either way. |
| I would rather wait and see if it leaks. | Understood, and the inspection commits you to nothing. It tells you whether there is damage up there. What you do with that is yours. |

## Alternate hooks

1. *"Four things people say at a storm-damaged door, and four answers that start by agreeing with them."* — used.
2. *"Every one of these objections is correct. That is why arguing loses."* — sharper, and it is a landing line.
3. *"The pitch is not the job. The four seconds after 'no' is the job."* — strong, but it opens on an abstraction.
4. *"We do not train reps to overcome objections. We train them to answer the true part first."* — cut. "We do not…" sets up a contrast with everyone else, which is an unnamed-competitor verdict.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"Never take the first no."* — this is close to the operator-side phrasing RESTRICTED flags: it reads as pressure and is the exact texture a critic quotes.
- *"Waiting has a cost — your carrier wants notice as soon as practicable."* — drafted, then cut. True of most policies, but a rep telling a homeowner what their policy requires is claim advice, and Colorado bars acting as a public adjuster without a licence (C.R.S. 6-22-105). Out on legal grounds, not stylistic ones.
- *"If they say they need to ask their spouse…"* — deliberately not included. RESTRICTED clears the spouse rebuttal but flags it as the single most screenshot-able line in the corpus. It is not going in a public asset.
- *"Most reps fold on the second one."* — invented statistic about our own people.
