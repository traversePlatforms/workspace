## The post

> Harvard Business Review, March 2011. Across 1.25 million sales leads at 42 US companies, firms that made contact within an hour of a query were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as firms that waited one hour longer. Wait a full day and they were more than 60 times behind. Qualifying meant one thing: a real conversation with the person who decides.
>
> Worth saying plainly: that is web leads, not doorsteps, and one of the three authors ran a company selling lead-response software. The direction survives both caveats.
>
> Our read: the gap between someone noticing damage on their property and someone competent standing in front of them is where this work is won or lost. Our version of the hour is the same day. If a rep is already at the door, the inspection happens right there. Free, either way.
>
> It is also the part of the job a new rep controls immediately. Not talent. Speed.
>
> We are hiring field reps out of our Englewood office.
>
> Where does your team lose the most time between interest and contact?

## Alternate hooks

1. *"Harvard Business Review, March 2011."* — used. Source-first, which suits a claim this large.
2. *"Seven times. That is the gap between calling within the hour and calling an hour later."* — punchier. Number-first openers read as a content-farm tell, and this post is arguing against exactly that.
3. *"Nobody loses this job on talent. They lose it on the two hours between a lead and a knock."* — the best line in the drafting, and it is a verdict about our own reps I cannot support.
4. *"Storm damage does not wait for your CRM."* — cut. We are not selling software and it is a slogan.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"Which is why the fast reps out-earn the slow ones."* — a direct income implication. Barred on recruiting content.
- *"Same-day inspections are something most of this industry cannot offer."* — a competitor verdict and unverifiable. Company spec §3.
- *"Speed is our advantage."* — as a claim about the company it edges toward a differentiation claim; §1 says the method is not unique. Kept as a claim about what a rep controls, which is a different sentence.
- *"60x."* — kept in words rather than as a stat graphic. Presented as a figure on its own it invites the reader to treat a 2011 B2B web-lead study as a law of doorstep selling.
