## The five — panel copy

**1. Small hail ages your roof invisibly.**
Hail under one inch does no damage you can see. IBHS testing found shingles hit by sub-severe hail and then weathered for a year were roughly ten times more susceptible to damage from the next severe storm. Cumulative granule loss from repeated small impacts exceeded a single two-inch stone.

**2. Leaks start at the details, not the shingles.**
Flashing at chimneys, walls and skylights, and the rubber boots around plumbing vents, are among the most common sources of active water intrusion. Corroded flashing needs replacing before the field of the roof does.

**3. Ice dams are an attic problem, not a gutter problem.**
Three conditions have to line up: snow on the roof, freezing temperatures, and an attic that leaks heat. Air sealing the attic is the single most important fix. Gutter work does not address the cause.

**4. The roof can rot from underneath.**
Warm indoor air leaking into a cold attic condenses on the underside of the sheathing. That drives rot, mould, and fastener corrosion, and soaks the insulation so it stops working — all of it invisible from the driveway.

**5. Granule loss is the wear gauge — and it collects in your gutters.**
Shingle grit at the downspout outlets is the roof telling you where it is in its life. Loss concentrated at outlets reads as normal ageing; widespread loss across slopes points to impact or a defect.

## Lines cut, with reason

- **"80–90% of roof leaks originate at flashing."** Cut. Appears only on roofing-contractor blogs, all recycling it without attribution. No primary source found. The qualitative claim survives without it — see item 2.
- **"90% of US homes have underperforming attic ventilation."** Cut. Same pattern, same problem. No study behind it.
- **"NRCA lists granule loss among the top five early indicators of roof failure."** Cut. Attributed to NRCA by contractor blogs; not found in NRCA's own material.
- **"Granule loss over 50% of a shingle cuts remaining life by up to 70%."** Cut. Two invented-looking precise figures in one sentence, sourced to a contractor blog.
- **"Asphalt shingles last about 20 years against warranties of up to 25."** Cut, reluctantly — it's a good line. Traces to the NAHB *Study of Life Expectancy of Home Components*, but every copy of that PDF I could reach failed to parse, so the figure is unconfirmed. Recoverable if a readable copy turns up.
