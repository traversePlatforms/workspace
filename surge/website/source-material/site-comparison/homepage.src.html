How to read this.
Left is surgeftw.com as it stands right now. Right is the new build, served from dist/ on this machine — it is not deployed anywhere. Sections are paired by the job they do, not by their order on the page, so the new homepage's proof band appears against the old homepage's award claim even though one sits higher than the other. Three sections on the new page have no old counterpart and are shown separately.

The shape

Same width, half again the length

At 1440 px wide the old homepage runs 4,999 px top to bottom. The new one runs 7,943 px — 59% longer. Almost all of that is content rather than air: the old page spends its first 900 px on a photograph of a forest.

Section by section

Six things both pages try to do

Pair 01

The first screen

Oldsurgeftw.com
Newnew build · /

What changed

The headline stops greeting and starts describing: welcome to Our Denver Outsource Sales and Marketing Firm becomes We'll handle your entire sales team. The hero photograph stops being stock pine forest and becomes Surge's own floor. One button (Call Us) becomes two — one to talk, one to read how an engagement actually runs. And the location line changes from Denver to Englewood, CO.

Why it matters

A visitor who lands and leaves without scrolling now knows what the company sells. On the old page they leave knowing a city and a mood. The forest is the sharper problem: it is the largest single element on the homepage of a business that has photographs of its own rooms and people, and it says nothing about any of it.

Pair 02

What you are being sold

OldOur Focus
NewThe jobs you stop doing

What changed

Three cards — Generate Revenue, Personal Growth, Career Growth — each carrying a stock mountain photograph, become four numbered jobs the client stops doing: hiring, onboarding, training, management. Our strategy is built on a foundation of cutting-edge solutions and a deep understanding of market dynamics is gone, and nothing replaces it.

Why it matters

The old row mixes two audiences on one line. Generate Revenue is addressed to a client; Personal Growth and Career Growth are addressed to someone thinking about applying. Nothing marks which is which, so both readers get a third of a page each. The new section picks one reader and answers one question — what do I hand over? The recruiting pitch moves to /careers/ rather than competing here.

Pair 03

The proof offered

OldAn award-winning goal driven sales team
NewWhat the work adds up to

What changed

An unattached superlative becomes four counted figures — 106,000+ conversations, 37,600+ presentations, 8,900+ supporters signed on, 172 days in the field — with the period, the campaign type, the supplier and the month printed underneath them. Vision, Mission and Process, three columns of unfalsifiable sentences, come off.

Why it matters

No award is named, dated or linked on the old page. The trade is a claim nobody can verify for numbers somebody can. It also raises the bar rather than clearing it: those figures are now the company's published position and have to hold. They are scoped to nonprofit campaigns only, and their source is currently an internal summary handed over in August rather than a filed report anyone could open.

Pair 04

Why this company

OldWhy Work With Us?
NewAnyone can get the method

What changed

A recruiting pitch containing we provide competitive compensation and benefits to ensure our employees' well-being becomes Anyone can get the method. The difference is the teaching. Both sections carry a real photograph of the office — this is the one place the old homepage was already doing the right thing.

Why it matters

Two separate problems come off the page. Employees asserts an employment relationship the company has not settled internally, and an unspecified benefits claim is a promise with no content behind it. The replacement concedes what critics say anyway — that the method is not proprietary — and puts the argument somewhere it can be tested. That is a harder line to attack than cutting-edge.

Pair 05

Where the company is

OldJoin The Surge Team · Locations
NewYou'll know everyone working on your campaign

What changed

A Locations block listing Denver CO, Houston TX and Asheville NC becomes one room in Englewood, with the campaign team described as sitting in it. The homepage contact form goes; the page routes to /contact/ instead.

Why it matters

This is the most checkable false thing on the old homepage — two of those three rooms are not operating, and anybody can pick up a phone. It also appears on every page of the old site that carries a form, not just this one. Against that, note what the new page gives up: the old homepage let someone enquire without a second click.

Pair 06

The close

OldLet's Create Something Legendary Together
NewTell us what you need sold

What changed

A second stock forest, Let's Create Something Legendary Together and a yellow button become a green band reading Tell us what you need sold. with the phone number on the button itself. The footer gains a one-line description of the business and the founding year; it loses the three-office list.

Why it matters

The old close asks for a feeling and the new one asks a question the reader can actually answer. Worth noticing that the old closing band is the second full-bleed landscape photograph on a page that has three photographs total — two of them are scenery.

What came off, and why it could not ship

What the old homepage does better

It invents nothing. Across the whole old site there is not one percentage, dollar figure, client count or timescale. Every problem found is a claim of quality or a fact that has since changed — not a fabricated statistic. For a site of this vintage in this sector that is genuinely unusual, and it is worth saying before anything else.

And the right description already existed. Not on the homepage — two clicks down, on /generate-revenue/: Owning a business is no small task. Between hiring the right people, training them, and driving sales, your time gets spread thin. That's why Surge exists. That is, near enough, what the new build leads with. The failure was never that nobody could describe the company. It was that the description sat two clicks down while the homepage said welcome over a photograph of a forest.

Still open on the new page

The title tag says Colorado. Outsourced Field Sales Teams in Colorado reads as a service area, and Englewood runs campaigns for out-of-state clients. The visible page gets this right — the hero says Englewood as a location, not a limit. The title tag is the one place it slips, and unlike almost every other decision in this build it carries no comment explaining itself. Worth a yes or no.

Both hero buttons land on a form that isn't real. /contact/ and /book/ are running in demo mode: they fake a submission and confirm an appointment nobody has. That is not a homepage fault, but it is where the homepage sends everybody, so it belongs on this page's list.

The old homepage took an enquiry in place. The new one needs a click first. Deliberate, but it is a step added.
