New file — where it came from.
The SVG on this page was traced from the 733 px PNG, because no vector original was found in the brand folder or the website repo. It overlays the source exactly at every size shown here. What it cannot recover is what a 733 px raster never held: the true curve on the S, U, R and G is approximated, and shows if you blow the mark up past about 3×. The two bars are exact — they were measured and rebuilt as rectangles. If an original Illustrator, EPS or PDF file exists anywhere, it should replace this.

02 · Construction

The idea in it

Each line fills the other line's empty half. The upper bar sits over RGE but on SU's line; the lower bar sits under SU but on RGE's line. They interlock, and they never touch — there is a clear 32 px channel between them. That interlock is the mark. Anything new should keep it or knowingly drop it.

The tracking is very wide

0.48 of cap height between letters, roughly double a normal display setting. It is what makes the wordmark read as calm rather than shouty despite the weight — and it is also why the lockup is 3.69:1 and why it collapses early at small sizes.

The typeface is unknown

A heavy geometric grotesque, closest in proportion to Archivo Black. Not confirmed, and no brand typeface has ever been supplied. The letterforms used in the new options on this page are cut from the real logo, not set in a substitute — so they match whatever it actually is.

04 · Placement

Clear space and minimum size

None of this was ever specified. These are proposals, derived from the mark's own geometry rather than picked — which is the usual way to make a rule that survives contact with a real layout.

Clear space= bar thickness × 2 (0.43 cap height)

Clear space

Keep a margin equal to twice the bar thickness on all four sides — 0.43 of cap height, or 38 px at the source size. It is a unit already in the mark, so it scales with it and nobody has to remember a number. Nothing enters that band: no rules, no photo edges, no other logos.

Minimum size

Digital: 72 px wide. Below that the 19 px bars fall under one device pixel and the channel between bar and letter closes up. Print: 25 mm wide at 300 dpi. Under either floor, use one of the compact lockups or a mark from section 06 instead of shrinking this one.

05 · The gap

Where the current mark runs out

Not faults — a wide two-line wordmark does its job well. But there are four places it cannot go, and each one is somewhere Surge already needs to be.

Social avatarUnreadable
Favicon 32 / 16 pxImpossible
Narrow column3.69:1 doesn't fit
Over a photoNo containment

The two-line construction is the reason. Its whole idea — each line filling the other's empty half — needs width to work. Squeeze it toward square and the interlock is what breaks first. That is not a problem to solve by redrawing the wordmark; it is the argument for a second, separate mark that lives beside it.

06 · New — for review

Seven candidate logomarks

All seven are built from the numbers in section 02: the same bar thickness ratio, and where a letter appears it is cut from the real logo rather than set in a substitute face. Each shows large, then at 40, 26 and 16 px — the row where options fail. None is recommended over the others here; the notes say what each one buys and what it costs.

Step

Device only

The two bars, alone, at the same offset they hold in the wordmark. The most literal extraction possible — it is the logo with the letters removed.

Buys: total consistency, tiny file, works as a pattern or a page rule. Costs: says nothing on its own. Only legible as Surge next to the wordmark.

Ascent

Device only

The same bar stepped three times instead of two. Reads as a rise without drawing an arrow or a chart.

Buys: movement, and it holds down to 16 px. Costs: "up and to the right" is the single most-used shape in sales marketing. Worth weighing against the reputation the company already has to argue with.

Spike

Device only

The bar stood on end, five times, one of them surging past the rest. A surge is a spike, not a steady climb — this is the version that says so.

Buys: the only option whose shape means the company's name. Costs: reads as a bar chart or an audio meter at a glance; that reading is hard to shake.

Corner

Device only

The same two bars turned through 90° and doubled, into an open frame that doesn't close. Structure rather than ascent — the thing a client is actually buying is the frame around a sales team they don't have.

Buys: square by construction, so it solves the avatar and the favicon. Costs: the most generic of the seven; open-bracket frames are everywhere.

S-Rule

Letterform

One letter carrying the wordmark's exact construction: a bar above and to the right, a bar below and to the left. The mark the existing logo already implies.

Buys: unmistakably Surge with no explanation, and it still reads at 16 px. Costs: a lone S is a weak monogram in the abstract — it does its work by association, so it needs the wordmark to have been seen first.

Cut

Letterform

The S struck through edge to edge by the bar, which runs past the letter on both sides. The bar stops being a rule and becomes motion through the letter.

Buys: the most distinctive shape of the seven, and the only one with a horizontal axis to sit in a header. Costs: a struck-through letter carries a "cancelled" reading. Needs testing on someone who hasn't been told what it is.

Badge

Letterform

S-Rule knocked out of a solid square. The square is the container the wordmark never had, so it survives being dropped on a photograph or a coloured ground.

Buys: the direct answer to all four failures in section 05 — avatar, favicon, narrow column, photo. Costs: a filled square is heavy; on a dark site it becomes a black hole unless it is set in green or reversed.

Descriptor options

All three are cleared statements of what the company does:

  • Outsourced sales & marketing
  • Sales teams, hired and managed
  • Est. 2024

What a descriptor can't say

  • No location. One office is not a service area — Englewood runs campaigns for out-of-state clients, and copy implying Colorado-only has been wrong before.
  • No claim to a unique, proprietary or exclusive method. The company's own position is that the method isn't differentiated, and the site says so.
  • No client name and no sector list in a lockup.

08 · Handling

Do and don't

Do

  • Use the SVG. It is one path, takes currentColor, and recolours with a CSS variable.
  • Reverse it by inverting. The glyphs are pure black, so filter: invert(1) is exact.
  • Keep both bars. They are the mark; the letters alone are just the word.
  • Give it two bar-thicknesses of clear space on every side.
  • Set it in black when the ground is the brand green.
  • Swap to a compact lockup or a section 06 mark below 72 px rather than shrinking the primary.

Don't

  • Colour the bars differently from the letters. It reads as two objects and falls apart small.
  • Stretch, condense, rotate, outline, emboss or add a shadow.
  • Close the 32 px channel between the two bars, or align them into a single rule.
  • Re-set the wordmark in a substitute typeface. The real one has not been identified.
  • Place it on a photograph without a containing shape — use the Badge.
  • Use the two white-background screenshot files anywhere. Only the PNG and the new SVG have transparency.
