## The blocker you need to know about

**The office wall reads "F✱CK AVERAGE / BE LEGENDARY".** It is painted on, the
asterisk is part of the original, and at full resolution it is unmistakable.
There is also **a $100 bill taped to the wall** beside the dartboard, and a
**GOALS whiteboard showing a legible "$39,000" weekly figure**.

None of that is a restriction violation — the 2026-09-07 revision cleared board
content and dollar figures *in photographs*. It is an **editorial** problem, and
a sharp one: profanity and cash on the wall of a sales office is the exact image
the standing test in `RESTRICTED.md` exists to catch. *Would this read as
confirmation to someone who already suspects the worst?*

**This now constrains every future shoot in that room, not just this batch.**
Either frame away from that wall, or the photos cannot go on the website.

## What went onto the site

`IMG_8374` → `drafts/surge-site/assets/img/morning-block-b.jpg` (1440×1080, 263 KB),
added as the **second frame of the first live photo rotator**, on the homepage

The crop takes the left 1900px of the frame specifically to **exclude the GOALS
board and its $39,000 figure**, which sits in the right third. Say the word and
it comes out in one line.

**It is a usable photo, not a strong one.** Roughly 45% of the frame is empty
carpet even after cropping, and one person is bent over mid-frame. Treat it as a
placeholder that proves the rotator works — replace it when the real shoot lands.

## The thing to fix on the next shoot

**All four were portrait.** 3024×4032 and 1980×3520. Every photo slot on this
site crops to **4:3 landscape**, so a portrait original loses most of its content
before it ever reaches the page. This was the first line of the technical section
in `SHOT-LIST.md` and it is worth repeating: **turn the phone sideways.** It is
the single highest-impact change to the next batch.

Second: get further back and lower. These were all shot from standing height
looking across an empty floor, which is why the carpet dominates. A frame taken
from inside the semicircle, at chest height, would put people across the width.

## HEIC

Three of the four were `.heic`. Windows on this machine **has no HEIF codec** —
PowerShell `System.Drawing` and WIC both fail with `0xC00D5212`, and the Read tool
does not treat `.heic` as an image. Converted with `heic-convert` (pure JS, no
system codec needed) — the working copy is in
`scratchpad/heic/`, and the recipe is recorded in the session notes. `.jpg`
versions now sit beside each `.heic` here, matching the pattern already used for
the other 51 HEIC files in this folder.
