Created 2026-08-13, when eight loose photos were sorted out of the `photos/` root. These four are good, usable field photos in every respect **except** that a client's name and mark are legible in them — on a rep's shirt, on a table banner, or both.

`RESTRICTED.md` Tier 4: **client identity, at any resolution. Sector only.** A logo on a shirt names the client louder than a caption would, and this is the same reason the client logos were pulled out of `Logos/`.

- `2026-07-09-two-reps-client-shirts.jpg` — two reps outside a shop with snow cones, both in branded tees with lanyards and badges. The mark is centre-frame on both.
- `2026-07-15-three-reps-selfie-client-shirt.jpg` — three-person sunny selfie, front camera so the shirt text is mirrored. Still fully legible.
- `2026-07-25-charity-booth-two-client-brands.jpg` — the booth. **Two different client brands in one frame**, one on the table banner and one on two shirts.
- `2026-07-31-shop-group-client-shirt.jpg` — three people indoors against a red wall, one in a branded tee.

**Cropping does not rescue these.** In every one the branding is on the subject rather than in the background, so a crop that removes it removes the photo.

Kept rather than deleted — they are fine for internal use, a client-facing deck, or a recap sent to the client themselves. They must not reach a public feed, an infographic, a carousel, or the website.

**The unlock is written permission from the client**, not a judgement call here. If that ever arrives, these move back into `Culture/` and `Office and work/` and the rows in `photos-notes.md` change with them.

## 2026-08-13 — 51 more files

The folder grew from four files to fifty-five. A large batch of field photography arrived and **the overwhelming majority of it names a client.**

### `canvassing-01` … `canvassing-41`

Forty-one photos from retail-frontage shifts: reps at branded kiosks and tables outside grocery stores, barbershops, liquor stores, nail salons and pet-supply shops. Three different client organisations are identifiable across the set — two Colorado charities and, in `canvassing-11`, a **Houston** animal-welfare organisation.

That last one carries a second problem on its own. RESTRICTED.md Tier 2 restricts **structure and scale**, naming the affiliate offices specifically and adding *"no footprint, scale, growth, or nationwide framing."* A photo of a rep working a Houston client is a footprint claim with a picture attached.

The branding appears on shirts, hats, beanies, lanyards, table banners and free-standing kiosks — usually several at once, always on the subject rather than in the background.

**Nine of the forty-one had no client mark I could see at contact-sheet size.** They were filed here anyway because they came from the same shifts and most show a badge or lanyard that was not readable at that size.

### Three were then rescued by retouching — 2026-08-20

Asked whether the marks could be edited out, three frames turned out to be
worth it: **`canvassing-08`, `canvassing-24` and `canvassing-25`**. In each,
the client's branding is *incidental* — a logo on a cap, on a chest, on a flat
tablecloth — rather than the subject. Those come out cleanly, and what is left
is a Surge rep working a booth with nothing in frame pointing at any
organization. The cleared copies, the per-mark record and the method are in
**`../field-cleared/`**; the originals here are untouched.

**This is not a precedent for the other thirty-eight.** The standard applied
was not "is the wordmark legible" but *does anything in the frame still point
at a specific organization*, and a branded kiosk fails that test even with the
wordmark painted out — the animal photographs and the kiosk's shape name the
charity on their own. Where the branding **is** the subject there is nothing to
remove. Read `../field-cleared/README.md` before assuming any other frame can
be treated the same way.

The two upstream fixes below are still worth more than retouching, which
rescued three photographs in an afternoon and does not scale.

A charity fundraising walk on 2026-05-02, with the HEIC masters kept alongside. Event-branded step-and-repeat frames, event T-shirts and bucket hats, the finish-line arch, and signage naming the host organisation and its web address. Some frames are nothing but client branding.

### The same conclusion, at larger scale

This intake makes the cost of the sector-only rule concrete rather than theoretical. **Roughly two-thirds of all the field photography Surge has is unpublishable**, not because the work looks bad — it looks genuinely good, people are enjoying themselves, the public is engaging — but because the client's logo is on the uniform.

Nothing here can be cropped out of the problem. The fix is upstream, and there are only two versions of it:

1. **Unbranded Surge shirts** worn on some shifts, so photography from those shifts is usable.
2. **Written permission** from a client to name them, which would unlock everything here at once.

Both are decisions above this folder. Until one of them happens, every field shift produces photographs that can only be used internally.
