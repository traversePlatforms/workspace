# Photos

Team, field, event and office photos. **I can see images** — I'll describe what's in one, judge whether it suits a post, and embed it into an infographic.

## Formats

PNG or JPG. WebP works too. Bigger is better than smaller — I can downscale, I can't invent detail that isn't there.

**HEIC I cannot open directly** — the Read tool treats it as binary. I can convert it (the Windows HEIF codec is installed on this machine), but say so, because otherwise a folder of HEICs looks empty to me. Drop them in and ask for conversion, or export as JPG from your phone.

## Two things to record

**Permission.** Photos of identifiable people need their agreement before anything goes out, and photos taken on a client's site or featuring their branding fall under the company spec's customer-name rule. `photos-notes.md` is the register — it lists every file, what it shows, and whether it's cleared. **Anything not listed there is NOT cleared.** Add new photos to it before using them. (This reverses the old default, which treated unlisted files as fair game — that would have cleared 65 photos including children and client logos.)

**What it shows.** Filenames like `IMG_4821.jpg` tell me nothing. Either rename them or keep a line per photo in `photos-notes.md`.

## Worth having

Real field photos — a doorstep, a booth, a morning meeting, a training room — are worth more than posed team shots. They're the honest version of the culture story, and specificity is the answer to the reputation problem rather than polish. See `brand-voice-company.md` §5.

Avoid stock. It reads as stock, and it undercuts everything the specific content is doing.
