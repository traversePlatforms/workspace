## 2. Mechanics

| Setting | Value |
|---|---|
| First person | **"I"**, and personal anecdotes are welcome |
| Emoji | **Sparing** — one or two max, only when carrying meaning. Never as list markers. |
| Length | **150-200 words** for LinkedIn |
| Closing move | **Question to the reader** |
| Hashtags | **3-5**, at the end |
| Links | **First comment, never in the post** — LinkedIn suppresses outbound links |
| Profanity | **Personal yes, company no.** Your own posts may swear; Surge's may not. |

## 3. Hard bans

**Formatting gimmicks** — emoji as bullet points, single-word paragraphs used as drum hits (*Bold. Simple. Powerful.*), three or more rhetorical questions stacked in a row.

**Unfalsifiable motivational language** — added 2026-08-05, and it is the single clearest signal in the calibration pair (§6). "Manifest the momentum" was the only line you rejected in a paragraph you otherwise accepted. The test: could a reader go do the thing tomorrow? "Stay consistent" and "communicate clearly" pass. "Manifest," "unlock your potential," "operate at a higher frequency," "the energy you put out" do not.

**Explicitly not banned:** LinkedIn-native connective tissue ("Here's the thing:", "And honestly,") and corporate vocabulary (leverage, unlock, game-changer). Permitted is not encouraged; reach for these only when they genuinely help.

> **Revised 2026-08-06.** This previously argued the register was "warm and conversational" because the retired near-miss sample kept "And honestly," unflagged. That reasoning is void — the sample wasn't his writing (§6). His real register is **plain and matter-of-fact**, not warm. Connective tissue stays permitted, but it isn't characteristic of him and shouldn't be added to make a draft feel friendlier. Corporate vocabulary in particular appears nowhere in his actual prose.

## 4. Stance

**Revised 2026-08-05.** The previous rule — "analytical, stop short of the verdict" — was written for posts analyzing named third-party companies. It does not fit your actual content. Split it:

**On your own professional opinion: state it.** Your own view on how the work goes is the reason to read the post. Don't hedge it into neutrality.

> **Evidence corrected 2026-08-06.** This previously rested on the retired calibration sample being "prescriptive." That sample wasn't his writing (§6), so the reasoning is void — **but the conclusion survives on better evidence.** His real prose is full of stated positions: "I dont really enjoy sales," "which I dont really like," "I ignored it." He is opinionated, just not rhetorically. **How he states an opinion matters:** flat, often as a trailing clause, frequently conceding the counterpoint in the same sentence — not as a polished thesis. State the view; don't dress it up.

**On other companies: stop short of the verdict.** When a named company, competitor, or client appears, present what is verifiably true and let the reader conclude. If a sentence tells the reader what to think about a named company, cut it or convert it to the underlying fact. This is a narrow rule about third parties, not a general posture. See the company spec §3 — in practice competitors should not be named at all.

**On Surge's own numbers: see company spec §2.** Metrics are the differentiator, so they carry the most weight and need the most care.

**Personal anecdotes:** yes. First-hand experience is the strongest opening material available.

**Never fabricate an anecdote.** Drafts leave a clearly marked `[ANECDOTE]` slot with a suggested shape. You fill it or cut it.

## 5. Known tension

**150-200 words with anecdotes is tight.** An anecdote costs 40-60 words, leaving ~120 for everything else. Consequences:

1. The anecdote must be one specific moment, not a narrative arc.
2. Supporting evidence compresses to its single best number. Three findings become one.

If a topic needs all three, either drop the anecdote or accept ~250 words. Flag the trade rather than silently picking.

### What replaced it

### Real excerpts

> "But essentially we get into the office early and begin preparation."

> "It can be simple things such as just writing the pitch down or the LOAs 5 steps etc. Just basically trying to get it to memory."

> "During this time they dont want us on cell phones and they dont want us sitting in the chairs we need to be standing which I dont really like."

> "On the one hand we can do 8-8 and make a shit ton of money because we dont have capped commissions but on the other hand we could work 8-8 and not get any sign ups."

> "I can retain information pretty well but I dont always execute it very well."

> "I ignored it and just went straight to the CEO with what I can do and what my skills are and eventually just got the role I wanted."

### What the voice actually does

**Concedes in the same breath.** The dominant structural habit by a distance. He states something and immediately gives the other side — "but on the other hand," "but again on the alternative," "but I dont always." He does not resolve the tension afterward. **This is the single most important pattern to reproduce.**

**Hedges openly and specifically.** "From my understanding," "I think," "I'm not sure why," "I don't know exactly," "I guess." He marks the edge of what he knows rather than writing around it. Never fake confidence in his voice; a marked uncertainty is more him than a clean assertion.

**Delivers opinion as a trailing clause.** "…we need to be standing which I dont really like." The judgement arrives tacked onto the end of a descriptive sentence, unemphasised. He does not build to a verdict or give it its own sentence.

**Concrete before abstract.** Times, dollar figures, counts, named steps. He reaches for the specific instance, not the principle. When he does state a principle it's short and unadorned.

**Runs clauses together with "and," "but," "so."** Long, additive sentences. Not clipped, not balanced, not rhythmic.

**Says the unflattering thing plainly.** "I dont always execute it very well." "I ignored it." "I dont really enjoy sales." No softening, no reframe. This is the most distinctive thing about him and the hardest to fake.

**No ornament.** No rhetorical build, no closing flourish, no callback, no landing line. Sentences stop when the thought does.

**Profanity, naturally.** Confirms §2 — permitted in personal register.

### What the old sample got wrong

- **The em-dash contrast is not his signature move.** "Isn't about X — it's about Y" appears nowhere in his real writing. It was in both retired paragraphs, which is evidence about their author, not about him.
- **He is not prescriptive second-person.** The old sample was generalised "you" advice. He writes first-person about specific things that happened.
- **He does not land on a named value.** The old sample resolved to "trust." He doesn't resolve; he concedes and stops.
- **Warmth is not the register.** He is plain and matter-of-fact, not warm. Not cold either — just uninterested in charming the reader.

### What transfers to a post, and what doesn't

The raw journal voice is not directly a LinkedIn voice, and the point is not to reproduce typos.

**Keep:** the concession structure, the open hedging, the trailing opinion, concrete-before-abstract, additive sentences, willingness to say the unflattering thing.

**Fix silently:** spelling, apostrophes, "etc," sentence fragments where they read as errors rather than as voice.

**Do not add:** rhetorical build, balanced antithesis, a closing flourish, a landing line, warmth he didn't put there.

**On the §2 closing question:** keep it, but it has to be a *genuinely open* question — the kind he actually asks himself ("I am not sure why but I think its budget cuts"). Engagement-bait rhetorical questions are the opposite of this voice.

An explanatory register with visible uncertainty is exactly what that person writes. It also matches post 3 of `drafts/training-content-linkedin-draft.md` — the plainer variant — far better than posts 1 and 2.

### Confirmed 2026-08-06

**He approved the flat register against a finished draft** (`drafts/teaching-ladder-linkedin-draft.md`, deliberately written to end on a subordinate clause with no landing line). His response: *"A flatter voice is fine. I often come off as monotone in real life."*

**That last part matters more than the approval.** The flatness isn't a stylistic preference he's agreeing to — it's how he actually is. So plainness in his writing is authentic rather than affected, which means:

- **Don't drift back toward polish over time.** No reintroducing landing lines, rhythmic build, or warmth because a draft "feels flat." Flat is correct.
- **But flat is about delivery, not substance.** Monotone describes how he says things, not whether he has something to say. A plain sentence still has to carry a real observation. The failure mode to watch is mistaking *no ornament* for *nothing to report* — the teaching-ladder draft works because the underlying insight is strong enough to survive being stated plainly. Weak material stated plainly is just weak.

## 9. Claims about working at Surge

**Revised 2026-08-05.** No recruiting posts are planned. The model is: posts earn attention, interested people go to the company site, and they apply from there if they like what they've seen. That's a sound structure, and it means the detail lives on the site rather than in the post.

So the rules are narrower than a recruiting-post checklist:

**A post that doesn't describe the job doesn't need to disclose anything.** A post teaching an objection-handling technique is content, not a job ad. Write it freely.

**The moment a post makes a claim about working at Surge** — income, advancement, "we're hiring," what the day is like, what someone achieved — these apply:

- Name the work as field sales in plain words. Never "marketing firm," "management trainee," or "business consultant" for an entry-level field sales role.
- No income figure that isn't a median or clearly labelled as a top performer. Never "unlimited earning potential."
- Never imply an entry-level rep is an owner, partner, or executive.
- No claim in a post that the landing page then contradicts or omits.

**The landing page carries the burden the post doesn't.** Since posts funnel there, that page should state the channel, how pay works, and the realistic hours before someone applies. Worth auditing — a vague post is survivable, a vague post feeding a vague careers page is the entire complaint in company spec §5.
