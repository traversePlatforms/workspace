The manual gives you the words. The door gives you the timing.

Three things we teach, and roughly when each one starts to matter. None of it is a new idea — the difference is how early somebody hears it and how specifically it gets debriefed.

Week one

Talking too much

The pitch gets memorised, so the conversation becomes a delivery problem. The door opens and the rep presents at somebody who hasn’t said anything yet.

Every week after

“I need to think about it”

Handled worst of any objection, because it doesn’t sound like one. It sounds reasonable, so it gets accepted and the rep leaves.

Month two

Rejection tolerance

The point where people quit. The manual covers rejection and the coverage is fine, but reading it does almost nothing on an actual doorstep.

Week one — ask one question, then stop

The fix is small and it feels wrong the first time. What makes it hard isn’t the technique — it’s that the silence after a question feels like losing control, and the instinct is to fill it. Learning to leave it alone is most of the job.

The objection that isn’t one

“I need to think about it” is a surface signal with three different causes underneath, and each one needs a different response. Which is why trying to overcome it directly is the wrong move.

Needs your material

Your actual phrasing for asking what they want to think about. The wording matters more here than anywhere else on this board.

Month two — what the manual can’t do

The manual has a section on rejection and it says what you’d expect: don’t take it personally, it isn’t about you, go to the next door. That teaches the concept. It doesn’t build the tolerance, and only one of those gets somebody through their second month.

None of this is a method nobody else has. It’s ordinary craft, taught early and debriefed specifically — and that turns out to be the part most teams skip.
