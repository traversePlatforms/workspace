Surge · what we teach · week one

Nine say no. That’s not the bad news — that’s the plan.

The law of averages isn’t ours and it isn’t new. Every field sales team knows it. What matters is whether a rep hears it in week one, or works it out alone in month three.

Ten conversations. One yes.

Scale it however you like — a hundred doors is ninety noes and ten yeses. The ninety aren’t failures, they’re the price of the ten, and the price is known before you leave the office.

So the ratio gets taught before the pitch

A new rep works at roughly one yes in fifteen, and brings it toward one in ten as they get better. Those are benchmarks we train against — not audited results. Product, territory and campaign all move them.

Getting better isn’t more yeses a day. It’s fewer conversations per yes.

What that changes about the work

A rep controls

  • How many conversations they have
  • Keeping the pitch consistent, adapting the delivery to the person
  • Whether they talk to everyone, or only the promising-looking ones

Where time goes missing

  • Pre-judging — deciding who’s worth a conversation before having it
  • Cherry-picking the easy doors
  • Reading a no as information about you, not as one of the nine

Pre-judging is the expensive one, because it feels like efficiency. You can’t tell from outside which one of the ten says yes. Filtering doesn’t remove the nine — it removes conversations, and the yeses go with them.

It’s a numbers game before it’s a sales game. The people I’ve watched leave field sales mostly didn’t leave because they couldn’t sell — they left because nobody told them what a normal day looks like.
