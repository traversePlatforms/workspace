## Why this was rewritten

Three separate problems, and only one of them is about voice.

1. **The two pay figures do not reconcile.** `$800–$1,100/week` is $41,600–$57,200 a year. The ad
   also claims "Expected First-Year Earnings: $40,000–$75,000+". $75,000 is $1,442/week — about
   $342/week above the top of the band stated in the same posting. Checkable arithmetic on one page.
2. **The ad and the website describe different jobs.** The ad says "community events and outreach
   campaigns". The site lists **door to door first** among the channels. Nothing in the ad is false;
   the omission is the problem, and §5 names misleading job ads specifically as a criticism critics
   cite. An omission is harder to defend than a claim.
3. **The framing is barred almost word for word** by Surge's own specs — see the cut list below.

**A fourth, now resolved.** The current ad states no application close date. Colorado's Equal Pay
for Equal Work Act requires each posting to disclose compensation, a general description of
benefits, *and* the date the application window is anticipated to close. Surge recruits
continuously, so there is no date — and CDLE guidance covers that case directly: where an employer
continuously accepts applications for a genuinely evergreen role, there is no deadline to list,
**but the posting must say that applications are accepted on an ongoing basis.** That line is a
requirement, not a nicety, and the current ad does not carry it. This draft does.
**Not legal advice — worth a glance from whoever handles HR.**

## The ad

**Fundraising Representative — Nonprofit Campaigns**

**Location:** Englewood, CO (Denver metro) · In person
**Job type:** Full-time
**Pay:** $800–$1,100 per week — a base wage plus commission
**Benefits:** Paid training, 401(k) matching
**Applications:** Accepted on an ongoing basis

---

Surge runs the sales and fundraising function for organisations that do not have a team of their
own. Most of the work in this role is for nonprofits, and the job is asking people, face to face,
to become donors.

**What the work actually is.** You work an assigned area or event and start conversations with
people who did not ask to be approached. You explain what the organisation does and what a donor's
support pays for, and you sign up people who want to support it. That happens door to door in
assigned neighbourhoods, at staffed booths, and at community events — not one of those, all of them.

Most conversations end in a no. That is the job, not a sign it is going badly, and the people who
last here are the ones it does not rattle.

**What the day looks like.** Mornings open with a development block — a taught subject, practice on
it, and time with a trainer or a team lead. Then you are out. Your numbers are logged the same day
you work them, so you always know where you stand.

**What we are looking for.**

- No experience needed. You are taught the product and the conversation before you are in front of
  anyone, and nobody is sent out on their first day to work it out alone.
- You can start a conversation with a stranger and keep your footing when it goes nowhere.
- You turn up when you said you would.
- Reliable transportation to the office and to wherever the team is working that day.
- Experience in customer service, hospitality, retail, events or sales helps, and is not required.

**What you get.**

- $800–$1,100 per week, base plus commission. **You are not commission-only** — the base is paid
  whether or not you hit target.
- Paid weekly.
- Paid training.
- 401(k) matching.
- Daily coaching from trainers and team leads who have done this job on the floor themselves.
- A route up. The people running training and managing teams here came up through the floor, and
  advancement is based on performance.

**How to apply.** Send an application through Indeed. A resume is welcome and not required. If it
looks like a fit you will have a phone conversation, then spend a day with the team and go out to
see the work before either side commits.

## Alternate titles

The title is the biggest single lever on both applicant volume and on whether this ad reads as
straight. Ranked by how plainly each says what the job is:

1. **Fundraising Representative — Nonprofit Campaigns** *(used above)* — says the sector and the
   activity. Will attract fewer, better-matched applicants.
2. **Community Fundraising Representative** — slightly softer, still names fundraising.
3. **Door-to-Door & Events Fundraiser** — the most explicit. Highest self-selection, lowest volume.
4. ~~Brand Ambassador | Community Outreach~~ — the current title. **This is the one to move away
   from.** "Brand Ambassador" is the euphemism the misleading-job-ad criticism attaches to, and a
   candidate reading it does not learn that the job is fundraising or that it involves doors.

**The trade-off is real and it is Talent Acquisition's call, not mine.** A plainer title means fewer
applications. It also means the people who apply know what they applied to, which shows up later as
retention rather than as volume. Given §5, I would take the trade.

## Cut, and why

Keeping these here so the reasoning is not re-litigated. Each is quoted from the live ad.

| Cut | Why |
|---|---|
| "Expected First-Year Earnings: $40,000–$75,000+" | §5 — no earnings claims, explicit or atmospheric, on recruiting content. Also the figure that does not reconcile with the stated weekly band. The compensation range is disclosed properly instead, which is what the law asks for. |
| "uncapped commissions" | RESTRICTED Tier 2 names this phrase **specifically** as an atmospheric income claim even without a figure attached. |
| "Travel opportunities and performance incentives" | Same Tier 2 entry, same reason — "travel opportunities" is named alongside "uncapped commissions". |
| "Supportive, high-energy team culture" | §5 — no culture content running on vibes. "High energy" is also in the Tier 2 compliance cluster. |
| "nationally recognized nonprofit organizations" | §5 — no scale or footprint framing, and it characterises the clients. Sector alone is cleared; "nationally recognized" is doing work it should not. |
| "Competitive base pay" | "Competitive" is atmospheric and says nothing. The actual range is stated instead, which is both stronger and required. |
| "Build Your Career While Making an Impact" / "Ready to Make an Impact?" | Reads as copy rather than as a description of a job. Replaced with what the work is. |
| The ✔ checklist ("Loves talking with people…") | Four lines of flattery aimed at the reader. The requirements section already says who does well. |
| "Denver, CO (Local Candidates Only)" | The ZIP in the same ad is **80110, which is Englewood**. Kept the metro name for searchability but put the real city first. |
| "Participate in ongoing sales, communication, and leadership development" | Vague. Replaced with the specific thing that happens: the morning development block. |

**Deliberately kept from the original:** the experience-not-required framing, the customer
service / hospitality / retail background line, paid training, 401(k) matching, weekly pay, and
advancement. Those are all true, concrete, and already public.

## Open questions — all four answered by Matthew, 2026-09-12

1. **Channels — confirmed not events-only.** Matthew: *"The role doesn't pertain to just events
   regardless of what the indeed site says."* Doors, booths and events all stand, and the draft now
   says so explicitly: *"not one of those, all of them."* **This is the substance of punch item 46**
   — the live ad describes only the events half of a job that is also door to door, and the site
   lists doors first. The two now agree.
2. **Benefits — the list is complete.** Paid training and 401(k) matching, nothing else. No health
   care, no paid time off. Stated as-is; nothing invented, nothing implied.
3. **Close date — there is none, and that is compliant *if it is stated*.** Matthew:
   recruiting runs continuously. CDLE guidance covers exactly this: where an employer continuously
   accepts applications, there is no deadline to list, but **the posting must say that applications
   are accepted on an ongoing basis.** So it is not an omission — it is a line the ad has to carry,
   and the draft now carries it. The caveat in the guidance is that this is only compliant for a
   genuinely evergreen role, which this is.
4. **Transportation — "reliable transportation", not a licence or a vehicle.** Matthew's wording.
   In the requirements as *"Reliable transportation to the office and to wherever the team is
   working that day."* Deliberately not "must have a valid driver's licence and own vehicle", which
   would be both untrue and a narrower filter than the job needs.

### One thing that stays out of the ad

The *reason* recruiting is continuous is turnover. That is context, not content: §5 names high
turnover as one of the things that makes recruiting copy mislead without stating a number, and it
is squarely the standing test — it would read as confirmation to someone already primed. **The ad
says applications are ongoing and says nothing about why.** That is honest and complete; the reason
is nobody's business but Surge's, and publishing it would help no one.

It does sharpen one thing though. Continuous recruiting against turnover is precisely the situation
in which an "Expected First-Year Earnings: $40,000–$75,000+" line does the most damage — a
first-year projection aimed at a population where many will not complete a first year. That is the
§5 argument for cutting it, stated at its sharpest.
