## The post

> Total US charitable giving passed $600 billion for the first time. $617.2 billion in 2025, per
> Giving USA.
>
> The split is the part worth sitting with. Individuals gave $394.2 billion of it — 63.9 cents in
> every charitable dollar. Foundations, $117.15 billion. Bequests, $62.19 billion. Corporations,
> $43.67 billion.
>
> Now look at which way each is moving. After inflation, bequests grew 16.6%. Foundations, 3%.
> Individuals, 1.4%. Corporations, half a point.
>
> So the largest source of charitable money in the country is also close to the slowest-growing, and
> the fastest-growing one is money left in wills.
>
> Our read: a foundation has a grants officer, an application window and a deadline. An individual
> has none of those. That column moves when somebody asks, and it moves by roughly the number of
> people who were asked well. Which makes asking a staffing question long before it is a messaging
> question.
>
> If your organisation's giving mix looks like the national one, where is the ask actually happening?

## Alternate hooks

1. *"Total US charitable giving passed $600 billion for the first time."* — used. A real threshold,
   and it dates the piece precisely.
2. *"Sixty-four cents in every charitable dollar comes from a person, not an institution."* — the
   sharpest single line and the board's headline. Held back from the post's first line because the
   number lands harder after the total.
3. *"The fastest-growing source of charitable money in America is dead people."* — genuinely the
   finding, and much too flip for a nonprofit audience in the company register.
4. *"Individual giving grew 1.4% last year. That is the whole problem."* — good, but it opens on the
   weakest number and needs the mix to make sense of it.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"And we raise it."* — the single most tempting line in the set and an outright §2 breach: it is a
  performance claim with no scope, no date and no pullable source, standing next to a $394 billion
  figure that would lend it borrowed weight.
- *"Our nonprofit clients see this first-hand."* — names a client relationship in prose. Tier 4,
  sector only, and this sentence is the exact shape the rule exists to stop.
- *"Face-to-face is the highest-converting channel in fundraising."* — no source found that clears
  the standard. Every hit was a vendor blog. Cut outright rather than softened.
- *"Donors are not a marketing problem, they are a headcount problem."* — good line, but it reads as
  a swipe at in-house fundraising teams.
- *"1986–1990, individuals were 80% of giving."* — the historical comparison surfaced in search but
  was not read first-hand in the report, so it is nowhere in the post or on the board.
