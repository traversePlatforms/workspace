## The post

> The talk-time target everyone quotes is a 2016 number: talk 43% of the call, listen 57%.
>
> Gong's current page analyses 326,000 sales calls of at least ten minutes. The average call is 60%
> rep talking. Deals that closed averaged 57%. Deals that were lost averaged 62%.
>
> Five points separate a won call from a lost one. The target asks reps to cut seventeen.
>
> The more useful finding is a different shape entirely. Low performers swing ten points between
> their own outcomes — 54% on calls they won, 64% on calls they lost. High performers hold roughly
> the same ratio either way.
>
> Our read: the ratio was never the skill. A rep whose talk time climbs ten points on a call going
> badly is not talking more on purpose. They have lost the thread and are filling silence. The swing
> is the tell, and a target number hides it — you can hit 57% on average across a fortnight of calls
> that swung wildly in both directions.
>
> What number does your team coach to that nobody has re-checked since it was published?

## Alternate hooks

1. *"The talk-time target everyone quotes is a 2016 number."* — used. Dates the rule in the first
   line without yet arguing with it.
2. *"Five points separate a won call from a lost one. The rule asks you to cut seventeen."* — the
   sharpest contrast in the piece and the board's real subject. Needs one sentence of setup.
3. *"Your reps' talk time is not too high. It is too unstable."* — states the thesis flat and is the
   most useful line for a sales manager, but it tells the reader their team's problem before
   showing them anything.
4. *"326,000 calls later, the golden ratio is still being quoted at people."* — accurate and a little
   sneering.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"Gong retired its own most famous number."* — **cut on a correction.** It is not what the page
  says; see below. This was the framing of the earlier draft on this topic and it does not survive
  re-reading the source.
- *"Every talk-time scorecard in the industry is measuring the wrong thing."* — a verdict about
  other companies, and an overreach: the data says the target is weak, not that measuring is wrong.
- *"We coach the swing, not the number."* — unsourced claim about Surge training. Nobody has
  confirmed what Surge coaches on talk time, and inventing it is the failure mode this project has
  already hit once.
- *"Most sales training is theatre."* — broad swipe, reads as confirmation under §5.
- *A third dumbbell row for high performers.* Built, then deleted: the page reports that they hold
  roughly constant but publishes no figures for them. Drawing an unquantified bar would have been a
  fabrication with a ruler on it. The board says so in print instead.

**Correction carried into this draft.** The earlier piece on this topic
(`../sales-research-set/01-talk-ratio…`) was built on the framing that Gong had *retired* the 43:57
ratio, and its own README recorded the loose end — that a second Gong page still stated 43:57 as
current. Re-reading the source on 14 Sep 2026, **the same page carries both**: it presents the 326k
findings and still references 43:57 as the benchmark for success. So "they retired it" is not
supportable and is not claimed anywhere here. The board and the post set the numbers side by side
and let the five-versus-seventeen arithmetic carry the point, which needs no claim about intent.

**Date note.** The page's stamp moved between the August 2026 check (20 March 2025) and this one
(4 March 2026). Re-read it before posting; the stamp, not the memory of it, is what goes in the copy.

**One open item.** Gong sells conversation-intelligence software, not outsourced selling, so naming
it does not trip §3. Flagged because it is the only company named in the set.
