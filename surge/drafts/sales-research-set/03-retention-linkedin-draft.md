## The post

> Everything taught in training week is on a curve, and the curve was drawn in the 1880s.
>
> Ebbinghaus measured his own forgetting and found most of the loss happens early. In 2015 Murre and Dros published a full replication in PLOS ONE: one subject, 70 hours of learning, relearning at six intervals from 20 minutes out to 31 days. The curve held.
>
> The more useful study is Roediger and Karpicke, Psychological Science, 2006. Students read a passage, then either re-read it or sat a recall test with no feedback, the same number of times. Five minutes later, re-reading won. Two days and a week later, testing won, substantially.
>
> The part worth sitting with: repeated studying increased students' confidence in their ability to remember. The group that felt readiest retained less.
>
> Our read: nobody forgets the pitch because they did not care. They forget it because that is what memory does. Which makes "we covered that in training" a statement about the calendar, not about what a rep can do at a door. The morning block is drilling and paired practice for exactly this reason — recall out loud, not review.
>
> What does your team do in the week after training week?

## Asset note

Hand-authored, not generated. **Every claim on the board carries its citation on the taped
slip at the bottom**, which is why this piece was not put through a Blotato template: two
peer-reviewed papers and a named journal are exactly the payload the image-family templates
invent a fake author for.

Built on the house whiteboard system copied from `5-steps-build-value-infographic.html` — real
brand palette, four-pen tray as legend, hand-drawn wobble on shape groups only, no entrance
animation so the headless capture is not blank.

**Two defects found in the first render and fixed:**

1. The subheading collided with the green rule under the headline. Rule moved down 14px, panels shifted with it.
2. The curve plotted five of the study's six relearning intervals — the 2-day point was missing while the caption said "six intervals." A seventh point was added so the drawing and the caption agree.

**The deliberate omission, and it is the point of the taped slip:** no per-interval retention
percentage is printed anywhere on the board. The "you forget 70% within 24 hours" figures that
circulate with this topic are content-farm mangling of Ebbinghaus, and the savings scores in
the Murre & Dros tables were not verified first-hand. The curve is drawn to show shape only and
the board says so in print.

## Alternate hooks

1. *"Everything taught in training week is on a curve, and the curve was drawn in the 1880s."* — used. The date does the work; it makes forgetting structural rather than a failure of the rep.
2. *"Your new reps have already forgotten most of week one. That is not a discipline problem."* — warmer and more directly useful to a sales manager, but it opens on an assertion about the reader's team.
3. *"Re-reading the manual is the study method that feels like learning while doing least of it."* — the sharpest single line in the piece. Held back for the body because it lands harder after the experiment is described.
4. *"In 2006 two psychologists proved that feeling prepared and being prepared are different things."* — "proved" is too strong for one study, and the framing oversells a well-replicated but bounded finding.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"This is why our training actually sticks."* — an outcome claim about Surge with no measurement behind it. §2 bars it and there is no internal figure to rescue it.
- *"Most sales onboarding is a fire hose followed by a shrug."* — a swipe at the category, unsourced.
- *"You forget 70% of it within 24 hours."* — **the exact content-farm number this project exists to catch.** Traced to mangled retellings of Ebbinghaus, not to either paper. Cut, and the cut is documented on the asset itself.
- *"Spaced repetition fixes this."* — true-ish and out of scope. Neither cited paper tests spacing; that is a different literature (Cepeda et al.) and would need its own sourcing.
- *"Ebbinghaus ran the experiment on himself, which is either rigour or a cry for help."* — good joke, wrong register for company voice.

**Two open items, both stated on the asset rather than hidden:**

1. The 2015 replication is **a single subject**. That is normal for this paradigm and it is still a single subject; the post says "one subject" in the copy rather than implying a large sample.
2. The specific savings percentages at each interval were not verified against the paper's tables. Nothing numeric from the curve is published as a result.

**Training-material check:** the closing line describes the morning development block —
goal-setting, whiteboard drilling, a taught subject, paired practice. That is on the **Clear to
use** list, with the standing condition of **no clock times**. None appear. The per-instance
sign-off gate on `training-source/` was lifted 2026-08-15, so this draft carries no approval block.
