## The post

> The most quoted number in sales coaching was retired by the people who published it.
>
> In 2016 Gong put out a golden ratio for sales calls: talk 43% of the time, listen 57%. It has been repeated ever since, usually with no date attached.
>
> In March 2025 Gong updated the piece against 326,000 calls of ten minutes or longer. The average call is 60% talking. Deals that closed averaged 57%. Deals that were lost averaged 62%.
>
> Five points separate a won call from a lost one. The old rule asked reps to cut seventeen.
>
> The finding that replaced it is a different shape. High performers held roughly the same talk ratio whether they won or lost. Low performers swung ten points, from 54% on deals they won to 64% on deals they lost.
>
> Our read: the ratio was never the skill. A rep whose talk time climbs ten points on a bad call is not talking too much on purpose. They have lost the thread and are filling silence. The swing is the tell, and a target number hides it.
>
> What is a number your team coaches to that nobody has re-checked?

## Alternate hooks

1. *"The most quoted number in sales coaching was retired by the people who published it."* — used. Puts the reversal first and makes the source the authority against itself.
2. *"43:57 is a 2016 number and Gong does not use it any more."* — sharper and more checkable, but it opens on jargon that only lands for people who already know the ratio.
3. *"Five percentage points separate a won call from a lost one."* — number-first. Strong, but it needs a sentence of setup before it means anything.
4. *"Your talk-time target is probably nine years old."* — good hook, slightly accusatory in a register that is supposed to be analytical.

## Lines cut, with reasons

- *"So every talk-time scorecard in the industry is measuring the wrong thing."* — a verdict about other companies, and §3 bars naming or characterising competitors. It also overstates: the data says the target is weak, not that measuring is wrong.
- *"We coach the swing, not the number."* — a training claim I cannot source. Nobody has confirmed what Surge coaches on talk time. Replaced with what the data implies for anyone coaching.
- *"This is why most sales training is theatre."* — landing line, and the kind of broad swipe §5 says reads as confirmation.
- *"Gong's own data now contradicts Gong's most famous blog post."* — true and quotable, but it makes the post about a company rather than about the finding.

| High performers hold roughly the same ratio win or lose; low performers swing 54% → 64% | **Verified** | Same page |
| "Five points, not seventeen" | **Arithmetic on verified figures** | 62 − 57 = 5; 60 − 43 = 17. Presented as a comparison, not as a Gong claim |

**Checked and rejected:** every aggregator result for this topic still reports 43:57 as current,
and several attribute a 46:54 discovery-call figure to Gong. The 2025 page carries neither.
This is the stale-number pattern the research standard exists to catch, and it is now also
the subject of the post.

**One open item:** Gong is a sales software company, not an outsourced sales firm, so naming
it does not trip §3. Flagging it anyway because it is the only company named in the set.
