01

The listening ratio everyone quotes is gone

tutorial-carousel / monocolor · e095104b
The most quoted number in sales coaching was retired by the people who published it. In 2016 Gong put out a golden ratio for sales calls: talk 43% of the time, listen 57%. It has been repeated ever since, usually with no date attached. In March 2025 Gong updated the piece against 326,000 calls of ten minutes or longer. The average call is 60% talking. Deals that closed averaged 57%. Deals that were lost averaged 62%. Five points separate a won call from a lost one. The old rule asked reps to cut seventeen. The finding that replaced it is a different shape. High performers held roughly the same talk ratio whether they won or lost. Low performers swung ten points, from 54% on deals they won to 64% on deals they lost. Our read: the ratio was never the skill. A rep whose talk time climbs ten points on a bad call is not talking too much on purpose. They have lost the thread and are filling silence. The swing is the tell, and a target number hides it. What is a number your team coaches to that nobody has re-checked?
198 words. Source: Gong, 326K calls, updated 20 March 2025.

02

11 to 14 questions, then it drops

quote-card / paper background · f941e306
Every sales team has heard the number: 11 to 14 questions on a discovery call. It comes from Gong's analysis of more than 519,000 recorded B2B calls. Success peaks in that band and falls back to average above it. It is also the least useful part of the finding. The same research looked at where the questions sat, not just how many there were. Average reps front-load them like a checklist. Top performers spread them evenly across the call. Fourteen questions in the first four minutes is an intake form. The same fourteen spread out is a conversation. The count is identical and only one of them is discovery. Our read: a question count is easy to hand a new rep, and easy to hit while still running an interrogation. Where the questions sit is the part that takes coaching, and it is the part nobody quotes. One caveat we are not going to hide. This is B2B call data. Our reps work doorsteps and events, where the conversation is shorter and nobody booked it. The spread finding travels. The count probably does not. Where does your team's discovery actually sit?
199 words. Source: Gong, 519,000+ recorded B2B calls.

03

What is left of training week by Monday

hand-authored whiteboard · house style
Everything taught in training week is on a curve, and the curve was drawn in the 1880s. Ebbinghaus measured his own forgetting and found most of the loss happens early. In 2015 Murre and Dros published a full replication in PLOS ONE: one subject, 70 hours of learning, relearning at six intervals from 20 minutes out to 31 days. The curve held. The more useful study is Roediger and Karpicke, Psychological Science, 2006. Students read a passage, then either re-read it or sat a recall test with no feedback, the same number of times. Five minutes later, re-reading won. Two days and a week later, testing won, substantially. The part worth sitting with: repeated studying increased students' confidence in their ability to remember. The group that felt readiest retained less. Our read: nobody forgets the pitch because they did not care. They forget it because that is what memory does. Which makes "we covered that in training" a statement about the calendar, not about what a rep can do at a door. The morning block is drilling and paired practice for exactly this reason — recall out loud, not review. What does your team do in the week after training week?
200 words. Sources: Murre & Dros 2015, PLOS ONE; Roediger & Karpicke 2006, Psychological Science.

04

Deliberate practice explains less than it is sold as

tweet-card / dark minimal · ba413be6
A number that a company which trains salespeople should be able to look at without flinching. Macnamara, Hambrick and Oswald, Psychological Science, 2014. A meta-analysis of how much deliberate practice explains differences in performance. Games: 26%. Music: 21%. Sports: 18%. Education: 4%. Professions: under 1%. The authors' own conclusion is that deliberate practice is important, but not as important as has been argued. Under 1% is the one that matters here, because sales is a profession. Our read: that is not evidence training does nothing. It is a measurement taken across domains where nobody has agreed what practice even is. A pianist running scales has a defined thing they are repeating and a clear way to tell whether it improved. In sales the practice target is usually "get better at the doors", and that is not a target, it is a hope. Which is the honest version of what a training operation is for. Not more practice. A named thing to practise, and a way to tell whether it moved. What is the specific thing your team is drilling this week?
197 words. Source: Psychological Science 25(8):1608–1618, 2014.

05

Buyers want no rep, and they want a rep

tutorial-carousel / flat · 2491f97b
Two Gartner findings that get reported as a contradiction. One: 67% of B2B buyers say they prefer a rep-free buying experience, and 70% would rather the whole purchase were digital and self-service. Two: 69% prefer to validate AI-generated insights with a sales rep. 45% had used generative AI during the purchase, mostly to research vendors and products, and buyers drew on an average of seven separate information sources. That is a survey of 645 B2B buyers, run across August and September 2025. The number that should stop you is further down. 51% said they were more likely to run into misleading information from generative AI. 49% said the same about sales reps. Gartner's Robert Blaisdell frames it as sellers needing to show up differently rather than mattering less. Our read: those two findings are not in tension. Information stopped being the thing a rep delivers — buyers have plenty of it and trust it about as much as they trust us. Verification is what is left, and half the market already doubts the verifier. What would make your team the source someone checks their research against?
196 words. Source: Gartner press release, 20 May 2026; survey of 645 B2B buyers, Aug–Sep 2025. The only piece aimed at a prospective client rather than a prospective rep.
