## 1. What Surge is

**Outsourced sales and marketing.** Surge takes over the hiring, onboarding, training, and management of sales teams for companies that don't have the infrastructure to run one themselves.

**Channels:** direct/field sales — door-to-door, cold calling, events, booths.

**Current clients** (sector only, never named — see §2): nonprofit organizations (the majority of the roster) and a roofing company. Six active plus one prospect — see [brand/notes/clients.md](brand/notes/clients.md), restricted, context only.

> **Corrected 2026-09-14.** This previously read "an air/HVAC company." **Surge has never worked in air/HVAC** — it was a mis-transcription of *aeration* that survived months as a confirmed fact. An **aeration** client is expected but the details are not settled, so it is a prospect, not a current client: do not write sector specifics for it, and do not count it in the roster, until Matthew says it has landed.

**Why customers choose Surge:** track record against metrics — money raised, products sold, customers acquired — plus company culture and work ethic.

**What Surge is not:** a differentiated methodology. Your own words: *"our method and process isn't unique and a lot of other sales teams run the same business model and practices."* **Never claim a proprietary system, a unique process, or a method nobody else has.** It isn't true, it's checkable, and in this industry that specific claim is a tell.

**But the distinction is narrower than "we have nothing to claim."** Refined 2026-08-05: the *information* is valuable and the *transfer* is where Surge is better — how it's taught, how well it lands with people, and the years of field experience behind it. That is claimable, because it's a claim about execution rather than invention.

The practical difference:

| Don't write | Do write |
|---|---|
| "Our proprietary sales system" | "Here's how we teach objection handling to someone in week one" |
| "A method nobody else has" | "This is the part most training skips, and it's why reps stall in month two" |
| "Revolutionary approach to field sales" | "Twelve years of door-to-door taught us this, and here's the specific move" |

Nobody has to believe the idea is new. They have to believe you can teach it. Showing the teaching proves that; asserting it doesn't.

**Stage:** new company. No published posts, no performance history, no audience yet. Everything is a first impression.

## 2. Constraints

**Cannot claim:**

- **Customer names in prose** — no sentence, heading, caption or alt text calls an organisation a client. Sector alone. The reason is consent, not roster size: no client hired Surge in order to become marketing material. "Our roofing client in Indiana" is out for the same reason a name is.
  > **Client logos and branding are cleared** — updated 2026-09-14. A client mark may be used *as a logo* (client wall, deck, social graphic), and client merch in photos and video is fine. Matthew confirmed 2026-09-14 that the clients have notified him and it is cleared currently. The earlier "explicit written permission" bar is lifted; the permission is verbal and relayed through him, which is a recorded risk, not a blocker. The prose rule above is unchanged and is the standing mitigation. See the 2026-09-11 revision in [brand/notes/RESTRICTED.md](brand/notes/RESTRICTED.md), which governs.
- **Security or compliance claims** — no certifications, audit status, or security posture claims about Surge.
- **A unique or proprietary sales method** — see §1.

**Permitted, with conditions:** revenue, growth, headcount, funding, and performance figures.

**The metrics condition.** Results are the entire differentiator, which makes numbers the most valuable and most dangerous thing in the copy. Every figure needs:

1. A **defined scope** — over what period, for which campaign. "$2M raised" means nothing without "across 14 months for nonprofit clients."
2. A **current source** — an internal record someone could pull today, not a number remembered from last year.
3. **Anonymization that survives** — aggregate across clients where possible, since per-client figures identify the client.
4. **No implied causation you can't support** — "we raised $X" and "revenue grew X% while we ran their sales" are different claims.

Permitted means "not categorically off-limits," not "unverified is fine."

## 3. Competitors — never name them

**The rule is now enforceable.** Competitors are other outsourced sales and marketing firms, direct-sales organizations, and contract sales teams — anyone selling companies a sales force they don't have. That's a category, not a list, so the test is: *does this company sell outsourced selling?* If yes, don't name it. Not neutrally, not as market context, not as a citation.

Because the model isn't differentiated (§1), comparison posts are a losing format anyway — they invite a direct feature comparison Surge can't win on method. Compete on results and on how the work is done.

## 4. Attribution and voice

- **"We"** for the organization, keeping the personal voice's directness and opinions
- Posts as the company, sounding like a person rather than a committee
- Where the personal version says "My read:", the company version says "Our read:" — the opinion survives the switch
- **No profanity** in company posts (personal posts may — personal spec §2)

### Confidence — added 2026-08-17

Everything public is read by a prospective **client** and a prospective **rep**
at the same time, and both have to come away certain Surge knows what it is
doing: the selling for one, the training and growth for the other. Established
by Matthew during a page-by-page pass of the website, and it applies to social
copy for the same reason.

**Cut from most copy:**

- **"If" used to question capability** — "if that describes the work", "if we
  can reach your customers". It reads as asking permission.
- **Questions the reader can answer no to** — "Need something else?", "Not one
  of these?" They hand over the frame and plant a doubt the reader did not
  arrive with. *This is narrower than it looks: it does not touch the closing
  question a post ends on (see the personal spec §2), which is a genuinely open
  question he wants an answer to, not a rhetorical one that invites a shrug.*
- **Advisory and conditional verbs** — "should ask us about", "can move across
  too", "usually enough", "whether we are a fit".

**The technique: cut the hedge, keep the bound.** The honest limit almost always
survives as a different part of speech. "If a person can start the conversation,
we can sell it" → "Anything that sells in a conversation, we can sell." Same
scope, no conditional, opens on "anything" rather than on a caveat. Changing the
grammar is the move; widening the claim is not.

**Confidence goes in the verb, never in the scope.** This does not loosen §2 or
§3 by one inch, and it must never be read as licence for "the best training in
the industry", a unique method (§1), or a metric without its scope and source.
Vague breadth is also exactly what reads as confirmation to a reader primed by
§5 — so the confident version of a claim is the *specific* one, not the big one.

Caveats are relocated rather than deleted: turnover, operational limits and
"we may not be a fit" are conversations for a meeting, not published lines.
Silence about a limitation is fine; asserting the opposite is not.

## 5. The reputation problem

**Rewritten 2026-08-06.** The previous version of this section concluded that "on the facts, Surge is neither an MLM nor a pyramid scheme." **That conclusion was wrong and has been removed.** It was written before the compensation structure was known. Anything drafted against the old version needs re-checking.

Surge is publicly accused of operating like an MLM or a devilcorp, including at least one r/Devilcorp thread. Asked directly on 2026-08-06 whether the company's own self-description reads that way, you said: *"I completely agree… and in a lot of ways I believe it is."* That is the honest starting point, and this section is written from it.

### What the terms mean

- A **pyramid scheme** pays participants primarily for recruiting others rather than for selling to genuine end customers, and usually requires a buy-in. It is illegal.
- An **MLM** pays people on their own sales plus a share of their recruits' sales.

### What is actually true of Surge

Working from [brand/notes/hiring.md](brand/notes/hiring.md) — all of it restricted, context only, never content:

- **Reps pay nothing to participate.** No buy-in, no training fee, no required purchases. Gas and food only, as in any field job.
- **There is a real product and a real end customer.** Reps sign up genuine donors for client charities. Money enters the system from outside, not from participants.
- **There is a guaranteed weekly base**, paid whether or not quota is hit.
- **But there is also a team-performance override.** Reps recruit and build their own team and are paid a bonus on that team's production. Your own words: *"similar to normal MLMs."*

### What follows, precisely

**"Surge is not a pyramid scheme" remains defensible.** No buy-in, and income originates from real end customers rather than from recruits paying in. Those are the two defining features and both are absent.

**"Surge is not an MLM" is no longer assertable, and must never be claimed.** Compensation on a downline's production is MLM-shaped by definition. The distinction is real but too fine to carry a public claim: the override supplements a base and per-signup pay rather than replacing them. That is a meaningful difference in degree, not a clean difference in kind — and it is nowhere near solid enough to stake the brand on.

**So the honest position is: the shape is genuinely there, the two illegal features are not.** That is a defensible place to stand in a conversation. It is a terrible place to stand in a comment section, because it requires three paragraphs of nuance and the accusation requires one word.

### The rules

- **Never argue with the accusation in a post.** Unchanged, and now doubly binding — the defence is narrower than previously believed. "We're not an MLM" is now also inaccurate. Handle this one-to-one and in interviews, never in the feed.
- **Never assert Surge is not an MLM** anywhere, in any register, including in replies and comments.
- **Answer structurally instead** — specificity about the actual job wherever it comes up (personal spec §9). Specificity is the counter-argument; making it beats claiming it.
- **No culture content that runs on vibes.** Team photos captioned with energy, hustle, and family are the exact texture cited as evidence. Culture posts carry a fact: what someone was taught, what they now do.
- **Never imply an entry-level rep is an owner, partner, or executive.**
- **No scale, footprint, or growth claims.** The Denver, Asheville and Indianapolis locations are affiliate, not Surge-operated; the Houston room is **closed**. No "offices in five cities," no maps, no nationwide framing. See [brand/notes/company-facts.md](brand/notes/company-facts.md).

  > **Service area is the exception, and it is not optional — settled 2026-09-14.** That bullet restricts *structure*, never *where Surge sells*. Copy may not imply Surge only sells in Colorado; it did, and it was false. **The settled phrase is "clients in multiple states."** Three wordings were tried and rejected:
  >
  > | Wording | Why not |
  > |---|---|
  > | "Every campaign runs out of Englewood, Colorado…" | Leads with the limitation. Matthew: *"it seems so limiting… it scares off out of state companies."* |
  > | "clients in **other** states" | Takes Colorado as the reference point, so it reads as *everywhere but here* — the same Colorado-shaped frame, inverted. |
  > | "clients **across the US**" | Nationwide framing, barred by name in the bullet above. |
  >
  > **Never name the states.** [brand/notes/clients.md](brand/notes/clients.md) records that the sector plus those two particular states identifies both organizations in one line — the Tier 4 prose rule. "Multiple states" carries the reassurance without the identification.
  >
  > **Where the location may still be named**, per Matthew's 2026-09-11 rule: only where the subject genuinely *is* the location, the office, or hiring. Pairing the location with the service line is positioning, and that is what made the site read as Colorado-only.
- **No earnings claims, explicit or atmospheric,** on recruiting-facing content. The clawback, the override, and high turnover mean a post can mislead without stating a number. Flag list in [brand/notes/hiring.md](brand/notes/hiring.md).
- **Never publish the influence-technique material.** GIFTS, the closing circle, and the scripted objection rebuttals are the strongest confirmation a critic could be handed, authored by Surge. See [training-publishability-review.md](training-publishability-review.md).

### The one thing that actually improves this position

Not argument — **verifiable specifics**, which is the same conclusion §1 and §6 reach by a different route. Teaching the material well, in Surge's own words, with real numbers once they exist, is the only move that changes anyone's mind. Everything else in this section is damage control.

## 6. Territory and content strategy

**Own:** sales, consulting, business management, onboarding, training.

**Revised 2026-08-06.** The engine was previously "teach the material" — post the training manuals directly. The user has since clarified that the training docs were supplied as **context about the business, not as content fodder**. That version is superseded.

### The engine now: researched fact + his read, with training material as seasoning

**Two ingredients per post.**

1. **A verifiable external fact** — from a named outlet or primary study. Sources that clear the research standard: Gong Labs, HubSpot and Salesforce annual research, HBR, academic sales research. **"Top sales blogs" mostly do not** — that's the SEO-farm pattern the standard exists to catch. If a topic has no solid source, say so rather than reaching for a listicle.
2. **His actual opinion on it.** Per §4, stating the professional view plainly is the point. The fact is citable by anyone; the read is not. **This is where the differentiation now lives** — when the underlying facts are commodity, the interpretation is the product.

**Training material is on the table, contextually.** Usable as a *"here's how we do it here"* counterpoint to the researched fact — not as the post's substance. It seasons; it doesn't carry.

> ### Sign-off rule — training material requires per-use approval
>
> **Any draft drawing on `training-source/` carries a visible block at the top: what's being used, how it's framed, and an explicit ask.** The user approves or strikes it before the draft is final.
>
> Deliver the draft *with* the ask attached rather than asking permission first — he reacts to concrete material better than he specifies against a question. Approval for one use is never approval for the next.
>
> This is a **workflow gate, not a restriction.** Genuinely restricted training material is a separate matter and listed in [brand/notes/RESTRICTED.md](brand/notes/RESTRICTED.md).

**What this costs, stated honestly:** the old engine's advantage was that it couldn't be copied off a competitor's site. Research-led content can be. The counterweight is the opinion layer and the occasional "how we do it here" — so if a draft has no real view in it, it has no reason to exist.

Formats that follow from that: the one thing new reps get wrong in week one; a specific objection and the actual response; what the manual says versus what the doorstep teaches; what happens before anyone picks up a phone (the morning development block — see [brand/notes/daily-process.md](brand/notes/daily-process.md)).

**Withdrawn 2026-08-06 — "what a real day looks like hour by hour."** This section previously recommended it. It's now a hard no. The real schedule documents roughly an hour of employer-directed activity before the paid day starts, and "long hours for low effective hourly rate" is one of the criticisms §5 names specifically. The honest version of that post is damaging and the flattering version is dishonest, so there's no version to write. **No clock times, no shift boundaries, no weekly-hours framing in any draft.** The morning *routine* is excellent material; the *timetable* is off-limits.

**Weakest available material:** anything implying the method is novel, and any culture post running on vibes rather than a fact.
